## The Scout's Take: Beaches, Downtown or the Stadium Corridor?

Los Angeles isn't a "stay in the centre and walk everywhere" city — it's a basin of distinct districts, and the right base depends on what your trip is built around. For a beach-led holiday, Santa Monica and the western edge win: sand, the pier, the Metro E Line straight into Downtown, and a walkable core that feels manageable after an 11-hour flight. For a culture-and-food trip, Downtown LA has the museums, the concert hall, the food halls and — crucially for the World Cup — the strongest public-transport links to Inglewood. For the classic LA postcard, Hollywood delivers the Walk of Fame, the sign and the studio history.

For the 2026 World Cup specifically, there's a fourth consideration most LA guides skip: the LAX and Inglewood corridor. It's not glamorous, but on match day it's the most practical place to sleep, sitting minutes from the stadium and on the Metro lines that feed it. Many UK fans split the difference — base in Santa Monica or Downtown for the atmosphere, then plan a tight match-day route into Inglewood rather than staying in the stadium's shadow, where hotel stock is thin.

## Going to the 2026 World Cup in Los Angeles?

The venue is Los Angeles Stadium — everyday name SoFi Stadium — in Inglewood, about 10 miles south-west of Downtown and a short hop from LAX. Los Angeles hosts multiple 2026 World Cup matches at SoFi Stadium in Inglewood, the most spectacular stadium in the tournament, so getting your base and your match-day route right matters more here than in almost any other host city.

**Where to base yourself.** Inglewood itself has limited hotel inventory, so most visitors sleep elsewhere and travel in. The three best strategies: stay along the **LAX/Westchester/El Segundo corridor** for the shortest match-day journey (10–20 minutes by car or rideshare); stay in **Downtown LA** for the best public-transport links plus a proper city-break base between games; or stay in **Santa Monica** for the beach holiday and accept a longer but scenic run into Inglewood on match days.

**How to reach the stadium.** The cleanest public-transport route is the **Metro K Line to Downtown Inglewood station**, then a short walk or local shuttle to the stadium. From Downtown LA, connect through the Metro network onto the K Line; from LAX, the C Line links across to the K Line via the airport's people-mover. Driving works too — the stadium has thousands of parking spaces — but **pre-book parking and allow 60–90 minutes of buffer**, because Inglewood traffic builds heavily around kickoff. Rideshare drop-offs sit a few minutes' walk from the gates and are often the simplest option for a group.

For the World Cup it's a longer run to Inglewood than the LAX corridor, but the trade-off is sand, sea air and a genuinely relaxing recovery zone between matches.
