## The Scout's Take: Downtown, Santa Monica, Hollywood, Anaheim or Long Beach?

Unlike a compact European city, Los Angeles doesn't have a single "old town" to anchor a guide around. It's five distinct bases, and picking the right one matters more here than in almost any city we cover.

**Downtown LA (DTLA)** is the culture play — the Walt Disney Concert Hall, The Broad, Grand Central Market, and the widest concentration of both luxury and mid-range hotels in this guide. It's also the most transit-connected base, with the Metro rail network reaching Hollywood, the airport corridor and beyond. Resort fees and $50-70 overnight parking are more common here than anywhere else on this list, so factor the all-in price before comparing headline rates.

**Santa Monica** is the beach-holiday base — sand, the pier, Third Street Promenade, and a genuinely relaxing recovery zone between busier days. It's also where this guide's single luxury beachfront pick, the Fairmont Miramar, sits. There's no budget tier here: beachfront Santa Monica commands a premium across nearly every property, which is exactly why the 29 budget hotels in this guide concentrate elsewhere.

**Hollywood** delivers the postcard LA — the Walk of Fame, the TCL Chinese Theatre, the studio tours — with a strong mid-range hotel cluster (Dream Hollywood, Kimpton Everly, HOLLYWOOD VOLUME) and one standout budget address, The Dixie Hollywood, the most-reviewed hotel across this entire guide's budget tier.

**Anaheim** is where Disneyland and Disney California Adventure actually are, 30 miles south-east of Downtown. It carries both ends of this guide's price range: the two on-site Disney resort hotels at the very top (£716-£798), and the overwhelming majority of the 29 budget hotels at the bottom (£62-£90), most within shuttle or walking distance of the parks. If Disney is the point of your trip, Anaheim is the only base worth seriously considering.

**Long Beach** rounds out the picture — a seaside city in its own right, home to the Aquarium of the Pacific and the retired Queen Mary ocean liner, roughly equidistant between Downtown and Anaheim. It contributes this guide's second-highest luxury pick (Hyatt Regency Long Beach, the most-reviewed hotel in the luxury tier) and a compact cluster of budget seaside motels, making it a smart compromise base for a trip that mixes a bit of everything.

For a first LA trip built around the city itself: Downtown or Santa Monica. For a Disney-first family trip: Anaheim, without much debate. For a cruise departure or a seaside compromise between the two: Long Beach.

## Beyond the Parks — Los Angeles's Essentials

A few non-Disney experiences worth planning your Downtown or Hollywood days around:

- **Griffith Observatory at sunset** — free admission, panoramic views over the LA basin and the Hollywood Sign, and one of the best free things to do in the city.
- **Hollywood Walk of Fame and TCL Chinese Theatre** — the stretch of Hollywood Boulevard lined with star plaques, best combined with a Dream Hollywood or Kimpton Everly stay from this guide's mid-range tier.
- **Universal Studios Hollywood** — the working studio tour and theme park, a natural pairing with a Hollywood-based stay.
- **Venice Beach boardwalk** — street performers, Muscle Beach and the canals a short walk south, best reached from a Santa Monica base.
- **A Malibu coastal drive** — the Pacific Coast Highway north from Santa Monica, with beach stops the whole way.
- **Rodeo Drive, Beverly Hills** — the palm-lined luxury shopping strip, a short drive or rideshare from any Downtown or Hollywood hotel in this guide.
- **Warner Bros. and Paramount studio tours** — behind-the-scenes access to working studio lots, both an easy add-on to a Hollywood-based stay.
- **Aquarium of the Pacific and the Queen Mary, Long Beach** — a full day of marine life and the retired 1936 ocean liner, both walkable from this guide's Long Beach hotel cluster.
- **Third Street Promenade and the Santa Monica Pier** — open-air shopping, the pier's Ferris wheel and arcade, and sunset over the Pacific, all within a short walk of the Fairmont Miramar.

Weaving a couple of these into a Downtown or Hollywood stay costs nothing beyond a Metro ticket or a short rideshare, and turns even the budget-tier trips in this guide into a full LA holiday rather than a Disney-only visit.

## How Los Angeles Compares to Las Vegas and Miami

Los Angeles sits pricier than Las Vegas for comparable luxury — a Downtown 5-star like The Aster or Conrad runs well above a Center Strip icon like Bellagio once resort fees are accounted for, though LA has no resort-fee culture to match the Strip's. Against Miami, LA's luxury tier is broadly similar in price but spread across a much larger area, meaning the choice of sub-region (Downtown, Santa Monica, Anaheim) matters more to the overall cost of the trip than in either Vegas or Miami. Where LA pulls furthest away from both cities is the Disney premium: nothing in Vegas or Miami touches the £700-800 on-site Disneyland resort rates, and nothing in either city has quite the density of sub-£90 motels that Anaheim offers as the trade-off. The three cities pair naturally for a UK multi-city US trip — LA to Vegas is a 4-hour drive or 70-minute flight across the Mojave, and LA to Miami is a cross-country hop and a complete change of coast and climate.

- **Direct UK flights**: BA, Virgin Atlantic, American, United and Delta all fly LHR-LAX direct in around 11 hours.

- **Getting between sub-regions**: the Metro rail network links Downtown, Hollywood and the airport corridor reasonably well, but Anaheim and Long Beach are best reached by hire car or a pre-booked transfer — factor this into your budget if your trip spans more than one of this guide's five sub-regions.

## Booking Los Angeles Hotels in 2026: What Drives the Price

Anaheim hotel rates track the Disneyland Resort's own calendar closely — school holidays, major park events and US public holidays all push rates up across every tier, from the on-site resorts to the budget motel strip. Downtown and Santa Monica rates move more with general LA tourism and convention season, so a quiet week for one sub-region can be a peak week for another depending on what's driving demand. The cleanest way to save across any tier: book midweek, avoid school holiday weeks if Disney isn't the point of the trip, and compare the all-in price (room plus resort fee plus parking) rather than the headline rate — a £150 Downtown mid-range room with a $50 resort fee and $60 overnight parking can end up costing more per night than a luxury-tier Anaheim hotel with neither.
