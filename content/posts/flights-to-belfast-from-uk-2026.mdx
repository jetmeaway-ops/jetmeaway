## Quick answers

**What's the cheapest airline to Belfast?** **easyJet** on headline price (from ~£25-£50 off-peak) to both airports. **Aer Lingus** has the most flights into the central City airport; **Jet2** to International with a bag; **BA and Ryanair** also fly.

**How long is the flight?** About **1 hour to 1 hour 25 minutes** from GB.

**City or International?** **Belfast City (BHD)** is 2 miles from the centre (Aer Lingus, easyJet, BA) — the convenient choice. **Belfast International (BFS)** is 20 miles out (easyJet, Jet2, Ryanair) — often cheaper.

**Which UK airport is cheapest?** Whichever is **nearest** — Belfast flies from many GB airports.

**When should I book?** **3-6 weeks** ahead most months; **6-10 weeks** for summer, the Twelfth and Christmas.

**Do I need a passport?** No — it's a **domestic** flight, but airlines require **photo ID**.

<a id="airports"></a>

## Belfast City (BHD) vs Belfast International (BFS)

Belfast has two airports at very different distances from the city — this is the key decision on the route.

| Airport | Distance to centre | Transfer | Airlines |
|---|---|---|---|
| George Best Belfast City (BHD) | ~2 miles | Airport Express 600 bus, ~15 min | Aer Lingus, easyJet, BA |
| Belfast International (BFS) | ~20 miles | Airport Express 300 bus, ~30-40 min | easyJet, Jet2, Ryanair |

**The convenience call.** **Belfast City (BHD)** is barely outside the centre and mostly handles domestic UK flights — for a short city break it's the easier arrival. **Belfast International (BFS)** is 20 miles out with a longer bus, but often has cheaper fares (especially on Jet2 and Ryanair). If a City fare is close, it usually wins on convenience; if International is clearly cheaper and you don't mind the transfer, it can be better value. Compare the all-in cost including the transfer.

## Airline-by-airline comparison (2026)

### easyJet — the price leader

**Typical fare 2026:** £25-£60 return off-peak, hand-luggage only.
**Pros:** The lowest headline fares, flying to both Belfast airports from a wide spread of GB airports. Frequent flights, regular sales.
**Cons:** Cheapest fares are cabin-only; hold bag and allocated seat cost extra.

### Aer Lingus — the most flights to the central airport

**Typical fare 2026:** £35-£80 return; cheapest fares cabin-only.
**Pros:** The most flights into the convenient Belfast City (around 166 a week), a comfortable product, and higher fare tiers include a bag.
**Cons:** Higher headline fare than easyJet; check the fare tier for baggage.

### Jet2, BA & Ryanair

**Typical fare 2026:** Jet2 with baggage included to Belfast International; BA to Belfast City with a bag on standard fares; Ryanair cheapest to International.
**Pros:** Jet2 for all-in value with a bag; BA for the central airport and Avios; Ryanair for the lowest headline fare to International.
**Cons:** Check the airport and fare type.

**The honest read:** hand-luggage only, easyJet wins on fare. For the central City airport, Aer Lingus or BA; for a bag included, Jet2. Always compare the all-in price and the airport.

## Fly or ferry to Belfast?

Belfast is one of the few UK routes with a real ferry alternative, since it's across the Irish Sea:

- **Flying** is fastest (about an hour) and often similarly priced for foot passengers — the default for a short trip without a car.
- **The ferry** (Cairnryan to Belfast or Larne, or Liverpool to Belfast) wins if you're **taking a car**, travelling with lots of luggage, or prefer not to fly. It's a scenic crossing but takes several hours.

For most short city breaks, fly. For a road trip around Northern Ireland and the Antrim Coast with your own car, the ferry is usually the answer.

## Do you need ID or a passport?

Belfast is in the UK, so a flight from Great Britain is **domestic** — no passport is legally required. But **every airline requires an accepted form of photo ID**, and each sets its own list. Most accept a valid passport, and some accept a photo driving licence on domestic routes. **Check your specific airline's accepted-ID list before travelling** and take an ID it accepts.

## Common mistakes UK-Belfast travellers make

1. **Booking Belfast International without checking the transfer.** It's 20 miles out — a City fare that's only slightly more can be better value.
2. **Judging fares on the headline price.** A cheap International fare plus a bag and the longer bus can match a City fare with a bag included.
3. **Travelling with no accepted photo ID.** It's domestic, but airlines require ID — take one they accept.
4. **Ignoring the ferry when taking a car.** For a road trip around NI, the car ferry is usually better than flying and hiring.
5. **Booking mid-July late.** School holidays and the Twelfth lift demand — book earlier.
6. **Adding baggage at the airport.** Always add it online, or travel hand-luggage only for a weekend.
7. **Not planning the Causeway.** The Giant's Causeway and Antrim Coast need a car or tour — sort it before you go.
