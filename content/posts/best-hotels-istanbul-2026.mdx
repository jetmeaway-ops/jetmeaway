## The Scout's Take: Sultanahmet, Beyoğlu, Beşiktaş, or the Asian Side?

**Sultanahmet (Old City)** is the historic peninsula — Hagia Sophia, the Blue Mosque, Topkapı Palace, the Grand Bazaar and the Basilica Cistern within a 10-15 minute walk of each other. This is also where **Fatih**, the wider historic district around it, holds the deepest cluster of budget hotels in this guide — real 3★ and unrated hotels from around £30-40 a night, all inside walking distance of the monuments. Quiet after dark, tourist-dense by day.

**Beyoğlu (Taksim and Galata)** is the 19th-century European-influenced quarter — İstiklal Caddesi's pedestrian mile, the Galata Tower, Karaköy's boutique-hotel and rooftop-bar scene. This is Istanbul's nightlife and design centre, walkable from Sultanahmet across Galata Bridge and connected by the T1 tram and the funicular.

**Beşiktaş** is the Bosphorus palace shore — Çırağan Palace, Dolmabahçe Palace, and most of this guide's luxury waterfront tier. **Şişli**, just inland, is the modern business-and-shopping district with a deep run of well-priced 4-5★ hotel stock and several of the guide's Bosphorus-facing towers.

**Kadıköy (Asian side)** is the unmediated, neighbourhood Istanbul — a fish market, a produce market, cafe culture that runs late without tourist infrastructure. Reached by a 20-minute ferry from Eminönü, the finest urban boat journey in the world.

For first-time Istanbul: Sultanahmet. For nightlife and design: Beyoğlu/Karaköy. For the Bosphorus at its most cinematic: Beşiktaş. For tight budgets: this guide's [budget tier](#budget-hotels) — Sultanahmet, Fatih and Şişli are where £30-35 a night is still real. ### Sultanahmet, Beyoğlu, Beşiktaş or the Asian Side — How to Choose

The **Asian side** (Kadıköy) is the unmediated, neighbourhood Istanbul most visitors miss — base there only on a repeat trip, but take the 20-minute Eminönü–Kadıköy ferry regardless.

## How Istanbul Compares to Athens, Bangkok, Dubai and Marrakech

Istanbul sits in a different price and character bracket to each of the other hubs UK travellers compare it against. [Athens](/blog/best-hotels-athens-2026) is the small, walkable one — the Acropolis as the single defining sight, the Plaka and Monastiraki core crossable in 30 minutes — where Istanbul is the layered giant, 16 million people with Byzantine, Ottoman and 19th-century European architecture stacked on top of each other across a city Athens' size wouldn't cover in a single district. [Bangkok](/blog/best-hotels-bangkok-2026) undercuts Istanbul at the very top of the market — Bangkok's five-star towers routinely run cheaper than Istanbul's Bosphorus palaces — but Istanbul's budget tier is more walkable, with Sultanahmet's £30-40 cluster sitting inside the same historic radius as the major sights, rather than requiring a Skytrain ride. [Dubai](/blog/best-hotels-dubai-2026) is the luxury outlier of the group — nothing in Istanbul touches Burj Al Arab pricing, and Dubai has almost no real budget hotel tier at all, making Istanbul the far better choice for anyone travelling on less than £100 a night. [Marrakech](/blog/best-hotels-marrakech-2026) is the closest match in spirit — both cities layer a historic old-town core (the Medina, Sultanahmet) with a modern new-town extension (Gueliz, Şişli/Beyoğlu) — but Istanbul's budget stock is deeper and its luxury tier more expensive, reflecting its status as a genuine global capital rather than a boutique-riad destination.

## Beyond the Hotel: Istanbul's Essential Experiences for 2026

**Hagia Sophia at Opening (9am).** Now a functioning mosque, free entry for worshippers, ticketed entry for tourists (book at muze.gov.tr). Go before the tour groups arrive.

**Topkapı Palace and the Harem.** The administrative and residential heart of the Ottoman Empire for 400 years. The Harem section is a separate, guided-tour-only ticket. Allow a full day.

**Grand Bazaar — Wednesday Morning.** The world's oldest covered market (1461), 61 covered streets, 4,000 shops. Wednesday mornings are the least tourist-pushy version.

**Bosphorus Ferry — Eminönü to Kadıköy.** The 20-minute public commuter ferry costs under £0.50 and delivers the finest urban ferry view in the world — Topkapı Palace, Hagia Sophia, the suspension bridge, the Asian hills. Take it at dusk.

**Basilica Cistern.** The 532 AD underground Byzantine water cistern beneath Sultanahmet — 336 columns, two Medusa-head column bases. Book at yerebatan.com; queues build by midday.

**Turkish Hammam — Çemberlitaş Hamamı.** The 1584 hammam designed by Sinan, the Ottoman Empire's chief architect — the finest functioning traditional hammam in the city. Book the private room for couples.

**Galata Tower Sunset.** The 14th-century Genoese tower — the best 360° Istanbul view available without paying hotel rates. Book at galatakulesi.com.

**Kadıköy Fish Market and Moda Cafe Culture.** Cross to the Asian side and walk the fish market, then continue to Moda's seafront promenade for coffee.

**Spice Bazaar and Galata Bridge.** The 1664 covered spice market, smaller and more focused than the Grand Bazaar — the best source for Turkish saffron, dried figs and lokum.

**Princes' Islands (Büyükada) — Day Trip.** Nine islands in the Marmara Sea, no cars allowed, accessible by ferry from Kabataş (55 minutes). The most peaceful hour available within Istanbul's administrative boundary.

## The Turkish Breakfast (Kahvaltı) Ritual

The defining Istanbul meal is breakfast — *kahvaltı* — a two-hour spread of 12-15 small plates: white cheese, olives, sliced tomato and cucumber, sucuk fried in butter, menemen, börek, simit, kaymak with honey, fresh bread, and Turkish tea served continuously in tulip-shaped glasses. Van Kahvaltı Evi in Cihangir is the canonical address. Allow 90 minutes minimum, then walk it off through the Galata Tower district.

## UK Flights and Practicalities

- **Direct UK–Istanbul**: Turkish Airlines flies direct from Heathrow, Gatwick and Manchester into IST. British Airways flies LHR–IST direct. Pegasus Airlines runs budget direct routes from several UK regional airports, mostly into SAW. [Search flights to IST](/flights?to=IST).
- **Airports**: **IST** (Istanbul Airport), European side — roughly 45 minutes by metro/havaist bus to Sultanahmet, Beyoğlu, Beşiktaş or Şişli hotels. **SAW** (Sabiha Gökçen), Asian side — only sensible if you're based in Kadıköy or flying a carrier that only serves SAW; otherwise adds 60-90 minutes to reach European-side hotels.
- **Visa**: e-Visa required for UK passport holders — apply at evisa.gov.tr before travel (~$50, usually instant). Visa on arrival is no longer available for UK nationals.
- **Currency**: Turkish Lira (TRY). High inflation has kept Istanbul cheap for UK visitors through 2024-2026 — a full restaurant dinner with wine in a good Beyoğlu restaurant costs £15-25 per person. Cards are widely accepted; carry some cash for the Grand Bazaar and hammam tips.
- **Getting around**: The T1 tram covers Sultanahmet, Karaköy and the lower reaches of Beyoğlu — most hotels in this guide's Old City cluster are a 5-10 minute walk from a stop. The Marmaray tunnel and ferries connect to the Asian side.
- **Best months**: April-June and September-October (mild, 18-25°C, manageable crowds). July-August is hot (30-35°C) and crowded. November-March is cold (5-10°C) but cheap and quiet — Hagia Sophia and Topkapı without queues, hammam season at its most atmospheric.
- **Budget**: Bosphorus-palace trip — £150-500/night all-in. Budget-tier trip — £30-45/night all-in, £25-40/day food and sights. A 4-night budget trip built on this guide's cheap tier can land well under £400 per person before flights.
