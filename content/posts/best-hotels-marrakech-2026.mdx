## The Scout's Take: Medina, Gueliz/Hivernage, or Palmeraie?

The **medina** is the walled old city — everything you picture when you picture Marrakech. Riads here put you inside the maze: the souks, the Djemaa el-Fna, the tanneries become your neighbourhood, reached through 15–20 minutes of derb (narrow dead-end lane) navigation to an unmarked wooden door and a sudden courtyard silence. A riad isn't a boutique hotel in the Western sense — most have 4–20 rooms, no proper lobby, no concierge desk, the owner often lives on the property. On arrival they'll send someone to meet you at a recognisable landmark and escort you through. After that, you learn the route yourself. That transformation, chaos-to-silence, IS the experience.

**Gueliz and Hivernage** are the modern, French-built districts just outside the ramparts — Gueliz to the north-west is the commercial Ville Nouvelle (cafés, shopping streets, a real street grid a taxi driver finds instantly); Hivernage to the south-west, between Gueliz and the medina walls, is quieter and greener. This is where most of this guide's branded budget hotels sit: a normal check-in, a lift, air conditioning you don't have to ask about, and a 10–20 minute walk or a MAD 15–20 taxi into the medina.

The **Palmeraie** sits 6km north in the palm grove — a resort-hotel world, a taxi to everything, pool-and-tagine pace with Atlas views to the south. Different holiday entirely.

For first Marrakech: medina riad. For an easier logistics trip or families needing more room: Gueliz or Hivernage. For pure resort downtime: Palmeraie. For tight budgets: this guide's [budget tier](#budget-hotels) — genuine medina riads from £18 undercut almost everything else in the city. ## Neighborhood Intelligence: Medina, Souks & Tanneries Essentials

A few things to plan around your stay, whichever tier you book:

- **Djemaa el-Fna at dusk** — the largest open-air spectacle in Africa. 200 food stalls of tagine, harira, snail broth, merguez and fresh orange juice; storytellers, gnawa performers, snake charmers. Watch from the terrace of Café de France or Café Argana first, then descend in.
- **The Souks on Tuesday morning** — quietest day. The spice souk's argan oil, saffron, and ras el hanout (the 30-spice blend that varies by family recipe) are the most worth buying. Negotiate every price — final price typically 40–60% of opening.
- **The Tanneries, north-east medina** — open-air leather dye-pits running the same process for centuries; you'll smell them before you see them (ask for a sprig of mint at the entrance, the traditional trick). Go with a guide or a clear map — the surrounding lanes are a known spot for pushy unofficial "guides."
- **Majorelle Garden + Yves Saint Laurent Museum** — Jacques Majorelle's cobalt-blue villa (the colour is trademarked), restored by Saint Laurent and Bergé from 1980. Book online — both sell out in peak season.
- **Hammam ritual** — Les Bains de Marrakech (Mellah edge) or Royal Mansour's private hammam for guests. The full circuit is 2 hours: steam, kessa exfoliation, ghassoul clay wrap, savon beldi black soap, cool room. Avoid the tourist "hammam experiences" near the Djemaa.
- **Atlas Mountains day trip** — Imlil at 1,740m, 90 minutes south. Trailhead for Jbel Toubkal (4,167m, the highest peak in North Africa). Berber tea-house hospitality (with the obligatory glass of mint tea) is the day's primary reward regardless of whether you hike.
- **Koutoubia Mosque** — the 1158 AD landmark whose minaret is the model for Seville's Giralda and Rabat's Hassan Tower. Non-Muslims can't enter the mosque, but the gardens around it are free and best at sunset when the sandstone lights gold above the medina rooftops.
- **Bahia Palace, near the Mellah** — a 19th-century vizier's palace of carved cedar ceilings and zellij courtyards, a short walk from the Kasbah and Mellah riads in this guide's budget and luxury tiers.
- **Sahara desert overnight from Merzouga** — 8 hours east of Marrakech via the Tizi n'Tichka mountain pass and Ouarzazate (where Game of Thrones, Gladiator and parts of Lawrence of Arabia were filmed). Most riads can arrange a 2-night camel-trek-and-desert-camp package.
- **Mint tea as ritual** — every riad serves it the same way: three glasses, the first "bitter as life", the second "sweet as love", the third "gentle as death" (the Berber saying). Refusing it politely is rude; accepting it is the start of every Moroccan negotiation.

## UK Practicalities

- **Direct UK flights**: easyJet, Ryanair, British Airways, Royal Air Maroc, TUI fly LGW/LHR/STN/MAN–RAK in about 3h20m. easyJet and Ryanair run the most frequent budget schedules; BA and Royal Air Maroc from Heathrow suit onward connections. [Search flights to RAK](/flights?to=RAK).

- **Visa**: UK passport holders get 90 days visa-free on arrival. See the [UK Foreign Office travel advice for Morocco](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/morocco) for the latest entry rules.
- **Currency**: Moroccan Dirham (MAD). £1 ≈ MAD 14. Dual-price system in the souks — always negotiate. Cash is essential in the medina; cards work more reliably in Gueliz and the Palmeraie tier.
- **Tipping**: MAD 20–50 for riad/hammam staff per service, round up or 10% at restaurants, small change for petit taxis, MAD 100–150 for a half-day guide.
- **Best months**: March–May and September–November (22–28°C, manageable crowds). June–August is 35–42°C. December–February is cooler (15–20°C), cheaper, and the Atlas has snow.
- **Budget**: Medina icon trip — £250–500+/night all-in. Gueliz/Hivernage mid-range — £80–150/night. Budget-tier trip — £18–46/night, £15–25/day food and souk spending. A week built on this guide's cheap tier can land under £400 per person before flights.

## Booking Marrakech Hotels in 2026: Rates, Seasons and the Dates to Watch

Marrakech room rates swing by both season and district — a medina riad and a Gueliz hotel don't move in lockstep, since the riads fill up around European school holidays while the resort hotels track conference and golf-season demand. The cheapest stretch for rooms is deep summer (June–August), when the heat pushes leisure travellers elsewhere and the medina itself goes quiet by early afternoon; the most expensive is March–May and the run-up to Christmas/New Year. Two things to plan around:

- **Ramadan** (dates shift yearly on the Islamic calendar) — many restaurants and some souk stalls close during daylight hours; hotels and riads operate normally, and rates are often lower. A different but still rewarding trip.
- **School holiday weeks** (Easter, October half-term, Christmas) — the medina riads book out first; reserve 6–8 weeks ahead if travelling then.

The number that decides your real cost isn't just the headline nightly rate — it's whether you've chosen the riad-vs-hotel trade correctly. The budget tier proves a well-reviewed riad at £18–24 beats a branded hotel at £40+ on both price and atmosphere; the trade is navigation, not comfort.
