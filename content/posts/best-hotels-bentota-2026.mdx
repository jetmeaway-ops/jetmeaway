## Things to do in Bentota

Bentota is a resort town built around its **beach and river**. The long golden beach is the anchor, but the town's real signature is where the **Bentota River (Bentota Ganga)** meets the sea, creating a calm lagoon that has made this one of Sri Lanka's premier **water-sports** spots — jet-skiing, windsurfing, water-skiing and banana-boat rides, with gentler lagoon water for beginners. Along the coast at **Kosgoda** you'll find sea-turtle hatcheries and conservation projects. The dry, calm season for all of it runs **December to March**.

**Is Bentota good for couples?**
Very much so. Bentota has a romantic side, from adults-only reserves like Jetwing Saman Villas to intimate boutique villas such as Taru Villas The Long House. Sunset river cruises, quiet beach stretches and Bawa-designed gardens nearby all add to the appeal for couples and honeymooners.

**How many days should I spend in Bentota?**
Two to four nights suits most trips. That's enough for beach days, a Bentota River boat safari, a turtle hatchery visit and a trip to the Bawa gardens, while leaving time to relax. Many travellers combine Bentota with nearby Hikkaduwa or use it as a first or last stop on a wider south-coast tour from Colombo.

**What's the difference between Bentota and Hikkaduwa?**
Both are southwest-coast beach towns, but Bentota leans toward resorts, the river-and-lagoon setting and water sports, with a calmer, more upscale feel. Hikkaduwa, a little further south, is known for surfing, coral reef snorkelling and a livelier, backpacker-friendly scene. They pair well on a single coastal trip.

**Is Bentota safe for tourists?**
Bentota is a well-established resort town and generally relaxed and welcoming for visitors. Standard travel awareness applies as anywhere — take care with sea conditions and currents, use reputable water-sports and boat operators, and check the latest UK Foreign Office advice before you travel.

**Can I reach Bentota by train?**
Yes. Bentota has its own station on Sri Lanka's coastal railway line, which runs south from Colombo Fort along the shore. The train is a cheap and scenic way to arrive, with sea views for much of the journey, taking a little longer than the expressway but far more memorable. Book ahead in peak season for reserved seats.
