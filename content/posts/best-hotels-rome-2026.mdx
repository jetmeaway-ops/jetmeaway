## The Scout's Take: Which Rome Are You Staying In?

The hotel you pick determines which Rome you wake up in.

**Historic Centre** (Pantheon, Piazza Navona, Spanish Steps): for first-timers and those wanting maximum walking access to the Vatican + Pantheon axis. Tourist density is highest here.

**Trastevere**: medieval lanes, ivy-covered facades, the best local trattorias at the paper-tablecloth tables. The neighbourhood reaches its best hour at 10pm on a weekday.

**Aventine and Celio Hills**: quiet luxury within walking distance of the Colosseum, residential streets, the most extraordinary city views. The Aventine's orange-tree garden (Giardino degli Aranci) and keyhole-view St Peter's are 5 minutes from a Celio hotel.

**Testaccio**: working-class food neighbourhood, the best restaurants Romans actually eat at. Mercato di Testaccio (Tue–Sat) has the best producers in the city.

**Prati**: residential, wider streets, Vatican-adjacent without the historic-centre tourist density.

For first Rome: Pantheon area or Colosseum view. For second Rome: Trastevere. For honeymoon: Villa Spalletti Trivelli or J.K. Place Roma.

## Neighborhood Intelligence: The Bookings Nobody Tells You About

A few non-obvious Rome rules that turn the city from a queue into something extraordinary:

- **Colosseum underground (the hypogeum)** — the tunnels below the arena floor where gladiators were held before being lifted into the arena. Requires a separate booking and sells out weeks ahead. Book it. The arena floor is the photograph; the underground is the experience.
- **Vatican Museums at 8am first entry** — the Sistine Chapel has 100 people at 8am vs 2,000 at midday. Book online months ahead.
- **Pantheon timed-entry ticket** (pantheonroma.com, €5) — required since 2023. The dawn first-entry slot has the oculus light falling directly onto the floor in a precise column.
- **Borghese Gallery** — 2-hour timed entry, 360 visitors per slot, book months ahead at galleriaborghese.it. The world's greatest collection of Bernini sculpture in two adjacent rooms. Non-negotiable.
- **Trastevere at 10pm on a weekday** — not a tourist experience. Cacio e pepe and supplì on Via della Lungaretta at the paper-tablecloth tables. Romans having dinner.

## UK Practicalities

- **Direct UK flights**: BA, Ryanair, easyJet, ITA Airways fly LHR/LGW/STN/LTN-FCO in 2h30m. Ciampino (CIA) is the budget-airline airport, 40 min by bus to Termini.
- **Airport transfer**: Leonardo Express train FCO–Roma Termini, 32 minutes, €14 (buy on the train or online, not from station touts).
- **Visa**: No visa required. 90 days in Schengen per 180.
- **Currency**: Euro. Rome is cheaper than London for restaurants — a proper cacio e pepe at a Testaccio trattoria is €12–15. Tourist-trap restaurants near the major sights charge 3–4× this. Walk two streets from the Pantheon for half the price.
- **Best months**: April–June and September–October. July–August: very hot (30–35°C), crowded. January–February: quiet, cold, affordable — the best-value Rome and the emptiest Vatican.
