The [official Philippines tourism board](https://philippines.travel/) rates Boracay among the country's most celebrated beaches. Boracay is a small island — only about seven kilometres long — off the northwest tip of Panay in the Western Visayas. Its west coast is **White Beach**, a four-kilometre arc of fine, cool, powder-white sand divided into three informal **Stations**: Station 1 to the north (the widest sand and the big resorts), Station 2 in the middle (D'Mall, the dining-and-nightlife hub), and Station 3 to the south (quieter, cheaper). The whole strip faces west over the Sibuyan Sea, which is why the nightly sunset — best watched from Station 1 with a paraw sailboat drifting past — is the island's signature ritual. On the windward east side, Bulabog Beach is one of Asia's top kitesurfing spots in season.

Getting here from the UK takes some doing: there are no direct flights, so you connect through Manila or a Gulf/Asian hub, take a domestic hop to **Caticlan (MPH)** — the airport right by the jetty — then a short boat across to the island, paying a modest terminal and environmental fee at the port. ## The Scout's Take: Station 1, 2, or 3?

White Beach is really three beaches in one, and picking your **Station** is the single decision that shapes your Boracay trip.

**Station 1** (north) has the widest, whitest, most photographed sand and the marquee beachfront resorts — Discovery, Ambassador in Paradise, The Lind. It's the calmest, most upmarket stretch, and the classic spot to watch the sunset with a paraw sailboat crossing the horizon. It's also the priciest.

**Station 2** (middle) is the island's beating heart — **D'Mall**, the open-air maze of restaurants, shops, dive operators and street food, opens straight onto the sand here, and most of the mid-range hotels and beach bars cluster around it. First-timers who want everything within a two-minute walk should base here.

**Station 3** (south) is quieter and cheaper — the same powder sand, fewer crowds, and the densest concentration of budget guesthouses. For the [budget tier](#budget) below, Station 3 and the inland Balabag backstreets are where £15–25 rooms are real, with the beach still a short walk away.

## Beyond White Beach — Boracay's Essentials

A few non-beach experiences worth planning around your stay:

- **Sunset from Station 1** — the island's nightly ritual, best watched with a paraw sailboat drifting past. Free, and the reason to base near or walk to the north end at dusk.
- **Paraw sunset sailing** — traditional outrigger sailboats leave White Beach as the light drops; around £10–20 for a golden-hour cruise.
- **Island-hopping to Puka Shell Beach and Crystal Cove** — a half-day boat trip to the island's quieter northern beaches and snorkelling stops, roughly £15–25 booked locally.
- **Kitesurfing and windsurfing at Bulabog Beach** — the windward east side is a top Asian kite spot during the amihan season (roughly November–April). Lessons and gear rental on the beach.
- **Cliff diving at Ariel's Point** — a boat-trip day of cliff jumps, kayaks and lunch off the northwest coast.
- **D'Mall and D'Talipapa** — the open-air dining-and-shopping maze at Station 2, and the wet market where you buy fresh seafood and have it cooked on the spot.
- **Mount Luho viewpoint** — the island's highest point, with a panorama over White Beach and the surrounding sea — a short tricycle ride inland.

## UK Practicalities

- **Getting there**: no direct UK flights — connect via Manila (MNL) or a Gulf/Asian hub, then a domestic hop to **Caticlan (MPH)** (closest, by the jetty) or **Kalibo (KLO)** (1.5–2 hours by van). [Search flights toward Caticlan (MPH)](/flights?to=MPH). Total journey 17–22 hours door to door. Check the [UK government entry rules for the Philippines](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/philippines) before you travel.
- **Island transfer**: from Caticlan jetty, pay the terminal and **Environmental Admission Fee** (a few hundred pesos, per person, cash), take a 10–15 minute boat to Cagban port, then a tricycle or hotel shuttle. Under an hour from the airport.
- **Currency**: Philippine peso (PHP). Carry cash for tricycles, market meals, island-hopping and the arrival fees; cards work at bigger hotels and restaurants.
- **Best months**: dry season November–May (peak and priciest at Christmas, New Year and Easter/Holy Week); wet season June–October is cheaper and quieter with occasional rain. Shoulder late-May and early-November are the value sweet spot.
- **Budget**: budget-tier trip — £15–35/night bed, £15–20/day food and drink, £15–25 island-hopping. Two people sharing a £20 guesthouse can holiday on Boracay for less than a UK city weekend, flights aside.

## Booking Boracay Hotels in 2026: Seasons, Fees and the Dates to Avoid

Boracay room rates swing more by season than by hotel — a £20 guesthouse in June can be £35+ over Christmas, and the beachfront resorts move even more. The cheapest stretch of 2026 is the wet season (June–October), and the priciest are the Christmas–New Year and Easter (Holy Week) weeks, when the best-value rooms and the beachfront resorts sell out well ahead. The one add-on that surprises first-timers isn't a hotel charge at all — it's the **Environmental Admission Fee and terminal/boat fees** at Caticlan jetty, a modest per-person cash cost on arrival. Budget a little in pesos for it, and book your dates around the peak weeks rather than into them.
