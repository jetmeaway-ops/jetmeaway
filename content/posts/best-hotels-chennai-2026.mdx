  - q: "When is the monsoon in Chennai?"

So while Kerala and Mumbai are drying out, Chennai is having its wettest weeks. Heavy rain can cause street flooding and traffic chaos. Plan around it if you can, or accept lower hotel rates and a wetter trip."

    a: "Hot and humid rather than dry-hot. March to June is the tough stretch, with sticky days and warm nights, and May is usually the peak. Sightsee early in the morning and late in the afternoon, keep water with you, and use the middle of the day for museums, malls or a long lunch."

## What to See and Do in Chennai

Start where the city starts: **Marina Beach**, one of the longest urban beaches in the world, running for kilometres along the Bay of Bengal. Nobody really swims — the currents are strong and it is not that kind of beach — but the evening promenade, with families, kite sellers, sundal vendors and horses on the sand, is one of the great free spectacles in India. Go at sunrise or after five, never at midday.

The old heart of Chennai is **Mylapore**, built around the **Kapaleeshwarar Temple** with its huge painted gopuram tower and its temple tank. The streets around it — flower stalls, bronze shops, filter-coffee places — are the version of the city most visitors fall for. A short walk away, **San Thome Basilica** is built over what is traditionally held to be the tomb of St Thomas the Apostle, one of only a handful of churches in the world built above an apostle's grave, and a reminder that Christianity has been on this coast for a very long time.

For history, **Fort St George** was the first English fortress in India, founded in 1644 — the settlement that grew around it became Madras and eventually Chennai. It still houses the Tamil Nadu state government, with a museum and St Mary's Church, the oldest Anglican church east of Suez, inside the walls. The **Government Museum** on Pantheon Road in Egmore is the other essential stop, and its bronze gallery of Chola-period sculpture is genuinely world class. For quiet, the **Theosophical Society** at Adyar spreads over hundreds of acres of gardens beside the river, with one of the largest banyan trees in India.

Then there is what Chennai does better than almost anywhere: **eating**. Idli, dosa, vada, sambar and chutney for breakfast, a banana-leaf meal at lunch, and **filter coffee** poured between tumbler and davara at any hour. Learn the word *mess* — it means a simple local restaurant, and the busy ones are usually the best. In **December and January** the city fills with the **Margazhi music season**, hundreds of Carnatic music and Bharatanatyam dance concerts across the sabhas, many of them free or nearly so, with famously good canteen food attached.

Beyond the city, the coast road south is the trip to take. **Mahabalipuram (Mamallapuram)** is about an hour away and its Pallava-era monuments are a UNESCO World Heritage Site — the Shore Temple standing right on the beach, the Five Rathas carved from single boulders, and the enormous Descent of the Ganges rock relief. On the same road, **DakshinaChitra** is a living-heritage museum of relocated traditional South Indian houses and crafts. Further south, **Pondicherry (Puducherry)** — French quarter, seafront promenade, Auroville — is three to four hours away and deserves an overnight rather than a day dash.

## Where to Stay in Chennai

**Egmore and the Central / Anna Salai belt** is the best base for a first visit. Egmore and Chennai Central stations put the rest of Tamil Nadu within reach, the Government Museum is right there, the metro runs through, and most of this guide's mid-range hotels sit in this belt. It is not the prettiest part of Chennai, but it is the most useful.

**Mylapore** is where to stay if you came for temples, heritage and atmosphere. Kapaleeshwarar Temple, San Thome Basilica and the southern end of Marina Beach are all within walking distance, and the early mornings are the best hours of the day in the city. Savera Hotel is the obvious pick here.

**T Nagar** is the shopping base — Ranganathan Street, silk saris, gold and textiles — and it is crowded, loud and central-south, with plenty of mid-priced hotels including Grand Chennai by GRT and The Residency. Stay here if browsing markets is your idea of a holiday; avoid it if you want quiet evenings.

**ECR and Injambakkam** — the East Coast Road running south towards Mahabalipuram — is the coastal option, quieter and more resort-like, with sea air instead of city heat. The cost is a long drive back into town for sightseeing, so it works best for the relaxed end of a trip. **OMR**, the parallel Old Mahabalipuram Road IT corridor, is the business equivalent and where the Citadines and Fairfield sit.

**Near the airport at Meenambakkam** is worth a night if you land late or fly out at dawn. Chennai International is about 20 kilometres south-west of the centre — closer than Bangalore's airport, but still up to an hour in bad traffic or heavy rain. Trident Chennai at the luxury end and Hotel Nk Grand Park Airport Hotel at the budget end both exist for that reason. Otherwise stay in town.

One last practical point in Chennai's favour and against it: **November to February** is the comfortable season and the one to aim for, **March to June is very hot and humid**, and the **north-east monsoon runs roughly October to December** — the reverse of most of India, and the single thing UK visitors most often get wrong when planning a Chennai trip.

## Chennai Hotels FAQs

**Do UK passport holders need a visa for Chennai?**
Yes — UK passport holders need an Indian e-Visa, and it is NOT issued on arrival. Apply online in advance through the official Indian government e-Visa portal, well before you fly. Approval usually comes back within a few days, but leave a comfortable margin. Your passport must be valid for at least six months and have two blank pages. Only use the official government portal — third-party "visa services" charge a large mark-up for exactly the same document.

**What currency is used in Chennai?**
The Indian Rupee (INR). Every price in this guide is quoted in pounds as a rough conversion only, because that is what UK readers compare against — the hotel itself charges in rupees, and the exact sterling figure moves with the exchange rate. Cards work in hotels, malls and larger restaurants, but carry cash for autorickshaws, temple donations and small idli shops.

**Are there direct flights from the UK to Chennai?**
Yes. British Airways flies direct from London Heathrow to Chennai International Airport (MAA) in roughly ten and a half hours. Other airlines connect through the Gulf hubs — Emirates via Dubai, Qatar Airways via Doha, Etihad via Abu Dhabi — which is usually cheaper but adds several hours. MAA is also a big domestic and Sri Lanka / South East Asia hub.

**When is the best time to visit Chennai?**
November to February. That is the coolest, driest and by far the most comfortable stretch, and December to January is also the famous Chennai music and dance season. March to June is very hot and humid — genuinely draining if you are not used to it. Chennai does not really have a cold month, so November to February is "pleasant" rather than "cool".

**When is the monsoon in Chennai?**
This is the thing most visitors get wrong: Chennai gets the north-east monsoon, roughly October to December, not the June-to-September south-west monsoon that soaks most of India. So while Kerala and Mumbai are drying out, Chennai is having its wettest weeks. Heavy rain can cause street flooding and traffic chaos. Plan around it if you can, or accept lower hotel rates and a wetter trip.

**How hot does Chennai actually get?**
Hot and humid rather than dry-hot. March to June is the tough stretch, with sticky days and warm nights, and May is usually the peak. Air conditioning stops being a luxury and becomes the reason you pick one hotel over another. Sightsee early in the morning and late in the afternoon, keep water with you, and use the middle of the day for museums, malls or a long lunch.

**Which area should I stay in for a first visit to Chennai?**
Egmore or the Central / Anna Salai belt. You are close to Egmore and Chennai Central railway stations, the Government Museum, and the main roads into the rest of the city, and it is on the metro. That transport access matters in a city this spread out. Most of this guide's mid-range hotels sit in this central belt.

**Where should I stay for temples and old Chennai?**
Mylapore. It is the oldest and most characterful part of the city, built around the Kapaleeshwarar Temple and its tank, with San Thome Basilica a short walk away and Marina Beach at the end of the road. Mornings in Mylapore — flower sellers, temple bells, filter coffee — are the version of Chennai people remember.

**Where should I stay for shopping?**
T Nagar. Ranganathan Street and the surrounding blocks are one of the busiest shopping districts in India, famous for silk saris, gold and textiles. It is crowded and loud, which is either the appeal or the problem depending on your mood. It is central-south, well connected, and full of mid-priced hotels.

**Is it worth staying on the coast at ECR?**
If you want beach and calm rather than city, yes. The East Coast Road runs south from the city past Injambakkam, Kovalam and on towards Mahabalipuram, and the hotels out there are quieter and more resort-like. The trade-off is a long drive back into the centre for sightseeing, so it suits a relaxed second half of a trip rather than a first-time base.

**Should I stay near Chennai airport?**
Only for early or late flights. Chennai International is at Meenambakkam / Tirusulam, about 20 kilometres south-west of the centre — much closer than Bangalore's airport, but still an hour in bad traffic. Several budget properties in this guide, including Hotel Nk Grand Park Airport Hotel, exist for exactly this reason. Otherwise stay in town.

**Is the Chennai Metro useful for visitors?**
Yes, and more than people expect. It is clean, air-conditioned, cheap and signed in English, and it links the airport, Guindy, Egmore, Chennai Central and the shopping districts. It does not cover everything — Mylapore and the coast still need a taxi — so treat it as the spine and use apps for the rest. Suburban trains also run along the coast towards the south.

**Should I use Uber and Ola in Chennai?**
Yes. Both work well across the city, you get a fixed fare and a tracked route, and there is no meter argument. Autorickshaws are everywhere and can be booked through the same apps, which is much easier than negotiating on the street. Allow far more time than the app estimate at rush hour, especially during monsoon rain.

**What should I wear at Chennai's temples?**
Dress conservatively — shoulders and knees covered for everyone, and loose cotton is far more comfortable in the humidity. You remove your shoes before entering temples, so slip-ons save time. Some temples, including parts of Kapaleeshwarar, restrict inner-sanctum access to Hindus, and photography is often not allowed inside. Chennai is a more traditional city than Bangalore or Mumbai and dressing modestly is simply easier.

**Can I drink the tap water in Chennai?**
No — drink sealed bottled or properly filtered water only. Hotels almost always supply bottled water in the room. Avoid ice at street stalls until your stomach has adjusted. The food itself is one of the best reasons to come: idli, dosa, vada, sambar and filter coffee at a good Chennai mess is a genuine highlight, and busy places with high turnover are usually the safest.

**Is Mahabalipuram worth a day trip?**
Absolutely — it is the single best day trip from Chennai. Mahabalipuram (Mamallapuram) is about an hour south down the East Coast Road, and its seventh and eighth-century Pallava monuments are a UNESCO World Heritage Site: the Shore Temple on the beach, the Five Rathas carved from single rocks, and the huge Descent of the Ganges relief. Go early to beat the heat.

**Can I visit Pondicherry from Chennai?**
Yes, but treat it as a longer trip. Puducherry is roughly 150 kilometres south, about three to four hours by road down the coast, and its French quarter, seafront promenade and Auroville make it worth an overnight rather than a rushed day return. Many people combine it with a Mahabalipuram stop on the way.

**What is the Chennai music season?**
The Margazhi season, roughly mid-December to mid-January, when the city hosts hundreds of Carnatic music and Bharatanatyam dance performances across its sabhas and halls. It is one of the largest classical arts festivals in the world, many concerts are free or very cheap, and the canteen food at the sabhas is famous in its own right. Hotels book up, so reserve early.

**What is there to actually see in Chennai?**
Marina Beach, one of the longest urban beaches in the world; the Kapaleeshwarar Temple in Mylapore with its towering painted gopuram; San Thome Basilica, built over the tomb of St Thomas the Apostle; Fort St George, the first English fortress in India and still the seat of the state government; the Government Museum with its outstanding bronze gallery; and the vast banyan-shaded grounds of the Theosophical Society at Adyar.

**How many days do I need in Chennai?**
Two full days for the city — one for Mylapore, San Thome, Marina Beach and the Theosophical Society, and one for Fort St George, the Government Museum and T Nagar shopping. Add a third day for Mahabalipuram and DakshinaChitra, and a couple more if you want to continue down the coast to Pondicherry.

**What is DakshinaChitra?**
A living-heritage museum on the East Coast Road between Chennai and Mahabalipuram, made up of relocated and rebuilt traditional houses from across the four South Indian states, with craft demonstrations and performances. It pairs naturally with a Mahabalipuram day and gives useful context for everything else you see in Tamil Nadu.

**Which side of the road do they drive on in India?**
The left, as in the UK — but Chennai traffic is dense and lane discipline is looser than you will be used to. Very few visitors self-drive and there is no need to: app taxis, autorickshaws and the metro cover the city. For Mahabalipuram or Pondicherry, hiring a car with a driver is normal, cheap and far less stressful.

**Do Chennai hotels charge extra fees?**
There is no resort fee, but Indian hotel rates carry GST, which varies by room-rate band, and some properties add a service charge or price breakfast separately. Budget rooms are the most likely to exclude breakfast. Always check what is included on the hotel's live page before comparing two similar-looking rates.

**Is breakfast usually included in Chennai?**
At the luxury and mid-range tiers, usually yes, and often an excellent South Indian and continental buffet — which in Chennai is a real reason to take the inclusive rate. In the budget tier it is inconsistent, with some properties charging a small supplement. Breakfast is cheap to add locally, so compare the room rate first.

**Is Chennai a good first trip to India?**
It can be, if you go for food, temples and classical arts rather than headline monuments. It is less overwhelming than Delhi but hotter and more traditional than Bangalore, and English is widely spoken alongside Tamil. The pairing that works best is Chennai plus Mahabalipuram and Pondicherry, which gives you city, coast and heritage in one week.

**Is Chennai safe for tourists?**
It is generally regarded as one of India's calmer big cities, and millions of people move around it daily without incident. The realistic risks are heat, traffic, petty theft in crowded markets and overcharging rather than anything more serious. Use app taxis rather than street cabs after dark, and read the UK Foreign Office travel advice for India before you go.
