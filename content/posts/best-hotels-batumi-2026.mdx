## Batumi Hotels FAQs

**Do UK citizens need a visa for Batumi or Georgia in 2026?**
No — and this is one of Georgia's genuine standouts. UK passport holders can enter Georgia visa-free and stay for a full year, no application, no fee, no e-visa. You simply arrive at Batumi airport (BUS) and get stamped in. That year-long allowance is one of the most generous visa-free arrangements anywhere in the world.

**What is the cheapest area to stay in Batumi?**
The New Boulevard and Orbi high-rise district holds the deepest budget stock — apartment-style stays like Orbi Luxury Apartments from around £36 a night and Hotel 725 from about £37, both within reach of the seafront. The Old Boulevard and Piazza core costs a little more for the walkable location, but even there real rooms start in the £60s.

**How much does a budget hotel cost in Batumi in 2026?**
Real, bookable budget stays in this guide start at around £36 a night (Orbi Luxury Apartments) and most of the cheapest cluster sits in the high £30s to low £50s. That buys an apartment or 3★ room, often with a sea or city view given Batumi's high-rise skyline. The budget tier here runs up to about £155 for the priciest 5★-rated option.

**Is Batumi good value for UK travellers?**
Very. Georgia is one of the best-value destinations Europe-adjacent, and Batumi's food, taxis and drinks are cheap by UK standards — a big khachapuri and Georgian wine for a few pounds. Hotels start at around £36 a night, and even the 5★ towers undercut equivalent Mediterranean resorts. The strong pound against the Georgian lari stretches a UK budget a long way here.

**When is the best time to visit Batumi?**
May to September is the resort season — Batumi is a Black Sea summer city, and July and August are the warm, buzzing peak with beaches, festivals and full nightlife. May, June and September are the sweet spot for milder heat and lower prices. Winter is quiet and wet; the casinos stay open but the beach scene shuts down.

**How do I get to Batumi from the UK?**
Batumi has its own international airport (BUS), a few minutes south of the centre, taking one-stop routes from the UK via Istanbul or European hubs. There are no year-round direct UK flights, so most UK visitors connect through Istanbul with Turkish Airlines or Pegasus. In peak summer, seasonal charters sometimes appear. Search live UK routes to BUS to compare.

**What currency is used in Batumi?**
The Georgian lari (GEL, symbol ₾). Cards are widely accepted in Batumi's hotels, restaurants and larger shops, but carry some cash for markets, taxis and small cafes. We quote hotel prices in pounds as from-rates; you'll pay in lari or on card at the property. ATMs are plentiful along the Boulevard.

**Is Batumi safe for tourists?**
Yes — Georgia has low violent crime and Batumi is a well-touristed resort city where visitors are common. Standard resort awareness applies around the casinos and nightlife at night, and take licensed taxis or an app. Georgians are famously hospitable, and the tourist areas along the Boulevard are busy and well-lit through the summer season.

**Which Batumi hotels have the best sea views?**
Batumi is a vertical skyline city, so sea views are common even at budget prices. In the luxury tier the Radisson Blu, Le Meridien, Wyndham and Sheraton all face the Black Sea. On a budget, apartment-style towers like the Orbi buildings and GLOBALSTAY Batumi View Apartments deliver genuine sea outlooks from high floors for a fraction of the luxury price.

**Is Batumi a good beach destination?**
It's the beach capital of Georgia, though the beach itself is pebble rather than sand — bring water shoes. The palm-lined Boulevard runs the length of the seafront with the beach on one side and cafes, bike paths and sculpture on the other. Beach season is roughly June to September; outside that the Boulevard is still a lovely walk but swimming stops.

**What is there to do in Batumi besides the beach?**
Plenty — the seaside Boulevard, the moving Ali & Nino kinetic sculpture, the Alphabet Tower celebrating the Georgian script, the Piazza with its mosaics and clock tower, the Botanical Garden just north of town, cable-car rides, casinos, and day trips into the Adjara mountains and waterfalls. It's a compact, walkable resort with more variety than a typical beach town.

**Do people drink alcohol in Batumi? Is Georgia a Muslim country?**
Georgia is not a Muslim country — it's one of the world's oldest Christian nations, Christian since the 4th century, and the cradle of wine with 8,000 years of qvevri winemaking. Alcohol is central to the culture and the supra (feast) is a way of life. Batumi has a lively bar, wine and nightlife scene, and Georgian wine is superb and cheap.

**Which is the best luxury hotel in Batumi?**
The Sheraton Batumi is the marquee 5★ address, in the landmark lighthouse-shaped tower on the seafront, and carries the highest from-price in this guide at around £304 a night. The Radisson Blu and Le Meridien are the other headline seafront five-stars. For a 5★ at a far lower entry price, the Wyndham Batumi (from ~£123) and Sky Tower (from ~£84) are strong value.

**Are the Orbi towers hotels or apartments?**
The Orbi buildings (Orbi City, Orbi Residence, Orbi Beach) are large apartment-hotel high-rises where individual units are let out — so you book a specific managed apartment rather than a traditional hotel room. They're excellent budget value with sea-view floors and kitchenettes, from around £36-67 a night in this guide. Check exactly which unit and floor you're booking, as management varies.

**What is khachapuri and where do I try it in Batumi?**
Khachapuri is Georgia's iconic cheese bread, and Batumi has its own signature version — Adjaruli khachapuri, a boat-shaped bread filled with molten cheese, topped with a raw egg and butter you stir in. Batumi is the home of this dish. You'll find it everywhere from £2-4; it's a meal in itself. Pair it with a glass of local Georgian wine.

**How many days do you need in Batumi?**
Three to four days covers the city comfortably — the Boulevard, the beach, the Piazza and Old Town, the Botanical Garden and a cable-car ride, plus one day trip into the Adjara countryside for waterfalls and wine. Beach-focused visitors often stay a week and treat Batumi as a resort base with occasional excursions.

**Is Batumi cheaper than Turkish or Greek beach resorts?**
Generally yes on the ground — food, drinks, taxis and mid-range hotels tend to be cheaper in Batumi than comparable Turkish or Greek resorts, and the visa-free-for-a-year rule removes any entry cost. Flights involve a connection rather than a direct hop, so factor that in, but once you land, Batumi stretches a UK budget notably further.

**Which Batumi hotels are best for families?**
Larger resort-style properties with pools work best — the Grand Bellagio (from ~£75) has a big pool and grounds, the Colosseum Marina and Greenwood offer spa and family facilities, and apartment-style stays like GardenVille and the Orbi towers give families kitchen space and room to spread out. The flat, pram-friendly Boulevard is a genuine family draw.

**Are there casinos in Batumi?**
Yes — Batumi is a regional gambling hub, and several hotels are casino resorts, including the Grand Bellagio Convention & Casino and the JRW Welmond Hotel & Casino. Gambling is legal and the casinos draw visitors from neighbouring countries where it isn't. If you'd rather avoid the scene, plenty of hotels in this guide have no gaming floor.

**What is the Batumi Boulevard?**
The Boulevard is Batumi's palm-lined seafront promenade, running for several kilometres along the Black Sea. It splits into the historic Old Boulevard near the centre and the newer New Boulevard to the south, and it's the spine of the city — beaches, cafes, dancing fountains, bike hire, the Ali & Nino sculpture and sculpture parks all sit along it. Most hotels in this guide are a short walk from it.

**Do I need to speak Georgian in Batumi?**
No. Georgian has its own beautiful, unique alphabet, but in Batumi's tourist areas you'll manage easily — Russian is widely understood and English is growing fast, especially among younger staff in hotels and restaurants. A few Georgian words (gamarjoba for hello, gaumarjos for cheers) are warmly received but never required.

Those are the seafront 5★ headline addresses. The budget tier tops out far lower, at around £155 for the Greenwood Hotel Restaurant & Spa.

**Are apartment-hotels a better deal than hotels in Batumi?**
Often yes, for stays of a few nights or for families. Batumi's apartment-hotel towers (Orbi buildings, GLOBALSTAY, Tangerine, Wyn Residence) give you a kitchenette, more space and frequently a sea view from a high floor, from around £36-71 a night — usually cheaper per person than an equivalent hotel room, with the trade-off of variable management and fewer hotel services.

**How far is Batumi airport from the city centre?**
Batumi International Airport (BUS) sits just a few kilometres south of the city, one of the closest resort airports to its centre anywhere — a taxi runs around 10-15 minutes to the Boulevard hotels. That short transfer is a genuine convenience compared with resorts where the airport is an hour away.

**Is Batumi worth visiting for a UK traveller?**
If you want a warm, cheap, slightly surreal Black Sea resort with a subtropical skyline, superb-value food and wine, a year-long visa-free welcome and genuine Georgian hospitality, yes. It rewards travellers who like somewhere a bit different from the standard Mediterranean beach break — and the low on-the-ground costs make it easy to justify the connecting flight.
