## The Scout's Take: Polanco, Roma/Condesa, Reforma or Centro?

**Polanco** is Mexico City's address for luxury — Avenida Presidente Masaryk is the city's Bond Street, lined with flagship boutiques, the rippling silver Museo Soumaya, and Chapultepec Park's green expanse a few minutes' walk away. It's the most comfortable, most polished base, and the furthest from the stadium.

**Roma Norte and Condesa** are the leafy, low-rise neighbourhoods that most first-time visitors fall for — Art Deco mansions, jacaranda-shaded streets, oval parks, mezcal bars, third-wave coffee and some of the city's best taquerías. Central, eminently walkable, and well connected south towards the stadium via Metro Line 2.

**Reforma** is the grand ceremonial boulevard sweeping from Chapultepec Park towards the Centro Histórico past the Ángel de la Independencia — its glass towers hold the city's biggest international hotels, well-placed for both the historic centre and the museums.

**Centro Histórico** puts you among the cathedral, the Zócalo and the Templo Mayor ruins, and it's also the most direct base for the stadium — Metro Line 2 runs straight down to Tasqueña, where the Tren Ligero carries you the rest of the way to the historic Estadio Azteca.

**Coyoacán**, home to the Frida Kahlo Museum and the closest neighbourhood to the stadium itself, sacrifices some nightlife and hotel choice for the shortest, calmest matchday journey. The **airport (MEX) belt** in Colonia Peñón de los Baños is a practical, budget-friendly cluster for late arrivals, early departures or short layovers — not a sightseeing base.

For first-time visitors: Roma or Condesa. For luxury and shopping: Polanco. For sightseeing and stadium transit: Centro Histórico. For tight budgets: this guide's [budget tier](#budget-hotels) — the Centro fringe and airport belt are where £10–30 a night is still real.

## Going to the 2026 World Cup in Mexico City?

Mexico City hosts the **World Cup opening match on 11 June 2026** at **Mexico City Stadium** — the legendary **Estadio Azteca**, the only stadium ever to host three World Cups (1970, 1986 and 2026). The opening game traditionally belongs to the host nation, so the atmosphere in the city that week will be unlike anything else on the tournament calendar. Expect every hotel to fill, prices to climb, and the southern boroughs around the stadium to become the centre of gravity for a few days.

The stadium itself sits in the Santa Úrsula district of **Coyoacán**, in the south of the city — a long way from the tourist core. That gives you two sensible strategies. The first is comfort: base yourself in **Polanco**, on **Reforma**, or in **Condesa/Roma**, enjoy the city's best hotels and dining, and treat match day as a planned expedition south (35–50 minutes by car off-peak, longer in match-day traffic). The second is proximity: stay in the **Coyoacán/Tlalpan** area to the south, sacrificing some nightlife and hotel choice for a shorter, calmer journey to the turnstiles.

For match day itself, public transport is your friend. The historic Estadio Azteca is served by the **Tren Ligero** (light rail) at the **Estadio Azteca** stop, which links to **Metro Line 2** at **Tasqueña**. From Condesa, Roma or the Centro Histórico, ride the Metro to Tasqueña and change onto the Tren Ligero. Allow **60–90 minutes** door to door and set off **at least three hours before kick-off** — roads around the stadium close early, and rideshare surge pricing on the night is severe.

## Beyond the Hotel — Mexico City's Essentials

A few non-negotiable experiences worth planning around your stay:

- **Teotihuacán at sunrise or by hot-air balloon** — the pyramids of the Sun and Moon, an hour north of the city. Go early to beat both the heat and the crowds.
- **Frida Kahlo Museum (Casa Azul), Coyoacán** — the cobalt-blue house where Kahlo was born, lived and died, now the most-visited house museum in Latin America. Book timed tickets in advance.
- **Xochimilco's floating gardens** — hire a colourful trajinera boat by the hour on the UNESCO-listed canal network; go on a weekday morning for a calmer experience than the lively weekend party atmosphere.
- **Templo Mayor and the Zócalo** — the excavated heart of the Aztec capital Tenochtitlan, steps from the Metropolitan Cathedral and the Palacio Nacional's Diego Rivera murals.
- **Chapultepec Park and the National Anthropology Museum** — one of the world's great archaeology museums, inside the largest city park in Latin America.
- **A taco crawl through Roma Norte and Condesa** — no reservation needed, and the best meal of most visitors' trip costs less than a London sandwich.
- **Mercado de San Juan and Mercado Roma** — for adventurous eating and a proper look at the city's ingredient culture, from exotic meats to artisanal everything.
- **Lucha libre at Arena México** — Friday and Sunday night wrestling in a full arena is one of the most purely fun nights out in the city, regardless of whether you follow the sport.

## UK Practicalities

- **Direct UK flights**: British Airways flies LHR–MEX direct in around 11h30m; Aeroméxico, Iberia, Lufthansa and KLM offer one-stop routings from across the UK. [Search flights to MEX](/flights).
- **Airport transfer**: Official airport taxi from MEX to Condesa, Roma or Polanco is roughly 250–400 pesos, 25–45 minutes depending on traffic. Registered rideshare apps are usually cheaper.
- **Visa**: UK passport holders do not need a visa for tourism — you receive a free FMM tourist permit on arrival for stays up to 180 days. Check the latest [UK government travel advice for Mexico](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/mexico) before you travel.
- **Currency**: Mexican peso (MXN). Cards are widely accepted in hotels and restaurants; carry some cash for taquerías, markets and the Tren Ligero.
- **Altitude**: At 2,240m, the city sits high — take the first day gently, drink plenty of water, and pace yourself on match day and at Teotihuacán.
- **Getting around**: The Metro is 5 pesos a ride and covers the whole city; registered rideshare apps are the comfortable option for evenings or luggage.
- **Best months**: March–May (dry, warm, jacaranda season) and September–November (rains taper off, Día de Muertos in late Oct/early Nov). June–September is rainy season; December–February is cool and dry.
- **Budget**: Luxury trip — £150–350/night all-in. Mid-range trip — £60–130/night. Budget-tier trip — £10–40/night all-in, plus £15–25/day food and transport. A 4-night budget trip built on this guide's cheap tier can land well under £400 per person before flights.
