## Where to Stay in Mumbai: Choosing Your Base

**Colaba and Fort (south Mumbai)** — the sightseeing core. The Gateway of India, the Taj waterfront, Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Terminus, the museums, Colaba Causeway and the Elephanta ferry jetty are all here or within walking distance. Most expensive per square foot, and roughly an hour from the airport in traffic. This is where a first-time visitor should stay if the budget allows.

**Bandra** — food and nightlife. Restaurants, cafes, bars, the Bandstand and Carter Road seafront promenades, and the most design-conscious streets in the city. Roughly midway between the airport and the southern sights, which makes it a fair compromise base.

**Andheri and BKC** — business and airport access. Andheri has the metro, the airport, the office parks and the deepest run of budget and mid-range hotel stock; BKC is the polished corporate district where the modern five-stars sit. Practical, not atmospheric.

**Juhu** — the beach. Sand, sunsets, famous street food and a calmer pace, in the northern suburbs near the airport. Good for a few relaxed days at the end of a trip; a long way from the historic core.

## Beyond the Hotel: What to See in Mumbai

The **Gateway of India** on the Colaba waterfront, with the **Taj Mahal Palace** hotel facing it across the plaza, is where almost every Mumbai trip starts — and it is also where the ferries to the **Elephanta Caves**, a UNESCO World Heritage Site of rock-cut temples on an island in the harbour, depart from. **Marine Drive** curves along the bay and earns its nickname, the "Queen's Necklace", when the streetlights come on after dark; walk it down to **Chowpatty Beach** for bhelpuri at sunset. **Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Terminus** is a UNESCO site in its own right, and the surrounding **Victorian Gothic and Art Deco Ensemble** of Mumbai is separately UNESCO-listed — an extraordinary stretch of architecture that most visitors walk past without realising what it is.

Beyond that: **Colaba Causeway** for market shopping and haggling; **Haji Ali Dargah**, the mosque and tomb reached by a causeway that floods at high tide; **Juhu Beach** in the north for the local version of the Chowpatty evening; and **Sanjay Gandhi National Park** on the city's northern edge, where the ancient **Kanheri Caves** sit inside a genuine forest within the city limits. **Bollywood** is here too — Mumbai is the centre of the Hindi film industry, and Film City and studio tours are widely available. Guided visits to **Dharavi** run as well; if you go, choose an operator that uses local guides, returns money to the community and does not treat a working neighbourhood as a spectacle.

## Mumbai Hotels FAQs

**Do UK passport holders need a visa for Mumbai?**
Yes — UK passport holders need an Indian e-Visa, and it is NOT available on arrival. Apply online in advance through the official Indian government e-Visa portal, well before you fly. Approval usually comes back within a few days, but leave a comfortable margin. Your passport must be valid for at least six months and have two blank pages. Only use the official government portal — third-party "visa services" charge a large mark-up for the same document.

**What currency is used in Mumbai?**
The Indian Rupee (INR). Every price in this guide is quoted in pounds as a rough conversion only, because that is what UK readers compare against — the hotel itself will charge in rupees, and the exact sterling figure moves with the exchange rate. Cards are widely accepted in hotels, malls and chain restaurants; carry some cash for taxis, street food, markets and smaller guesthouses. Use bank ATMs rather than airport exchange desks.

**Are there direct flights from the UK to Mumbai?**
Yes. London to Mumbai (BOM) is a direct route of roughly 9 hours, flown by British Airways, Virgin Atlantic and Air India depending on the season. That makes Mumbai one of the easier Indian cities to reach from the UK — unlike Jaipur or Udaipur, you do not need to connect through Delhi. [Search flights to BOM](/flights?to=BOM) to compare fares and departure airports for your dates.

**When is the best time to visit Mumbai?**
November to February. That is the cool, dry window — comfortable for walking Colaba and Marine Drive, and the only stretch of the year when the humidity is genuinely pleasant. March to May gets hot and sticky. June to September is the monsoon: Mumbai's rain is heavy, streets flood, and ferries to Elephanta Caves can be suspended for the season. Rates fall in the monsoon, so it is a real budget option — just go in with the right expectations.

**How bad is the Mumbai monsoon really?**
Heavy. Mumbai receives most of its annual rainfall between June and September, and it arrives in downpours rather than drizzle, which can flood low-lying roads and disrupt suburban trains. Elephanta Caves ferries typically stop running for the monsoon. If you travel then, pack proper waterproofs and sandals you do not mind soaking, stay somewhere with easy metro or main-road access, and build flexible days into the plan.

**Which area should I stay in for a first visit to Mumbai?**
Colaba or Fort. That is where the Gateway of India, the Taj Mahal Palace waterfront, Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Terminus, the Victorian Gothic and Art Deco ensemble, the museums and Colaba Causeway shopping all sit within walking distance of each other. Bandra is the choice for food and nightlife, Andheri and BKC for business and airport access, and Juhu for the beach.

**What is the cheapest area to stay in Mumbai?**
The suburbs. The deepest cluster of sub-£30 rooms in this guide sits around Andheri, Malad, Borivali, Goregaon and the airport corridor, plus Vashi across the creek in Navi Mumbai. Colaba and Fort — the sightseeing core — carry a location premium, so budget travellers who want to be central should expect to pay more or accept a smaller room.

**How much does a budget hotel cost per night in Mumbai?**
Roughly £17 to £32 a night for the 29 budget properties in this guide, pulled on live searches while writing. Hotel Nest N Rest starts around £17 and Hotel Arma Residency around £18, with most of the tier landing between £21 and £30. Those are lead-in rates for the cheapest available room on flexible dates — festival weeks, cricket fixtures and big conference dates push them up.

**What is the most expensive hotel in this guide?**
Taj Mahal Tower, Mumbai, at around £242 a night on the searches we ran while writing — the landmark on the waterfront beside the Gateway of India. That is the true top of this guide's price range; the rest of the luxury tier mostly sits between £74 and £167, which by global five-star standards is unusually good value.

**Should I use the local trains, the metro, or Uber and Ola in Mumbai?**
Mix all three. The suburban trains are the fastest way to cover long north-south distances and cost very little, but they are extremely crowded at peak times and are not a comfortable introduction to the city with luggage. The metro is newer, air-conditioned and much easier for visitors. Uber and Ola are cheap by UK standards and the sensible default for evenings, airport runs and anything with bags.

**How long does it take to get from Mumbai airport to Colaba?**
Plan for around an hour, and up to two in heavy traffic — Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj International Airport sits in the northern suburbs while Colaba is at the southern tip of the peninsula. If you have an early flight or a short stay, the airport-corridor hotels in this guide exist precisely to remove that journey.

**Is Mumbai safe for tourists?**
Mumbai is generally regarded as one of India's safer big cities for visitors, and the tourist areas stay busy late. The realistic risks are petty theft in crowds, traffic when crossing roads, and taxi or tour overcharging rather than anything dramatic. Keep valuables close on trains and in markets, agree fares or use an app, and check the UK Foreign Office travel advice for India before you go.

**Is Mumbai good for solo travellers?**
Yes, with normal city sense. It is walkable in the south, has plenty of cafes and late-opening restaurants, and app taxis make evenings straightforward. Solo women travellers commonly report Mumbai as more relaxed than many Indian cities, though modest dress at religious sites and avoiding empty streets very late are still sensible. Ladies-only carriages on the suburban trains are worth using.

**Can I drink the tap water in Mumbai?**
No — stick to sealed bottled water or properly filtered water. Most hotels supply bottled water in the room or have a filtered dispenser. Avoid ice from street stalls, and be a little cautious with raw salads at the very cheapest places until your stomach has settled in. Street food is one of Mumbai's great pleasures; pick busy stalls with high turnover.

**Which side of the road do they drive on in India?**
The left, as in the UK — but Mumbai traffic is far denser and lane discipline is looser than you will be used to. Very few visitors self-drive here and there is no need to: taxis, autorickshaws in the suburbs, the metro and the trains cover everything. If you do hire a car, hiring it with a driver is the normal and much easier option.

**How many days do I need in Mumbai?**
Three full days covers the essentials: one for Colaba, the Gateway of India, the museums and Colaba Causeway; one for Marine Drive, Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Terminus, the Victorian Gothic and Art Deco ensemble and Haji Ali Dargah; and one for the Elephanta Caves ferry. Add a fourth for Bandra's food scene, Juhu beach, or Sanjay Gandhi National Park and the Kanheri Caves.

**Is the Taj Mahal Palace worth the price?**
If a landmark stay is the point of the trip, yes — the hotel faces the Gateway of India across the waterfront and is a Mumbai institution in its own right. In this guide, Taj Mahal Tower comes in around £242 a night, the most expensive rate here. If you want five-star comfort rather than that specific address, The Oberoi Mumbai on Marine Drive and Trident Bandra Kurla deliver it for considerably less.

**Are the airport hotels a good idea in Mumbai?**
More than in most cities. Mumbai's airport sits in the northern suburbs, so airport-area hotels save an hour or more of traffic each way on arrival and departure days, and the cluster is unusually strong — Radisson Blu, Hilton and JW Marriott Sahar at the top end, Fairfield and Ginger in the middle, and a long run of budget properties. They suit short business trips and stopovers; they do not suit sightseeing-led holidays.

**Is Bandra a good base?**
Bandra is the best base for food and nightlife — restaurants, bars, cafes and the Bandstand and Carter Road promenades, with a young, design-led feel. It is roughly halfway between the airport and the southern sights, so it is a reasonable compromise if you want both. It is not the cheapest area, and it is not where the historic sightseeing is.

**What is BKC and should I stay there?**
Bandra Kurla Complex is Mumbai's modern financial and corporate district — planned, clean, and full of offices, which is why the polished business hotels cluster there. Stay in BKC if you are in Mumbai to work or want quiet modern rooms with easy road links. Skip it if you are here to sightsee; it is a business district, not a neighbourhood with atmosphere.

**Can you visit Dharavi, and is it appropriate?**
Guided visits to Dharavi run and can be done responsibly. Dharavi is a working residential and industrial neighbourhood with a large recycling and manufacturing economy, not an attraction — so choose an operator that employs local guides, puts money back into the community, and has a clear no-photography-of-people policy. If a tour is marketed as a spectacle, book a different one or skip it.

**Is there really a Bollywood tour?**
Yes. Mumbai is the home of the Hindi film industry, and studio and Film City tours are widely available, sometimes including a look at a working set. Availability depends on what is shooting, so book ahead and treat the itinerary as flexible. It is a fun half-day, not a substitute for the historic core.

**What should I know about Elephanta Caves?**
The Elephanta Caves are a UNESCO World Heritage Site on an island in Mumbai Harbour, reached by ferry from the Gateway of India — allow most of a day, including the boat each way, the climb up to the caves and time inside the rock-cut temples. Ferries generally do not run during the monsoon, so this is a November-to-May excursion in practice. Go early to beat both the heat and the crowds.

**Where is the best street food in Mumbai?**
Chowpatty Beach at the end of Marine Drive is the classic sunset spot for bhelpuri and pav bhaji, and Juhu Beach in the northern suburbs is the local equivalent. Mohammed Ali Road is legendary during Ramadan evenings. Pick stalls with long queues and fast turnover, drink bottled water, and skip anything sitting out uncovered.

**Do Mumbai hotels charge extra fees?**
There is no Las Vegas-style resort fee, but Indian hotel rates carry GST, which varies by room rate band, and some properties add service charges or charge separately for breakfast. Cheap rooms in the budget tier are more likely to exclude breakfast. Always check what is included on the hotel's live page before comparing two similar prices.

**Is breakfast usually included in Mumbai?**
At the mid-range and luxury tiers, usually yes — often a generous Indian and continental buffet. In the budget tier it is inconsistent: some properties include a simple breakfast, others charge a small supplement or offer nothing. Since a hotel breakfast in Mumbai is rarely expensive to add, the room rate matters more than the inclusion.
