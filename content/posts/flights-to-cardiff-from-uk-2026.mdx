## Which UK cities fly direct to Cardiff

Cardiff's domestic flight network is genuinely limited:

- **Edinburgh** — Loganair, around seven flights a week (the main domestic route).
- **Belfast** — Aer Lingus (a sea crossing, so flying makes sense).
- **Glasgow** — cited among the popular routes to Cardiff.

From **England** — London, Birmingham, Manchester, Bristol and the rest — there are **no scheduled domestic flights** to Cardiff, because rail and road are faster. Cardiff (CWL) also handles international routes on easyJet, Ryanair and Aer Lingus, but for UK-domestic travel it's really a Scotland-and-Northern-Ireland story.

<a id="fly-vs-train"></a>

## Fly, take the train, or drive to Cardiff?

The honest answer for most of the UK is: **don't fly.**

- **From England:** the **train or driving wins** comfortably. London-Cardiff is about two hours by rail; Bristol is next door; Birmingham and Manchester are straightforward by train or motorway. There are no scheduled flights because the overland journey is short.
- **From Scotland (Edinburgh, Glasgow):** flying (Loganair) is faster than the very long train, so it's a genuine option.
- **From Northern Ireland (Belfast):** flying (Aer Lingus) makes sense across the Irish Sea.

Match the mode to your origin: rail or road from England, air from Scotland and NI.

**Typical fare 2026:** £40-£90 return from Edinburgh; baggage varies by fare.

**Cons:** Limited frequency; check the fare for baggage.

**The honest read:** for Scotland-Cardiff, Loganair; for Belfast-Cardiff, Aer Lingus. From England, skip the flight and take the train.

## Do you need ID or a passport?

Cardiff is in the UK, so a flight from elsewhere in Britain is **domestic** — no passport is legally required. But **every airline requires an accepted form of photo ID**, and each sets its own list. Most accept a valid passport, and some accept a photo driving licence on domestic routes. **Check your airline's accepted-ID list and take an ID it accepts.**
