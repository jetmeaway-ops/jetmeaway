## The Scout's Take: Central Pattaya, Jomtien, or the Headland?

**Central Pattaya** — the strip along Beach Road and Second Road — is where first-timers should base themselves. The malls, Walking Street, Bali Hai Pier for the islands, and the densest cluster of £12–25 guesthouses are all here, walkable or a few-baht songthaew away. It's busy and it's fun, and it's the cheapest place to stay.

**Jomtien**, the long beach south of the headland, is the calmer, more family- and long-stay side of the bay — a longer, quieter stretch of sand with plenty of budget and mid-range hotels a short walk back. Families and couples often prefer it, taxiing into the centre for the sights.

**The headland and the north (Naklua, Wong Amat)** hold the beachfront luxury resorts — the Royal Cliff estate on its private cliff, Amari at the quiet north end, the Wong Amat design hotels. This is where you pay for a sea view and a private stretch of sand away from the strip.

For a first Pattaya trip on a budget: central Pattaya. For calmer sand and families: Jomtien. For a beachfront resort: the headland or Wong Amat. All three are inside a cheap ride of each other.

## Beyond the Beach — Pattaya's Essentials

A few things worth planning around your stay:

- **Koh Larn (Coral Island)** — the clear turquoise water and white sand central Pattaya lacks, a 15-minute speedboat or 45-minute ferry from Bali Hai Pier. Beaches Tawaen, Samae and Nual are the ones to aim for.
- **Sanctuary of Truth** — the vast hand-carved all-teak temple-palace on the Naklua headland, still under construction after decades. Go in the morning before the heat.
- **Water sports off Jomtien** — jet skis, parasailing and banana boats run off Pattaya and Jomtien beaches; agree the price and check the gear first.
- **Nong Nooch Tropical Botanical Garden** — 500 acres of landscaped gardens, orchid houses and cultural shows, a short drive inland.
- **Walking Street and the night markets** — South Pattaya's neon strip for nightlife, plus night markets like Thepprasit for food and cheap shopping.
- **Pattaya Floating Market** — a purpose-built market on the water with crafts, food stalls and boat rides, good for a half-day.

## UK Practicalities

- **Getting there**: no direct UK flights — fly to Bangkok Suvarnabhumi (BKK) via a Gulf hub (Doha, Dubai, Abu Dhabi) or Europe, then transfer overland (90 minutes to 2 hours). U-Tapao (UTP) is the local airport for regional and charter flights. [Search flights to UTP](/flights?to=UTP).
- **Airport transfer**: BKK to Pattaya by bus, minibus or private car; UTP is about 45 minutes from central Pattaya.
- **Currency**: Thai baht (THB). Carry cash for songthaews, street food, markets and small guesthouses; ATMs and 7-Elevens are everywhere; bigger hotels and malls take cards.
- **Getting around**: shared songthaews (baht buses) loop Beach Road, Second Road and Jomtien for a few baht; the Grab app for metered private rides; agree fares before chartering.
- **Best months**: November–February is the cool, dry peak (best weather, highest prices); June–October is the rainy season with short downpours and the lowest rates; March–May is hot. Shoulder months (May, November) are the budget sweet spot.
- **Budget**: budget-tier trip — £12–30/night rooms, a few pounds per meal, pennies for songthaews. Water sports and Walking Street are where costs climb.
