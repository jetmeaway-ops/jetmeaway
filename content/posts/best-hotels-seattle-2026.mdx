## The Scout's Take: Downtown, SeaTac, Bellevue or Renton?

**Downtown Seattle** is the postcard version of the city — Pike Place Market's fishmongers and the original Starbucks, the ferry terminals, the retail core between Fourth and Sixth Avenue, and the monorail up to the Space Needle. It's where every luxury and mid-range hotel in this guide sits, and it's genuinely walkable: most of the sights, restaurants and museums are within 20 minutes on foot of each other. The catch is price — Downtown's cheapest options in this guide start around £105, more than double the SeaTac belt's cheapest chain motel, and parking runs $40–55 a night on top.

The **SeaTac airport belt**, 14 miles south around Seattle-Tacoma International Airport, is where the real budget savings live — two dozen chain motels and hotels from £57, almost all with free parking, and the Link light rail running direct to Downtown in under 45 minutes for about $3. It's not scenic and there's genuine runway noise at some properties, but for anyone renting a car, catching an early flight, or simply prioritising price over postcode, this is where the maths works.

**Bellevue**, across Lake Washington to the east, is Seattle's tech-suburb answer — home to its own downtown, Bellevue Square's shopping and dining, and a growing skyline of its own. Hotel prices here sit in the £100–135 range, a genuine middle ground between SeaTac's rock-bottom motels and Downtown Seattle's towers, and it suits business travellers visiting the Eastside tech corridor as much as leisure travellers who want a quieter base with easy access back into the city.

**Renton**, southeast on the shore of Lake Washington, is smaller and more residential — but it's home to this guide's single cheapest hotel, and its extended-stay suites properties (Sonesta Simply Suites, Larkspur Landing) are worth a look for anyone staying more than a couple of nights and wanting a kitchenette to cut food costs.

For a first Seattle trip with a flexible budget: Downtown. For the tightest budget or an early flight: the SeaTac belt. For a quieter, suburban base with its own scene: Bellevue. For the single cheapest bed with a lake view: Renton.

## How Seattle Compares for UK Budget Travellers

Seattle sits in an unusual spot among US West Coast cities: its luxury tier is priced in line with Los Angeles and San Francisco's grand hotels, but its budget tier is deeper and, in places, cheaper — the SeaTac airport belt alone offers two dozen genuine sub-£130 options within a short Link ride of Downtown, a density few comparably sized US cities can match. Where Seattle differs most from LA is transit: you don't need a hire car to make the budget tier work, because the Link light rail connects the airport belt to Downtown directly. The trade-off for that convenience is Seattle's weather and its combined hotel tax, both of which push the case toward travelling outside the July–September peak if price is the priority.

## Pike Place, the Space Needle and Seattle's Free Essentials

A few non-hotel essentials worth planning around your stay:

- **Pike Place Market** — arrive by 8am to watch the fishmongers set up before the crowds build; the original Starbucks on the market's edge opens early with a shorter queue than midday. Free to wander, and the single best free morning in the city.
- **Space Needle and Chihuly Garden and Glass** — the monorail from Westlake Center (near several luxury-tier hotels) reaches the Space Needle in about two minutes. Chihuly's glass sculpture garden sits at its base; the exterior grounds can be glimpsed without paying full admission.
- **Kerry Park, Queen Anne Hill** — the classic postcard view of the Space Needle with the skyline and Mount Rainier behind it on a clear day. Free, a short drive or rideshare from Downtown or the MarQueen Hotel.
- **Ferries to Bainbridge Island** — Washington State Ferries leave the downtown waterfront for a 35-minute crossing, one of the cheapest scenic half-days in the city.
- **Cruise-to-Alaska season at Pier 91** — Smith Cove, northwest of Downtown, is Seattle's Alaska cruise gateway from spring through autumn — factor pre-cruise hotel logistics in as covered above.
- **The tech-hub backdrop** — Amazon's headquarters in South Lake Union and Boeing's manufacturing presence south of the city (near the SeaTac belt) shape a lot of Seattle's business travel; several mid-range and budget hotels in this guide sit conveniently for both.

## UK Practicalities

- **Direct UK flights**: British Airways and Delta fly London Heathrow to Seattle-Tacoma (SEA) direct in around 9h45m; Virgin Atlantic and others connect via partner hubs. [Search flights to SEA](/flights?to=SEA).
- **Airport transfer**: Link light rail from Sea-Tac to Downtown, ~38–45 minutes, around $3. Rideshare runs $35–45 and 25–40 minutes depending on I-5 traffic.
- **Visa / entry**: ESTA required (esta.cbp.dhs.gov, $21, usually approved within minutes, valid 2 years). Check the latest entry rules on the [UK government's USA travel advice](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/usa) page before you fly.
- **Currency**: US dollar. Tipping runs 18–20% at restaurants, $1–2 per drink, $2–5 per bag, $5–10 a night for housekeeping.
- **Taxes and parking**: Combined hotel tax runs to roughly 15.6% — rarely shown in headline prices. Downtown hotel parking runs $40–55 a night; almost every SeaTac, Renton and Bellevue budget hotel in this guide includes free parking instead.
- **Best months**: July–September for warm, dry, long evenings — also the priciest. November–March (excluding Christmas week) is the cheapest stretch of the year.
