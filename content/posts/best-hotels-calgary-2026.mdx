## Where to Stay in Calgary: Choosing Your Base

Calgary is a big, low-rise, car-oriented city on the prairie where the Bow and Elbow rivers meet, and the honest truth is that **where you sleep matters less here than in a dense European capital and more than the map suggests** — because distances are large and the airport is a long way from the core. Here is the breakdown.

**Downtown (the Commercial Core)** is the practical first choice for a city stay. It is a compact grid of office towers along the north bank of the Bow, busy on weekdays and noticeably quiet at weekends when the office workers go home. What it offers a visitor is the deepest hotel stock, the free downtown stretch of the **C-Train** along 7th Avenue, the **+15 skywalk network** for crossing the core indoors in winter, and walking access to the **Calgary Tower**, **Stephen Avenue**, the Glenbow, Studio Bell and **Prince's Island Park** on the river. **Hotel Le Germain**, **Hotel 11**, the **Sandman Signature**, **Best Western Plus Suites Downtown** and the **Ramada Plaza Downtown** are all here across a wide price range. It is not a nightlife district, but it is the most efficient base.

**The Beltline** sits immediately south of downtown and is the livelier choice. Built around **17th Avenue SW — the Red Mile — and 4th Street SW**, it holds the best concentration of restaurants, bars, cafés and independent shops in the city, and it is walking distance from both the downtown core and the Stampede grounds. If you want an evening scene on your doorstep rather than a quiet business district, base here. It has fewer big-brand hotels than downtown but plenty within a short walk, and it is where Calgary actually feels like a city rather than an office park.

**The airport belt in the north-east**, around Calgary International, should be booked only for the nights either side of a flight — or as a comfortable first night before an early drive to the Rockies. This is where a large share of the cheap and mid-range hotels in this guide sit, because they sell to crews and connecting passengers rather than sightseers. The **CLIQUE hotels**, the **Radisson**, the **Holiday Inns**, the **Hampton Inns**, the **Grand Hotel**, the **Wingate** and many more are all out here. **Motel Village and the university area**, in the north-west near the **University of Calgary**, **SAIT** and **McMahon Stadium** off Crowchild Trail, is the traditional budget quarter — a cluster of inexpensive inns and motels with the useful advantage of a **C-Train station** for a straight run downtown. **The south-west edge and the suburbs** — where the **Grey Eagle Resort** and the outlying guesthouses sit — are really only for those with a car and a specific reason to be there, whether that is the casino and spa, a quiet suburban base, or a fast getaway west to the mountains the next morning.

## Beyond the Hotel: What to See in Calgary

Calgary rewards a day or two even if the Rockies are your real target. Start downtown. The **Calgary Tower**, the 191-metre landmark with a glass-floored observation deck, gives you the whole city laid out and, on a clear day, the wall of the Rockies on the western horizon — the quickest way to understand the geography of your trip. A short walk away, **Stephen Avenue** is the pedestrianised heart of downtown, a historic sandstone high street that fills with office workers, buskers and patio tables and is the best place in the core to eat and people-watch.

**Studio Bell, home of the National Music Centre**, is the cultural standout — a genuinely striking piece of architecture in the East Village housing an excellent, hands-on museum of Canadian music and a remarkable collection of instruments. Nearby, the **Glenbow** (reopening in a major new form) holds Western Canadian art and history, and the whole **East Village** riverfront has been transformed into one of the nicest walking areas in the city, anchored by the swooping **Peace Bridge** a little upstream.

For families and anyone curious about how the West was settled, **Heritage Park Historical Village** on the Glenmore Reservoir is one of Canada's largest living-history museums — a full working village of the pre-1950 prairie, with a steam train, a paddle steamer and costumed interpreters, that eats up an easy half-day 👪. The **Calgary Zoo** on St George's Island and the **TELUS Spark** science centre are the other big family draws.

**Get to the river.** **Prince's Island Park**, a green island in the Bow just north of downtown, and the extensive **Bow River pathway** network are where Calgary goes to walk, run and cycle, and on a warm day they show the city at its most relaxed. The pathways run for miles in both directions and are genuinely lovely.

And then there is **the Stampede** itself, if your timing lands on it in July — ten days of rodeo, chuckwagon racing, midway rides, concerts, pancake breakfasts and a city that puts on a cowboy hat and means it. It is a spectacle unlike anything else in Canada, and if you are here for it, it is the main event rather than a sideshow. Just book your hotel very early, as the price section above explains.

## Getting Around Calgary

The **C-Train**, Calgary's light-rail system, is the backbone for visitors and it has one great feature: travel along the **7th Avenue corridor through downtown is free**. Two lines — Red and Blue — run through the core, and the Red Line in particular is useful, connecting downtown south to **Chinook** and north-west towards the **university** and the suburbs, passing several hotels in this guide. Buy a ticket or a day pass, which also covers city buses, from the machines at any station. It is clean, frequent and the easiest way to move between the core and the value hotel zones without a car.

**From the airport**, the key fact is that **there is no rail link to Calgary International (YYC)**. The airport sits about 17km north-east of downtown, a 25 to 35 minute drive. Your options are Calgary Transit's **Route 300** bus between the airport and downtown, a **free shuttle** run by many of the airport-belt hotels, or a taxi or ride-hail on a metered fare. Most Rockies-bound visitors, though, do the simplest thing of all: pick up a **hire car** at the terminal desks and drive straight west without going into the city at all.

**For the city itself, and especially for the Rockies, a hire car is the natural choice.** Calgary is spread out and built for driving, parking is plentiful and cheaper than in eastern Canadian cities (downtown aside), and the mountains are the whole reason many people come. Canada drives on the right. If you are staying purely downtown and in the Beltline for a short city break, you can manage on the C-Train, buses and your feet — but the moment Banff, Lake Louise or Kananaskis enters the plan, a car earns its keep many times over.

**Two money habits to build.** First, the good news: outside hotels, **the price you see is almost the price you pay**, because Alberta adds only 5 per cent GST rather than a big provincial tax — so a menu or shelf price is much closer to your final bill than anywhere else in Canada. Second, **tip 15 to 20 per cent** in restaurants and bars, around 10 to 15 per cent for taxi drivers, and a couple of dollars a bag for porters. The card machine will offer percentage buttons; the tip goes on top of the bill. And drink the **tap water** — it is excellent, straight off the mountains.
