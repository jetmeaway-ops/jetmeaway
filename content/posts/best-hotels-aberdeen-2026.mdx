## The Scout's Take: Book Aberdeen for the Weekend

Here is the single most useful thing to know about booking a hotel in Aberdeen, and it runs opposite to almost every other city we cover. Because the city's demand is driven by the **offshore energy industry** rather than tourism, its hotels fill **Monday to Thursday** with contractors, crews and conference visitors — and empty out on **Friday and Saturday** when they go home. That means midweek rates climb and weekend rates soften, the reverse of a leisure city like Edinburgh or Bath.

If your trip is flexible, **book a weekend**. You will frequently pay noticeably less for the same room — especially at the big business hotels like [The Aberdeen Altens Hotel](/hotels/la_lp1c11a?city=Aberdeen) and the airport-belt properties near Dyce. The from-prices in this guide were pulled on live searches; a midweek business date will read higher, a low-season weekend lower. There is no festival spike to plan around and no five-star premium, so the calendar — not the address — is the lever that moves your bill most.

The city itself is compact and walkable along the flat granite spine of **Union Street**, though the side streets drop away steeply toward the harbour. Stay central for the shops, the harbour and the ferries; head to the West End suburbs or the Deeside road for country-house character and free parking if you are touring the castle trail.

## Neighbourhood Intelligence: Aberdeen's Best Half-Days

A few essentials for the Granite City and its doorstep:

- **Union Street and the granite heart** — the mile-long spine of the city, lined with silver granite buildings that glitter after rain. Marischal College (the world's second-largest granite building), Union Terrace Gardens and the shops are all here; it is flat and walkable, though the side streets drop steeply to the harbour.
- **The harbour and the ferries** — one of the busiest working harbours in the UK, with supply vessels for the North Sea rigs and the NorthLink ferries to Orkney and Shetland sailing overnight. Walk out to the harbour mouth and Torry Battery for the dolphins.
- **Dolphin watching from Torry Battery** — the resident bottlenose dolphins of the Moray Firth are regularly seen from the headland, free and without a boat, especially on a rising tide in summer. Bring binoculars and a windproof layer.
- **Aberdeen Beach and the esplanade** — a long sweep of sand backed by a leisure strip, a 15-minute walk from Union Street. Bracing and windswept rather than a sunbathing resort — perfect for a blow-away walk and a bag of chips.
- **Old Aberdeen and King's College** — the medieval university quarter a mile north, built around King's College (1495) with its crown-spire tower, the cobbled High Street and St Machar's Cathedral. One of the loveliest corners of the city; reach it by a short bus or taxi.
- **Dunnottar Castle, Stonehaven** — the dramatic clifftop ruin about 30 minutes south, one of Scotland's most photographed castles, perched on a headland above the sea. Pair it with fish and chips in Stonehaven harbour.
- **Royal Deeside and Balmoral** — the River Dee valley running west through Banchory, Aboyne and Ballater to Balmoral Castle, the royal Highland home (about 90 minutes; check visitor dates before travelling). One of the finest drives in eastern Scotland.
- **The Speyside whisky trail** — Glenfiddich, Macallan, Glenlivet and dozens more distilleries begin about 60–90 minutes northwest. Use an organised tour or a designated driver, as Scotland's drink-drive limit is strict.

## Booking Aberdeen Hotels in 2026: The Weekday-vs-Weekend Rule

The one booking rule that matters most in Aberdeen is the calendar, not the address. Because the city's hotel demand is driven by the **offshore oil-and-gas industry** rather than tourism, rooms fill **Monday to Thursday** with contractors, crews and conference visitors and empty on **Friday and Saturday**. That inverts the normal pattern: midweek is the peak, the weekend is the bargain. If you can, book a Friday or Saturday night — you will often pay less for the same room, especially at the big business hotels like [The Aberdeen Altens Hotel](/hotels/la_lp1c11a?city=Aberdeen) and the airport-belt properties near Dyce.

There is no festival spike to plan around and no true five-star premium, so unlike Edinburgh in August there is no single date to avoid — just the midweek business rhythm to work with. The quietest, cheapest stretch of the year is November to February outside Christmas and New Year. **Why are Aberdeen hotels sometimes more expensive midweek than at weekends?**

Because Aberdeen is Europe's offshore oil-and-gas capital, and business demand drives the calendar. Monday to Thursday nights fill with energy-industry crews, contractors and conference visitors, so midweek rates climb; Friday and Saturday nights, when the business travellers go home, often fall — the reverse of most leisure cities. If your trip is flexible, book a weekend and you will frequently pay less for the same room, especially at the larger business hotels like The Aberdeen Altens Hotel and the airport-belt properties.

**Is Aberdeen expensive compared with Edinburgh and Glasgow?**

Aberdeen is generally cheaper than Edinburgh and roughly level with Glasgow for hotels, with one twist: the energy industry pushes midweek rates up while leaving weekends soft. There is no Edinburgh-style festival spike and no true five-star premium, so the ceiling is lower — the top room here is about £194 versus several hundred pounds in Edinburgh. Book a weekend and Aberdeen is one of the better-value city breaks in the UK.

**How do I get to Aberdeen from London?**

Direct flights from London to Aberdeen (ABZ) take about 1h30m, with BA and easyJet flying from Heathrow, Gatwick and Luton. The train from London King's Cross via LNER takes roughly 7 hours through Edinburgh, so many travellers fly or take the Caledonian Sleeper overnight. Driving is around 8–9 hours up the A1 and A90. For most people flying is quickest, but the train delivers you straight into the city centre at Aberdeen station.

**Is there a sleeper train to Aberdeen?**

Yes — the Caledonian Sleeper runs overnight between London Euston and Aberdeen most nights, letting you board in the evening and wake up in the Granite City. You can book a berth in a cabin (some with en-suite) or a cheaper seat. It is a genuinely useful option that saves a night's hotel and a day's travel, and it arrives right in the city centre; book well ahead as cabins sell out, especially in summer.

**How long is the train from Edinburgh or Glasgow to Aberdeen?**

The train from Edinburgh to Aberdeen takes about 2h20m, and from Glasgow around 2h40m, both running up the east coast through Dundee. Services are frequent and drop you at Aberdeen station in the heart of the centre, a short walk from the Union Square shopping centre and several hotels in this guide including Leonardo Hotel Aberdeen and The Station Hotel. It is an easy add-on if you are already touring central Scotland.

**Does Aberdeen have an airport, and where does it fly?**

Yes — Aberdeen International Airport (ABZ) sits about 7 miles northwest of the centre at Dyce, roughly 20–30 minutes in by taxi or bus. It has strong UK domestic links (London, Manchester, Birmingham, Bristol, Belfast) plus European routes and a heavy schedule of North Sea helicopter and business flights tied to the energy industry. The Hilton Aberdeen TECA and several airport-belt hotels are handy for early departures.

**Do I need a visa or passport to visit Aberdeen?**

No — Aberdeen is in Scotland, part of the United Kingdom, so UK residents need no visa or passport to visit (though you will need photo ID for flight check-in). International visitors who already hold a valid UK visa or qualify for visa-free entry to the UK need nothing extra to travel on to Scotland, as the entry requirement is for the UK as a whole rather than Scotland specifically.

**What is the weather like in Aberdeen?**

Cool, changeable and often windy year-round — Aberdeen sits on the exposed North Sea coast and rarely gets hot even in summer, when highs are typically 15–18°C. Bring a windproof, waterproof layer in every season; the light is beautiful when it breaks, but haar (sea fog) can roll in off the water and the wind off the harbour is a constant. May, June and September give the best balance of dry days and long daylight.

**What is Aberdeen known for?**

Aberdeen is the Granite City — built almost entirely from locally quarried silver-grey granite that sparkles with mica after rain — and the offshore oil-and-gas capital of Europe, which is why business hotels dominate its skyline. It is also a historic university and fishing city with a working harbour, a long beach and esplanade, and a role as gateway to Royal Deeside, Dunnottar Castle and the Speyside whisky country. The energy industry shapes its hotel prices more than tourism does.

**Is Aberdeen a good base for castles and the Highlands?**

Excellent — Aberdeenshire is often called Scotland's castle country, with more castles per acre than anywhere in the UK, and Aberdeen is the natural base. Dunnottar Castle, Royal Deeside and Balmoral, Craigievar, Crathes and Fraserburgh's coast are all day trips, and the Cairngorms National Park and Speyside whisky trail lie to the west. A hire car opens the whole region up; several guide hotels offer free parking for exactly this kind of touring trip.

**Can I visit Dunnottar Castle from Aberdeen?**

Yes — Dunnottar Castle, the dramatic clifftop ruin near Stonehaven, is one of the easiest and most rewarding day trips from Aberdeen, about 30 minutes south by car or a short train to Stonehaven plus a scenic coastal walk. It is one of Scotland's most photographed castles, perched on a headland above the North Sea, and pairs well with fish and chips in Stonehaven harbour. Allow a half day and wear sturdy shoes for the cliff path.

**Can I visit Balmoral and Royal Deeside from Aberdeen?**

Yes — Royal Deeside runs west from Aberdeen along the River Dee, and Balmoral Castle, the royal family's Highland home, is about 50 miles (an hour and a half) up the A93 near Ballater and Braemar. The castle grounds and exhibitions open to visitors for much of the year (check dates before travelling, as access varies with the royal calendar). The drive itself through Banchory, Aboyne and Ballater is one of the finest in eastern Scotland, and Ardoe House or Macdonald Norwood Hall make natural first-night bases on the route.

**Can I do a Speyside whisky day trip from Aberdeen?**

Yes — the Speyside whisky region, home to Glenfiddich, Macallan, Glenlivet and dozens more distilleries, begins about 60–90 minutes northwest of Aberdeen, making a distillery day trip very doable by car. Many visitors base a night or two in Aberdeen and drive out to two or three distilleries on the Malt Whisky Trail. If you plan to taste, use an organised tour or a designated driver, as Scotland's drink-drive limit is strict and lower than England's.

**Where can I go dolphin watching near Aberdeen?**

Aberdeen is one of the best city-based dolphin-watching spots in the UK — the resident bottlenose dolphins of the Moray Firth are regularly seen right from the harbour mouth and Torry Battery headland, especially on a rising tide in summer. It is free, needs no boat, and the same waters host seals and seabirds. Boat trips also run in season for a closer look; bring binoculars and a windproof layer as the headland is exposed.

**Can I get a ferry to Orkney or Shetland from Aberdeen?**

Yes — NorthLink Ferries sail overnight from Aberdeen harbour to Kirkwall (Orkney) and Lerwick (Shetland), right from the heart of the city, making Aberdeen the mainland gateway to the Northern Isles. You can book a cabin or a reclining seat, and take a car across. It is a popular add-on to an Aberdeen trip; a city-centre hotel near the harbour like Leonardo Hotel Aberdeen keeps you within a short walk or taxi of the terminal for an evening sailing.

**Where should I stay for Union Street and the city centre?**

For Union Street, the granite mile at the city's heart, base yourself at a central address like Mercure Aberdeen Caledonian Hotel on Union Terrace, Leonardo Hotel Aberdeen near the stations, or budget picks such as Brentwood Aberdeen City Centre and Citi Hotel Aberdeen City Centre. From here you can walk to the shops, Marischal College, Union Terrace Gardens and the harbour. The centre is compact and flat along Union Street itself, though the side streets drop away steeply toward the harbour.

**Where should I stay near Aberdeen beach?**

Aberdeen's long sandy beach and esplanade run along the northeast edge of the centre, a 15-minute walk or short taxi from Union Street, backed by a leisure strip with a cinema, restaurants and the football stadium area. Few hotels sit directly on the sand, so most beach visitors stay centrally and walk over, or pick a Bridge of Don address like the Belvilla Aberdeen Bridge of Don Sea View just north of the beach. The beach is bracing and windswept rather than a sunbathing resort — beautiful for a blow-away walk.

**Where is Old Aberdeen, and should I stay there?**

Old Aberdeen is the historic university quarter about a mile north of the modern centre, built around King's College (founded 1495) with its distinctive crown-spire tower, cobbled High Street and St Machar's Cathedral. It is one of the loveliest corners of the city and well worth a wander, but it has few hotels, so most visitors stay centrally and reach it by a short bus or taxi ride. Base yourself in the centre and treat Old Aberdeen as a half-day walking visit.

**Do hotels in Aberdeen have parking?**

Many do, especially outside the tight city centre — the business and suburban hotels such as The Aberdeen Altens Hotel, Village Hotel Aberdeen, Atholl Hotel and the airport-belt properties offer free on-site parking, which matters if you are touring the castle trail by car. Central Union Street hotels more often rely on nearby public car parks with a charge. If you are driving to Aberdeen for a Deeside or Speyside trip, filter for a hotel with its own car park to avoid daily centre parking fees.

**Which hotels in Aberdeen are best for families?**

**Which hotels in Aberdeen are best for business travel?**

Aberdeen is built for business travel thanks to the energy industry. The Aberdeen Altens Hotel serves the southern industrial parks, Hilton Aberdeen TECA sits at the main conference and arena complex, and Leonardo Hotel Aberdeen and Mercure Aberdeen Caledonian Hotel cover the central business district near the stations. Book these midweek well ahead, as energy-sector demand fills them Monday to Thursday; you will often find the same rooms cheaper on a leisure weekend.

**Are Scottish banknotes accepted across the UK?**

Scottish banknotes (issued by Bank of Scotland, RBS and Clydesdale) are legal currency throughout the UK but are not always legal tender in England, meaning some English shops may refuse them even though they are valid money. This is rarely a problem in Scotland itself. If you are travelling on south of the border afterwards, spend your Scottish notes before you leave or swap them at a bank for Bank of England notes.

**Do I need to tip in Aberdeen restaurants and hotels?**

Tipping in Scotland is appreciated but not obligatory, and less expected than in the United States. In restaurants, 10 percent for good table service is normal (check whether a service charge is already added before adding more), while for bar drinks, taxis and hotel housekeeping a small tip is welcome but optional. There is no pressure to tip on top of an added service charge, and rounding up is perfectly acceptable.

A Friday or Saturday night in low season can be dramatically cheaper than a midweek night in the same week. Booking a few weeks ahead for a weekend usually beats a last-minute midweek stay; the reverse of how leisure-driven cities price.
