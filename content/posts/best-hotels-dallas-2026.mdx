## Going to the 2026 World Cup in Dallas?

The venue everyone means when they say "Dallas" is **Dallas Stadium (everyday name: AT&T Stadium), in Arlington** — not in Dallas city centre at all, but in the suburb of Arlington, sitting almost exactly halfway between Downtown Dallas and Downtown Fort Worth. It is one of the largest domed stadiums in the world, with a retractable roof and the vast video board it is famous for, and across the tournament it stages nine matches. The one circled on every English calendar: **Dallas hosts England vs Croatia on 17 June 2026 — England's opening match — at AT&T Stadium in nearby Arlington.**

Because the stadium is in Arlington, there is no single "city centre" within walking distance — Arlington is a low-rise suburb organised around its Entertainment District, where the stadium shares the neighbourhood with the Texas Rangers' ballpark and a cluster of hotels. If your priority is walking to the gates and being in the thick of the build-up, base yourself in **Arlington** at one of the Entertainment District hotels below. If you'd rather have proper nightlife, a wide choice of restaurants and a buzzing place to celebrate (or commiserate) after the match, base yourself in **Downtown or Uptown Dallas** (a 25–35 minute drive) or in **Downtown Fort Worth** (a 20–25 minute drive) — the two cities flank the stadium roughly equally. On a tight budget, this guide's [budget tier](#budget-hotels) — concentrated around the DFW airport belt and Love Field — sits roughly equidistant from all three and holds rates well below Downtown's convention pricing.

Getting to the stadium on match day is a road exercise: there is no rail line that runs to the venue. From Downtown or Uptown Dallas it's 25–35 minutes via I-30 West; from Downtown Fort Worth it's 20–25 minutes via I-30 East. Arlington runs a seasonal Trinity Metro shuttle for big events, and rideshare drop-off zones operate near the stadium, but both fill up and surge around kick-off. If you drive, pre-book a parking pass; if you don't, build in extra time and expect queues. As a rule of thumb, allow at least 90 minutes door-to-gate on the day of a major fixture. ## The Scout's Take: Downtown, Uptown, or the Budget Corridor?

**Downtown Dallas** is the high-rise core: the Dallas Arts District (the largest contiguous arts district in the United States), the West End, Dealey Plaza, and the DART light-rail hub that ties the city together. For World Cup fans it's the best-connected base in the metroplex — a straight I-30 run to Arlington, walkable restaurants and bars for the build-up, and DART trains to the airport and the wider city. This is where the grand hotels and the newest luxury towers cluster.

**Uptown Dallas**, just north of Downtown, is the city's most walkable district — McKinney Avenue's restaurants and bars, the free M-Line Trolley looping down to the Arts District, and a more relaxed, leafy feel than the high-rise core. It's the easiest first-timer base in Dallas and still only a 25–35 minute drive to AT&T Stadium.

**Deep Ellum**, just east of Downtown, is Dallas's live-music and street-art neighbourhood — thin on hotel stock of its own, best treated as a short rideshare from a Downtown base rather than somewhere to book directly. **Bishop Arts**, across the Trinity River south-west of Downtown, is the city's most walkable indie food-and-boutique district, and holds one of this guide's budget picks. The **Design District**, between Downtown and Uptown, is Dallas's warehouse-turned-gallery-and-brewery quarter, a short drive from the Market Center hotel cluster.

For tight budgets: this guide's [budget tier](#budget-hotels) — the **DFW airport belt** (Irving, Las Colinas) and the **Love Field / Market Center corridor** just north-west of Downtown are where £48-79 a night is still real, with free parking almost everywhere and DART or short drives to Downtown, Uptown and, eventually, Arlington. ## Beyond the Stadium — Dallas's Essentials

A few non-match experiences worth planning around your stay:

- **The Dallas Arts District** — the largest contiguous arts district in the United States, anchored by the Nasher Sculpture Center, the Dallas Museum of Art (free general admission) and the Winspear Opera House.
- **Klyde Warren Park** — a 5.4-acre deck park built over a sunken freeway, connecting Uptown and the Arts District with food trucks, games and free events most weekends.
- **Bishop Arts District** — independent boutiques, murals and some of the city's best casual dining across the Trinity River, reachable by rideshare or DART's coming extension.
- **Deep Ellum** — Dallas's live-music and street-art neighbourhood, a short drive or DART hop east of Downtown, busiest after dark.
- **The Sixth Floor Museum at Dealey Plaza** — the former Texas School Book Depository, now a museum on the JFK assassination, in the West End near Downtown hotels.
- **Fort Worth Stockyards** — 20-25 minutes from Downtown Fort Worth, twice-daily cattle drives and Western heritage a world away from Dallas's glass towers.
- **Reunion Tower** — the geodesic-domed observation tower on the Downtown skyline, a short walk from several of this guide's Downtown picks.
