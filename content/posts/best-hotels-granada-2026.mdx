## The Scout's Take: Inside the Alhambra, Facing It, or Down in the City?

Every Granada hotel orbits the Alhambra in some way. The question is whether you want to sleep *inside* the palace grounds, wake up *looking at* them, or base yourself down in the old town where the tapas bars and your budget both stretch further.

If you're the kind of traveller who plans a once-in-a-lifetime stay around a single building, **[Parador de Granada](/hotels?city=Granada)** is the answer — the only hotel actually inside the Alhambra grounds, a 15th-century convent built by Ferdinand and Isabella inside the conquered Nasrid complex. You walk out of your room and you're inside the most-visited monument in Spain, before the first tour bus arrives. It books out 12 months ahead for peak season, there's no pool or spa, and the wifi is honest about the building being 600 years old. You pay for the address.

Compare that to **[Alhambra Palace Hotel](/hotels/la_lp5bcb4?city=Granada)** — same hill, same view-of-a-lifetime, but a completely different proposition. Built in 1910 in Moorish-revival style, it sits *on* the Alhambra hill (not inside the grounds), and its terrace looks straight at the Nasrid Palaces and the snow-capped Sierra Nevada beyond. Easier to book, easier on the wallet, and the sunset cocktail bar is the city's best one-liner of an experience.

For everyone watching the budget, the smart move is the [budget tier](#budget-hotels) below — old-town hostels and aparthotels from £28 a night, a 20-25 minute uphill walk to the same Alhambra gate, and the Realejo and Albaicín tapas bars closer than they'd be from the hill anyway. ## How Granada Compares to Seville and Córdoba

The three classic Andalucía cities — Granada, Seville, Córdoba — each ask UK travellers for a different week of Spain. Granada is the mountain Andalusian capital: the Sierra Nevada at altitude, the Alhambra hanging above the old town, a working university city with a student tapas culture. Seville is the flat capital of southern Spain: the largest Gothic cathedral on earth, the Alcázar's mudéjar interiors, the Triana flamenco district across the Guadalquivir. Córdoba is the smallest and most intense of the three: the Mezquita-Cathedral (the 8th-century mosque with the 16th-century cathedral built inside the prayer hall) and the Jewish Quarter walkable in a single day.

Hotel-wise the three trade in tight bands, and Granada consistently comes out cheapest. Granada's luxury tier tops out around £330 a night against Seville's Alfonso XIII and Hotel Casa 1800 Seville, which run €350-700; Córdoba's Hospes Palacio del Bailío and Eurostars Palace land at €200-400, mid-table between the two. Where Granada pulls furthest ahead is the budget end: Arc House Granada at £28 and a genuine cluster of sub-£45 hostels and pensiones beat anything comparable in central Seville or Córdoba. Granada's other edge is the *carmen* inventory — hillside houses with private walled gardens that Seville and Córdoba can't match. Combine all three on a five-day Andalucía loop: the Renfe AVE makes Seville–Córdoba 45 minutes, Córdoba–Granada 1h20m, and the three together give you the most complete Spain trip you can take in a week. ## Beyond the Hotel: 10 Things to Do in Granada (2026)

Granada rewards travellers who book ahead — and Alhambra tickets are non-negotiable. The single biggest mistake we see in 2026 is leaving them to the day-of. Here's the prioritised list:

- **Alhambra & Generalife** — The most visited monument in Spain, year-round. The Nasrid Palaces are the headline; Generalife gardens are the under-rated companion. Book at least 60-90 days ahead via tickets.alhambra-patronato.es. Morning slots are quietest and coolest.
- **Albaicín UNESCO Walk** — The old Moorish quarter, all whitewashed walls and wrought-iron lanterns. Start at Plaza Nueva, climb the Cuesta del Chapiz, get lost on purpose. Wear shoes you can grip cobblestones with.
- **Mirador de San Nicolás at Sunset** — The most photographed view in Andalusia. Be there 45 minutes before sunset, expect a crowd, expect a busker. For a quieter alternative, walk five more minutes uphill to Mirador de San Cristóbal.
- **Sacromonte Cave Flamenco** — The original flamenco, performed in 16th-century caves carved into the hillside. Cuevas Los Tarantos and Venta El Gallo are the reliable venues. Book 2-3 days ahead.
- **Cathedral & Royal Chapel (Capilla Real)** — Ferdinand and Isabella's tomb is in the Capilla Real — the same monarchs who took the Alhambra in 1492 and signed off Columbus's first voyage. Tickets sold separately for cathedral and chapel; do both.
- **Hammam Al Ándalus Arab Baths** — A modern reconstruction of a traditional 11th-century Granadian hammam, two minutes' walk from Plaza Nueva. Two hours, three pools, optional ras al hanout massage. Book 24-48 hours ahead.
- **Free Tapas Crawl (the Sacromonte tradition)** — Granada is the last Spanish city where every drink universally comes with a free tapa — a tradition that survives nowhere else in Spain, traced back to the working-man bars of Sacromonte and the old labourer quarters. Order a caña (small beer) or vermut for €2–3 and a portion of jamón, croquetas, or albóndigas (meatballs) lands at the same moment the drink does. Realejo and Albaicín are the best routes. Bar Los Diamantes, Bodegas Castañeda, El Trillo. Three drinks = dinner. Skip the Plaza Nueva tapas spots — they cut corners on the free tapa.
- **Monasterio de la Cartuja** — The most over-the-top Spanish Baroque interior outside Salamanca. Twenty-minute walk from the centre or a quick taxi. Astonishingly empty most days.
- **Day Trip to Sierra Nevada** — Forty-five minutes by car. Skiing November-April; hiking and the Pico Veleta drive May-October. The Sierra Nevada is mainland Spain's highest range — you'll see it from your hotel terrace, you might as well drive into it.
- **Carmen Walk in the Realejo** — Granada's old Jewish quarter. Quieter than the Albaicín, lower altitude, dotted with private *carmen* houses behind unmarked doors. The Casa de los Tiros museum is a free 30-minute detour.

## Booking Granada Hotels in 2026: Rates, Alhambra Tickets and the Dates to Avoid

Granada room rates swing by season more than by day of the week, unlike Vegas-style event cities — but they do spike hard around two recurring dates. **Semana Santa (Easter week)**, when Granada's processions fill the old town and hotel rates city-wide jump sharply, and the **Sierra Nevada ski season peak (late February-early March)**, when skiers competing for the same beds push up even old-town budget rates. Outside those windows, late January through early March and late June are typically the softest stretches for hotel prices.

The number that decides your real cost isn't just the room rate — it's whether you've secured your **Alhambra ticket**. A cheap hotel booked around a sold-out Alhambra slot is a wasted trip; book the ticket first via tickets.alhambra-patronato.es, then match your hotel dates to it.
