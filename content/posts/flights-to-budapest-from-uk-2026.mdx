## Money in Budapest: forint, not euro

A quick but important tip that catches many UK visitors out: **Hungary keeps its own currency, the Hungarian forint (HUF)** — it is not in the euro despite being in the EU and Schengen. In mid-2026 the exchange rate was roughly **410-420 forints to the pound**.

- **Pay in forints**, not euros. Some tourist-facing venues accept euros, but almost always at a poor rate — you lose money every time.
- **Use a card** where you can, and when a card machine offers to charge you in pounds instead of forints, **always choose forints** (declining "dynamic currency conversion" avoids a hidden markup).
- **Don't change money at the airport** — rates there are the worst you'll find. Withdraw forints from a reputable bank ATM in the city, or spend on a card that doesn't charge FX fees.

The favourable exchange rate is part of what makes Budapest such good value for UK travellers — food, drink and the famous thermal baths all go a long way — so it's worth handling the money the smart way.

## Common mistakes UK-Budapest travellers make

1. **Paying in euros.** Hungary uses the forint — paying in euros or using airport exchange loses you money every time.
2. **Accepting "pay in pounds" on card machines.** Always choose forints to dodge the dynamic-currency-conversion markup.
3. **Routing through London unnecessarily.** Budapest flies from many UK airports — your local one is often cheapest.
4. **Judging budget fares on the headline price.** A £30 Ryanair fare plus a £45 hold bag can match a Jet2 fare that includes baggage.
5. **Booking the Christmas markets late.** Late November-December is Budapest's peak — book 8-12 weeks ahead.
6. **Adding baggage at the airport.** Always add it online at booking.
7. **Using unofficial airport taxis.** Stick to the official rank or the cheap 100E bus.
