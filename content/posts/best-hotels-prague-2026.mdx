## The Scout's Take: Old Town, Malá Strana, or the Outer Districts?

**Old Town (Staré Město)** on the east bank: the Astronomical Clock, Gothic towers, Baroque churches, the Jewish Quarter, the Old Town Square where Czech independence was declared. The tourist infrastructure is thick — every second building is a restaurant, a boutique hotel, or an amber shop. The beauty is real; so is the price premium.

**Malá Strana (Lesser Town)** on the west bank: Baroque palaces of the Czech nobility, wisteria-covered lanes, the gardens cascading down from Prague Castle. Far fewer tourists, far more residents, the best lunch restaurants.

**Vinohrady, Žižkov, Karlín, Vršovice and Smíchov** — the outer, residential districts where this guide's budget tier lives. Vinohrady is where Czech professionals actually live and eat; Žižkov has the city's densest concentration of bars and some of its cheapest hotel beds; Karlín has reinvented old industrial buildings into Prague's loft-and-café district; Smíchov and Vršovice sit a short tram ride south with their own quieter charm. All are a single tram or metro hop from the historic core — the Prague that exists independently of the tourist economy, and where £40–50 still buys a proper hotel room.

For first-timers: stay Old Town for proximity to the Astronomical Clock and Charles Bridge. For repeat visitors: Malá Strana every time. For tight budgets: this guide's [budget tier](#budget-hotels) — the outer districts are where £36–50 a night is still real, without giving up a tram-ride connection to everything you came to see.

## How Prague Compares to Vienna and Krakow

Prague occupies an interesting middle position in the Central European luxury hotel scene. It costs noticeably less than Vienna — a Four Seasons Prague room is roughly 60% of a Hotel Sacher rate, and the Mandarin Oriental Prague in the Dominican monastery routinely undercuts the Mandarin Oriental Vienna — but the Baroque and Gothic building stock that hotels here occupy is arguably the best-preserved in Europe. Communist-era neglect actually protected the city: there were no funds for "modernising" the historic centre, so Prague emerged in 1989 with its UNESCO-listed core nearly intact. Against Paris and Rome, Prague is simply cheaper across the board — a 4-star with 10,000+ reviews from under £100 is a mid-range Prague reality that neither capital can match.

Against Krakow — its Polish counterpart in the medieval-Central-European bracket — Prague is more expensive but with deeper hotel pedigree. UK travellers who've done Vienna usually find Prague the right second-leg destination; those who've done Prague twice tend to graduate to Krakow for the more authentic Central European pacing. ## Neighborhood Intelligence: Prague's Best Hours

A few timing rules that turn Prague from a queue-shuffle into something genuinely beautiful:

- **Charles Bridge at 5:30am** — mist rising from the Vltava, the castle emerging from dawn light, occasionally a local running across to work. At 11am: shoulder-to-shoulder with 40 nationalities. Set the alarm. The bridge is free and open 24/7, so there's no gate to beat.
- **Astronomical Clock at 9am** — the 1410 mechanical show runs every hour. The 9am performance has a tiny crowd; midday is unbearable.
- **A Vinohrady evening** — Veltlin (natural wine), Bokovka (Czech wine with kitchen), Vinograf (Moravian wine) on Mánesova Street. A Vinohrady evening costs a quarter of an Old Town tourist restaurant and is four times more interesting.
- **Lokál Dlouhááá** — Dlouhá Street, Old Town. Unfiltered Pilsner Urquell tank beer delivered weekly from the Plzeň brewery, served the correct Czech way (three pours, the last a dense šnyt foam head). Authentic hospoda food alongside.
- **Petřín Hill funicular** — climbs to the Czech Eiffel Tower, the Mirror Maze, and the Strahov Monastery's Baroque library hall (the finest Baroque library room in Central Europe).
- **The lager-with-svíčková ritual at U Pinkasů** — the proper Czech beer-hall meal: order a tank Pilsner Urquell (drawn slowly, three-pour technique, dense foam head), then svíčková na smetaně — beef sirloin in a creamy root-vegetable sauce, served with bread dumplings (knedlíky), a slice of lemon, cranberry jam, and a dollop of whipped cream. U Pinkasů (Jungmannovo náměstí, open since 1843) is the original location where Pilsner Urquell was first served in Prague in 1843, a year after the brewery's founding.
- **Prague Castle by the back gate** — most visitors enter from Hradčanské náměstí (the front gate, packed with tour groups). The eastern entrance from Old Castle Stairs (Staré zámecké schody) climbs through Malá Strana's gardens and arrives via a quieter side. The Royal Garden behind the castle (the Belvedere summer palace, the Singing Fountain) is rarely visited.
- **Old Town Square at 8am** — the Týn Church towers, the Astronomical Clock face, the Hus monument, the pastel Baroque townhouses. Almost empty. By 11am you're sharing it with 5,000 people from cruise ship coach tours.
- **Žižkov and Karlín after dark** — Žižkov has more bars per square metre than any other Prague district, almost entirely locals; Karlín's converted industrial buildings house some of the city's best modern Czech restaurants. Both feel a world away from Old Town Square's tourist restaurants, at a fraction of the bill.
- **A day in Český Krumlov** — 3 hours south of Prague, the medieval Bohemian town the UNESCO listing exists for. Doable as a long day trip; better as an overnight, with rooms at Hotel Růže (a former Jesuit college overlooking the Vltava bend).

- **Direct UK flights**: BA, easyJet, Ryanair, Jet2, Wizz Air and TUI fly UK airports (LHR/LGW/STN/LTN/MAN and regional) to Prague Václav Havel (PRG) in about 2 hours. [Search flights to PRG](/flights?to=PRG).

- **Visa**: No visa required for UK passport holders (Schengen, 90 days per 180). ETIAS online travel authorisation applies from 2026 — check the [UK government's Czech Republic travel advice](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/czech-republic) page nearer your travel date.
- **Currency**: Czech Koruna (CZK). Prague does NOT use the Euro. £1 ≈ 28–30 CZK. Don't exchange money at airport bureaux — use ATMs in the city, and decline "dynamic currency conversion" at card machines.
- **City tax**: A municipal accommodation fee of roughly €2 (~50 CZK) per person, per night applies at every hotel, budget included, usually paid at check-in.
- **Best months**: May–June (long evenings, castle gardens in bloom), September–October (golden-hour light on the Baroque). December for the Christmas markets — book 3–4 months ahead. November–March (outside Christmas) is cheapest.
- **Budget**: Luxury trip — £180–625/night hotel. Mid-range trip — £75–150/night. Budget-tier trip — £36–62/night, £40–50/day food and sights. A 3-night budget trip built on this guide's cheap tier can land well under £400 per person before flights.
