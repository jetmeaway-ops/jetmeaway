## What to See and Do in Bandung

Bandung earns its "Paris van Java" nickname from its Dutch Art-Deco heritage and café culture, but the real draw is the mix of highland city and surrounding nature.

**Braga Street.** The historic colonial-era café strip in the heart of the city — Art-Deco facades, galleries, restaurants and coffee houses. The most atmospheric place for an evening stroll and dinner.

**Gedung Sate.** Bandung's most iconic building, the 1920s seat of the West Java government, topped by a distinctive skewer-like ornament. The surrounding Gasibu square is a popular local gathering spot.

**Tangkuban Perahu.** An active volcano about 30km north, with a drive-up crater rim and steaming vents — the classic Bandung day trip. Go early for clear views before the afternoon cloud.

**Kawah Putih.** A striking turquoise-white volcanic crater lake near Ciwidey, roughly two hours south — surreal, photogenic, and cool at altitude. Pair it with the Ciwidey tea plantations and strawberry farms.

**Factory-outlet shopping.** Jalan Riau (Jalan LLRE Martadinata) and Jalan Cihampelas are lined with clothing outlets and boutiques that pull Jakarta weekenders. Big malls include Trans Studio Mall and Paris Van Java.

**Trans Studio Bandung.** A large indoor theme park attached to Trans Studio Mall — a rainy-day and family favourite.

**Tea plantations.** The hills around Bandung — Ciwidey, Pangalengan, Lembang — are carpeted in tea estates, cool, green and scenic, an easy escape from the city.

## Bandung Hotels FAQs

**Do UK travellers need a visa for Bandung, Indonesia?**
UK passport holders get a 30-day visa on arrival (VOA) for tourism, currently around IDR 500,000, payable in rupiah or by card at the airport. It can be extended once for a further 30 days. There's also an e-VOA you can apply for online before travel via the official Indonesian immigration site. Bandung has no international airport for UK arrivals, so you'll clear immigration at Jakarta.

**How do I get to Bandung from the UK?**
There are no direct UK flights to Bandung. You fly into Jakarta's Soekarno-Hatta International Airport (CGK) — served from London with one stop via Gulf, Asian or European hubs — then continue to Bandung. From Jakarta the fastest option is the Whoosh high-speed train (around 45 minutes to Tegalluar/Padalarang, then a short shuttle into the city), or road, roughly three hours depending on traffic.

**What is the cheapest hotel in Bandung?**

**How much does a budget hotel in Bandung cost per night?**
Real, bookable budget hotels here run from about £9 to £25 a night — RedDoorz, Zodiak, Amaris and Ibis branches cluster in the low teens, while the pricier end reaches the mid-£20s. That's before any weekend or holiday premium.

**What currency is used in Bandung?**
The Indonesian Rupiah (IDR). Pound figures in this guide are rough conversions for comparison only — pay in rupiah on the ground. Cards work at hotels, malls and larger restaurants; carry cash for street food, warungs, angkots and markets.

**Is Bandung cheaper than Jakarta?**
Generally yes — Bandung's hotel stock skews affordable, with a deep budget tier from around £9 and five-star properties starting in the £50-100 band. It's long been a value weekend destination for Jakarta residents.

**What is the best time of year to visit Bandung?**
The dry season, roughly April to October, is best — cooler highland air, clearer skies for volcano views, easier day trips. The wet season (November to March) brings frequent afternoon downpours. At around 750m, Bandung stays cooler than Jakarta all year.

**How do I get around Bandung?**
Grab and Gojek ride-hailing apps are easiest — cheap, metered and cashless. Angkot shared minibuses cover fixed routes but confuse first-timers. Traffic is heavy, especially at weekends. Many central hotels put Braga Street and the outlets within walking distance.

**What is Bandung famous for?**
Its Dutch colonial Art-Deco architecture, factory-outlet and clothing shopping (Jalan Riau and Cihampelas), Braga Street café culture, and surrounding nature — the Tangkuban Perahu volcano, Kawah Putih crater lake and tea plantations. It's a favourite weekend escape from Jakarta.

**Can you do a volcano day trip from Bandung?**
Yes. Tangkuban Perahu, an active volcano with a drive-up crater rim, is about 30km north. Kawah Putih, a turquoise crater lake, is around two hours south near Ciwidey. Both work as day trips by hired car with driver or Grab. Go early for clearer views.

**Is Bandung good for shopping?**
Very — it's one of Indonesia's shopping capitals. The factory outlets along Jalan Riau and Jalan Cihampelas draw weekend crowds, and there are big malls like Trans Studio Mall and Paris Van Java. Many budget hotels sit within reach of the outlet strips.

**Which area is best to stay in Bandung?**
Central Bandung around Braga, Asia Afrika and Merdeka puts you near the Art-Deco core, cafés and landmarks. Dago (north) is leafier and hillier, with upscale hotels and views. Cihampelas and Setiabudi suit shoppers. For a first visit, the central heritage area is the most walkable base.

**Do they drive on the left in Indonesia?**
Yes, Indonesia drives on the left, like the UK. Most visitors don't self-drive in Bandung — traffic is heavy and Grab, Gojek or a hired car with driver is far easier and inexpensive.

**What is the Whoosh high-speed train?**
Whoosh is Indonesia's high-speed rail line linking Jakarta and Bandung, opened in 2023. It covers the journey in roughly 45 minutes at speeds around 350km/h, terminating at Tegalluar/Padalarang with a shuttle into the city. It's the fastest, most comfortable way to reach Bandung from Jakarta.

**Is Bandung suitable for families?**
Yes. Trans Studio Bandung, a large indoor theme park, is a family highlight, and the volcano and crater-lake trips suit older children. The cooler climate makes sightseeing more comfortable than in humid Jakarta, and many hotels are large modern properties with pools and family rooms.

**Are there luxury hotels in Bandung?**
Yes. The luxury tier here runs from around £54 to £188 a night — The Trans Luxury Hotel, Crowne Plaza, InterContinental Dago Pakar, Sheraton, Padma Hotel Bandung and The Gaia — genuine five-star properties, several with hillside Dago views, at prices well below equivalent brands in Europe.

**How many days should I spend in Bandung?**
Two to three days — enough for the Art-Deco heritage walk and Braga cafés, a shopping afternoon, and a volcano or crater-lake trip. Many treat it as a two-night weekend escape from Jakarta, arriving Friday by Whoosh and returning Sunday.

**Is Bandung colder than the rest of Indonesia?**
Noticeably cooler. At roughly 750m in the West Java highlands, Bandung enjoys a mild, spring-like climate — comfortable days and genuinely cool evenings. It's a big reason Jakarta residents escape here at weekends.

**Is it safe to travel to Bandung?**
Bandung is generally safe for tourists, with standard urban precautions — watch belongings in crowded markets and malls, and use registered Grab/Gojek rides at night. Check your government's current travel advice, and note volcanic areas like Tangkuban Perahu can close on activity alerts.

**Can I use Grab and Gojek in Bandung?**
Yes, both work well across Bandung — cars, motorbike taxis and food delivery. They're the cheapest and simplest way to move around a city with heavy traffic and confusing minibus routes. Download the apps and set up payment before you arrive.

**What food is Bandung known for?**
It's a Sundanese food city — try nasi timbel, batagor, siomay, and its many cafés and dessert spots. Braga Street and Dago are full of cafés, and street-food warungs across the city are cheap and excellent.

**How far is Bandung from Jakarta?**
About 150km southeast. By the Whoosh high-speed train it's roughly 45 minutes plus a short shuttle; by road it's around three hours in normal traffic, longer on busy weekends. There are no direct international flights into Bandung for UK travellers, so Jakarta (CGK) is the entry point.
