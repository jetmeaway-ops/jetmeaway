## Going to the 2026 World Cup in Miami?

Miami's matches are played at Miami Stadium (everyday name: Hard Rock Stadium) in Miami Gardens, roughly 16 miles north of South Beach and about 10 miles north-west of Downtown. It is the same ground that hosts the Miami Dolphins and the Miami Open tennis, a 65,000-plus-seat venue surrounded by large car parks just off Florida's Turnpike and I-95. Miami hosts 2026 World Cup matches at Hard Rock Stadium in Miami Gardens, in a city that turns every game into a Latin-American football party — so the question for UK fans is whether to base yourself near the stadium or in the city and commute.

There is no metro line directly to the ground, which shapes everything. If you want the shortest match-day journey, base yourself in **Aventura** or **Miami Gardens** — Aventura is about a 15-minute drive from the stadium and sits on the Brightline rail line, with a mall, beaches and restaurants on the doorstep. If you'd rather have South Beach sand and Brickell nightlife and treat the stadium as a day trip, that works too: plan on a 30–45 minute transfer by car or rideshare in normal traffic, and considerably longer in the hours around kick-off.

On match day itself, the cleanest public-transport route from central Miami is **Brightline** to Aventura station, then a rideshare or an official match-day shuttle for the final stretch — shuttle buses typically run from Aventura and other park-and-ride points on event days. Driving is the alternative: the stadium has extensive paid parking, but expect heavy congestion for an hour or more after full-time, so pre-book parking and leave early. Whatever your base, give yourself at least 60–75 minutes door-to-seat from the beach or Brickell, and more on a knockout night.

## South Beach

South Beach is the Miami of the postcards — pastel Art Deco hotels along Ocean Drive, the white sand of Lummus Park, Lincoln Road's open-air shopping, and a restaurant scene that runs from Cuban diners to celebrity-chef dining rooms. It's the most walkable base in the city and the natural first-timer choice, with the trade-off that it is the furthest of our bases from Hard Rock Stadium — budget 30–45 minutes by car for matches.

## Brickell & Downtown

Brickell is Miami's high-rise financial district just south of Downtown — glass towers, rooftop pools, a dense and excellent restaurant scene, and a more urban, year-round-city feel than the beach. It's well placed for the airport and for getting north to the stadium, and it sits at the heart of the city's football energy on match nights.

## Coral Gables

Coral Gables is the city's leafy, low-rise Mediterranean-revival quarter, planned in the 1920s and full of banyan-shaded avenues, fountains and golf. It's calmer and greener than the beach or Brickell, popular with families and travellers who want a quieter base, and it's roughly a 20-minute drive from the airport.

<HotelPhoto hotelName="The Biltmore Hotel Coral Gables" city="Miami" />

### The Biltmore Hotel Coral Gables: Best Quiet Grandeur

1200 Anastasia Avenue. The Biltmore is a 1926 National Historic Landmark crowned by a 93-metre tower modelled on Seville's Giralda, set in its own golf course and gardens. Its vast pool was for decades the largest hotel pool in the United States. Rooms are grand and traditional, the grounds are made for slow mornings, and the whole estate feels a world away from South Beach.
