## The Scout's Take: South Beach, Brickell, or Inland?

**South Beach and Mid-Beach** are the Miami of the postcards — pastel Art Deco hotels along Ocean Drive, the white sand of Lummus Park, Lincoln Road's open-air shopping, and Faena's red-and-gold spectacle further up Collins Avenue. It's the most walkable base in the city and the natural first-timer choice, with the trade-off that it's the furthest of our bases from Hard Rock Stadium — budget 30–45 minutes by car for World Cup matches.

**Brickell and Downtown** form Miami's high-rise financial district just across the bay — glass towers, rooftop pools, a dense and excellent restaurant scene, and a more urban, year-round-city feel than the beach. It's well placed for the airport and for getting north to the stadium.

**Wynwood** is the arts-district alternative — mural-covered warehouses, craft breweries and a younger creative crowd, inland and best reached by rideshare from the beach. Several of this guide's strongest budget picks sit right in the district.

**Coral Gables and Coconut Grove** are the city's leafy, low-rise quarters — 1920s Mediterranean-revival streets, banyan trees, golf and bay frontage, calmer and greener than the beach or Brickell, popular with families and quieter luxury stays.

**Airport belt (MIA / Blue Lagoon / Doral)** is where the real budget floor lives — recognised chain hotels a short shuttle from the terminals, 20–30 minutes from both the beach and Brickell, the practical base for a stopover or a tight-budget trip with a hire car.

For first-timers: South Beach. For business or Brickell nightlife: Brickell. For the World Cup: Aventura or the stadium side. For tight budgets: this guide's [budget tier](#budget-hotels) — the airport belt and Wynwood are where £84–100 a night is real.

## Going to the 2026 World Cup in Miami?

Miami's matches are played at Miami Stadium (everyday name: Hard Rock Stadium) in Miami Gardens, roughly 16 miles north of South Beach and about 10 miles north-west of Downtown. It is the same ground that hosts the Miami Dolphins and the Miami Open tennis, a 65,000-plus-seat venue surrounded by large car parks just off Florida's Turnpike and I-95. There is no metro line directly to the ground, which shapes everything. If you want the shortest match-day journey, base yourself in **Aventura** or **Miami Gardens** — Aventura is about a 15-minute drive from the stadium and sits on the Brightline rail line. If you'd rather have South Beach sand and Brickell nightlife and treat the stadium as a day trip, that works too: plan on a 30–45 minute transfer by car or rideshare in normal traffic, and considerably longer around kick-off.

On match day itself, the cleanest public-transport route from central Miami is **Brightline** to Aventura station, then a rideshare or an official match-day shuttle for the final stretch. Driving is the alternative: the stadium has extensive paid parking, but expect heavy congestion for an hour or more after full-time, so pre-book parking and leave early. Whatever your base, give yourself at least 60–75 minutes door-to-seat from the beach or Brickell, and more on a knockout night.

## Where to Stay: South Beach, Brickell, Wynwood, Coral Gables, Coconut Grove & the Airport Belt

**South Beach and Mid-Beach** are the Miami of postcards — Art Deco hotels along Ocean Drive, Lummus Park's white sand, Lincoln Road's open-air shopping, and Faena's theatrical spectacle further up Collins Avenue. It's the most walkable, most photogenic base, and the furthest from Hard Rock Stadium.

**North Beach**, quieter and less developed than South Beach, sits further up the barrier island toward Bal Harbour and Sunny Isles — a calmer, more residential stretch of sand favoured by longer-stay visitors and locals rather than the Ocean Drive crowd.

**Downtown and Brickell** form the high-rise mainland core — Brickell for finance-district towers, rooftop pools and restaurants; Downtown for the arena, Bayfront Park and the Brightline station, Miami's rail link toward Fort Lauderdale, Orlando and, eventually, Aventura for the stadium.

**Wynwood** is the arts district — mural-covered former warehouses, galleries, craft breweries and a young creative crowd, inland and best reached by rideshare or a short drive from the beach.

**Coral Gables** is the city's leafy, low-rise Mediterranean-revival quarter, planned in the 1920s and full of banyan-shaded avenues, fountains and golf, home to the historic Biltmore.

**Coconut Grove** sits on the bay south of Brickell — Miami's oldest neighbourhood, with a sailing-club, bohemian-turned-upscale feel, marinas and tree-lined streets.

**The airport belt** around MIA, Blue Lagoon and Doral is where this guide's budget floor lives — recognised chain hotels a short shuttle from the terminals, the practical base for a stopover or a tight-budget trip with a hire car.

## Beyond the Hotel — Miami's Essentials

### Do I need a visa for the UK to visit Miami?

You need an ESTA (Electronic System for Travel Authorization), not a full visa — apply at esta.cbp.dhs.gov for $21, usually approved within minutes and valid for two years of visits. Apply at least 72 hours before you fly and check your passport has enough validity left.
