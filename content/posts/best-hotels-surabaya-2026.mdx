  - q: "Is Surabaya a good base for Mount Bromo?"
    a: "Yes, it's the main international gateway. Mount Bromo sits in the Bromo Tengger Semeru National Park, roughly a 3-4 hour drive southeast of Surabaya. Because the classic experience is a pre-dawn sunrise over the caldera, it's done as an overnight trip — most people sleep in Malang or a village like Cemoro Lawang near the crater rim, not as a city day trip from Surabaya."
  - q: "How do I visit Mount Bromo from Surabaya?"
    a: "Book an organised tour or a private driver; public transport is fiddly. A typical trip drives out the afternoon before, overnights near the park, then leaves around 3am for a jeep to the Penanjakan viewpoint to catch sunrise over the smoking cone and the Sea of Sand. Reckon on one or two nights away from Surabaya. The dry season (May-October) gives the clearest views."
  - q: "How do I see the Ijen blue fire from Surabaya?"
    a: "The Kawah Ijen blue-fire crater is in far-eastern Java near Banyuwangi, around 5-7 hours from Surabaya, so it's not a day trip. The electric-blue flames (burning sulphuric gas) are only visible in the dark, so the hike starts around 1am. Most people base in Banyuwangi and often combine Ijen with Bromo over a two or three-day loop rather than tackling either from Surabaya in a single day."
  - q: "What is the House of Sampoerna?"
    a: "The House of Sampoerna is a preserved Dutch-colonial compound in the old town that was once a kretek (clove cigarette) factory and is now a free museum on the industry, with a gallery and café. It also runs the free Surabaya Heritage Track — a small bus tour of the colonial centre — making it a good first stop for understanding the city."
  - q: "Why is Surabaya called the 'City of Heroes'?"
    a: "Surabaya earned the nickname Kota Pahlawan ('City of Heroes') for the Battle of Surabaya on 10 November 1945, when residents fought British and Indian troops after the Second World War during Indonesia's fight for independence. That date is now the national Heroes' Day, and the towering Heroes Monument (Tugu Pahlawan) commemorates it."
  - q: "What is there to see in Surabaya's old town?"
    a: "The revitalised Kota Lama (Old Town) around the Red Bridge (Jembatan Merah) is the historic core — faded Dutch-colonial trading houses, the Arab quarter around the Sunan Ampel Mosque, and the Chinatown lanes of Kembang Jepun with the Kya-Kya night-food street. Add the House of Sampoerna and the Heroes Monument and you have a solid day's walking."
  - q: "What is the Suramadu Bridge?"
    a: "The Suramadu Bridge is the roughly 5.4km toll bridge across the Madura Strait linking Surabaya to Madura island — the longest bridge in Indonesia. Madura is known for its bull racing (karapan sapi), salt production and satay; the bridge itself, lit up at night, is a local landmark and an easy add-on if you have a driver."
  - q: "What is the Submarine Monument in Surabaya?"
    a: "The Submarine Monument (Monumen Kapal Selam, or Monkasel) is a real former Indonesian Navy submarine — the Soviet-built KRI Pasopati — hauled ashore beside the Kalimas river and turned into a walk-through museum. It's a quirky, kid-friendly stop in the city centre near Surabaya Plaza."
  - q: "When is the best time to visit Surabaya?"
    a: "Surabaya is hot and humid all year, typically 28-33°C. The dry season runs roughly May to October and is the better window — especially for clear Bromo and Ijen sunrises. The wet season, November to April, brings heavy afternoon downpours and sticky air. There's no cool season, so pack light, breathable clothes whenever you go."
  - q: "How do I get around Surabaya?"

The city also has the Suroboyo Bus and Trans Semanggi routes, but traffic is heavy and distances are large, so most visitors rely on Grab or Gojek."
  - q: "Which side of the road do they drive on in Indonesia?"
    a: "Indonesia drives on the left, like the UK. If you hire a car or scooter the side of the road feels familiar, but Surabaya traffic is dense, fast and scooter-heavy, so most visitors use Grab, Gojek or a hired driver rather than self-driving."
  - q: "Can you drink the tap water in Surabaya?"

## What to See in Surabaya

Surabaya is a working port city, not a resort, so treat its sights as a rewarding day rather than a week. The obvious first stop is the **House of Sampoerna**, a handsome Dutch-colonial former cigarette factory now a free museum on the kretek (clove cigarette) trade, with a café and the free Surabaya Heritage Track bus tour of the old town. From there the **Kota Lama (Old Town)** around the **Red Bridge (Jembatan Merah)** rewards a wander past faded trading houses, and flows into the **Arab quarter** around the venerable **Sunan Ampel Mosque** — a genuine pilgrimage site — and the **Chinatown** lanes of Kembang Jepun, where the **Kya-Kya** street comes alive with food at night.

The city's identity is bound up in the **Battle of Surabaya** of 10 November 1945, when residents fought British and Indian troops during the independence struggle, earning Surabaya its title as the **City of Heroes (Kota Pahlawan)**. The towering **Heroes Monument (Tugu Pahlawan)** and its underground museum tell that story. Nearby, the **Submarine Monument (Monkasel)** — a real Soviet-built navy submarine hauled onto the riverbank — makes a quirky, kid-friendly stop. For a longer outing, the **Suramadu Bridge**, Indonesia's longest, sweeps across the strait to the island of **Madura**.

But the honest truth is that most travellers are here for what lies beyond the city. Surabaya is the main international gateway to **Mount Bromo**, the otherworldly volcanic caldera 3-4 hours southeast, and a common starting point for the **Kawah Ijen blue-fire crater** far to the east near Banyuwangi. Both are pre-dawn experiences done as overnight trips — usually based in Malang, Probolinggo or Banyuwangi rather than as day trips from the city. Cooler, calmer **Malang** and the highland resort town of **Batu**, two to three hours south, make a pleasant escape from Surabaya's heat and a natural Bromo base. The dry season, roughly May to October, is the best window for all of it.

## Surabaya Hotels FAQs

**Do UK travellers need a visa for Surabaya, Indonesia?**
UK passport holders get a 30-day visa on arrival (VoA) for tourism in Indonesia, currently around IDR 500,000 (roughly £25), payable in rupiah or by card at the airport — or bought in advance online as an e-VoA. It can be extended once for a further 30 days. Keep at least six months' passport validity and an onward or return ticket, and check the UK Foreign Office page for the current rules before you fly.

**How do I fly to Surabaya from the UK?**
There are no direct UK flights. You connect through a hub — Singapore (SIN), Kuala Lumpur (KUL), Jakarta (CGK) or a Gulf airport such as Doha or Dubai — then fly into Surabaya's Juanda International Airport (SUB). Total journey time is roughly 17-20 hours depending on the connection. Singapore Airlines/Scoot, AirAsia, Malaysia Airlines, Garuda and Citilink all feed SUB.

**Is Surabaya worth visiting?**
Be honest with yourself about why you're going. Surabaya is Indonesia's big, hot, humid second city — a business and industrial hub rather than a classic tourist stop, with no beaches and few headline sights. Most travellers use it as a launch pad for Mount Bromo and the Ijen blue-fire crater, or come for business, and that's a perfectly good reason to book here. Give the city a day for its heritage old town, Arab and Chinatown quarters and WWII history, then move on.

**What is the cheapest area to stay in Surabaya?**
The central districts around Tunjungan, Embong Malang and Genteng hold the deepest budget stock — 2★ chains and simple hotels from about £9-15 a night, walkable to malls and the old town. The Darmo and Margorejo areas to the south are also cheap and handy for the airport road. This guide's budget tier starts at about £9 a night.

**How much does a budget hotel in Surabaya cost in 2026?**
Genuinely cheap. Verified budget rooms in this guide start around £9 a night (Autumn Living Hotel Kapsul, SWK 95 Hotel), and most of the budget tier sits in the £13-27 band before any peak-season premium. For a clean private room in a big Indonesian city, that's excellent value.

**What currency is used in Surabaya?**
The Indonesian rupiah (IDR). Cards work at hotels, malls and larger restaurants, but carry cash for warungs, street food, becak rides and small shops. ATMs are widespread across the city; withdraw a decent amount at once to reduce per-transaction fees.

**Is Surabaya a good base for Mount Bromo?**
Yes, it's the main international gateway. Mount Bromo sits in the Bromo Tengger Semeru National Park, roughly a 3-4 hour drive southeast of Surabaya. Because the classic experience is a pre-dawn sunrise over the caldera, it's done as an overnight trip — most people sleep in Malang or a village like Cemoro Lawang near the crater rim, not as a city day trip from Surabaya.

**How do I visit Mount Bromo from Surabaya?**
Book an organised tour or a private driver; public transport is fiddly. A typical trip drives out the afternoon before, overnights near the park, then leaves around 3am for a jeep to the Penanjakan viewpoint to catch sunrise over the smoking cone and the Sea of Sand. Reckon on one or two nights away from Surabaya. The dry season (May-October) gives the clearest views.

**How do I see the Ijen blue fire from Surabaya?**
The Kawah Ijen blue-fire crater is in far-eastern Java near Banyuwangi, around 5-7 hours from Surabaya, so it's not a day trip. The electric-blue flames (burning sulphuric gas) are only visible in the dark, so the hike starts around 1am. Most people base in Banyuwangi and often combine Ijen with Bromo over a two or three-day loop rather than tackling either from Surabaya in a single day.

**What is the House of Sampoerna?**
The House of Sampoerna is a preserved Dutch-colonial compound in the old town that was once a kretek (clove cigarette) factory and is now a free museum on the industry, with a gallery and café. It also runs the free Surabaya Heritage Track — a small bus tour of the colonial centre — making it a good first stop for understanding the city.

**Why is Surabaya called the 'City of Heroes'?**
Surabaya earned the nickname Kota Pahlawan ('City of Heroes') for the Battle of Surabaya on 10 November 1945, when residents fought British and Indian troops after the Second World War during Indonesia's fight for independence. That date is now the national Heroes' Day, and the towering Heroes Monument (Tugu Pahlawan) commemorates it.

**What is there to see in Surabaya's old town?**
The revitalised Kota Lama (Old Town) around the Red Bridge (Jembatan Merah) is the historic core — faded Dutch-colonial trading houses, the Arab quarter around the Sunan Ampel Mosque, and the Chinatown lanes of Kembang Jepun with the Kya-Kya night-food street. Add the House of Sampoerna and the Heroes Monument and you have a solid day's walking.

**What is the Suramadu Bridge?**
The Suramadu Bridge is the roughly 5.4km toll bridge across the Madura Strait linking Surabaya to Madura island — the longest bridge in Indonesia. Madura is known for its bull racing (karapan sapi), salt production and satay; the bridge itself, lit up at night, is a local landmark and an easy add-on if you have a driver.

**What is the Submarine Monument in Surabaya?**
The Submarine Monument (Monumen Kapal Selam, or Monkasel) is a real former Indonesian Navy submarine — the Soviet-built KRI Pasopati — hauled ashore beside the Kalimas river and turned into a walk-through museum. It's a quirky, kid-friendly stop in the city centre near Surabaya Plaza.
**When is the best time to visit Surabaya?**
Surabaya is hot and humid all year, typically 28-33°C. The dry season runs roughly May to October and is the better window — especially for clear Bromo and Ijen sunrises. The wet season, November to April, brings heavy afternoon downpours and sticky air. There's no cool season, so pack light, breathable clothes whenever you go.

**How do I get around Surabaya?**
The ride-hailing apps Gojek and Grab (both cars and motorbikes) are the cheapest and easiest way to move around, and far simpler than flagging taxis. The city also has the Suroboyo Bus and Trans Semanggi routes, but traffic is heavy and distances are large, so most visitors rely on Grab or Gojek.

**Which side of the road do they drive on in Indonesia?**
Indonesia drives on the left, like the UK. If you hire a car or scooter the side of the road feels familiar, but Surabaya traffic is dense, fast and scooter-heavy, so most visitors use Grab, Gojek or a hired driver rather than self-driving.

**Can you drink the tap water in Surabaya?**
No. Tap water in Surabaya is not safe to drink — stick to bottled or properly boiled water, which is cheap and everywhere. Use bottled water for brushing teeth too if your stomach is sensitive, and be a little cautious with ice at the very cheapest street stalls.

**What should I wear in Surabaya's mosques and temples?**
Dress modestly. Surabaya is a fairly conservative, majority-Muslim city, and at religious sites such as the Sunan Ampel Mosque you should cover your shoulders and knees; women are usually asked to cover their hair, and robes are often provided at the entrance. Lightweight, covering clothing also helps with the heat and sun.

**Do I need to tip in Surabaya?**
Tipping isn't obligatory. Many mid-range and upmarket restaurants already add a service charge; if not, rounding up or leaving 5-10% is appreciated. Small tips for hotel porters and drivers are welcome but not expected. For Grab and Gojek rides the app fare is the fare.

**Where should first-time visitors stay in Surabaya?**
Central Surabaya around Tunjungan Plaza, Embong Malang and Genteng is the most convenient base — walkable to malls, restaurants and the old town, with hotels across every budget. It's also handy for arranging Bromo and Ijen tours. If you want quiet and space, the western Pakuwon/Ciputra area has newer malls and apartments.

**How many days do I need in Surabaya?**
One or two days covers the city itself — the old town, House of Sampoerna, Heroes Monument, the Arab and Chinatown quarters and the Submarine Monument. Most travellers then use Surabaya as a launch pad, adding two or three days for a Bromo and/or Ijen loop, or a night in cooler Malang.

**Are there luxury hotels in Surabaya?**
Yes, and they're excellent value. The historic Hotel Majapahit MGallery, the JW Marriott, Sheraton, Shangri-La, DoubleTree by Hilton, Westin and Vasa Hotel all offer genuine 5★ stays, most from well below £120 a night — a fraction of comparable brands in Europe or Singapore.

**Do budget hotels in Surabaya include breakfast?**
Some do and some don't — it's inconsistent at the cheapest end. Chains like ibis budget and Amaris keep things bare-bones, while some guesthouses include a simple breakfast. **Is Surabaya safe for tourists?**
Surabaya is generally safe and sees far fewer tourists than Bali or Yogyakarta, so scams aimed at visitors are uncommon. Standard city sense applies — watch your belongings in crowded markets, use registered Grab/Gojek rides at night, and take care crossing the busy roads. Check the UK Foreign Office advice for any current guidance before you travel.

**How far is Juanda Airport (SUB) from central Surabaya?**
Surabaya's Juanda International Airport (SUB) is about 20km south of the city centre, in Sidoarjo — roughly a 30-45 minute drive depending on traffic. Use Grab, Gojek, an airport taxi or the Damri airport bus. The airport has two terminals (T1 and T2), so check which one your flight uses.

**Can I visit Malang and Batu from Surabaya?**
Yes. Malang is a relaxed, cooler colonial university city about two to three hours south, and nearby Batu is an apple-growing highland resort town with theme parks and viewpoints. Both are popular overnight stops and common bases for a Mount Bromo sunrise trip, giving you a break from Surabaya's heat.
