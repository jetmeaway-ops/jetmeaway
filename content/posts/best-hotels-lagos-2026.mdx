## What to See in Lagos

**Lagos is one of the world's biggest cities and the beating heart of Afrobeats, Nollywood and Nigerian fashion and art.** Nothing about it is quiet, and that is the point. The single best introduction is the **Nike Art Gallery** in Lekki, several floors of Nigerian painting, sculpture and textiles founded by the artist Nike Davies-Okundaye and free to enter. For music, the **New Afrika Shrine** in Ikeja is where Fela Kuti's Afrobeat legacy still lives, run by his family and still hosting live nights.

For green space and air, the **Lekki Conservation Centre** protects a patch of coastal wetland forest and carries one of the longest canopy walkways in Africa across the treetops. For water, **Tarkwa Bay** is a sheltered beach reached by a short boat ride from Victoria Island and makes the easiest half-day escape from the traffic, while **Elegushi Beach** in Lekki is the loud, sociable option with bars and music. Take local advice on currents before swimming on the open Atlantic side.

On Lagos Island, **Freedom Park** turns a former colonial prison site into a cultural venue with music, food and open-air events, and the **National Museum** holds Nigerian antiquities including Benin and Nok material. For shopping, **Lekki Market** and the vast **Balogun Market** are where you go for fabric, crafts and the full sensory version of Lagos commerce — go with a local if you can, bargain cheerfully, and keep your phone away. Then there is the eating and drinking: **Victoria Island** and **Ikoyi** hold the city's best restaurants, bars and clubs, and Lagos nightlife genuinely runs late. West of the city, **Badagry** preserves sobering slave-trade history sites including the point of no return, and it makes a full day out.

Before you book any of it, read the current [UK FCDO travel advice for Nigeria](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/nigeria) — it is region-specific and matters here more than in most places — and start your visa application through the official [Nigeria Immigration Service](https://immigration.gov.ng/) well ahead of travel.

## Choosing Where to Stay in Lagos

Lagos is really two cities joined by a small number of very congested bridges, so the biggest decision is not the hotel but the side.

- **Victoria Island (VI) and Ikoyi** — the usual first choice for visitors. The safest, smartest hotels, the best restaurants and nightlife, and most business addresses. Ikoyi is the quieter and greener of the two.
- **Lekki** — newer developments stretching east along the coast, with the Nike Art Gallery, the Lekki Conservation Centre and Elegushi Beach. Good value and modern, though the Lekki traffic can be its own ordeal.
- **Ikeja and the GRA** — the mainland district around Murtala Muhammed airport. Better value than the islands and by far the most sensible base for short trips, early flights or mainland business. The New Afrika Shrine is here too.
- **The wider mainland** — Surulere, Apapa, Festac and the outer districts, where the cheapest listings in this guide sit. Plainest standards, lowest prices, longest journeys to the islands.

Be honest with yourself when you choose. **Crossing between the mainland and the islands can take hours**, so pick the side of the city where you will actually spend your time rather than the side with the nicest photographs.

## Getting Around Lagos and Staying Safe — the Honest Version

**Lagos traffic — go-slow, as everyone here calls it — is genuinely extreme.** Allow hours, not minutes, especially crossing the Third Mainland Bridge or the Lekki–Ikoyi link at peak times, and expect the rains between April and October to make it substantially worse. Plan two things a day, not five. Travel outside peak hours where you can. And take seriously the advice above about basing yourself on the right side of the city.

For transport, **use hotel cars or trusted ride-hailing apps rather than street taxis**. Keep the windows up and phones, bags and jewellery out of sight while you are stationary in traffic, which is when opportunistic theft happens. **Avoid walking around after dark**, arrange your ride home before a night out rather than looking for one at 3am, do not travel by road between cities at night, draw cash inside banks or malls rather than at street ATMs, and use the hotel safe. Ask your hotel reception which specific areas and routes to avoid at which times — they give straight, current answers.

Most importantly, **read the UK FCDO travel advice for Nigeria before you book**. This is not boilerplate. The guidance is **region-specific**: some Nigerian states carry advise-against-all-travel or advise-against-all-but-essential-travel warnings while others do not, and travelling against that advice can invalidate your travel insurance. Read it before you commit to flights and hotels, and check it again shortly before you fly. On the same note, remember that a **yellow fever certificate is required for entry**, that **malaria is a real risk** and worth a conversation with your GP or a travel clinic well before departure, and that UK passport holders **need a Nigerian visa arranged in advance**.

## Lagos Hotels FAQs

**Is this Lagos in Nigeria or Lagos in Portugal?**
This guide is **Lagos, Nigeria** — the country's commercial capital, the largest city in Africa, served by **Murtala Muhammed International Airport (LOS)**. Every hotel listed here is in Nigeria. **Lagos in Portugal's Algarve is a completely different place**, a small coastal town of a few tens of thousands of people, and nothing in this guide applies to it.

**Do UK travellers need a visa for Nigeria?**
Yes. Nigeria is **not visa-free** for UK passport holders and there is **no simple visa-on-arrival route** for most travellers. You must **apply in advance** through the official Nigerian Immigration Service process before you fly. Start early — supporting documents and processing time are involved — and check current rules on the official immigration site and the UK FCDO travel advice page before booking.

**What currency is used in Lagos?**
The **Nigerian Naira (NGN/₦)**. Cards work in Victoria Island and Ikoyi hotels, malls and larger restaurants, but **cash still matters** for markets, boats, small places and tips. Exchange rates have moved a great deal in recent years, so treat every pound figure here as a rough conversion for guidance only, not an exact quote.

**Are there direct flights from London to Lagos?**
Yes. **British Airways and Virgin Atlantic both fly direct from London to Murtala Muhammed International Airport (LOS) in roughly 6.5 hours.** There are also many one-stop options via the Gulf and European hubs, which are often cheaper if you do not mind a connection.

**Is Lagos safe for tourists?**
Lagos is a huge, fast-moving city that requires real care rather than nervousness. Use hotel cars or trusted ride apps rather than street taxis, keep windows up and phones out of sight in traffic, avoid walking around after dark, do not travel by road between cities at night, and use the hotel safe. Read the UK FCDO travel advice for Nigeria before booking.

**Do I need to read the FCDO advice before travelling to Nigeria?**
Yes, and it matters more here than for most destinations. The advice is **region-specific**: some states carry advise-against-travel warnings while others do not, and travel insurance can be invalidated if you go against it. Read it before you book flights or hotels, and again shortly before you fly.

**Do I need a yellow fever certificate for Nigeria?**
⚠️ Yes. A **yellow fever vaccination certificate is required for entry to Nigeria**, and you should carry it with your passport. Arrange the vaccination well ahead of travel through your GP or a travel clinic — it needs time to take effect and appointments can be scarce.

**Is there malaria in Lagos?**
⚠️ **Malaria is a real risk** throughout Nigeria, Lagos included. **See your GP or a travel clinic well before you fly** about antimalarial tablets, and pack repellent, cover up at dusk and use the air conditioning or nets your hotel provides. This is general information, not medical advice.

**How bad is Lagos traffic really?**
It is genuinely extreme, and locals call it **go-slow** for good reason. Crossing between the mainland and the islands at peak times can take **hours rather than minutes**. Plan your day around it, allow far more time than the distance suggests, travel outside peak hours where you can, and pick a hotel on the side of the city where you will actually spend your time.

**Which area of Lagos should I stay in?**
**Victoria Island and Ikoyi** are the usual first choice: the smartest hotels, best restaurants and most business addresses. **Lekki** suits newer developments, the beaches and the art gallery. **Ikeja and the GRA** are close to the airport and better value. The wider **mainland** is cheapest. Because of the traffic, the island-versus-mainland choice matters more than the street.

**How do I get from Lagos airport to the city?**
**Murtala Muhammed International Airport (LOS)** is in Ikeja on the mainland. Book a hotel car or use a trusted ride-hailing app rather than accepting an offer inside the terminal. To Victoria Island or Ikoyi the drive runs from about 45 minutes late at night to **well over two hours in peak traffic**, so plan arrival and departure timings generously.

**Lagos or Abuja — which should I visit?**
They are genuinely different cities. **Lagos is the commercial and cultural engine** — enormous, loud, creative and exhausting — and the place for Afrobeats, Nollywood, art, fashion, beaches and nightlife. **Abuja is the purpose-built capital**: greener, calmer, more orderly and far less congested, suiting business, government meetings and a gentler first taste of Nigeria. Many trips take in both.

**What is there to see in Lagos?**
The **Nike Art Gallery** in Lekki, the **New Afrika Shrine** in Ikeja, the **Lekki Conservation Centre** canopy walkway, **Tarkwa Bay** and **Elegushi** beaches, **Freedom Park** on Lagos Island, the **National Museum**, **Lekki Market** and **Balogun Market** for fabric and crafts, the dining and nightlife of **Victoria Island** and **Ikoyi**, and the slave-trade history sites at **Badagry** to the west.

**Is Lagos good for music and nightlife?**
It is one of the great nightlife cities. Lagos is the **home of Afrobeats** and the nights run very late, especially on Victoria Island, in Ikoyi and in Lekki. The **New Afrika Shrine** in Ikeja is the spiritual home of Fela Kuti's Afrobeat and still hosts live music. Arrange your ride home in advance rather than looking for transport on the street at 3am.

**When is the best time to visit Lagos?**
The **dry season, roughly November to March**, is the most comfortable window and the easiest for getting about. The **rains run roughly April to October, heaviest around June**, and heavy downpours flood roads and make the traffic dramatically worse. Lagos is hot and humid all year.

**Can I drink the tap water in Lagos?**
No — stick to **bottled or properly filtered water**. Hotels provide bottled water and many have filtered dispensers. Use bottled water for brushing teeth if you have a sensitive stomach, and be careful with ice and raw salads at the cheapest stalls.

**How many days do you need in Lagos?**
**Three to four days** is a sensible minimum, largely because the traffic eats so much of each day. That covers the Nike Art Gallery and Lekki Conservation Centre, a beach day at Tarkwa Bay, Freedom Park and the National Museum, a market morning and a couple of proper nights out. Add a day for the Badagry history trip.

No — **Nigeria drives on the right**, unlike the UK. Almost no visitor should self-drive in Lagos; the traffic, the junctions and the local driving culture make hotel cars and ride apps far cheaper, easier and safer.

Small tips for porters, drivers, boat operators and restaurant staff are appreciated and normal, and small naira notes are useful for that. Some hotels and restaurants add a service charge, so check the bill before adding more.
