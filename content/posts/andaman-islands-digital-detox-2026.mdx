## Why UK Travellers Are Choosing the Andamans Over the Maldives in 2026

The comparison UK travellers actually make isn't Andamans-versus-Goa, it's Andamans-versus-Maldives — both are marketed as the tropical-paradise-with-good-diving trip, and both attract the same honeymoon and milestone-anniversary booking pattern. The Andamans win on three specific things: price (a Havelock luxury villa runs meaningfully below Maldives overwater equivalents for a comparable standard of room and service), genuine cultural context (you're in India, with Bengali and South Indian food, the Cellular Jail's freedom-struggle history, and a naval-town texture the Maldives' single-resort-atoll format can't offer), and — the theme of this whole guide — disconnection that isn't optional. A Maldives resort gives you fast WiFi by default and asks you to switch it off. The Andamans simply don't give you the option in the first place, which for a genuine digital-detox trip is the difference between an intention and a fact.

The trade-off is real and worth naming honestly: getting there takes longer (a domestic connection through Delhi, Chennai or Kolkata versus a direct London-Malé flight plus seaplane), the luxury tier is thinner (a handful of top properties versus dozens across the Maldives' atolls), and infrastructure is more basic outside the top two or three hotels. Travellers who prioritise seamless luxury and minimal travel friction should book the Maldives. Travellers who want a genuinely different, more textured trip — and who are honestly a little relieved to have their phone taken away by circumstance rather than discipline — should book the Andamans.

## The Reality of Disconnection

Forget 5G. In the Andamans, connectivity is a relic. Even in 2026, while high-end resorts like Taj Exotica have limited satellite uplinks, the jungle canopy and military-governed infrastructure act as a natural Faraday cage. Bandwidth is allocated to the Navy first, government services second, hotels third, and tourists never.

**The rule**: your phone is offline. Accept it, and your holiday begins. Set up an autoresponder before you fly. Tell your bank you'll be off-grid, particularly if you plan any card spend in Port Blair — an unexpected fraud freeze mid-trip is a genuinely bad way to discover you forgot this step. Download Maps offline (Google Maps, OsmAnd) for both islands BEFORE you leave Delhi, Chennai or Kolkata — you will not get a useful data connection to do this once you're island-hopping.

This is the part most UK travellers under-prepare for. Don't fight it; this is why you came. The islands do something a "Do Not Disturb" toggle on your phone can't: they remove the choice. You can't doom-scroll a beach with no signal, and after the first uncomfortable day of phantom-vibration checking, most travellers report the same thing — a kind of mental quiet that a week in a fully-connected resort never delivers, because the connected resort always leaves the decision to disconnect in your hands, and most of us make the wrong call under pressure.

**What "no signal" actually feels like, day by day.** Day 1-2 (Port Blair): near-normal connectivity, BSNL signal is decent, you'll still check your phone out of habit. Day 3-4 (Havelock village area): patchy 4G in the main village, nothing at the beaches or dive sites — you start leaving the phone in the room. Day 5-7 (deeper into Havelock, Radhanagar side): genuinely intermittent, most travellers report going a full day without checking anything. Day 8-9 (Neil Island): close to zero — spotty 3G at best, and most travellers describe this as the point where the trip properly "clicks" into detox mode. Day 10 (return transit): signal returns gradually through Port Blair and then fully at Delhi/Chennai, and almost every traveller we've spoken to describes the reconnection moment as slightly dreadful — a flood of notifications that makes the previous week's quiet obvious by contrast.

## UK Routing Reality — Delhi, Chennai or Kolkata, and Why There's No Direct Flight

Let's be straight about this upfront, because it's the single most under-explained part of Andamans planning: **there is no direct flight from the UK to the Andaman Islands, and there won't be one for the foreseeable future.** Port Blair's Veer Savarkar International Airport is built and scaled for domestic Indian narrow-body traffic (IndiGo A320s, Air India Express 737s) and a handful of short regional international routes — not long-haul widebody service from Europe. Every UK traveller routes through a mainland Indian hub and takes a domestic connection onward. Budget your itinerary around this reality rather than hunting for a shortcut that doesn't exist.

**The three mainland gateway options, honestly compared:**

**Via Delhi (DEL)** — the default choice for most UK travellers, and for good reason: Delhi has the widest direct UK flight options (British Airways, Virgin Atlantic, Air India/Vistara all fly London-Delhi direct, roughly 8.5 hours), and the broadest onward schedule of flights to Port Blair. The domestic Delhi-Port Blair leg is around 5 hours total including a layover (occasionally routed via Chennai or Kolkata rather than truly direct, depending on the day and carrier), making Delhi the longer total-transit option of the three but the one with the most flight choice and the easiest to combine with a Golden Triangle add-on (Delhi, Agra/Taj Mahal, Jaipur) either side of your island week.

**Via Chennai (MAA)** — the shortest domestic hop to Port Blair, at roughly 2 hours direct on IndiGo or Air India Express. If your UK-to-India routing works well through Chennai (commonly via Doha with Qatar Airways, via Dubai with Emirates, or via Mumbai), this is the routing that minimises total transit fatigue and gets you into Port Blair fastest. It's also the natural pairing if you're combining the Andamans with a South India leg — Kerala or Tamil Nadu — since Chennai sits at the geographic hinge between the two.

**Via Kolkata (CCU)** — the least-used of the three by UK leisure travellers, mainly because Kolkata has fewer direct UK links than Delhi. Worth considering specifically if you're combining the Andamans with a Northeast India or Bhutan itinerary, or if you find a genuinely good UK-Kolkata fare, since Kolkata is geographically the closest mainland hub to the Andaman archipelago and historically the administrative gateway to the islands.

## Visa and the RAP Permit — What You Actually Need

Two separate documents, both mandatory, easy to get right if you plan a week ahead.

**1. The Indian e-Tourist Visa.** Every UK traveller needs this to enter India at all, regardless of which mainland city or the Andamans is your first stop. Apply online at the official government portal, indianvisaonline.gov.in — avoid third-party sites that charge a large markup for something that costs a set government fee. Budget roughly £20-25 for a 30-day single-entry e-Tourist Visa, or around £55 for a 1-year multiple-entry version if you're likely to return to India within the year. Processing typically takes 3-5 working days; apply at least a week before you fly, longer around Indian public holidays when processing can slow down. You'll need your passport bio-page scan and a passport-style photo uploaded as part of the application.

**2. The RAP (Restricted Area Permit).** This is the Andaman-specific layer on top of your Indian visa, and it cannot generally be pre-arranged online — it's issued in person at Port Blair airport immigration (or the seaport if you arrive by sea), alongside your passport check. It's free of charge, takes 15-30 minutes at the counter, and is valid 30 days from issue. Crucially, one RAP stamp covers Port Blair, Havelock (Swaraj Dweep), Neil (Shaheed Dweep), Ross Island and the standard tourist circuit in a single permit — you don't need separate permits per island. It does **not** cover the Nicobar group (a further-restricted zone rarely open to foreign tourists at all), North Sentinel Island (permanently off-limits, enforced by a Naval exclusion zone) or the Jarawa Tribal Reserve itself (you'll pass through it on the Andaman Trunk Road under armed escort, but cannot stop, photograph, or interact).

**Build the extra time into your Port Blair arrival plan.** Arriving jetlagged after an 18-20 hour total journey and then queuing 20-30 minutes for a RAP stamp is not the moment to be caught off guard — it's a completely routine, expected part of arrival, not a bureaucratic surprise, but only if you've budgeted for it.

<a id="port-blair"></a>

## The "Jet Lag Buffer" — Port Blair and Ross Island (Days 1-2)

Don't rush to the islands. UK travellers arriving from London via Delhi or Chennai arrive exhausted — the LHR to DEL flight is 8.5 hours, the DEL to IXZ leg adds another 5 hours including the layover. By the time you land in Port Blair you've been awake for 24+ hours, and Havelock's beaches will still be there tomorrow.

**The stay (Night 1-2)**: Book **Fortune Resort Bay Island** (~£100/night) or **Sea Shell Port Blair** (~£60/night). Fortune is 15 minutes from the airport with sea views and the perfect "buffer" base to:

- Send your "I'm offline for a week" emails and set autoresponders before the infrastructure disappears
- Pull out 30,000+ rupees in cash (Neil Island's ATM is often empty for days, and Havelock's aren't much better)
- Buy a local **BSNL SIM** at the airport's BSNL kiosk (₹500 / £5 for 30 days, passport required)
- Book ferry tickets for the next morning via **Makruzz**, **Nautika** or **Green Ocean**
- Get your RAP stamped at immigration if you haven't already
- Visit the **Cellular Jail** at sunset (free Light & Sound show in English at 6pm — moving and historically essential context on the freedom-struggle history the whole island group is named for)

**Ross Island (Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose Dweep)**, a 20-minute boat ride from Aberdeen Jetty, is worth a half-day if your buffer allows a second Port Blair morning. Once the British administrative headquarters for the penal colony — complete with a Chief Commissioner's residence, church, bakery and ballroom — it was abandoned after a 1941 earthquake and the WWII Japanese occupation, and today the jungle is slowly reclaiming the ruins through strangler fig roots, with spotted deer and peacocks (both introduced by the British) wandering freely. Most visitors combine it with nearby North Bay Island on the same boat package from Aberdeen Jetty — North Bay offers coral viewing, jet-skiing and semi-submarine rides for those who want an early taste of the underwater Andamans before Havelock's dive sites. Budget 3-4 hours total, and go in the morning, both for the light and to beat the day-tripper crowds.

If you're properly tired after the flights, do nothing more than the jail visit on Day 1 and save Ross Island for a relaxed Day 2 morning before your ferry. Sleep early. The islands aren't going anywhere.

## The Ferry Strategy — Your Secret Sales Tool

In the Andamans, the ferry is the only highway. Ignore the **government ferries** — they're for locals at ₹500 a leg, notoriously hard to book online, depart from a separate jetty, and run on Indian Standard Time (loosely interpreted). They are perfectly fine if you have a relaxed week's buffer; they are stress for a 10-day trip with a fixed return flight.

**The pro move**: Book a **private ferry** — **Makruzz Gold** or **Makruzz Discovery**, **Nautika** (newer, slightly faster), or **Green Ocean**. These are tourist-class catamarans with AC, reserved seating, reliable schedules, and an English-speaking crew. Prices: Port Blair → Havelock ₹1,200-₹1,500 / £12-£15, Havelock → Neil ₹1,000 / £10, Neil → Port Blair ₹1,200-₹1,500 / £12-£15. Book 4-7 days ahead in November-February; book 2-4 weeks ahead for Christmas-New Year week, when both ferries and flights fill fast with Indian domestic holiday travellers.

**The hotel hack**: Stay at SeaShell Havelock or Symphony Palms — their concierge can pre-book your ferry tickets, saving you a 4-hour queue at the Port Blair jetty when you arrive at 6am with luggage. Email or WhatsApp the concierge 5-7 days before your ferry; pay on arrival in cash. This single hack is worth £20 in saved stress and an extra hour of sleep.

**Ferry insurance — build a buffer day.** Sea conditions can cancel sailings with little notice, especially at the shoulder edges of the November-April window. If the sea is rough, your Neil → Port Blair ferry will be cancelled and you'll wait 24-48 hours for the next viable crossing. Don't book your Delhi/Chennai return flight for the same day you ferry back from Neil — give yourself at least one full buffer day in Port Blair before your onward flight.

<a id="havelock"></a>

## Havelock / Swaraj Dweep (Days 3-7) — Deep Immersion

The headline island. Renamed Swaraj Dweep in 2018 but still universally known as Havelock. Home to **Radhanagar Beach** (consistently ranked Asia's #1 beach by *TIME* and *Travel + Leisure*), Asia's best diving school cluster, and the most developed tourism infrastructure on the islands — meaning a half-dozen genuinely good hotels and a dive scene that rivals Indonesia or the Maldives.

**The insider stays**:

**Taj Exotica Resort & Spa** (Radhanagar Beach side, ~£500-£700/night). 50 acres of jungle and beachfront, 50 villas with private outdoor decks, a spa at the high-luxury Taj benchmark. The choice if you want one splurge night to bookend your trip. Their concierge handles every logistic — ferries, dive bookings, restaurant reservations — and the property has the islands' best (though still limited) satellite connectivity if you genuinely need to check in once.

**Barefoot at Havelock** (Radhanagar side, ~£200/night). Eco-cottages and treehouses in 14 acres of forest, 200m from Radhanagar Beach via a private trail. The original Havelock luxury, family-run, no TV, real candlelit dinners. The pro angle: their private trail to Radhanagar means you walk to one of the world's best beaches without a torch in pitch dark.

**SeaShell Havelock** (Vijaynagar Beach, ~£150/night). The dependable mid-range — pool, three restaurants, on-site dive partner with DiveIndia, and the concierge desk that pre-books your onward ferries. Best base for a 5-day trip combining beach, dive course, and exploration.

**Symphony Palms Beach Resort** (Vijaynagar #5, ~£100/night). Beach-bungalow style at honest prices. 50m walk to the sand. Heavy-duty generators that keep the AC running through the daily 1pm-3pm "load shedding." The smart-money mid-range pick.

**The diving secret**: Havelock is one of Asia's best PADI training grounds — 25-30m visibility, 28-30°C water, healthy reefs, manta rays at South Button (a day-trip dive site), and dive schools (DiveIndia, Barefoot Scuba, Andaman Bubbles) that take training seriously. PADI Open Water 4-day course ~£280-£330 including everything. Book direct via WhatsApp; their website forms are slow.

**Pro-tip — the bioluminescence**: Ask your hotel about **kayaking the mangroves at Havelock during a New Moon**. The mangroves glow blue-green under your paddle blade. World-class natural phenomenon, almost no tourists know about it, organised by a couple of small local operators (your hotel concierge has the contact). ₹1,500 / £15 for a 90-minute night kayak. Goes on the "I'll never forget this" list of most travellers who do it.

**Elephant Beach**, reached by a 20-30 minute jungle trek from the main road or a short boat ride, is the best beginner snorkelling site on Havelock — shallow, calm coral gardens without needing a PADI certification. Combine it with Radhanagar and Kalapathar Beach (quieter, rockier, best at sunrise) for a complete Havelock beach circuit over a few days.

<a id="neil"></a>

## Neil / Shaheed Dweep (Days 7-9) — The Slow-Down Island

After Havelock's relative bustle (one main road, ten dive shops, twenty restaurants), Neil Island is the deceleration. Smaller (13 km²), quieter, fewer beachfront bars, the island you can cycle around in 90 minutes.

The three named beaches:

- **Bharatpur Beach**: glass-bottom boat trips, snorkelling over coral gardens, the busy beach.
- **Laxmanpur Beach**: 4 km of empty sand, sunset point, the quiet beach.
- **Sitapur Beach**: sunrise side, smaller, the early-morning beach if you can wake up.

Plus the **Natural Bridge / Howrah Bridge**: a stone arch carved by the sea on the western shore. Visit at low tide (your hotel will tell you when); high tide submerges it.

**The insider stays**:

**Summer Sands Beach Resort** (Laxmanpur side, ~£80/night). Beach-bungalow eco style, hammocks, simple Indian + Continental kitchen, reliable generator. The default mid-range Neil pick.

**Holiday Inn Resort Neil Island** (Bharatpur, ~£140/night). The newer, larger option with a pool. Best if you're combining Neil with diving/snorkelling at Bharatpur.

**Pearl Park Beach Resort** (Bharatpur side, ~£60/night). Family-run, simple cottages, the budget pick. Arrive by ferry, walk 5 minutes to your room, walk 2 more minutes to the beach.

**The 500-Metre Holiday angle**: Rent a bicycle from your hotel (₹150 / £1.50 for the day). The whole island is rideable in 20 minutes from end to end. You can see Bharatpur in the morning, Laxmanpur for sunset, Sitapur the next dawn. No taxi required for 3 days. The radius of your "useful world" shrinks to your hotel and the beach, and that's the point — Neil is where the digital detox thesis of this whole guide actually completes itself. There's less to photograph, less to plan, and — by design — nowhere to post it even if you wanted to.

## Diving and Snorkelling — What's Actually Worth Doing Underwater

Havelock's dive scene is the Andamans' strongest card and arguably under-marketed relative to how good it is. Visibility routinely hits 25-30m, water sits at a comfortable 28-30°C year-round, and because the islands see a fraction of the dive tourism of Southeast Asia, the reefs at sites like South Button, Aquarium and Dixon's Pinnacle are in noticeably better condition than many more heavily-dived destinations.

**PADI Open Water (4 days, ~£280-£330)** is the standard entry certification, run by DiveIndia, Barefoot Scuba and Andaman Bubbles among others — all take training seriously, run small groups, and include all gear, manuals, the certification fee and 4-5 open-water dives. You need to be reasonably fit, comfortable in water, and 10+ years old. Book direct via WhatsApp (the numbers are on each school's website) rather than through a third-party booking platform, since availability and instructor scheduling move faster than most platforms sync.

**Fun dives for certified divers** run £35-50 per dive including full gear, with multi-dive packages bringing the per-dive cost down further. South Button's manta ray sightings are seasonal but a genuine highlight when the timing lines up.

**Snorkelling without certification** is a completely legitimate way to see the underwater Andamans on a shorter visit. Elephant Beach on Havelock (reached by a 20-30 minute jungle trek or a short boat ride) and Bharatpur Beach on Neil both offer shallow, calm coral gardens with reef fish, no certification required, typically sold as half-day boat packages (£15-25pp) including gear, a guide, and often a glass-bottom-boat component.

## The Andaman Survival Checklist

- **Connectivity**: 90% of UK eSIMs fail in the Andamans. Get a local **BSNL SIM** at Port Blair airport. Even then, expect genuinely offline days on Neil.
- **Cash is king**: Neil Island's lone ATM is often empty for 2-3 days at a time, and Havelock's aren't much more reliable. Withdraw ALL the rupees you'll need at Port Blair. Budget: ₹3,000-₹5,000 per day on Neil including hotel + food + bicycle + boat trips.
- **Power**: Frequent "load shedding" (1-3 hour planned outages, typically early-to-mid afternoon) happens daily on Havelock and Neil. SeaShell, Symphony Palms and Holiday Inn run heavy-duty diesel generators that keep AC running through outages. Budget hotels usually don't.
- **Moonlight warning**: The walk to Radhanagar Beach is genuinely pitch black after sunset (no streetlights, jungle either side). Either stay at Barefoot for direct beach access, or carry a real torch for any post-sunset movement.
- **Bioluminescence secret**: Ask your hotel about night-kayaking during a new moon. The mangroves glow.
- **Sunblock**: Bring high-SPF (50+) reef-safe sunblock from London. Local sunblock is expensive and frequently low-SPF.
- **Ferry insurance**: Build a buffer day into your schedule. If the sea is rough, your Neil to Port Blair ferry will be cancelled and you'll wait 24-48 hours. Don't fly to Delhi or Chennai the same day you ferry from Neil.
- **Photography**: Photographing tribal communities or restricted areas is illegal. The Cellular Jail and Ross Island's heritage sites are fine. The Jarawa Reserve along the Andaman Trunk Road is strictly NO photography — armed checkpoints enforce it.
- **Health prep**: Visit a UK travel clinic 4-6 weeks ahead to discuss malaria prophylaxis (a real consideration for this region, unlike much of urban mainland India), routine vaccinations, and travel insurance that explicitly covers diving to your planned depth.
- **Two permits, not one**: The Indian e-Tourist Visa gets you into India; the RAP (issued free on arrival at Port Blair) gets you into the Andamans specifically. You need both.

**Best months**: November-April. November and February are sweet spots. Avoid Christmas-New Year week (prices double, ferries booked solid). Avoid June-September monsoon.

## The Bottom Line

The Andamans in 2026 are India's last properly off-grid destination — disconnected by infrastructure, protected by law, and slow by design. UK travellers wanting a genuine reset will find it here in a way the Maldives or Bali cannot deliver, because the infrastructure makes the reset *involuntary*.

Don't fight the disconnection. Don't book a rushed-itinerary tour. Don't try to compress this into a long weekend. Get the BSNL SIM, withdraw the cash, sort the visa and the RAP, board the morning ferry, and let 10 days of near-zero bandwidth reset something in you that even Rishikesh's silence can't touch.
