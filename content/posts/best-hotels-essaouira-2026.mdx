## The Scout's Take: Medina, Beachfront, or Sidi Kaouki?

Essaouira is small, so the choice is simpler than in the big cities — but it still matters.

The **medina** — the walled, car-free old town — is where most travellers stay and where we'd send you first. It's compact, safe, full of characterful riads and guesthouses at every price, and it puts the harbour, ramparts, beach, galleries and food all within a short walk. This is the atmospheric heart of Essaouira and the budget stock is deep here.

The **beachfront and golf side** — the seafront hotels and the resorts out towards the golf course and Diabat — suit travellers who want sea views, a pool and a quieter, more resort-style stay, at the cost of walking or taxiing into the medina.

**Sidi Kaouki**, a wild surf-beach village about 25 minutes south, is for travellers here mainly to surf, windsurf or ride horses — rustic and remote, with a few lodges and far fewer crowds.

For a first Essaouira trip, stay in the medina; for sea views and a pool, the beachfront; for surf, Sidi Kaouki. [Compare live Essaouira hotel prices](/hotels?city=Essaouira) or [search flights to ESU](/flights?to=ESU).

## How Essaouira Compares to the Rest of Morocco

Essaouira is Morocco's breezy, artsy antidote to the intensity of the big cities. Where [Marrakech](/blog/best-hotels-marrakech-2026) hits you with heat, souks and hustle, Essaouira gives you sea air, a small walkable UNESCO medina, gnaoua music, art galleries and a wild windswept beach — smaller, calmer and far less hassly. It's the classic escape from Marrakech (a ~2.5-hour drive west), and its £33 budget floor makes it a genuinely cheap coastal base full of characterful independent riads.

The smart move for most UK travellers is to pair the two: a few nights in Marrakech for the souks and palaces, then two or three in Essaouira to slow down by the sea. Just remember the wind — Essaouira is superb for watersports, walks and atmosphere, but it's not a still-air sunbathing resort. For that, Agadir down the coast is Morocco's dedicated beach resort. Essaouira gives you the character and the sea breeze; the other cities give you the history and the heat.

## Essaouira Hotels FAQs

**What is the cheapest area to stay in Essaouira?**
The medina — the walled UNESCO old town — is where the cheapest rooms cluster, and it's also the nicest place to stay, so budget and atmosphere line up. On live searches while writing, real bookable rates started at £33 a night: the Atlantic Hostel and The Chill Art Hostel from ~£33–37, Riad chez brahim and Chez Ali Apartments from ~£34–36, and dozens of small riads in the £38–70 band. Staying inside the ramparts puts you steps from the harbour, the beach, the galleries and the food, all car-free and walkable.

**How much does a budget hotel in Essaouira cost per night in 2026?**
On live searches while writing, genuine bookable budget rooms ran roughly £33–138 a night. The floor is excellent value — hostels from ~£33, characterful medina riads £50–80 — and the tier climbs to about £138 at the top for a sea-view 3★ (Hotel Coté Océan Mogador). Essaouira is full of independent riads and guesthouses rather than big chains, so prices are set by real owners and stay genuinely cheap outside the summer and festival peaks.

**Is Essaouira windy? Can I sunbathe on the beach?**
Yes — Essaouira is famously the 'Windy City' (the Atlantic trade winds blow hard most afternoons, especially April to September), which is exactly why it's a world-class kitesurfing and windsurfing spot. The huge sandy bay is brilliant for watersports, long walks, camel and horse rides and beach football, but it's not a still-air lie-on-a-towel sunbathing beach like Agadir. Come for the wind and the atmosphere; if you want calm hot sun, the sheltered south coast suits better.

**How do I get to Essaouira from the UK?**
Two routes. Essaouira has its own small airport (Essaouira-Mogador, ESU) with seasonal direct and connecting flights, but the schedule is thin. Most UK travellers fly to Marrakech (RAK) — well served by Ryanair, easyJet and others from London and regional airports in around 3.5 hours — then take the scenic ~2.5-hour drive or Supratours/CTM coach west to the coast. A private transfer from Marrakech airport is the easiest door-to-door option.

**How far is Essaouira from Marrakech?**
About 190 km, or roughly 2.5 hours by road through the argan groves. Supratours and CTM run several comfortable coaches a day from Marrakech for a few pounds, private transfers take you door to door, and many visitors do Essaouira as a two- or three-night side trip from a Marrakech base. It's one of the most popular escapes from the imperial city — cooler, calmer and by the sea.

**Is Essaouira worth visiting?**
Very much so, if you want the artsy, breezy, low-hassle side of Morocco. Essaouira gives you a walkable UNESCO-listed medina of whitewashed-and-blue lanes, a working fishing harbour (the Skala ramparts played Astapor in Game of Thrones), art and argan-oil shops, gnaoua music, fresh seafood grills and a vast windswept beach. It's far more relaxed than Marrakech or Fez — smaller, gentler and easy to explore on foot in a couple of days.

**Was Essaouira used in Game of Thrones?**
Yes — Essaouira's Skala de la Ville ramparts and harbour walls stood in for the slaver city of Astapor (the Season 3 scenes with Daenerys and the Unsullied). The honey-coloured sea bastions with their old cannons are the town's signature view, and you can walk them for free. Several small operators run informal filming-locations walks around the ramparts and harbour.

**What is the best area to stay in Essaouira?**
The medina (old town) is the classic and best choice — compact, car-free, safe and packed with riads and guesthouses, with the harbour, beach, ramparts and food a short walk away. For sea views and a quieter, more resort-style stay, the beachfront and the golf-course edge towards Diabat suit better. Surfers sometimes base further out at Sidi Kaouki, a wild beach village ~25 minutes south.

**What is a riad and should I stay in one in Essaouira?**
A riad is a traditional Moroccan townhouse built around an interior courtyard, usually converted into a small guesthouse with a handful of individually decorated rooms and often a rooftop terrace. Most of Essaouira's characterful budget and mid stays are riads inside the medina — Riad Al Madina, Riad Beldy, Riad Le Grand Large, Dar Nafoura and more. They give you courtyard-house atmosphere and personal service that big hotels can't, usually for £50–90 a night here.

**Do I need a car in Essaouira?**
No — the medina is entirely car-free and everything in town is walkable, so a car is more liability than help. If you arrive by transfer or coach from Marrakech you won't need one. A car only makes sense if you plan to explore the coast independently — Sidi Kaouki, the argan cooperatives, or the drive up to Safi — otherwise skip it and use taxis or organised day trips.

**When is the best time to visit Essaouira?**
Spring (April–June) and early autumn (September–October) are the sweet spot — warm, bright and a little less windy than midsummer. Essaouira stays mild and breezy year-round thanks to the Atlantic, rarely getting the fierce heat of Marrakech or Fez. June's Gnaoua World Music Festival is the big event (book well ahead). Winters are cool, quiet and cheap, good for walking but not for the beach.

**What is the Gnaoua Festival?**
The Gnaoua World Music Festival is Essaouira's famous free music festival, usually held over a long weekend in June, celebrating the trance-like gnaoua music of Morocco's sub-Saharan heritage alongside jazz, fusion and world artists on open-air stages across the town. It draws hundreds of thousands of visitors, so hotels book out months ahead and prices rise sharply for those dates — plan early if you want to be there for it.

**Is Essaouira good for surfing and windsurfing?**
Excellent — the steady Atlantic wind that makes Essaouira the 'Windy City' also makes it one of the world's top windsurfing and kitesurfing destinations, with schools and board hire on the town beach and at nearby Sidi Kaouki and Moulay Bouzerktoun. Regular wave surfing is better at Sidi Kaouki and the breaks south of town. Several budget stays here — Surf HouseMaroc, Yousurf Surf House, the hostels — cater specifically to the surf and wind crowd.

**Is Essaouira safe for tourists?**
Yes — Essaouira is widely regarded as one of the most relaxed and low-hassle towns in Morocco, noticeably calmer and less pushy than Marrakech or Fez. The medina is safe to wander day and night with normal precautions, and the compact car-free layout makes it easy. Watch your bag in the busy souk lanes, agree taxi fares in advance, and take the usual care after dark. Solo and female travellers generally report Essaouira as easy and friendly. Check the UK Foreign Office's [Morocco travel advice](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/morocco) for the latest entry rules and safety guidance before you travel.

**What currency is used in Essaouira and do I need cash?**
Morocco uses the Moroccan dirham (MAD), a closed currency you cannot buy before you arrive — get it from an ATM or bureau de change once you land (roughly MAD 12 = £1). Riads and larger hotels often take cards, but the harbour fish grills, the souk stalls, small cafés, taxis and the medina generally run on cash, so keep dirham notes on you. There are ATMs around the main squares and the Bab Doukkala gate.

**Can I drink alcohol in Essaouira?**
Yes, in the right places — Morocco is a Muslim country and alcohol isn't sold everywhere, but Essaouira is used to tourists and you'll find it served in licensed riads, hotels, beach clubs and many tourist restaurants, plus a few shops. It's not part of medina café culture and is more discreet during Ramadan. As always in Morocco, drink where it's served, and dress and behave modestly out of respect, especially in the souks.

**How many days do I need in Essaouira?**
Two to three nights is ideal — enough to walk the ramparts and harbour, get lost in the medina lanes, browse the art and argan shops, eat at the fish grills, spend a windy afternoon on the beach or a watersports session, and take a half-day trip to Sidi Kaouki or an argan cooperative. Day-trippers from Marrakech only scratch the surface; the town's slow, breezy rhythm is the whole point.

**Is Essaouira better than Agadir for a beach holiday?**
They're different. Agadir is Morocco's big purpose-built resort with a long sheltered sun-and-sand bay — better for classic lie-on-the-beach sunbathing. Essaouira is a windy, artsy historic port with a UNESCO medina and a wild beach that's superb for watersports and walks but too breezy for still sunbathing. Choose Agadir for a straightforward beach week; choose Essaouira for atmosphere, culture, wind sports and character.

**What is there to do in Essaouira besides the beach?**
Plenty: walk the Skala de la Ville and harbour ramparts (the Game of Thrones views), watch the blue fishing boats and buy grilled fish at the port, wander the whitewashed medina and its galleries and argan-oil cooperatives, visit the Sidi Mohammed Ben Abdallah museum, shop for thuya-wood marquetry (an Essaouira craft), listen to gnaoua music, ride a camel or horse on the beach, and take a day trip to Sidi Kaouki or the argan groves.

**Are there luxury hotels in Essaouira?**
Yes, a handful — Essaouira is a small town so the top tier is boutique rather than big-chain. The standouts are the Heure Bleue Palais (a Relais & Châteaux member and the town's grande dame), Le Médina Essaouira Thalassa Sea & Spa (an MGallery seafront resort with a thalassotherapy spa), the Atlas Essaouira Riad Resort and Hotel Le Golf D'Essaouira & Spa out by the golf course. Expect roughly £118–305 a night for the five-star stays.

**Do Essaouira hotels have air conditioning and heating?**
It varies, and thanks to the climate it matters less than inland. Essaouira's Atlantic breeze keeps it mild — rarely very hot — so many riads rely on the natural cool of thick medina walls rather than air conditioning, and evenings can feel cool and windy. Winters are chilly, so if you visit November to February, check that your riad has heating, as not all budget medina rooms do. Confirm on the individual listing if it matters to you.

**Is Essaouira suitable for families?**
Yes — it's one of the easier Moroccan towns for families: compact, car-free, safe, walkable, with a big beach for camel rides, football and gentle paddling, and riads and apartments that suit groups. The wind means it's not a baking sunbathe-all-day beach, but children love the boats, the ramparts and the space. Aparthotels and villas here (Chez Ali Apartments, Villa Kamilia, Appartement Pour Famille) give kitchens and room to spread out.

**What food is Essaouira known for?**
Seafood, above all — Essaouira is a working fishing port, and the classic experience is picking fresh fish and sardines at the harbour grills and having them cooked on the spot. Beyond that you'll find Moroccan tagines and couscous, argan-oil and amlou (an argan-almond spread) the region is famous for, fresh juices, and a growing scene of arty cafés and rooftop restaurants in the medina. It's a genuinely good food town for its size.

**How do I get from Marrakech airport to Essaouira?**
The simplest options are a pre-booked private transfer (door to door in about 2.5 hours, ideal with luggage or a group) or the Supratours coach, which runs several times a day from Marrakech to Essaouira's Bab Marrakech gate for a few pounds. Grand taxis can also be negotiated for the run. There's no direct train — the line ends at Marrakech — so road is the only way west.

**Can I do Essaouira as a day trip from Marrakech?**
You can, and many people do — organised day trips run daily — but it's a long day for a ~2.5-hour drive each way, leaving only a few hours in town. Essaouira rewards an overnight far more: you get the town at dusk and dawn without the day-tour crowds, time for the beach and the ramparts, and the slow, breezy atmosphere that is the whole reason to come. If you can spare two nights, do.

**What is Sidi Kaouki?**
Sidi Kaouki is a wild, windswept beach village about 25 minutes (25 km) south of Essaouira, popular with surfers, windsurfers, kitesurfers and horse riders for its big empty sands and consistent waves and wind. It's far quieter and more rustic than Essaouira town, with a scattering of surf lodges and guesthouses (our Ranch de Sidi kaouki listing sits out this way). A good half-day trip, or a base if you're here mainly to surf.
