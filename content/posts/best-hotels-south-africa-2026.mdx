## Where to Base Yourself in South Africa

South Africa rewards a multi-stop route — distances are large and internal flights save time, but each region has its own character and its own best season.

- **Cape Town** — the marquee base and the headline of any trip: Table Mountain, the buzzing V&A Waterfront, Cape Point and the Cape of Good Hope, and the penguins at Boulders Beach. The winelands of Stellenbosch and Franschhoek are under an hour away. Best in the warm, dry summer, November to March.
- **The Winelands (Stellenbosch & Franschhoek)** — a short drive from Cape Town, this is South Africa's celebrated wine country of vineyards, mountain scenery and superb restaurants, easily done as day trips or a short overnight.
- **Johannesburg** — the biggest city, business hub and main safari-flight gateway, home to the powerful Apartheid Museum, the historic township of Soweto and Constitution Hill. A cultural and historical base, usually a night or two on arrival or before flying to Kruger.
- **Pretoria** — the administrative capital and "Jacaranda City", famous for streets that turn purple with blossom in October and November, the grand Union Buildings and a relaxed, leafy feel. Some of the best-value guesthouses in this guide.
- **Durban** — warm Indian Ocean beaches, surf, the Golden Mile promenade and a rich Indian-influenced curry culture (home of bunny chow), with upmarket Umhlanga just up the coast. A great-value beach base, good year-round.
- **Hermanus** — about 90 minutes from Cape Town, one of the world's best spots for land-based whale watching, with southern right whales close to shore roughly June to November.
- **The Garden Route & Knysna** — the scenic coastal drive between Mossel Bay and Storms River, all forests, lagoons and beaches, with Knysna the best-known stop. A classic multi-day self-drive from Cape Town.
- **Hoedspruit / Kruger** — the gateway to Kruger National Park and the private reserves along its edge, prime Big Five safari country, reached by flying from Johannesburg or Cape Town. Best for game-viewing in the dry winter, May to September.

Remember to match the region to the season: Cape Town and the Garden Route shine in the summer (November to March), while Kruger safaris are best in the dry winter (May to September), and Hermanus whales appear from June to November.

**Do UK travellers need a visa for South Africa?**
No — UK passport holders are visa-free for tourism stays of up to 90 days. You need a passport valid for at least 30 days beyond departure, with two blank pages, and proof of onward or return travel. Always check the latest FCDO travel advice before you fly, as rules can change.

**What is the best time to visit South Africa?**
It depends what you want. Cape Town and the Garden Route are best in the warm, dry summer, November to March. Kruger and the safari areas are best for game-viewing in the dry winter, May to September. Whale watching in Hermanus runs roughly June to November. It's a genuine year-round destination if you match the region to the season.

**Are there direct flights from the UK to South Africa?**
Yes — British Airways and Virgin Atlantic fly direct from London to both Johannesburg (JNB) and Cape Town (CPT), taking around 11 to 12 hours. Johannesburg is the main hub and the usual connecting point for safari flights, while Cape Town is the gateway to the winelands and the Cape Peninsula.

**Which airport do you fly into for South Africa?**
The two main gateways are OR Tambo International in Johannesburg (JNB) and Cape Town International (CPT), both served by direct BA and Virgin flights from the UK. Johannesburg is the busiest hub and the springboard for safaris; Cape Town is the choice for the Mother City and the Winelands. King Shaka (DUR) serves Durban.

**Is South Africa safe for tourists?**
South Africa is a rewarding, hugely popular destination, but crime is a genuine concern and it pays to be sensible. Check current FCDO advice, use registered taxis, ride-hailing apps or hotel transfers rather than walking with valuables, avoid walking alone at night, keep car doors locked and valuables out of sight, and stick to well-touristed areas. Most visits are trouble-free with normal big-city precautions.

**What is load-shedding and will it affect my stay?**
Load-shedding is South Africa's system of scheduled rolling power cuts, used when the national grid is under strain, and can mean a few hours without mains power at a time. The good news is that most hotels, especially mid-range and above, run backup generators or batteries, so lights, Wi-Fi and hot water usually keep working. Check the current status for your dates and ask your hotel about backup power when booking.

**Should I self-drive or take guided tours in South Africa?**
Both work well. Self-driving is popular and rewarding along the Garden Route and around the Cape, where roads are good and distances manageable — keep doors locked, park securely, and avoid driving at night in unfamiliar places. For safaris and big cities, many prefer guided game drives and private transfers or ride-hailing apps. A common plan is to self-drive the Cape and Garden Route, then fly and be guided for the safari leg.

**Where can I go on safari in South Africa?**
The flagship is Kruger National Park and the private reserves along its western edge (Sabi Sand, Timbavati), reached by flying into Hoedspruit or Nelspruit, or driving from Johannesburg — prime Big Five country. Closer to Cape Town, malaria-free reserves in the Eastern Cape (near Port Elizabeth) pair easily with the Garden Route. This guide covers cities and coast; book dedicated safari lodges separately.

**What currency is used in South Africa?**
The South African rand (ZAR). We quote every price here in pounds as a from-rate. Cards are widely accepted in hotels, restaurants and shops, and ATMs are common, but carry some cash for tips, markets and rural areas. Tipping around 10-15% in restaurants and for guides is customary.

**What is the budget per night for backpackers in South Africa?**
Backpackers can sleep comfortably from around £27-£40 a night in this guide's budget tier — guesthouses, backpacker lodges and value 3-stars across Johannesburg, Pretoria, Durban and Cape Town. Add cheap local eats, affordable ride-hailing and free beaches and hikes, and it's one of the better-value long-haul trips going.

**Is Cape Town worth staying in?**
Absolutely — Cape Town is the headline base for most trips. Table Mountain towers over the city, the V&A Waterfront buzzes with restaurants and boat trips, Cape Point anchors a stunning peninsula drive, and you can meet the penguins at Boulders Beach. The winelands of Stellenbosch and Franschhoek are under an hour away, and hotels range from £40 backpacker stays to £465 estates.

**What is there to do in Johannesburg?**
Johannesburg is South Africa's biggest city and business hub, and the main gateway for safari flights. Its standout draws are the powerful Apartheid Museum, the historic township of Soweto (best on a guided tour) and Constitution Hill. It's about history, culture and nightlife rather than beaches — many travellers spend a night or two here on arrival or before flying to Kruger.

**Why visit Pretoria?**
Pretoria, part of greater Tshwane just north of Johannesburg, is the administrative capital and the "Jacaranda City" — its streets turn purple with blossom in October and November. The grand Union Buildings and gardens, museums and a relaxed, leafy feel make it a pleasant, good-value base with some of the cheapest guesthouses in this guide.

**What is Durban known for?**
Durban, on the warm Indian Ocean coast of KwaZulu-Natal, is South Africa's beach and surf capital, with a balmy year-round climate, the Golden Mile promenade and a rich Indian-influenced food culture — home of bunny chow (curry served in a hollowed-out loaf). It's more affordable than Cape Town and a great-value beach base, with upmarket Umhlanga just up the coast.

**Can you go whale watching in Hermanus?**
Yes — Hermanus, about 90 minutes from Cape Town, is one of the best places in the world for land-based whale watching. Southern right whales come close to shore to calve, and you can often spot them from clifftop paths without a boat. The season runs roughly June to November, peaking around September, when the town holds a Whale Festival.

**What is the Garden Route?**
The Garden Route is a scenic stretch of coast between roughly Mossel Bay and Storms River, famous for forests, lagoons, beaches and viewpoints. Knysna, with its lagoon and heads, is the best-known stop, alongside Wilderness and Plettenberg Bay. It's a classic multi-day self-drive from Cape Town and pairs beautifully with a city stay.

**How many days do you need in South Africa?**
Ten to fourteen days is the sweet spot for a first trip: a few days in Cape Town, a Garden Route drive, and a safari leg near Kruger. A shorter week can focus on either Cape Town and the winelands or Johannesburg and a Kruger safari. Distances are large and internal flights save time, so plan around one or two hubs.

**Is South Africa good for families?**
Yes — it's a varied, affordable family destination: safaris are a lifelong memory, Cape Town has beaches, cable cars and penguins, Durban offers warm-sea swimming, and many hotels have family rooms and pools at low prices. Choose malaria-free reserves for young children, watch sea currents, and factor in the long-haul flight, which is at least on a convenient overnight schedule.

**What language is spoken in South Africa?**
South Africa has 11 official languages, including English, isiZulu, isiXhosa and Afrikaans. English is very widely spoken and understood in hotels, restaurants and tourist areas across the country, so UK travellers rarely face a language barrier. A few local greetings are always appreciated.

**Is Port Elizabeth worth visiting?**
Port Elizabeth (now officially Gqeberha) sits at the eastern end of the Garden Route on the Indian Ocean and is a handy, affordable base for beaches and for malaria-free Big Five reserves nearby in the Eastern Cape. It's less touristy than Cape Town, with good-value guesthouses, and works well as the finish of a Garden Route drive before flying back via Johannesburg.

**Do I need to book hotels in advance in South Africa?**
For peak season — Cape Town and the Garden Route from December to February, and school holidays — booking the best-value hotels ahead is wise, as they fill fast and prices climb. Off-peak you have more flexibility. Either way, reserving your first night near your arrival airport makes landing after a long-haul flight far smoother.
