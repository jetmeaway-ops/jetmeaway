## The Scout's Take: Old City, Nimman, or Riverside?

The **Old City** is the square inside the moat — the historic heart, packed with temples, cheap guesthouses, cafes and the Sunday Walking Street. Stay here for your first visit: everything is walkable, and this is where the budget tier is at its best. The catch is a little market noise on weekend evenings.

**Nimman (Nimmanhaemin)** is the trendy district west of the Old City — coffee shops, design boutiques, co-working spaces, the MAYA mall and a young nightlife scene. It suits digital nomads, cafe lovers and repeat visitors. It is a 10-minute songthaew from the temples.

**The riverside and Night Bazaar** area east of the Old City is quieter and greener, with the resort-style hotels, spa retreats and the nightly market. Choose it for a pool, a river view and easy dinners.

For a first trip: the Old City. For cafes and nightlife: Nimman. For a resort feel: the riverside. All three are 10–15 minutes apart, so you are never far from anything.

## Beyond the Hotel — Chiang Mai's Essentials

A few experiences worth planning around your stay:

- **Wat Phra That Doi Suthep at sunrise** — the golden mountain temple 15 km northwest, with a city viewpoint. Go early to beat the heat and crowds. The classic Chiang Mai half-day.
- **An ethical, no-riding elephant sanctuary** — the hills around Chiang Mai are home to Thailand's best-known ethical sanctuaries, where you feed and bathe elephants but never ride them. Book directly, check the ethics, and use hotel pickup.
- **The Sunday Walking Street** — the Old City's main road fills with food, crafts and music every Sunday evening. The Saturday Walking Street (Wualai) is the smaller sibling.
- **Old City temple loop** — Wat Chedi Luang, Wat Phra Singh and Wat Chiang Man on foot in an easy morning inside the moat.
- **Khao soi at a local shop** — the northern curried-noodle soup that defines Chiang Mai food. Try the shops around the Muslim quarter and Nimman.
- **Doi Inthanon day trip** — Thailand's highest mountain, with waterfalls, twin royal chedis and cool forest, about two hours southwest.

## UK Practicalities

- **Flights**: no direct UK–Chiang Mai flights. Connect via Bangkok (BKK/DMK) or a Gulf hub (Dubai, Doha, Abu Dhabi), then a 75-minute domestic hop to CNX. [Search flights to CNX](/flights?to=CNX).
- **Visa & entry**: UK passport holders currently enter Thailand visa-free for tourism — see the [UK government's Thailand travel advice](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/thailand) for the current allowance and entry rules before you fly. Passport valid at least six months.
- **Airport**: Chiang Mai International (CNX) is ~4 km from the Old City — a 10–15 minute Grab or taxi, roughly 120–200 baht (£3–5).
- **Currency**: Thai baht (THB), cash-first — guesthouses, street food, songthaews and markets prefer cash. 7-Eleven and ATMs are everywhere.
- **Getting around**: songthaews (shared red trucks, ~30 baht in town), tuk-tuks, and the Grab app for metered rides. The Old City is walkable.
- **Best months**: November–February (cool, dry, peak). June–October is green, wet and cheapest. Avoid the March–April burning season for air quality; expect a spike around Songkran in mid-April.
- **Budget**: budget trip — £30–45/day all-in on a £12–20 room, cheap food and songthaews. Mid-range with a pool hotel and sit-down dinners rarely tops £80/day.
