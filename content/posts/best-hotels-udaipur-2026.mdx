## The Scout's Take: Floating Palace or Old City Rooftop?

Every Udaipur hotel, at every price, pitches the same view: the lake, the City Palace, the sunset. The question is how much of that view you're paying for.

If you're planning the once-in-a-lifetime trip — honeymoon, anniversary, milestone birthday — **[Taj Lake Palace](/hotels/la_lp657a07d5?city=Udaipur)** is the answer that doesn't have a real alternative. It occupies an entire small island in the middle of Lake Pichola, accessible only by the hotel's private boat from the Bansi Ghat jetty, with every suite facing the water. There is no road, no traffic, no horns. You arrive by boat, you leave by boat, and in between you sleep inside a building with no connection to the mainland. It's expensive, it's hard to book (6–9 months ahead in peak season), and it's the closest thing to a fairy-tale stay India can offer — the same setting that stood in for the villain's lair in the Bond film *Octopussy*.

Compare that to **[The Oberoi Udaivilas](/hotels/la_lp309d5?city=Udaipur)** — same lakeside, same five-star territory, but a completely different proposition. It sweeps across fifty acres on the western shore of Lake Pichola and has been repeatedly ranked among the world's best hotels by international travel press. There's more space than the Lake Palace, more privacy, more spa, more grounds to walk — and you don't have to time your day around the boat schedule.

Then there's the version of Udaipur most UK travellers on a 2026 budget are actually planning: a rooftop guesthouse in the Old City, walking distance from the City Palace, with a partial lake view thrown in for £15–25 a night. ## How Udaipur Compares to Jaipur and the Taj Mahal (Agra)

The classic UK "Golden Triangle plus Rajasthan" trip pairs Udaipur with Delhi, Agra and Jaipur. **Udaipur** is the slowest and the most romantic: the Aravalli foothills, the artificial lakes of the Mewar maharanas, and a hotel scene built around water views, now with a genuine backpacker layer underneath the palace hotels. **Delhi** is the UK gateway — most Udaipur trips fly in via Delhi or Mumbai, and Delhi is worth two or three nights for Old Delhi and the government-quarter monuments before heading on. **[Agra](/blog/best-hotels-taj-mahal-2026)**, home to the **Taj Mahal**, is usually a day trip or overnight from Delhi or Jaipur rather than Udaipur itself — the marble-mausoleum equivalent of Udaipur's marble lake palace, but a monument rather than a place to base a holiday. **[Jaipur](/blog/best-hotels-jaipur-2026)** (roughly 4 hours from Udaipur by road, or a short flight) is the largest and most monument-dense of the group — Amber Fort, Hawa Mahal, the City Palace, Jantar Mantar. A typical 10–14 day circuit runs Delhi (arrival) → Agra (Taj Mahal day trip) → Jaipur (monuments) → Udaipur (lakefront wind-down, honeymoon or budget). If you can only do one and the trip is honeymoon-led, Udaipur wins — for the romance, the lake setting, and the simple fact that nowhere else in India looks like it.

## Beyond the Hotel: Udaipur's Essentials

A few non-negotiable experiences worth planning your stay around:

- **City Palace Complex** — the largest royal complex in Rajasthan, still partially occupied by the Mewar royal family. Multiple separate palaces inside the walls, including the Crystal Gallery. Buy the combined ticket; allow at least 3 hours.
- **Lake Pichola Sunset Boat Ride** — the single most photographed Udaipur moment. Sunset boats leave from Bansi Ghat and Rameshwar Ghat — book the 5pm departure, sit on the right-hand side, watch the City Palace turn gold then pink then red. Around 45 minutes.
- **Jag Mandir** — Lake Pichola's other island palace, the one Taj Lake Palace isn't. Visit by boat from Bansi Ghat.
- **Bagore-ki-Haveli Folk Dance Show** — a nightly traditional Rajasthani dance performance in the courtyard of an 18th-century lakefront haveli. Buy tickets at the door 30 minutes early for a front-row floor cushion.
- **Monsoon Palace at Sunset** — a hilltop palace overlooking Udaipur from the west, built as a royal monsoon retreat. The view back across the lakes at sunset is one of the best photographs you'll take in Udaipur.
- **Vintage and Classic Car Museum** — the actual royal car collection, including the Rolls-Royce Silver Ghost used in the 1983 Bond film *Octopussy*, much of which was filmed around the Lake Palace and City Palace. An easy add-on after the City Palace.
- **Day Trip to Kumbhalgarh Fort** — around 2 hours by car. A UNESCO World Heritage Site with one of the longest continuous fort walls in the world.
- **Day Trip to Ranakpur Jain Temple** — around 2.5 hours by car. A white-marble Jain temple with over a thousand individually carved pillars, no two identical.

## UK Practicalities

- **Getting there**: No direct UK flights to Udaipur. Fly LHR–DEL or LHR–BOM on British Airways, Air India or Virgin Atlantic, then connect domestically to Udaipur (UDR) on IndiGo, Vistara, Air India or SpiceJet. Total door-to-door time is typically 14–17 hours including the connection. [Search flights to UDR](/flights?to=UDR).
- **Airport**: Maharana Pratap Airport (UDR) is near Dabok, roughly 25–30 minutes from the Old City by prepaid taxi (~₹500–700 / £5–7).
- **Visa**: Indian e-Visa required for UK passport holders (indianvisaonline.gov.in, ~£25 for 30-day single-entry, apply 4+ days ahead).
- **Currency**: Indian Rupee (INR). Cards work at luxury/mid-range hotels; carry cash for tuk-tuks, street food and small Old City shops. ATMs cluster around Jagdish Chowk and Fateh Sagar.
- **Getting around**: Tuk-tuks (auto-rickshaws) cost roughly ₹50–250 (50p–£2.50) depending on distance — agree the fare first, or use a ride-hailing app for a fixed price. The Old City itself is very walkable.
- **Best months**: October–March (18–28°C, dry). Avoid April–June (40–43°C). July–September is monsoon — under-rated, 30–40% cheaper across every tier, but pack a waterproof.
- **Budget**: Palace-tier trip — £150–1,100+/night all-in. Mid-range trip — £25–130/night. Budget-tier trip — £10–37/night, plus roughly £10–15/day for food. A 4-night budget trip built on this guide's cheap tier can land under £250 per person before flights.

## Booking Udaipur Hotels in 2026: Rates and the Dates to Avoid

Udaipur room rates swing hardest around Rajasthan's wedding season — **December and February** in particular, when domestic wedding demand stacks on top of the UK/international peak-season crowd and pushes every tier, budget included, noticeably higher. **October and March** sit just outside both the monsoon and the wedding-season spike, giving dry skies and a fuller lake without the December/February premium. **July–September** (monsoon) is the cheapest stretch of the year across the board — rates drop 30–40% at hotels that stay open through it, and the Aravalli hills turn green, but book flexible dates in case of heavy rain disrupting boat rides or day trips.
