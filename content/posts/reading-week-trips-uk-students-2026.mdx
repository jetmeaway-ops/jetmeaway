## When is Reading Week — and why it's the cheapest week to fly

UK universities run **three predictable low-demand windows** a UK student can build a trip around:

- **Autumn Reading Week — early November.** Most universities schedule this in week 6 or 7 of autumn term, a genuinely non-teaching week. It sits squarely between October half-term (expensive, families travelling) and the Christmas rush (very expensive), which is exactly why fares bottom out here.
- **Spring / February reading week — mid-to-late February.** A growing number of universities now run a short mid-semester break here too, sometimes under a different name (consolidation week, innovation week, enhancement week). It sits after the January price floor but before Easter, and carries a similar discount pattern to November, adjusted for colder weather.
- **Easter break — late March to April.** Longer (2-3 weeks) and slightly pricier than the other two windows because it overlaps the tail end of the UK's spring half-term demand, but still meaningfully cheaper than summer, with noticeably better weather across the board.

All three windows work on the same underlying mechanic: **UK leisure demand for flights is lumpy**, clustering hard around school holidays. Universities' teaching calendars deliberately don't align with school terms, so a student's free week is frequently the airline industry's quietest week — and quiet weeks are where the airfare floor lives.

[Search flights for your dates](/flights) to compare live prices across every UK departure airport before you commit, then lock in a hostel bed the same day — the cheapest beds in each city (covered destination by destination below) sell out first even in the off-season.

## Booking strategy — when to lock it in

We track price movement on these routes and the pattern holds steady across all three windows:

- **8 weeks before**: best average prices on Ryanair, Wizz Air and easyJet. Sub-£30 returns to most Eastern European capitals regularly available.
- **6 weeks before**: still good, but the very cheapest seat-only fares are thinning out.
- **4 weeks before**: prices climbing noticeably — expect 20-40% above the 8-week floor.
- **2 weeks before**: avoid if you can. Prices typically double from their floor on these routes.
- **Last-minute (3-7 days)**: occasional flash deals appear on under-sold flights but they're route-specific and not worth planning around.

For **November Reading Week 2026**, book in early September. For **February reading week**, book in mid-to-late December. For **Easter**, book 10-12 weeks ahead — slightly earlier than the other two windows because Easter overlaps spring demand.

## Eastern & Central Europe — the price floor

This is where the annual cheapest fares on the entire continent live. Ryanair and Wizz Air compete hard on nearly every route into this region, and November/February demand is close to zero outside the destinations themselves.

## Krakow, Poland

**Fare estimate:** £25-£45 return from Stansted, Manchester or Edinburgh on Ryanair; Birmingham has thinner but occasionally cheaper Ryanair availability at £30-£50. **Cheapest hostel:** Greg & Tom Hostel or Mundo Hostel in Kazimierz, dorm beds from £9/night.

The default Reading Week destination and for good reason. Ryanair flies from Stansted, Manchester, Edinburgh and Bristol, with November and February returns regularly under £40. Hostels in Kazimierz (the old Jewish quarter) start at £9/night for a dorm bed; private double rooms at the Greg & Tom hostel chain run £40-45/night.

**Must-do:** the Auschwitz-Birkenau day trip (£12 by public bus, free entry, 1.5 hours each way) is sobering but essential. The Wieliczka Salt Mines (£20 entry, 30 minutes from the city) are the lighter counterbalance — an underground cathedral carved from rock salt.

**Total budget: £30 flight + £40 hostel (4 nights) + £35 food/transport = £105 all-in.** November and February are both genuinely cold (3-8°C) — layer up.

<a id="bratislava"></a>

## Bratislava, Slovakia

**Fare estimate:** from £19 return, London Stansted on Ryanair — one of the cheapest fares on this entire list on flexible dates. Manchester has occasional Ryanair or Wizz Air availability from £35. Birmingham and Edinburgh don't have a direct route; connect via Stansted. **Cheapest hostel:** Wild Elephants Hostel or Downtown Backpackers, dorm beds from £11/night.

The hidden Slovak capital sits 60km from Vienna by bus, which is exactly its trump card. Once you land, a £4-6 RegioJet or FlixBus takes you to Vienna in under an hour — a genuinely brilliant way to see two capitals on one set of flights.

**Must-do:** the compact Old Town (walkable in an afternoon), Bratislava Castle for river views (free grounds), and the beer — some of the cheapest in Europe at £1.50-£2 a pint. If you bolt on Vienna, budget a full extra day for the Ringstrasse and a coffeehouse.

**Total budget: £25 flight + £35 hostel (4 nights) + £6 bus to Vienna + £35 food/transport = £101 all-in** (Bratislava only; add Vienna's higher costs if extending).

<a id="sofia"></a>

## Sofia, Bulgaria

**Fare estimate:** from £28 return on Wizz Air from Luton or Manchester; occasional Ryanair availability from Stansted and Edinburgh at similar prices. Birmingham connects via Wizz Air from around £40. **Cheapest hostel:** Hostel Mostel, dorm beds from £8/night, includes free dinner and breakfast.

Sofia is the European capital that still feels like a discovery in 2026 — genuinely under-visited relative to how much there is to do, and the cheapest overall trip on this list.

**Must-do:** the free 2-hour walking tour (daily at 11am, tip-based) is the best orientation in the city. The Vitosha mountain cable car (£5) gets you real mountain views 30 minutes from the centre, with skiing on day-passes from £15 if there's snow by late November or February.

**Total budget: £28 flight + £32 hostel (4 nights) + £35 food/transport = £95 all-in.** The single best-value trip on this entire list.

<a id="prague"></a>

## Prague, Czech Republic

**Fare estimate:** £30-£55 return from Stansted, Luton, Manchester or Edinburgh on Ryanair/easyJet; Birmingham via Ryanair from £40. **Cheapest hostel:** Sir Toby's Hostel (Holešovice) or Hostel One Prague Home, dorm beds from £11/night.

A reliably excellent city break year-round, with a notable price spike around the Christmas markets that starts in late November — book the earlier part of the Reading Week window to dodge it. For the full picture on where to stay if you want to upgrade from a hostel, see our [Prague hotels guide](/blog/best-hotels-prague-2026).

**Must-do:** Prague Castle and Charles Bridge before 9am (both free, both empty at that hour), and the Astronomical Clock in Old Town Square. Skip the Old Town restaurants and eat in Žižkov or Vinohrady for half the price and better food.

**Total budget: £35 flight + £44 hostel (4 nights) + £40 food/transport = £119 all-in.**

## Budapest, Hungary

**Fare estimate:** £35-£55 return from Stansted, Luton or Manchester on Wizz Air/Ryanair; Birmingham and Edinburgh both have Wizz Air availability from £40-50. **Cheapest hostel:** Maverick Hostel & Ensuites or Retox Party Hostel, dorm beds from £10/night.

The most popular of the Eastern European capitals, so prices have crept up slightly relative to Sofia or Bucharest, but it's still excellent value and arguably has the best single evening activity on this list. If you want a proper hotel for part of the trip, our [Budapest hotels guide](/blog/best-hotels-budapest-2026) covers the full range.

**Must-do:** the Széchenyi Thermal Baths (£16 entry, open until 10pm, genuinely one of Europe's great budget nights out) and the ruin bars in the Jewish Quarter — Szimpla Kert is the famous one, worth one visit even though it's touristy.

**Total budget: £40 flight + £40 hostel (4 nights) + £40 food/transport = £120 all-in.**

<a id="riga"></a>

## Riga, Latvia

**Fare estimate:** from £30 return, Luton or Stansted on Wizz Air/airBaltic; Manchester has thinner Wizz Air availability from £45. No direct route from Birmingham or Edinburgh — connect via Luton. **Cheapest hostel:** Naughty Squirrel Backpackers Hostel, dorm beds from £9/night.

Riga has a serious student bar scene and the best-preserved Art Nouveau architecture in the Baltics — over 800 buildings in the style, more than any other European city.

**Must-do:** the Art Nouveau district (Alberta iela is the standout street, free to walk), the Central Market (housed in former Zeppelin hangars, cheap and excellent food), and — if you have 6 days rather than 4 — the bus onward to [Vilnius](#vilnius).

**Total budget: £32 flight + £36 hostel (4 nights) + £35 food/transport = £103 all-in.**

<a id="vilnius"></a>

## Vilnius, Lithuania

**Fare estimate:** from £35 return, Luton or Stansted on Wizz Air/Ryanair; Manchester and Edinburgh have limited or no direct routes — connect via Luton or Riga. **Cheapest hostel:** Downtown Forest Hostel, dorm beds from £10/night.

The least-visited of the three Baltic capitals and, for that exact reason, the one that feels most like a genuine discovery — a beautifully preserved Baroque Old Town with almost none of the crowds of Riga or Tallinn.

**Must-do:** the Old Town (a UNESCO World Heritage site, free to wander), Uzupis — a self-declared "independent republic" artists' quarter with its own tongue-in-cheek constitution posted on a wall — and the Hill of Three Crosses for a free panoramic view.

**Total budget: £38 flight + £40 hostel (4 nights) + £35 food/transport = £113 all-in.**

<a id="tallinn"></a>

## Tallinn, Estonia

**Fare estimate:** from £35 return, Luton, Stansted or Edinburgh on Wizz Air/easyJet — Edinburgh's route is a genuine standout for Scottish students. Manchester and Birmingham connect via Luton or Riga. **Cheapest hostel:** Tallinn Backpackers, dorm beds from £11/night.

The most complete medieval Old Town in Northern Europe, still fully walled, and one of Europe's most advanced digital societies sitting behind those medieval walls — the contrast is part of the appeal.

**Must-do:** the Old Town walls and towers (several sections are free to walk), Kalamaja — the hip, colourful wooden-house district that's become the city's café and design quarter — and Patarei Prison, a genuinely eerie former Soviet-era prison now partly open to visitors (£8).

**Total budget: £38 flight + £44 hostel (4 nights) + £35 food/transport = £117 all-in.**

<a id="bucharest"></a>

## Bucharest, Romania

**Fare estimate:** from £28 return, Luton on Wizz Air; Manchester and Edinburgh both have Wizz Air availability from £35-45. Birmingham connects via Luton. **Cheapest hostel:** Pura Vida Hostel or Little Bucharest Old Town Hostel, dorm beds from £8/night.

The Bucharest hostel scene is properly developed and the city's grand, slightly chaotic Belle Époque architecture (it was genuinely nicknamed "Little Paris" a century ago) is under-appreciated relative to the price.

**Must-do:** the Palace of the Parliament tour (£15, the world's heaviest building by some measures), the Old Town bar crawl scene (drinks £2-3), and Therme Bucharest — a genuinely enormous thermal spa complex, day pass £25, one of the best-value spa days in Europe.

**Total budget: £28 flight + £32 hostel (4 nights) + £40 food/transport = £100 all-in.**

<a id="belgrade"></a>

## Belgrade, Serbia

**Fare estimate:** from £45 return, Luton or Stansted on Wizz Air/Ryanair; Manchester has occasional Wizz Air availability from £55. No direct route from Birmingham or Edinburgh — connect via Luton or Stansted. **Cheapest hostel:** Hostel Bongo or Doggy Style Hostel, dorm beds from £9/night.

The rawest, least-polished capital on this list, and increasingly a favourite with students specifically for that reason — genuinely excellent nightlife, a serious history layered into every street corner, and prices that undercut even Sofia on food and drink.

**Must-do:** Kalemegdan Fortress (free, sweeping views over the Sava and Danube confluence), the Museum of Yugoslavia (£4, Tito's mausoleum included), and the Savamala riverside club district after dark — genuinely one of Europe's best nightlife scenes for the price.

**Total budget: £48 flight + £36 hostel (4 nights) + £35 food/transport = £119 all-in.**

## Warmer Mediterranean options — worth the extra spend

Slightly pricier than the Eastern European cluster, but genuinely warm weather (15-24°C) makes these the pick if you want a beach day or a coat-free evening.

<a id="lisbon"></a>

## Lisbon, Portugal

**Fare estimate:** £45-£75 return from Stansted, Luton, Manchester, Bristol or Edinburgh on easyJet/Ryanair; Birmingham via Ryanair from £55. **Cheapest hostel:** Yes! Lisbon Hostel or Home Lisbon Hostel, dorm beds from £15/night.

The warmest of the mainland European options on this list — November averages 16-19°C, February 14-17°C, both mild enough for a genuine beach day at Cascais. Check our full [Lisbon hotels guide](/blog/best-hotels-lisbon-2026) if you want to upgrade for a night or two.

**Must-do:** Tram 28 (an actual public transport tram that doubles as a city tour, £3 ticket), Belém pastries from Pastéis de Belém (£1.30 each — budget for four), and the Sintra day trip (£3 train, £14 Pena Palace entry).

**Total budget: £55 flight + £60 hostel (4 nights) + £40 food/transport = £155 all-in.** Above the Eastern European cluster but the most likeable of the warm options.

<a id="naples"></a>

## Naples, Italy

**Fare estimate:** £40-£70 return from Stansted, Luton or Manchester on Ryanair/easyJet; Birmingham and Edinburgh don't have a reliable direct route — connect via Stansted or Manchester. **Cheapest hostel:** Hostel of the Sun, dorm beds from £14/night.

Chaotic, loud, and unapologetically itself — Naples doesn't perform for tourists the way Rome or Florence do, and that's precisely its appeal. It's also the gateway to Pompeii and the Amalfi Coast without Amalfi Coast prices.

**Must-do:** a proper Neapolitan pizza (this is where it was invented — Sorbillo and Di Matteo are the classic cheap-and-excellent picks, £5-7 a pizza), the Naples Underground tour (£10, Greek-Roman tunnels beneath the city), and Pompeii as a half-day trip (£18 entry, 40 minutes by train).

**Total budget: £48 flight + £56 hostel (4 nights) + £40 food/transport = £144 all-in.**

## Palermo, Sicily

**Fare estimate:** £40-£75 return from Stansted, Luton or Manchester on Ryanair; Birmingham and Edinburgh connect via Stansted. **Cheapest hostel:** A Casa di Amici, dorm beds from £15/night.

Sicily's capital layers Arab, Norman, Byzantine and Baroque architecture into one dense, edible city — the street food scene alone (panelle, arancini, sfincione) justifies the trip on a student budget.

**Must-do:** the Ballarò and Vucciria street markets (free to wander, cheap to eat at), the Norman Palace and Palatine Chapel (£17 combined), and Mondello beach — a 20-minute bus ride, genuinely swimmable into early November in a mild year.

**Total budget: £50 flight + £60 hostel (4 nights) + £40 food/transport = £150 all-in.**

<a id="rome"></a>

## Rome, Italy

**Fare estimate:** £45-£85 return from Heathrow, Gatwick, Stansted, Manchester or Edinburgh on BA/Ryanair/easyJet; Birmingham via Ryanair from £55. Ryanair flies into the smaller Ciampino (CIA) at a discount to BA/easyJet's Fiumicino (FCO) — Ciampino is closer to the centre than most people expect. **Cheapest hostel:** The Beehive or Yellow Hostel, dorm beds from £16/night.

The most expensive city on this list, but it earns the premium — no student trip list is complete without at least considering it, and off-peak Reading Week timing is genuinely the cheapest and least crowded you'll ever see the Colosseum. See our [Rome hotels guide](/blog/best-hotels-rome-2026) for splurge-night options.

**Must-do:** the Colosseum and Roman Forum (£18 combined, book ahead to skip the queue), the Pantheon (free), and Trastevere for dinner — genuinely excellent, less touristy trattorias than the centro storico.

**Total budget: £55 flight + £64 hostel (4 nights) + £45 food/transport = £164 all-in.** The priciest trip on this list but justifiably so.

<a id="athens"></a>

## Athens, Greece

**Fare estimate:** from £55 return, London, Manchester or Edinburgh on Ryanair/Sky Express/Aegean partner routes; Birmingham via Ryanair from £60. Off-peak (October-March excluding Christmas) is the target window. **Cheapest hostel:** Athens Backpackers or Pergamon Studios, dorm beds from £13/night.

The budget Mediterranean option once you escape July and August — Athens in November or February is genuinely pleasant walking weather and the Acropolis without the summer crowds is a completely different (better) experience.

**Must-do:** the Acropolis and Acropolis Museum (£20 combined, book the first morning slot), the Plaka neighbourhood for cheap tavernas (£8-12 a head with wine), and Lycabettus Hill at sunset (free, funicular £8 if you don't want to walk up).

**Total budget: £58 flight + £52 hostel (4 nights) + £40 food/transport = £150 all-in.**

<a id="marrakech"></a>

## Marrakech, Morocco

**Fare estimate:** from £55 return, Stansted, Manchester, Bristol or Luton on Ryanair/easyJet; Birmingham via easyJet from £60, Edinburgh connects via Luton or Stansted. **Cheapest hostel:** Riad dorms from £12/night; Equity Point Marrakech and Hostel Kif Kif are both well-reviewed budget picks.

The wildcard and the warmest destination on this entire list by a wide margin — November averages 20-24°C, and even February holds a mild 16-19°C. See our [Marrakech hotels guide](/blog/best-hotels-marrakech-2026) if you want to upgrade to a proper riad for a splurge night.

**Must-do:** Jemaa el-Fnaa souk at dusk (free, genuinely intense sensory experience), Bahia Palace and Ben Youssef Madrasa (£8 each), and — if your budget stretches — a 3-day desert tour to Merzouga in the Sahara (£85-110 all-in including 2 nights' camp accommodation).

**Total budget: £55 flight + £48 hostel (4 nights) + £45 food/transport = £148 all-in** (Marrakech alone; add the desert tour for a genuinely unforgettable but pricier extension).

## Berlin, Germany

**Fare estimate:** £50-£85 return from Stansted, Luton, Manchester, Edinburgh or Birmingham on easyJet/Ryanair/British Airways. **Cheapest hostel:** Wombat's City Hostel or Generator Berlin Mitte, dorm beds from £16/night.

The honest caveat first: Berlin is off-season but still not genuinely cheap — hostel rates here run higher than almost anywhere else on this list, even in November or February. It's included because the culture, history and nightlife are hard to match, and our [Berlin hotels guide](/blog/best-hotels-berlin-2026) has the fuller picture if you want to upgrade.

**Must-do:** the East Side Gallery and Brandenburg Gate (both free), the Berlin Wall Memorial on Bernauer Straße (free, more moving than the East Side Gallery's tourist-photo version), and a currywurst at Curry 36 (£4-5).

**Total budget: £58 flight + £64 hostel (4 nights) + £45 food/transport = £167 all-in.** Budget for this being the second-priciest trip on the list after Rome.

<a id="amsterdam"></a>

## Amsterdam, Netherlands

**Fare estimate:** £40-£75 return from any London airport, Manchester, Birmingham or Edinburgh — KLM, easyJet, BA and Ryanair (via Eindhoven, a genuine cheaper alternative) all compete hard on this route. **Cheapest hostel:** ClinkNOORD or Stayokay Amsterdam Vondelpark, dorm beds from £18/night.

The most expensive hostel city on this entire list, but the flight is genuinely one of the cheapest and most frequent from almost every UK airport, which makes it a strong pick if your dates are tight and you want zero flight-booking stress. Full detail in our [Amsterdam hotels guide](/blog/best-hotels-amsterdam-2026).

**Must-do:** free walking tour of the Jordaan district, the Rijksmuseum (£20, book ahead), and renting a bike for a day (£10-12) — genuinely the best way to see the canals and get out to the parks.

**Total budget: £48 flight + £72 hostel (4 nights) + £45 food/transport = £165 all-in.**

## Which UK airport to fly from

This matters more than most students realise — the same route can differ by £30-40 depending which UK airport you search.

**London (Stansted, Luton).** The budget capital of the UK — Ryanair's biggest base plus Wizz Air's main UK hub, and the cheapest source for almost every route on this list, especially the Eastern European capitals. Heathrow and Gatwick rarely beat Stansted or Luton on these specific short-haul budget routes.

**Manchester.** The broadest Ryanair and Wizz Air network in the North, and frequently beats a London-airport-plus-domestic-connection combination on both price and hassle for anyone outside the South East. Strong on Krakow, Sofia, Bucharest and the Mediterranean routes.

**Birmingham.** A solid middle-ground hub — competitive on Mediterranean and Turkey-adjacent routes via Jet2 and TUI's flight-only fares, thinner on the Baltics and the Balkans, where you'll often need to connect via Luton or Stansted.

**Edinburgh.** The standout for Scandinavia and the Baltics specifically (easyJet and Wizz Air both run direct routes Scottish students elsewhere don't get), and increasingly competitive on the Eastern European capitals too. Weaker on Morocco and Southern Italy, where a London or Manchester connection is usually needed.

[Compare live fares from your nearest airport](/flights) before committing — the "best" departure hub changes route by route, and a 30-minute extra train journey to a cheaper airport regularly pays for itself several times over.

## Hand luggage discipline

Every fare estimate in this guide assumes you fly with one small bag fitting under the seat. Add a 10kg cabin bag and Ryanair or Wizz Air charge £15-35 each way; add 20kg hold luggage and it's £25-55 each way. A £25 return can become £95 fast if you're not careful.

For a 4-6 day trip, packing into a budget-carrier-spec bag (40x20x25cm on Ryanair, similar on Wizz Air and easyJet) is genuinely doable: two t-shirts, one jumper or fleece, two pairs of socks/underwear, one pair of trousers worn on the flight, toothbrush plus 100ml toiletries. Wearing your coat onto the plane rather than packing it is the single biggest space-saver for the colder destinations on this list.

## The hidden cost: airport transfers

A £25 Sofia return can quietly cost more in airport transfers than in the flight itself if you don't plan ahead. Krakow, Sofia, Bucharest, Riga and Vilnius all have direct airport buses or trains into the centre for £2-5. Marrakech, Naples and Palermo are often cheaper and less stressful by pre-booked shared shuttle or licensed taxi (£10-20) than working out unfamiliar public transport with luggage after a late arrival. Always check the last public transport departure time against your flight's arrival time before you land.

## What to skip

A few "great Reading Week deals" that aren't, once you run the full numbers:

**Iceland.** Genuinely beautiful and genuinely cheap to fly to (Play and Icelandair from Edinburgh and Stansted), but November and February daylight is 5-7 hours and the on-the-ground costs (£8 a coffee, £25 a meal) push even a 4-day trip past £350-400, even on a £100 flight.

**Zurich, Geneva and most of Switzerland.** Off-season doesn't meaningfully move Swiss prices — hostel beds still run £35-45/night and a basic meal is £15-20. Not a Reading Week budget destination at any time of year.

**Anywhere with a forced UK transit/connection.** Connecting through a second UK airport on a non-local-departure ticket adds 3-5 hours and often £30-50 in extra transfer costs that erode the saving entirely. Prioritise a direct flight from your actual nearest airport over a marginally cheaper fare that requires a connection.

## The bottom line

Reading Week — in all three of its 2026 forms, November, February and Easter — is engineered for cheap travel: a non-teaching week landing squarely between the UK's expensive peak periods, with the airfare price-floor at its annual best. The cities that reward UK students most are the Eastern European capitals — [Sofia](#sofia), [Bratislava](#bratislava), [Bucharest](#bucharest) and [Krakow](#krakow) all cluster under £120 all-in for four to six days — with [Lisbon](#lisbon), [Marrakech](#marrakech) and [Athens](#athens) as the warmer £145-155 options, and [Rome](#rome), [Berlin](#berlin) and [Amsterdam](#amsterdam) as the pricier Western European classics worth knowing the honest numbers for before you commit.

The skill isn't loyalty to one airport or carrier — it's matching the trip to the price and booking inside the eight-week window. The £28 Sofia return becomes £70 by the time you're packing for it if you wait; book early for the November window in early September, for February reading week in mid-December, and for Easter about 10-12 weeks out.
