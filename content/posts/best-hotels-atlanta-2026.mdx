## Going to the 2026 World Cup in Atlanta?

Atlanta hosts 2026 World Cup matches at the Mercedes-Benz Stadium right in downtown — the venue is **Atlanta Stadium** (everyday name: **Mercedes-Benz Stadium**), and it is served by Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International, the world's busiest airport. Unlike host cities where the ground sits out in the suburbs, Atlanta's stadium is genuinely central, wedged beside the Georgia World Congress Center and a short walk from Centennial Olympic Park. That changes the whole calculus of where to stay.

**Where to base yourself for match day:** stay in **Downtown Atlanta** if you want to walk to the football. The cluster of hotels around Centennial Olympic Park, Peachtree Center and the Georgia World Congress Center keeps you within a 10–15 minute walk of the stadium, so you can avoid match-day traffic entirely. The **Omni Atlanta Hotel at Centennial Park** and the **Hyatt Regency Atlanta** are the standout walk-to-the-ground picks below, and the **Courtland Grand** and **The Connally** put the same downtown proximity within a budget-tier price. If downtown is sold out or over budget, **Midtown** is the smart fallback — it's two or three MARTA stops north, greener and calmer, with a better restaurant scene around Piedmont Park.

**How to reach the stadium:** ride **MARTA**, Atlanta's rail network — it is the single best decision a travelling fan can make. The GWCC/CNN Center and Vine City stations on the Blue and Green lines both sit a short walk from the stadium, with Five Points as the central interchange. A single ride costs only a few dollars on a reloadable Breeze card. Driving is the slow, expensive option: stadium-area parking is limited and surges on event days, and the streets around the GWCC clog badly before kick-off. From the airport, MARTA's Red and Gold lines run straight from inside the domestic terminal to downtown in about 20 minutes — you can be checked in and walking to the ground without ever touching a road.

### Where should I stay for the match at Atlanta Stadium (everyday name: Mercedes-Benz Stadium), downtown Atlanta?

Stay in Downtown Atlanta if you want to walk to the match. The Omni Atlanta Hotel at Centennial Park is the closest of our picks — it sits beside Centennial Olympic Park, roughly a 10-minute walk from Mercedes-Benz Stadium. Budget travellers can get almost as close for a fraction of the price at the Courtland Grand or The Connally, both inside a 15-minute walk of the ground. Any downtown hotel near Centennial Park, Peachtree Center or the GWCC keeps you within a 15-minute walk or a short MARTA hop, which beats fighting match-day traffic and parking around the stadium.

### How do I get to the stadium on match day in Atlanta?

Take MARTA, Atlanta's rail network. The GWCC/CNN Center and Vine City stations on the Blue and Green lines both sit a short walk from Mercedes-Benz Stadium, and Five Points is the central interchange. A single ride is a few dollars on a Breeze card. Driving is the slow option — stadium-area parking is limited and pricey on event days — so base yourself near a MARTA station and ride in. From the airport, MARTA's Red and Gold lines run straight from the domestic terminal to downtown in about 20 minutes.

### Is Atlanta hosting 2026 World Cup matches?

Yes. Atlanta is one of the 2026 FIFA World Cup host cities, with matches played at Mercedes-Benz Stadium (referred to as Atlanta Stadium during the tournament) right in downtown. The stadium hosts group-stage fixtures plus knockout-round football, and the city is served by Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International, the world's busiest airport, making it one of the easiest host cities to reach from the UK.

### How far is Atlanta airport from downtown hotels?

Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International (ATL) is about 10 miles south of downtown. The MARTA train runs directly from inside the domestic terminal to downtown stations such as Peachtree Center and Five Points in roughly 20 minutes for a few dollars. A taxi or rideshare is 20–35 minutes depending on traffic and typically costs more than the train, so for solo travellers and couples with light luggage, MARTA is the obvious choice.

### Which Atlanta neighbourhood is best for first-time visitors?

Downtown Atlanta is best for first-time and World Cup visitors because it puts you within walking distance of the stadium, Centennial Olympic Park, the Georgia Aquarium and the World of Coca-Cola. Midtown is the strongest second choice — quieter and greener around Piedmont Park, with the High Museum and a better restaurant scene, and still two or three MARTA stops from downtown. Buckhead suits travellers who want upscale shopping and dining over stadium proximity.

### Do I need a car to get around Atlanta during the World Cup?

No. For a World Cup trip based downtown or in Midtown you do not need a car — MARTA rail links the airport, downtown, Midtown and Buckhead, and the stadium is walkable from downtown hotels. The main trade-off for budget stays further out is that you'll want a car or rideshare rather than a walk to MARTA, not personal safety.

### What is MARTA and how do I use it as a tourist?

MARTA is Atlanta's rail and bus network — the fastest, cheapest way to move between the airport, downtown, Midtown and Buckhead. Buy a reloadable Breeze card at any station kiosk (a few dollars per ride, cheaper with multi-ride passes) and tap in and out like the London Underground. The Red and Gold lines run from the airport straight through downtown to Buckhead, covering nearly every hotel in this guide.

### Which Atlanta hotels are best for MARTA access?

### What's the best time of year to visit Atlanta?

Summer (June–August) is hot and humid, in the low 30s°C with regular afternoon thunderstorms; the World Cup's June dates fall right in this window, so pack for heat and rain.
