## The Scout's Take: Golf Resort, Family Village or Budget Self-Catering?

Belek doesn't split into neighbourhoods the way Bodrum or Antalya town do — it's one continuous resort belt along the coast, so the real decision is which **type** of stay suits your trip, not which street it's on.

If golf is the priority, the course-branded five-stars — **Kaya Palazzo**, **Cornelia Diamond**, **Titanic Deluxe Golf**, **Maxx Royal** and **Voyage Belek Golf & Spa** — sit inside or beside Belek's championship courses and often bundle green fees into resort packages. For families chasing the theme-park factor, **The Land Of Legends Kingdom Hotel** and its **Nickelodeon** sister property link directly to the Land of Legends water park, while **Asteria Family Resort** and **IC Hotels Santai** carry explicit kids-concept branding without the theme-park price tag.

Couples and anyone wanting a quieter week should look at **Sherwood Blue Belek (Adults Only)** or the smaller spa-led resorts like **Kirman Belazur** and **Papillon Zeugma Relaxury** — Belek skews family-heavy overall, so these are the exceptions worth knowing about. And for golfers, independent travellers or anyone who'd rather spend on excursions than the room, the budget tier's apartments and villas — **Villa Esma**, **EV Apartments**, **BlueHomes Villas** — offer a kitchen and a lower nightly rate over resort facilities.

The one variable that changes your total spend more than anything else is board type: **ultra all-inclusive** (à la carte dining, imported drinks, minibar) versus room-only or bed-and-breakfast. Always check the board on the live hotel page before you book, especially in the budget and villa tiers where board type varies hotel to hotel.

## Beyond the Resort — Belek's Essentials

A few non-resort experiences worth planning around your stay:

- **Belek's championship golf courses** — the region is Turkey's golf capital, with courses like Montgomerie Maxx Royal, Sultan Golf Club and National Golf Club woven directly between the resorts. Many hotels bundle green fees or offer package deals with neighbouring courses.
- **The Land of Legends theme and water park** — Belek's headline attraction, with several resorts (Kingdom Hotel, Nickelodeon Hotel, Rixos Park) offering bundled or discounted access.
- **Köprülü Canyon National Park** — a popular white-water rafting and canyoning day trip about an hour from the resort belt, a good change of pace from the beach.
- **Aspendos ancient theatre** — one of the best-preserved Roman theatres in the world, roughly 30–40 minutes from Belek and still used for concerts and events in summer.
- **Kurşunlu Waterfall Nature Park** — a shaded, walkable nature reserve with waterfalls and a small lake, popular for a half-day out of the resort.
- **Belek's long sandy-to-shingle beaches** — many with Blue Flag status, running the length of the resort belt with dedicated hotel beach sections.
- **Antalya Old Town (Kaleiçi)** — the historic harbour quarter of Antalya makes an easy half-day trip from Belek for those who want a change from resort life.

## UK Practicalities

- **Airport**: Antalya Airport (AYT) is 30–40 km from the Belek resort belt, roughly a 30–45 minute transfer — one of the shortest airport-to-resort hops on the Turkish Riviera. Most hotels and tour operators offer a private or shared transfer. [Search flights to AYT](/flights?to=AYT).
- **Visa**: none required — UK passport holders can visit Turkey visa-free for tourist stays under 90 days in any 180-day period. Make sure your passport has at least 150 days' validity from your arrival date, and check the [UK Foreign Office Turkey advice](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/turkey) for current entry rules.
- **Currency**: Turkish lira, though euros are widely accepted in the resort belt and many hotels quote in euros. Inside an ultra all-inclusive resort you'll barely spend day to day — carry a little cash for tips and excursions.
- **Best months**: May, June, September and early October (25–30°C) for the sweet spot; July–August is hot (33–38°C) and busiest; the season runs roughly April to late October/November for most resorts.
- **Budget**: budget-tier trip — £45–292/night; mid-range five-star — £200–562/night; luxury five-star — £222–1,306/night. Ultra all-inclusive settles almost everything except spa treatments and excursions.
- **Tipping**: not compulsory but customary — a few euros or lira for waiters, bar staff and housekeeping over a week is normal practice in the resort belt.
