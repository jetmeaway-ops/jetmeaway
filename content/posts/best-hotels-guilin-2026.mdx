Guilin sits in the Guangxi region of southern China, at the heart of a landscape unlike anywhere else on earth: thousands of steep, rounded limestone peaks rising straight out of flat rice plains and winding rivers. The classic image — the one printed on China's ¥20 banknote — is the stretch of the **Li River** between Guilin and **Yangshuo**, about 65km downriver. Because "Guilin" really means this whole karst-scenery region, several of the hotels below are not in the city itself but out in **Yangshuo**, the smaller, more scenic traveller town at the end of the cruise, or up near the **Longsheng (Longji) Rice Terraces**. Where our data shows the town, we name it in the hotel's line so you know exactly where you'd be sleeping. The Li River karst landscape and the nearby Longji terraces sit among China's [UNESCO World Heritage sites](https://whc.unesco.org/en/statesparties/cn), and it's worth reading [the UK Foreign Office's China travel advice](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/china) for entry and safety guidance before you travel. ## What to See in Guilin and the Karst Region

**The famous karst peaks.** Guilin's whole reason for being on the map is its scenery — thousands of steep limestone hills rising straight out of the plains and rivers. You don't have to seek it out; it surrounds the city, the river and every drive between towns.

**A Li River cruise from Guilin to Yangshuo.** The signature experience — a 4-5 hour boat journey downriver past the peaks, fishing villages and the exact scene printed on the ¥20 banknote. Cruises leave from piers near Guilin and end at Yangshuo.

**Yangshuo West Street and countryside cycling.** Yangshuo, at the end of the cruise, is a relaxed traveller town wrapped in karst peaks. West Street is its cafe-bar-and-shopping heart; hire a bike and ride out into the Yulong River countryside for the quiet, close-up version of the scenery.

**The Longji (Longsheng) Rice Terraces.** About 2-2.5 hours north, tiers of rice paddies are carved into steep hillsides — flooded and mirror-like in spring, gold at harvest. Ping'an and Dazhai are the main terrace villages.

**Reed Flute Cave.** A large limestone cavern on the edge of the city, its stalactites and stone pillars lit in colour — an easy, atmospheric half-day trip.

**Elephant Trunk Hill.** Guilin's very symbol — a riverside peak with an arch that looks like an elephant dipping its trunk into the water. Best seen from the riverbank at the meeting of the Li and Peach Blossom rivers.

**The scene on the ¥20 note.** Pull out a ¥20 banknote on your cruise and match the printed landscape to the peaks around Xingping — a fun, only-in-Guilin moment.

## Guilin Hotels FAQs

**Do UK passport holders need a visa for Guilin, China?**
China's unilateral visa-free policy for UK passport holders allows stays of up to 30 days for tourism, business, family visits and transit — a scheme extended into 2026. It's being renewed periodically rather than made permanent, so always confirm the current end date and conditions on the official China visa/embassy site before booking flights. Your passport should be valid for at least six months.

**Which airport do I fly into for Guilin?**
Guilin Liangjiang International Airport (KWL), about 30km west of the city. There are no direct flights from the UK — you connect through a mainland hub such as Shanghai, Guangzhou, Beijing or Chengdu, or via Hong Kong, then take a short domestic hop into KWL. High-speed rail also reaches Guilin from Guangzhou and Shenzhen.

**What currency is used in Guilin and can I pay by card?**
The currency is the Chinese Yuan, also called Renminbi (RMB or CNY). Everyday payment runs on Alipay and WeChat Pay, both of which now let overseas visitors link an international Visa or Mastercard. Cash is accepted but increasingly rare; foreign physical cards work at big hotels but often not small shops, so set up Alipay or WeChat Pay before you travel.

**Do I need a VPN in Guilin?**
If you rely on Google, Gmail, WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook or Western news, yes — these are blocked on the mainland, and a reputable VPN installed before you arrive is the usual workaround (app stores inside China restrict downloading them). Chinese apps like WeChat, Baidu Maps and Amap work without one. Hotel Wi-Fi does not bypass the block.

**What is the Li River cruise and does it start in Guilin?**
It's the classic Guilin experience — a 4-5 hour boat journey from Guilin down to Yangshuo, past the karst peaks, fishing villages and the ¥20-note view. Full tourist cruises depart from piers near Guilin. Shorter bamboo-raft trips run on quieter stretches near Yangshuo itself.

**What are the Longji (Longsheng) Rice Terraces?**
Dramatic tiers of rice paddies carved into steep hillsides about 2-2.5 hours north of Guilin, near Longsheng. They're stunning year-round but especially when flooded and mirror-like in spring and gold at harvest. Ping'an and Dazhai are the main terrace villages, some with their own guesthouses.

**When is the best time to visit Guilin?**
April to October is prime — warm weather and the greenest scenery, with the rice terraces flooded in late spring and lush through summer. Summer can be hot, humid and rainy, and July-August is the busiest domestic-tourism stretch. Spring and autumn shoulder months give the best balance of weather and crowds.

**How much does a budget hotel in Guilin cost per night?**
Real, bookable budget rooms run from about £13 to the low £40s — the deepest cluster of tidy 2-3★ city hotels sits in the £16-38 band. Yangshuo and scenic-town stays tend to sit a little higher. All figures are rough pound conversions from Yuan.

**Is Guilin or Yangshuo a better base?**
Guilin city is the transport hub — airport, high-speed rail and the main cruise piers — with the widest, cheapest hotel choice. Yangshuo is smaller, more scenic and more relaxed, with West Street's cafes and easy countryside cycling. Many travellers do both: a couple of nights in Guilin, then the cruise down to Yangshuo.

**How do I get from Guilin to Yangshuo?**
The scenic way is the Li River cruise, roughly 4-5 hours downriver. Faster options are a bus (about 1.5 hours) or a high-speed train to Yangshuo station (around 30 minutes, though the station is outside town). Many visitors cruise down and take the bus back.

**What is Guilin famous for?**
Its karst-mountain scenery — thousands of steep, rounded limestone peaks rising straight out of flat land and rivers. The stretch of the Li River between Guilin and Yangshuo, including the ¥20-note view, is the postcard image of rural China.

**What can I see in Guilin city itself?**
Elephant Trunk Hill (a riverside peak shaped like an elephant drinking, Guilin's symbol), Reed Flute Cave (a large illuminated limestone cavern), and the Two Rivers and Four Lakes waterway ring, beautiful when the Sun and Moon Twin Pagodas are lit at night. Seven Star Park and Fubo Hill add more karst and greenery.

**Is Guilin good for a Li River bamboo raft trip?**
Yes — while the full cruise uses large boats, shorter bamboo-raft rides on gentler sections (especially around Yangshuo and the Yulong River) are a favourite: slower, closer to the water and better for photos. Several Yangshuo hotels in this guide sit right by the Yulong River.

**How many days do I need in Guilin?**
Three to four days: a day for Guilin city (Elephant Trunk Hill, Reed Flute Cave, the night waterways), a full day for the Li River cruise to Yangshuo, and a day or two around Yangshuo for cycling and bamboo rafting. Add a day for the Longji Rice Terraces if you want to see them.

**Is Guilin expensive for UK travellers?**
On the ground it's very affordable — budget hotel rooms from around £13, cheap and excellent local food, and low-cost public transport. The main cost is getting there, since it needs a connecting flight through a Chinese hub. Once you're in Guilin, day-to-day spending is low by UK standards.

**Do people speak English in Guilin?**
English is limited outside major hotels and the main tourist spots, more so in the city than in traveller-focused Yangshuo, where West Street's cafes and operators are used to foreign visitors. A translation app (with offline Chinese downloaded) and your hotel's name and address in Chinese characters make things much easier.

**What is the food like in Guilin?**
The signature dish is Guilin rice noodles (Guilin mifen) — rice noodles in a savoury broth with pickled vegetables and toppings, eaten everywhere and very cheap. Yangshuo's speciality is beer fish, river fish braised with local beer, tomatoes and peppers. Street food and night markets are excellent value.

**Are the Longji terraces doable as a day trip from Guilin?**
Yes — many people visit the Longsheng terraces on a long day trip (about 2-2.5 hours each way by bus or organised tour). To catch sunrise or sunset over the paddies, stay overnight in a Ping'an or Dazhai guesthouse. This guide includes a Longsheng-area listing for that.

**Is Guilin suitable for families?**
Yes — the boat cruises, caves, cable cars and countryside cycling appeal to children, and hotels are inexpensive so family rooms stay affordable. Yangshuo in particular is relaxed and easy to get around, with bike hire and gentle river trips rather than long museum days.

**What should I know about mobile payments as a tourist in Guilin?**
Set up Alipay or WeChat Pay before you arrive and link an international card — both now support foreign Visa and Mastercard for most purchases, and they're how locals pay for almost everything. Carry a little cash as a backup, and don't rely on your physical card being accepted at small vendors.

**Which is the best-value luxury hotel in Guilin?**
The Shangri-La Guilin (from around £85) and the Sheraton Guilin (from around £89) are the two international five-star names here, both riverside in the city, and both cost a fraction of what an equivalent brand would in London.
