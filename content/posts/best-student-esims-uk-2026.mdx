## 1. Quick check: does your phone even support eSIM?

**Yes if you have any of these:**
- iPhone XR, XS, XS Max, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15 or 16 (any model, any storage, any carrier)
- Google Pixel 4 and later (4, 4a, 5, 5a, 6, 6a, 7, 7a, 8, 8a, 9 series)
- Samsung Galaxy S20 and later (S20, S21, S22, S23, S24)
- Samsung Galaxy Z Fold/Flip 1 and later
- Most current OnePlus and Xiaomi flagships sold in the UK

**No (or check before buying) if you have:**
- iPhone X, 8 or earlier — physical SIM only
- iPhone SE 1st or 2nd generation — physical SIM only (3rd gen does have eSIM)
- Most budget Android phones under £200 — frequently no eSIM hardware
- Any phone bought in mainland China — Chinese-spec phones often have eSIM disabled

If you're in the "yes" group, every option below works for you. If "no", skip eSIMs and either buy a local SIM at the destination airport or pay your UK network's roaming fee.

**One extra check specific to a year abroad or gap year:** confirm your phone is unlocked (not tied to a UK network contract) before you leave, since some travel eSIMs can behave unpredictably alongside a locked handset's carrier restrictions. Most contract phones bought outright or paid off are unlocked automatically after a set period — check with your network (usually via their app or a simple online request) at least a week before you fly, since unlock requests can take a few days to process.

## 2. The five eSIMs worth a UK student's money

#### Airalo — the cheapest catalogue, biggest country list

Airalo is the Amazon of travel eSIMs. 200+ countries, plans from 1GB up, granular regional bundles. For a UK student doing 1-2 European trips a year plus the occasional bigger trip, this is the default pick.

**Best plans for UK students:**
- Eurolink Regional (39 European countries, 1GB / 7 days): around £4.50
- Spain (5GB / 30 days): around €4 — useful for the year-abroad first month

**Rating**: 4 stars. Cheapest per GB, granular plans, occasional ISIC discount.

#### Holafly — the truly unlimited option

Holafly built its name on "unlimited data with no per-GB anxiety." More expensive per day than Airalo but you stop counting megabytes. Best for content creators uploading 4K video, year-abroad students who'll tether a laptop, and anyone who hates checking data usage.

**Best plans for UK students:**
- Europe Unlimited (10 days): around $40
- USA Unlimited (10 days): around $36
- Asia Unlimited (10 days): around $45
- Year-abroad Spain (90 days unlimited): around $130

**Rating**: 4 stars. Best peace-of-mind option, premium price.

#### Yesim — the dual-network gap-filler

Yesim sits between Airalo and Holafly on price and offers true unlimited daily plans. Their pitch is dual-network coverage in some countries (e.g., Thailand uses both AIS and TrueMove) — when one network drops, you switch automatically.

**Best plans for UK students:**
- Thailand Unlimited (7 days): around $21 — properly cheap
- Europe Unlimited (7 days): around $25
- USA Unlimited (7 days): around $25

**Rating**: 4 stars. Best mid-tier choice.

#### Maya Mobile — the year-abroad option

Maya specialises in long-stay plans (30, 60, 90 days) for digital nomads and year-abroad students. Better per-day rates than Airalo if you need more than a month's data. Coverage is narrower than Airalo's but covers all major year-abroad destinations.

**Best plans for UK students:**
- USA 90 days, 50GB: around $90
- Spain 90 days, 30GB: around €35

**Rating**: 3.5 stars. Niche but useful for term-abroad scenarios.

#### Truphone (now part of 1GLOBAL) — the UK network compatibility option

Truphone runs an actual mobile network (now under the 1GLOBAL brand). Their advantage for UK students: a real UK number alongside your travel eSIM, useful if you don't already have dual-SIM or want a separate "travel line" identity. Pricier than the pure-data eSIMs but the only one that gives you a phone number to receive calls on.

**Best plans for UK students:**
- World Plan (UK number + global data, 1GB/month): around £15/month

**Rating**: 3 stars. Specialist use case but solid execution.

## 4. Common student scenarios with the right pick for each

**1-week city break in Europe (Krakow, Amsterdam, Lisbon)**: leave UK SIM on roaming if you're on a modern plan. Don't bother with an eSIM unless you're a heavy data user.

**Reading Week trip to Marrakech, Sofia or Tirana**: Airalo regional plan, ~£5-£8 for the trip. UK roaming outside the EU costs £5-£7/day so you save £20+.

**Inter-rail or month-long backpacking**: Airalo Discover Regional Europe (10GB, 30 days, ~£25) — the per-GB rate beats single-country plans once you cross 3+ countries.

**Year abroad in Spain, France, Germany, Italy**: Airalo for the first 30 days while you settle in, then switch to a local Movistar / Bouygues / Vodafone DE / TIM contract for the rest of the year. Local contracts at €15-€25/month with 50GB+ destroy any eSIM on long-stay value.

**Year abroad in the US**: Skip eSIMs entirely after the first month. Mint Mobile prepaid 3-month bundle (£75) or T-Mobile prepaid (£40/month) are cheaper for stays over 1 month.

**Backpacking through Asia (Thailand, Vietnam, Indonesia, Japan)**: Airalo per-country plans or the Asialink regional bundle. Yesim is competitive in Thailand. Holafly if you'll tether a laptop.

**Conference / 2-day trip anywhere**: just hotspot off a friend or use hotel WiFi. No eSIM purchase justifies the friction for under 48 hours.

**Gap year spanning 3+ continents**: mixed strategy — regional eSIM for the first leg of each continent, local SIM once you know you're staying somewhere 3+ weeks. Full breakdown in the [gap year section](#gap-year-math) below.

**Erasmus or university exchange term**: eSIM for arrival week, local SIM-only contract for the rest of the term. Full breakdown in the [Erasmus section](#erasmus-math) below.

A "gap year" covers wildly different trip shapes — three months in one place, six months split across two, or genuine round-the-world hopping. The maths changes completely depending on which one you're doing, so here are three realistic gap-year shapes costed out in full.

Compare that to running Airalo or Holafly exclusively for the full 4 months at their standard multi-country rates (roughly £35-£45/month for consistent 10GB coverage): **~£140-£180**. Switching to a local SIM the moment you know you're staying in one place for more than 2-3 weeks roughly halves your total spend.

**Shape 2 — Two-region gap year (e.g., 3 months South America, 3 months working holiday in Australia)**

| Stage | Approach | Cost |

**Shape 3 — True round-the-world gap year (6 countries, 12 months, genuinely moving every 3-6 weeks)**

This is the shape where eSIMs earn their keep for longer, because you never settle long enough to make a local contract worth the hassle of buying a local number and registering ID.

| Stage | Approach | Monthly cost (approx.) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Months 1-3 (Southeast Asia hopping) | Airalo Asialink regional, ~10GB/month | £20/month |
| Months 4-6 (Australia/NZ, longer stays, some local SIM use) | Mixed: local SIM where 4+ weeks in one spot | £22/month avg |

A slower, more settled gap year in 1-2 regions can come in under £150 for the same period, because local contracts do the heavy lifting.

| Month 2 onward | Mint Mobile prepaid (bundle discount for 3-month or 12-month terms) or T-Mobile Connect prepaid | £20-£40/month |
| **Total, US year abroad (9 months)** | | **~£195-£340** |

**Key insight for year-abroad students specifically**: many UK mobile contracts include EU roaming (see the FAQ on this below) with a fair-use cap around 60-90 days abroad before reverting to pay-as-you-go rates. A full academic year exceeds that cap easily, so relying on your existing UK contract's "free EU roaming" for a full year-abroad placement is a mistake many students make — check your specific provider's day-cap, not just the data cap, before assuming you're covered for a full term.

<a id="erasmus-math"></a>

## 7. Erasmus and university exchange cost math

Erasmus-style placements (or any university exchange, Erasmus+ branding or not, post-Brexit UK equivalents included) typically run one semester (4-5 months) rather than a full year, which changes the maths again — the setup cost of a local contract needs to be amortised over a shorter window.

**Single-semester exchange (5 months, one EU country)**

| Approach | Total 5-month cost | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Airalo/Yesim eSIM for the entire term (large bundle, e.g. 30GB/90 days renewed once) | ~£90-£130 | Simplest, no local paperwork, but most expensive per GB |
| eSIM for arrival month, then local SIM-only contract for remaining 4 months | ~£75-£110 | Best balance — avoids the local bank/ID hassle for a contract you'd only use briefly anyway |
| Local prepaid SIM from day one (bought with just a passport, no contract) | ~£60-£95 | Cheapest if your destination allows passport-only prepaid registration (most of the EU does) |

For a single semester, the decision tree is simpler than a full year abroad: if your destination country lets you buy a prepaid SIM with just your passport (true for the large majority of EU countries — Spain, France, Germany, Italy, Portugal, Netherlands all allow this), skip the eSIM after week one and go straight to a local prepaid SIM. It's cheaper than any eSIM bundle sized for a full semester and gives you a local number that's genuinely useful for university enrolment, local delivery apps, and local bank verification if you open a student account.

**Where an eSIM still wins for a semester exchange**: if you're doing a "term abroad, then two weeks travelling before flying home" pattern common on many exchange programmes — the eSIM gives you continuity through the travel leg without another SIM swap, where a local SIM tied to your host city may not have useful coverage or roaming once you cross into a third country.

## 8. Reading Week cost math: the short-trip category in full

Reading Week (and equivalent short breaks — a long weekend, a 4-6 day half-term-style gap) is the category where the "just leave UK roaming on" advice is genuinely competitive, so it's worth breaking down properly rather than defaulting to an eSIM out of habit.

**5-day EU trip, moderate data use (Maps, WhatsApp, some social media, ~3-4GB total)**

| Option | Cost | Verdict |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| EE/Vodafone/O2/Three roaming (within fair-use cap) | £10-£15 (day-rate) or often included free if within cap | Competitive — check your specific plan's inclusive EU roaming terms first |
| Airalo regional Eurolink, 5GB | ~£8 | Cheaper than most day-rate roaming, and gives you a fixed cost you know upfront |
| Yesim Europe regional, 5-day unlimited | ~£18-£20 | Only worth it if you'll genuinely use 5GB+ (video calls, navigation-heavy road trip) |

**5-day non-EU trip (Morocco, Turkey, Albania, Georgia), moderate data use**

| Option | Cost | Verdict |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| UK SIM roaming, out-of-EU rate | £25-£35 (£5-£7/day) | Nearly always the worst option outside the EU |

The Reading Week rule of thumb holds: **inside the EU, check your existing plan's roaming terms before buying anything — you may already be covered. Outside the EU, buy the eSIM before you fly, every time**, because non-EU roaming rates are punitive on every major UK network and the eSIM pays for itself within the first day.

<a id="setup-walkthrough"></a>

## 9. Setting up your first eSIM: a step-by-step walkthrough

If you've never installed an eSIM before, the process is genuinely simpler than swapping a physical SIM card — but doing it for the first time at an airport gate, with a queue behind you and 4% battery, is the wrong moment to learn. Do this the night before you fly.

**Step 1 — Confirm compatibility and unlock status.** Go back to the [phone check](#phone-check) list above, and separately confirm your phone is network-unlocked (Settings → General → About on iPhone will usually show "No SIM restrictions"; Android varies by manufacturer, or just ask your network directly).

**Step 2 — Buy the plan on WiFi, not mobile data.** Open the Airalo or Yesim app (or website), pick your destination and data size, and pay. You'll get either a QR code or an activation link within a few minutes — occasionally instantly, occasionally after a short manual review on your first purchase with a new payment card.

**Step 3 — Install the profile.** Scan the QR code (iPhone: Settings → Cellular → Add eSIM → Use QR Code; Android: Settings → Network & Internet → SIMs → Add eSIM, wording varies by manufacturer) while still on UK WiFi. The profile installs in under 90 seconds and shows up as a new line in your phone's SIM settings.

**Step 4 — Label the lines and set data routing.** Rename your physical UK SIM "UK — Calls/Texts" and the new eSIM "Travel — Data" (or similar) so you can tell them apart at a glance. Set your default line for cellular data to the travel eSIM, and set your default line for iMessage/FaceTime and calls to your UK line. Crucially: turn off **Data Roaming** for your UK line specifically, so it can't accidentally rack up charges if the eSIM briefly drops signal and the phone tries to fall back.

**Step 5 — Don't activate yet.** Most eSIM plans start their validity countdown the moment the profile first connects to the destination network, not the moment you install it. Leave the eSIM installed but toggled off (or simply don't let your phone connect to a foreign network) until you actually land, so you're not burning days of a 7 or 30-day plan while still sat at Heathrow.

**Step 6 — Toggle on after landing, verify, and move on.** Once you land, switch the eSIM line on in Settings, wait for the network name to appear in your status bar (usually 10-30 seconds), and do a quick test — load a webpage or check WhatsApp connects. If it doesn't connect within a couple of minutes, toggle Airplane Mode on and off, which resolves the large majority of first-connection hiccups.

This whole process takes under five minutes once you've done it once, and the muscle memory carries over to every future trip — install before you fly, activate after you land, and the "phone doesn't work at the airport" scramble simply stops happening.

<a id="lost-phone"></a>

## 10. What happens if your phone is lost, stolen or breaks mid-trip

This is the scenario most eSIM guides skip, and it matters more for gap-year and year-abroad students than for a weekend trip, purely because the exposure window is so much longer.

**An eSIM cannot be physically removed and moved to a new phone the way a plastic SIM can.** If your phone is lost, stolen, or the screen smashes beyond use, your eSIM profile is gone with it — you'll need to buy a new eSIM plan (or a local SIM) for the replacement device. This is one of the few genuine downsides of eSIM versus physical SIM, and it's worth planning around rather than discovering the hard way.

**Before you travel long-term, do three things:**

1. **Note your eSIM provider account login separately from your phone** (a password manager synced to a laptop, not just remembered on the phone itself) — Airalo, Yesim and Holafly all let you re-purchase or, in some cases, re-download a profile to a new device from your account, but only if you can still log in.
2. **Keep a small cash or card reserve specifically for an emergency local SIM.** A £10-£15 local prepaid SIM bought at a phone shop is the fastest way back online if your primary eSIM setup is unreachable — most corner phone shops in tourist-heavy cities can activate one in minutes with just a passport.
3. **Tell a UK contact (family, flatmate, university welfare team) your rough itinerary and how to reach you by a second channel** (email, a friend's WhatsApp, Instagram DM) in case your primary number goes dark for a day or two while you sort out a replacement.

For year-abroad and Erasmus students specifically, most university exchange programmes and travel insurance policies (including the standard 4-in-1 UK student travel insurance products) cover phone loss or theft as standard content — check your policy's per-item limit before you go, since a recent-generation iPhone or Samsung flagship can exceed a policy's default single-item cap, in which case you may need to declare it separately.

<a id="five-mistakes"></a>

## 11. Five mistakes to avoid

**Don't buy the eSIM at the airport.** The "Tourist SIM" kiosks at Heathrow / Stansted / Gatwick mark up by 3-4x. Buy the eSIM in advance from your laptop, install before flying.

**Don't disable your UK SIM line.** You'll miss banking codes, 2FA texts and family calls. On dual-SIM phones, keep both active and let iOS/Android route data through the eSIM.

**Don't buy the smallest plan unless you really know your data usage.** "1GB / 7 days" sounds plenty until you accidentally stream a Netflix episode. 5GB is the sensible floor for any trip beyond 3 days.

**Don't forget to disable iCloud Photo Library backup over cellular.** A single uploaded video can burn 2-3GB silently. iPhone Settings → Photos → Cellular Data → Off (during travel).

**Don't trust "unlimited" without reading the fair-use policy.** Airalo's Unlimited Lite throttles after 1GB/day. Holafly throttles after a higher but undisclosed cap. Genuinely unlimited (no throttle) is rare.

**A sixth, specific to longer trips**: don't let a UK pay-as-you-go SIM go dormant for too long while you're away. Most UK PAYG SIMs deactivate after 6-12 months without a top-up or any usage — a cheap "keep-alive" top-up every few months protects the number you'll need for banking and official UK correspondence when you're back.

<a id="dual-esim-trick"></a>

## 12. The dual-eSIM trick

iPhone XS and later support **two active eSIMs simultaneously** plus your physical UK SIM. The pro move:

- UK SIM line: physical, kept active for SMS and calls
- eSIM #1: Airalo regional, your default data plan abroad
- eSIM #2: a small Holafly unlimited plan as a "fallback" if Airalo's network coverage drops

You can switch between eSIMs in settings without removing them. For long trips this resilience is genuinely useful — Airalo's Thailand network coverage is patchy in the islands, Holafly's is broader, having both means you're never offline.

**For year-abroad and gap-year students specifically**, the dual-eSIM slot is also useful for running a local SIM (as your primary eSIM once you've settled) alongside a small Airalo/Yesim regional bundle kept dormant as a travel-weekend fallback — so a spontaneous weekend trip to a neighbouring country doesn't require any setup, it's already installed and ready to activate.
