## What to See in Nairobi

Start with the fact that makes this city unique in the world: **Nairobi National Park is the only national park inside a capital city**. It begins about 20 minutes from the centre, and within it are lions, both black and white rhino, giraffe, zebra, buffalo, hippo and more than 400 bird species — with the city's skyline standing on the horizon behind them. Photographs of a rhino grazing in front of office towers are not a trick; that is simply what the park looks like. A morning game drive here is the best three hours you can spend in Nairobi, and it is perfectly possible before an afternoon flight.

A short drive on, in the Langata and Karen suburbs, sit the city's two most-loved attractions. The **David Sheldrick Wildlife Trust** runs an elephant orphanage on the park's edge, hand-raising calves orphaned by poaching and drought before rewilding them in Tsavo; it opens to visitors for a short window each day, and fostering an orphan gets you a quieter evening visit. Nearby, the **Giraffe Centre** in Langata lets you hand-feed endangered **Rothschild's giraffes** from a raised platform — eye to eye with an animal there are only a few thousand of. Children never forget it.

Then there is the literary Nairobi. The **Karen Blixen Museum** is the farmhouse where the Danish author of *Out of Africa* lived from 1917 to 1931 at the foot of the Ngong Hills, preserved much as she left it — the suburb of Karen still carries her name. The **Nairobi National Museum** covers Kenyan prehistory, including some of the most important early-human fossil finds on earth, and **Karura Forest** offers a genuinely lovely 1,000-hectare urban forest with waterfalls, walking and cycling trails. For crafts and souvenirs, the roving **Maasai Market** sets up in different locations across the week — haggling is expected and good-natured — and the **Bomas of Kenya** stages traditional dance and homestead displays from communities across the country.

Above all, Nairobi is **the gateway to Kenya's safari circuit**. From here you reach the **Maasai Mara**, **Amboseli** with its elephants framed by Kilimanjaro, **Tsavo East and West**, Samburu and the Rift Valley lakes, plus quick onward flights to **Mombasa** and **Diani** for the Indian Ocean coast. The **Great Migration** reaches the Mara roughly **July to October**. For planning, the official tourism board site [Magical Kenya](https://magicalkenya.com/) is a useful starting point, and you should read the current [UK FCDO travel advice for Kenya](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/kenya) before you book anything — it covers entry rules, health requirements and region-by-region safety guidance.

## Choosing Where to Stay in Nairobi

- **Westlands** — the easiest all-round base. The densest cluster of restaurants, cafés, bars and malls, plenty of hotels at every price, and comfortable to walk around by day.
- **Karen and Langata** — leafy, low-rise and calm, closest to the Giraffe Centre, the elephant orphanage, the Karen Blixen Museum and the national park gates. The most safari-flavoured place to stay, though you will taxi in for city dining.
- **Upper Hill and Kilimani** — the business districts, with gardens, pools and good four-star value a short drive from the centre.
- **The CBD** — cheapest rooms and best transport links, and several long-established hotels. Honestly, though: it is busy, crowded and not somewhere to wander on foot at night. Fine as a budget base if you take taxis in the evening.
- **Near JKIA airport** — the practical choice for dawn safari departures or a late arrival, given how badly Nairobi traffic can bite.

## Staying Safe in Nairobi — the Honest Version

Nairobi has a real petty-crime and mugging risk. Pretending otherwise would not help you, and neither would scaremongering: the vast majority of visitors have completely trouble-free trips by following a short list of habits that locals follow too.

Use **Uber or Bolt** rather than walking, especially after dark — they are cheap and remove any fare negotiation. Do not display phones, cameras or jewellery on the street. **Keep car doors locked and windows up** in stationary traffic. Use the hotel safe and carry only the cash and one card you need for the day. Draw money from ATMs inside malls or bank branches, not on the street. And **ask your hotel** which specific streets and areas to avoid at which times — reception staff give straight, current answers, and their advice beats any guidebook. Pair that with the UK FCDO travel advice for Kenya linked above, which is updated regularly.

## Nairobi Hotels FAQs

**Do UK travellers need a visa for Kenya?**
Kenya has replaced its visa system with an **eTA (Electronic Travel Authorisation)**, which UK passport holders must apply for **online in advance** of travel. It is not issued on arrival in the way the old visa was, so apply before you fly and allow processing time. Rules, fees and processing windows change, so always check the current requirements on the official Kenyan eTA site and the UK FCDO travel advice page before booking.

**What currency is used in Nairobi?**
The **Kenyan Shilling (KES)**. Cards work in hotels, malls and larger restaurants, and M-Pesa mobile money is used everywhere by locals. **US dollars are widely accepted** for safaris, park fees and tour operators, so many visitors carry some dollars alongside shillings. The pound figures in this guide are rough conversions for guidance only, not exact quotes.

**Are there direct flights from London to Nairobi?**
Yes. **British Airways and Kenya Airways both fly direct** from London to Jomo Kenyatta International Airport (NBO), typically around **8.5 to 9 hours**. That makes Nairobi one of the easiest long-haul African cities to reach from the UK, and it is why so many safari trips start and end here.

**Is Nairobi safe for tourists?**
Nairobi has a real petty-crime and mugging risk, and it is fair to plan around it rather than ignore it. Use Uber or Bolt rather than walking at night, do not display phones or jewellery on the street, keep car doors locked in traffic, use the hotel safe, carry only what you need, and ask your hotel which specific areas to avoid. Millions of visitors have trouble-free trips doing exactly this. Check the UK FCDO advice for Kenya before you go.

**Can you really see wild animals inside Nairobi?**
Yes — and it is the city's most remarkable fact. **Nairobi National Park is the only national park in the world within a capital city**, roughly 20 minutes from the centre, where lions, black and white rhino, giraffe, zebra and buffalo graze with the skyline on the horizon. It is a genuine park with genuine wildlife, and a half-day drive there is the single best thing to do in the city.

**How many days do you need in Nairobi?**
Two to three days is the sweet spot, usually before or after a safari. That gives you a morning in Nairobi National Park, the David Sheldrick elephant orphanage and the Giraffe Centre on one day, the Karen Blixen Museum and Nairobi National Museum on another, and time for the Maasai Market and Karura Forest. If Nairobi is only a stopover before the Mara, one full day still covers the park and the elephants.

**Which area of Nairobi should I stay in?**
**Westlands** for restaurants, malls and nightlife; **Karen and Langata** for leafy calm near the giraffes, the elephant orphanage and the national park; **Upper Hill and Kilimani** for business travel and value; the **CBD** for the cheapest rooms and easiest transport, though it is busier and less pleasant on foot after dark; and the **JKIA airport hotels** for early safari departures.

**Is Nairobi hot?**
Not in the way people expect from an equatorial city. Nairobi sits at roughly **1,700 metres**, so days are pleasantly warm rather than baking and evenings genuinely turn cool — **pack a fleece or light jacket**. The coast at Mombasa and Diani is a completely different, hot and humid climate, so do not pack for one and assume it covers the other.

**When is the best time to visit Nairobi and Kenya?**
The dry seasons: **January to February** and **June to October**. The June to October window is also the classic safari season, and the **Great Migration in the Maasai Mara runs roughly July to October**. The long rains fall March to May and the short rains November to December — travel is still possible then, and prices are lower, but game tracks get muddy.

**Do I need malaria tablets for Nairobi?**
⚠️ Nairobi itself is generally considered **low or no malaria risk** because of its altitude, but **the safari areas and the coast are risk areas** — and almost every Nairobi trip includes one of those. Get medical advice from your GP or a travel clinic well before you fly about antimalarials, and pack repellent and cover up at dusk. This is general information, not medical advice.

**Do I need a yellow fever certificate for Kenya?**
It depends on your route. A **yellow fever certificate may be required** if you are arriving from or transiting a country with yellow fever transmission risk, and some onward countries require proof of vaccination if you have been in Kenya. Requirements change, so check with a travel clinic and the official guidance well before departure.

**Can I drink the tap water in Nairobi?**
Stick to **bottled or properly filtered water**. Most hotels provide bottled water or filtered dispensers, and many now offer refill stations to cut plastic. Use bottled water for brushing teeth if you have a sensitive stomach, and be sensible about ice and raw salads at very cheap street stalls.

**How do I get around Nairobi?**
**Uber and Bolt** both operate widely, are inexpensive by UK standards, and are what most visitors use — they remove fare haggling and mean you are not walking at night. Hotels can also arrange drivers by the half-day, which works well for the Karen and Langata attractions. Traffic is heavy, so allow far more time than the distance suggests.

**What is the cheapest hotel in this Nairobi guide?**

**How much does a hotel in Nairobi cost per night?**
Budget rooms run roughly **£34 to £77**, mid-range hotels sit around **£42 to £173**, and the five-star landmarks here range from about **£71 to £171**. The **highest from-price on this entire list is around £173** at Four Points by Sheraton Nairobi Airport — which tells you something: Nairobi luxury costs far less than the equivalent in London or Cape Town.

**Where should I stay for an early safari flight?**
**Near Jomo Kenyatta International Airport (JKIA).** Crowne Plaza Nairobi Airport, Four Points by Sheraton Nairobi Airport, The Panari Hotel, PrideInn Plaza Nairobi Airport and Airport Landing Hotel are all built around early departures and late arrivals. Nairobi traffic can be brutal, so an airport-side room before a dawn flight is money well spent.

**Is Karen a good place to stay in Nairobi?**
Karen — named after Karen Blixen, author of *Out of Africa* — and neighbouring Langata are the leafy, low-rise, garden-suburb side of Nairobi. They put you closest to the Giraffe Centre, the elephant orphanage, the Karen Blixen Museum and the national park gates. Calmer and greener than the city, though you will rely on taxis for Westlands restaurants.

**Is Westlands a good base?**
Yes, for most first-time visitors. Westlands has the densest concentration of restaurants, bars, coffee shops and malls, plenty of well-reviewed hotels at every price, and it is well placed for the museum and Karura Forest. It is the easiest area to feel comfortable in on foot during the day.

**Should I stay in the Nairobi CBD?**
The CBD gives you the cheapest rooms and the most central transport links. Be honest about the trade-off: it is crowded, noisy and not somewhere you want to be wandering on foot after dark. If you take taxis in the evening it works perfectly well as a budget base.

**How far is Nairobi National Park from the city centre?**
About **20 minutes' drive** to the main Langata Road gate, traffic permitting. Many hotels arrange a morning game drive that has you back for lunch. Some hotels on the Mombasa Road side, such as Ole Sereni, actually overlook the park itself.

**Is Nairobi a good base for a safari?**
It is **the gateway to Kenya's entire safari circuit**. From Nairobi you fly or drive to the Maasai Mara, Amboseli with its Kilimanjaro views, Tsavo East and West, Samburu and the Rift Valley lakes, and there are frequent onward flights to Mombasa and Diani for the coast. Almost every Kenya itinerary starts here.

**When is the Great Migration in the Maasai Mara?**
Roughly **July to October**, when the wildebeest and zebra herds cross from the Serengeti into the Mara. Exact timing shifts each year with the rains, so treat those months as a window rather than a schedule. Book Mara camps and flights well ahead for that period.

**What are the must-do things in Nairobi?**
A game drive in Nairobi National Park, the David Sheldrick Wildlife Trust elephant orphanage, hand-feeding Rothschild's giraffes at the Giraffe Centre in Langata, the Karen Blixen Museum, the Nairobi National Museum, a walk in Karura Forest, the Bomas of Kenya for traditional dance, and the Maasai Market for crafts.

**Can you feed giraffes in Nairobi?**
Yes — the **Giraffe Centre** in Langata lets you hand-feed **endangered Rothschild's giraffes** from a raised platform. It is the most photographed experience in the city and works especially well with children. Go early to beat the coach groups.

**What is the David Sheldrick Wildlife Trust?**
An **elephant orphanage** on the edge of Nairobi National Park that rescues and hand-raises orphaned calves before rewilding them in Tsavo. It opens to visitors for a short window each day, and fostering an orphan gets you a quieter evening visit. Check current visiting arrangements and booking rules before you go — they change.

**Do they drive on the left in Kenya?**
Yes, **Kenya drives on the left**, like the UK. That said, most visitors do not self-drive in Nairobi — the traffic, matatu minibuses and unfamiliar junctions make Uber, Bolt or a hired driver much easier, and a driver costs very little by UK standards.

**Is tipping expected in Kenya?**
Tipping is customary for safari guides, drivers, porters and restaurant staff, and forms a real part of income in the tourism economy. Small shilling notes are useful, and US dollars are fine for safari guides. Some restaurants add a service charge — check the bill before adding more.
