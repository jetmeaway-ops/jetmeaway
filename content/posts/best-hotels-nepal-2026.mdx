## Where to base yourself in Nepal

Nearly every bookable hotel in Nepal clusters in two places — Kathmandu and Pokhara — with the Nagarkot ridge as a scenic add-on. Here's how to choose.

- **Kathmandu** — the capital and cultural heart, and where most of this guide's hotels sit. Base in or near **Thamel**, the traveller quarter packed with hotels, restaurants, gear shops and tour agencies. From here you can reach **Kathmandu Durbar Square**, the giant **Boudhanath** stupa, the hilltop **Swayambhunath** (the 'Monkey Temple') and the sacred Hindu complex of **Pashupatinath**. Two to three nights covers the valley highlights.
- **Pokhara** — Nepal's relaxed lakeside resort town, about 6-8 hours by tourist bus or a 25-minute flight from Kathmandu. Stay by **Phewa Lake** for mountain reflections, the World Peace Pagoda on the ridge above, paragliding over the valley, and the main gateway to the **Annapurna** treks. A second cluster of good-value hotels makes it an easy pairing with the capital.
- **Nagarkot** — a cool hill station about an hour east of Kathmandu, famous for its **Himalayan sunrise** panorama on clear autumn and spring mornings. A quiet ridge of view hotels and a popular one-night escape, often combined with the medieval town of **Bhaktapur** on the way.
- **Day trips and onward** — **Bhaktapur** and **Patan** add two more UNESCO Durbar Squares within the valley; **Chitwan National Park** in the southern lowlands offers rhino and tiger safaris; and **Lumbini**, the birthplace of the Buddha, is a pilgrimage add-on further west. These make great day trips or onward stops, though bookable hotel choice is thinner than in Kathmandu and Pokhara.

**Do UK travellers need a visa for Nepal?**
UK visitors can get a visa on arrival at Kathmandu's Tribhuvan International Airport, or apply online in advance through the official Nepal Immigration site. You pay the fee in cash (major currencies accepted) or online, and tourist visas are issued for 15, 30 or 90 days. Bring a passport valid for at least six months and a passport photo. Always use the official government portal rather than a third-party reseller.

**What is the best time to visit Nepal?**
Autumn (October-November) and spring (March-April) give the clearest skies and the best Himalayan mountain views, and are the prime trekking seasons. The monsoon runs roughly June to September (green but cloudy), and winter (December-February) is cold at altitude but crisp and quiet in the valleys. For guaranteed mountain panoramas from Pokhara or Nagarkot, aim for October-November or March-April.

**Are there direct flights from the UK to Nepal?**
No — there are no direct flights from the UK to Kathmandu. The quickest routes are one-stop via the Gulf (Qatar Airways via Doha, Etihad via Abu Dhabi, Emirates via Dubai) or via Delhi and Istanbul. Total journey time is typically around 12-15 hours including the connection. All international flights land at Kathmandu's Tribhuvan International Airport (KTM).

**Which airport do you fly into for Nepal?**
Tribhuvan International Airport (KTM) in Kathmandu is the country's only international gateway. It sits about 6 km from the tourist hub of Thamel, roughly 20-40 minutes by taxi depending on traffic. Domestic flights from the same airport connect onward to Pokhara and mountain airstrips such as Lukla for the Everest region.

**Where should I base myself in Nepal?**
Most trips split between two bases, and that's where nearly all bookable hotels cluster. Kathmandu is the capital and cultural heart — Durbar Square, Thamel, and the great stupas of Boudhanath and Swayambhunath. Pokhara is the lakeside resort town with Annapurna views, paragliding and the main trekking gateway. The Nagarkot ridge, an hour from Kathmandu, adds a classic Himalayan sunrise viewpoint. Bhaktapur, Chitwan and Lumbini make great day trips or onward stops.

**Do I need a permit to trek in Nepal?**
Yes — most treks require a TIMS (Trekkers' Information Management System) card plus a national-park or conservation-area permit (for example the Annapurna Conservation Area Permit or Sagarmatha National Park permit for Everest). These are arranged in Kathmandu or Pokhara through the Nepal Tourism Board or a trekking agency. Some restricted areas also need a special permit and a licensed guide. Arrange permits before you set off on the trail.

**How do you get around Nepal?**
Within Kathmandu and Pokhara, taxis, app-based ride-hailing (Pathao, InDrive) and cheap local buses handle most trips. Between cities, tourist buses run the Kathmandu-Pokhara route (roughly 6-8 hours) or you can take a short domestic flight (about 25 minutes). Roads are winding and journeys take longer than the distance suggests, so build in buffer time and consider flying to save a day.

**Do I need to worry about altitude in Nepal?**
In Kathmandu (1,400 m), Pokhara (820 m) and Nagarkot (2,100 m) altitude is not a concern for most visitors. It only becomes an issue on high treks — above roughly 2,500-3,000 m, ascend gradually, stay hydrated, and know the signs of altitude sickness. The hotels in this guide are all at comfortable valley or hill elevations where acclimatisation is not needed.

**What should I wear at temples in Nepal?**
Nepal is a Hindu and Buddhist country, and temples and stupas expect modest dress: cover your shoulders and knees, remove shoes (and sometimes leather items) before entering shrines, and walk clockwise around stupas and prayer wheels. Some Hindu temples, such as the inner areas of Pashupatinath, are open to Hindus only. Ask before photographing people or ceremonies, and dress respectfully at Boudhanath and Swayambhunath.

**Is Nepal safe for tourists?**
Yes — Nepal is generally a safe and welcoming destination with a long tradition of hosting travellers and trekkers. Standard precautions apply: watch belongings in busy areas like Thamel, agree taxi fares or use an app, take care with road traffic, choose reputable trekking agencies and guides, and check current official travel advice before you go. Solo and female travellers widely report feeling comfortable.

**What is the budget per night for backpackers in Nepal?**
Backpackers can sleep well from around £16-£25 a night in a Kathmandu or Pokhara guesthouse, eat a plate of dal bhat for a couple of pounds, and travel by local bus for very little. It is one of the cheapest countries in Asia — a daily budget of £25-£40 covers comfortable travel for most, more if you add flights and trekking permits.

**What food should I try in Nepal?**
Start with dal bhat — the national dish of rice, lentil soup, vegetable curry and pickles, usually with unlimited refills. Try momos (steamed or fried dumplings), thukpa (Tibetan noodle soup), and Newari specialities in Kathmandu such as chatamari and bara. Fresh Himalayan trout appears around Pokhara, and masala chai is everywhere. Local eateries cost just a few pounds a meal.

**What currency is used in Nepal?**
The Nepalese rupee (NPR). We quote every price in this guide in pounds as a from-rate for easy comparison. Cards are accepted in larger hotels and restaurants, but carry cash for taxis, local cafes, markets, temple entry fees and trekking. ATMs are widely available in Kathmandu and Pokhara but scarce on the trails, so withdraw before you set off.

**How many days do you need in Nepal?**
Seven to ten days suits a first trip that combines Kathmandu Valley (two to three days for the Durbar Squares and stupas), Pokhara (two to three days by the lake and for short treks or paragliding) and a night at Nagarkot for the sunrise. Add a week or more if you plan a longer trek such as the Annapurna Base Camp or Everest region routes.

**Is Pokhara worth visiting?**
Yes — Pokhara is Nepal's relaxed lakeside resort town and the main gateway to the Annapurna treks. Phewa Lake reflects the mountains on clear mornings, the World Peace Pagoda sits on the ridge above, and it's the country's paragliding capital with tandem flights over the valley. Many travellers pair a few Pokhara days with their Kathmandu time.

**Why stay at Nagarkot?**
Nagarkot is a hill station about an hour east of Kathmandu, famous for its panoramic Himalayan sunrise — on clear autumn and spring mornings you can see a long sweep of peaks, occasionally as far as Everest. It's a cool, quiet ridge of view hotels, a popular one-night escape from the capital, and often paired with the medieval town of Bhaktapur on the way.

**What are the must-see sights in the Kathmandu Valley?**
The Kathmandu Valley packs in seven UNESCO World Heritage sites. Don't miss the three Durbar Squares (Kathmandu, Patan and Bhaktapur) with their palaces and temples, the giant Boudhanath stupa, the hilltop Swayambhunath (the 'Monkey Temple'), and the sacred Hindu complex of Pashupatinath on the Bagmati River. Two to three days lets you see the highlights at a comfortable pace.

**Can you see the Himalayas without trekking?**
Yes — you don't have to trek to see the mountains. Nagarkot and Pokhara both offer big Himalayan panoramas from your hotel on clear mornings, a short scenic 'mountain flight' from Kathmandu flies past Everest, and paragliding in Pokhara puts you in the air with the Annapurnas behind you. October-November and March-April give the clearest views.

**Is Nepal good for families?**
Yes — Nepal is friendly, affordable and varied for families: gentle valley sightseeing in Kathmandu, boat rides on Phewa Lake in Pokhara, wildlife safaris at Chitwan National Park, and cheap comfortable hotels with family rooms. Distances between the main bases are manageable, and locals are warm with children. Keep younger children off high-altitude treks.

**Do I need to book hotels in advance in Nepal?**
For the peak trekking seasons (October-November and March-April) and around major festivals such as Dashain and Tihar, booking the best-value Kathmandu and Pokhara hotels ahead is wise as they fill fast. Off-peak you have more flexibility, but reserving your first night near Thamel always makes arrival smoother. Every hotel here links to its live rate for your dates.

**What is Chitwan and is it worth adding?**
Chitwan National Park in Nepal's southern lowlands (the Terai) is the country's premier wildlife destination — home to one-horned rhinos, deer, crocodiles and, with luck, Bengal tigers. It's a jeep- or canoe-safari add-on, roughly a half-day drive from Kathmandu or Pokhara, and pairs well with a temple-and-mountains itinerary if you have a spare few days.
