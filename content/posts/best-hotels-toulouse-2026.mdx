## Where to Stay in Toulouse: Choosing Your Base

Toulouse is a big city, but its sights cluster in a compact, walkable hypercentre, so the choice of base is mostly about how central you want to be against how much you want to pay. Here is the honest breakdown.

**The hypercentre around Place du Capitole** is the right answer for most visitors. This is the heart of the pink-brick old town, and staying on or near it puts the Capitole and its opera house, the Basilica of Saint-Sernin, the Couvent des Jacobins, the smart Carmes and Saint-Georges quarters and the Garonne quays within a flat walk. It holds the single five-star, most of the four-star tier and the best of the central budget hotels — the Ours Blanc Wilson, Hôtel Le Capitole, Le Moderne by the Victor Hugo market. The trade is price, but by French-city standards Toulouse is affordable, so a central address costs less here than the equivalent in Paris or Bordeaux.

**The Matabiau station quarter**, on the Canal du Midi north-east of the centre, is the value-and-convenience sweet spot. It carries several of this guide's better-priced hotels and aparthotels — the Innes, Matabi, Riquet, the Odalys and ibis budget by the Gare — it is a ten-to-fifteen-minute walk or a couple of metro stops from Place du Capitole, and it puts you next to the trains for Carcassonne, Albi and beyond. It is a working quarter rather than a postcard, but it is central, on the metro and cheaper than the old town.

**Saint-Cyprien**, on the left bank across the Garonne, is the pick for a slightly more local, arty base a short walk from the centre — home to Les Abattoirs modern-art museum, a lively market and a growing food scene, with the Adagio aparthotel among the options, the river bridges into the hypercentre and the metro line A running through it. It tends to be a little cheaper and quieter than the Capitole side.

**Compans-Caffarelli and the Canal de Brienne**, on the northern edge, hold the business-and-congress district and a cluster of good-value aparthotels — the Odalys, Residhome Occitania, Hôtel de Brienne — in a quieter, greener, canal-side setting a metro stop from the centre. Handy for the congress centre and for a calmer base with a fast link to the Capitole.

**The outer districts** — **Purpan** and **Basso Cambo** to the west, the **airport** fringe, **Labège** to the south, the **Cité de l'Espace** area to the east and the **Ponts Jumeaux** to the north — are where the budget chains and aparthotels cluster, with rates from about £37 to £52. You trade a central address for a metro or tram ride, but Tisséo is fast and cheap (Basso Cambo is even the terminus of metro line A), so this is a genuine saving. Best for drivers, families wanting self-catering space, early flights and anyone whose priority is price over proximity.

## Beyond the Hotel: What to See in Toulouse

Start in the centre, because in Toulouse the centre is the show. **Place du Capitole** is the vast main square, one side taken up entirely by the arcaded façade of the **Capitole** — the city hall and the **Théâtre du Capitole** opera house — with a great bronze Occitan cross set into the paving and cafés spilling out under the arcades. A few minutes north stands the **Basilica of Saint-Sernin**, the largest Romanesque church in Europe, a masterpiece of pink brick and stone raised from the 11th century as a major stop on the pilgrim **routes to Santiago de Compostela** and inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage list for that role. Nearby, the **Couvent des Jacobins** holds the relics of Thomas Aquinas beneath one of the most beautiful vaults in Gothic architecture — the famous "palm tree" of ribs fanning across the ceiling of the apse.

The city is built of that pink terracotta brick throughout, which is why it is **La Ville Rose**, the Pink City, glowing warmest at sunset — walk the **Garonne** quays at golden hour, cross the 16th-century **Pont Neuf**, and look back at the dome of **La Grave** hospital on the far bank for the classic view. The **Musée des Augustins** (fine art in a former convent), the Renaissance mansion of the **Hôtel d'Assézat** with the Bemberg Foundation, and the striking **Halle de la Machine** with its giant walking mechanical Minotaur are all worth time, and the **Victor Hugo covered market** is the place to eat like a local.

Then there is the aerospace, which is Toulouse's modern identity and, for many visitors, the reason to come. This is the home of **Airbus**, and the city wears it proudly: the **Cité de l'espace** on the eastern edge is a large space-themed park with a full-size Ariane 5 rocket, planetarium shows and real spacecraft; the **Aeroscopia** museum beside the Blagnac factory holds a Concorde, an A380 and much of the history of European aviation, going right back to the **Aéropostale** mail pilots — Saint-Exupéry among them — who flew out of Toulouse in the 1920s. Guided **tours of the Airbus assembly lines** run through an official operator but must be **booked well ahead** and require photo ID; do not leave them to the day.

And use Toulouse as a base. **Carcassonne**, the great restored medieval walled city and a UNESCO site, is under an hour south-east by frequent direct train; **Albi**, the red-brick UNESCO Episcopal City with its fortress cathedral and the world's largest collection of Toulouse-Lautrec, is about an hour north-east. The **Canal du Midi** — Pierre-Paul Riquet's 17th-century engineering marvel, another UNESCO listing — runs right through the city and out towards both, and its plane-tree-shaded towpaths make lovely walking and cycling. If your trip has three or four days in it, one or two spent on these day trips is time very well used.

## Getting Around Toulouse

**Walk the centre.** The hypercentre is compact, flat and, in the pink-brick old streets around the Capitole, largely given over to pedestrians, so the Capitole, Saint-Sernin, the Jacobins, the Carmes, Saint-Georges and the Garonne quays are all within a ten-to-fifteen-minute walk of each other. For a two-day city break you will barely need anything else.

**Take the Tisséo network for the rest.** Toulouse has a modern, fully automatic **metro** — two driverless lines, A and B, crossing at **Jean Jaurès** — plus a **tram** network (including the **T2 to the airport**) and a dense bus system, all on the same cheap ticket. Buy a single or a day pass from the machine at any stop and validate it as you board. It is fast, clean and easy, and it reaches every outer hotel in this guide, from Basso Cambo in the west to Jolimont in the east.

**Consider a bike.** Toulouse is flat, has an expanding network of cycle lanes and the **VélôToulouse** bike-share with hire points across the city, and the towpaths along the **Canal du Midi** and the **Garonne** quays make for genuinely lovely riding away from traffic. It is a pleasant way to link the centre, Saint-Cyprien and the canal without waiting for a bus; just keep to walking pace, or on foot, in the busiest pedestrian streets.

A few practicalities. The currency is the **euro**; cards and contactless work almost everywhere, including on the metro and tram machines, so you need little cash. **Tap water is safe to drink**, and a restaurant will bring a free carafe if you ask. **Tipping** is a rounded-up bonus, not an obligation — service is included by law. And if your trip falls on a big **rugby weekend** at the Stadium or a **trade-fair week** at the congress centre, book your room early, because those are the dates that tighten supply and lift prices.

**Do UK passport holders need a visa for Toulouse?**
No visa for tourism. UK passport holders can visit France and the rest of the Schengen area visa-free for up to 90 days in any 180-day period. **What are EES and ETIAS, and do they affect my trip?**
EES is the EU Entry/Exit System, a biometric border check that records a facial image and fingerprints instead of stamping your passport, and it has been rolling out in stages across Schengen external borders — expect a slightly slower first crossing while you are registered. ETIAS is a separate pre-travel authorisation that UK visitors will need to apply for online for a fee once it starts, and its launch date has been pushed back more than once. Because both timelines keep moving, check the UK government France travel advice page for the position on your own travel dates rather than trusting an older article.

**What currency does Toulouse use?**
The euro. Cards and contactless are accepted almost everywhere — hotels, restaurants, shops, the metro and tram ticket machines — and there is rarely any need for much cash. **Can you drink the tap water in Toulouse?**
