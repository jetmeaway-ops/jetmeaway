## The Scout's Take: Beach Strip, Lagoon Side, or Just Airport-Convenient?

Negombo's hotels split roughly three ways, and the right one depends on your trip. The **main beach strip** holds most of the resorts and mid-range hotels — sand on your doorstep, seafood restaurants along the road, and sunset over the Indian Ocean. The **lagoon side** is quieter and more scenic in a different way, with a couple of luxury reserves built around the water. And plenty of travellers simply want **airport-convenient** — a comfortable, well-priced bed 15 minutes from CMB for one night either side of the trip, with a shuttle laid on.

## What to See in Negombo

Negombo is the airport-side beach town, and it earns a day or two in its own right:

- **Negombo Beach.** A long, wide sandy beach running along the main strip — pleasant for walking and relaxing, and calmest in the December-to-March dry season. The run of seafood restaurants along the beach road is part of the appeal.
- **The Fish Market (Lellama) and Lagoon.** Negombo's famous morning fish market is a working, chaotic, photogenic scene, and the Negombo Lagoon behind it is dotted with traditional outrigger boats. Go early for the market at its busiest.
- **Dutch Canal Boat Rides.** The old Dutch-era canal system that once carried cinnamon and spices still threads through town — a boat ride along the canal and lagoon is the classic Negombo half-day, gliding past mangroves, birdlife and fishing communities.
- **Angurukaramulla Temple.** A Buddhist temple known for its large reclining Buddha and painted interior — the town's main cultural sight, alongside Negombo's Portuguese and Dutch colonial churches that give it the nickname "Little Rome."

Above all, Negombo's role is **the ideal first or last night**: it's about 15 minutes from Bandaranaike International Airport (CMB), so you can land, reach the beach in a quarter of an hour, and sleep off the flight — then do the same in reverse before departure, instead of a long transfer to or from Colombo.

## Negombo Hotels FAQs

**Where is Negombo and why do so many Sri Lanka trips start here?**
Negombo is a relaxed fishing-and-beach town on Sri Lanka's west coast, roughly 15 minutes by road from Bandaranaike International Airport (CMB) near Colombo. Because it's so close to the airport — far closer than Colombo city centre in traffic — it's the natural first and last night of almost any Sri Lanka itinerary, letting you sleep off the flight by the beach rather than fighting into the capital.

**How far is Negombo from Colombo airport (CMB)?**
About 15 minutes by taxi or tuk-tuk in normal traffic — Negombo sits just north of Bandaranaike International Airport (CMB), which is itself closer to Negombo than to Colombo city. Many Negombo hotels offer a free or low-cost airport shuttle, which is the single best reason to base your arrival and departure nights here.

**Do UK travellers need a visa for Sri Lanka in 2026?**
Yes — UK passport holders need an Electronic Travel Authorization (ETA) before arrival. Apply through the official Sri Lankan government ETA portal ahead of travel; it's quick and inexpensive. The Sri Lankan Rupee (LKR). The pound prices in this guide are rough conversions to help you compare — the actual rate you pay depends on the day's exchange rate. **When is the best time to visit Negombo?**
December to March is the west coast's dry season and the most reliable window for beach weather in Negombo. This is peak season, so book earlier. The inter-monsoon shoulders can still be pleasant, but the May-to-September southwest monsoon brings the wettest, roughest conditions to this coast.

**Is Negombo beach good for swimming?**
Negombo has a long, wide sandy beach that's pleasant for walking and relaxing, and calmer in the December-to-March dry season. As with any open Indian Ocean beach, check local conditions and any flag warnings before swimming, as currents vary by day and season.

For most travellers, Negombo — it's much closer to the airport in real traffic terms, it's on the beach, and it's more relaxed after a long flight. Choose Colombo only if you specifically need to be in the capital for business or city sightseeing on day one.

The morning fish market (Lellama) is the big draw, along with boat rides on the Dutch-era canal and Negombo Lagoon, the Angurukaramulla Temple, and the town's Portuguese and Dutch colonial heritage. It's a working fishing town, so the harbour and outrigger boats are part of the everyday scene.
