## The Scout's Take: Which Dublin Are You Staying In?

Dublin is tiny by capital-city standards — you can walk the entire Georgian core in fifteen minutes — so neighbourhood choice is more about atmosphere and budget than about access.

**South City / Georgian Dublin** (Fitzwilliam Square, Merrion Square, St Stephen's Green, Ballsbridge) is Dublin at its most architecturally complete — red-brick Georgian terraces, colourful front doors, iron railings, garden squares. This is where nearly the entire luxury tier sits, and it's the most walkable base for Trinity College, the National Gallery and Grafton Street.

**Temple Bar / City Centre** (the cobbled lanes around Wellington Quay, and the O'Connell Street/Parnell Street grid north of the river) is the tourist-dense, pub-heavy heart of the city — worth an evening out, and home to most of the easyHotel/Blooms/Point A budget cluster that puts you a five-minute walk from the action.

**Docklands** (Grand Canal Square, Point Village) is contemporary Dublin — the tech quarter, the 3Arena, Daniel Libeskind's glass-and-steel Grand Canal Theatre district, and this guide's Marker/Gibson/Premier Inn cluster.

**Smithfield / Northside** (the cobbled market square near the Guinness Storehouse) mixes café culture with the Maldron and Hendrick budget hotels, five minutes from the Storehouse and on the Luas tram line.

**Southside residential** (Sandymount, Ballsbridge, Grand Canal) trades city-centre buzz for quiet streets, DART rail access and, in Ballsbridge's case, most of the InterContinental/Leinster/Dylan luxury cluster near the Aviva Stadium.

For first Dublin: the Georgian Quarter. For nightlife-first trips: Temple Bar's edge (not its centre). For concerts and business: the Docklands. For tight budgets: this guide's [budget tier](#budget-hotels), concentrated in South City student-aparthotels and the Smithfield/Docklands fringe.

## UK Practicalities

- **Direct UK flights**: Ryanair, Aer Lingus and easyJet fly from London Luton, Stansted and Gatwick, Manchester, Birmingham and Edinburgh, among others — flight times of roughly 1-1.5 hours. [Search UK flights to Dublin (DUB)](/flights?to=DUB).
- **Airport transfer**: Aircoach to O'Connell Street, about 40 minutes, around €12. Taxi €25-35, 20-30 minutes. No rail link from the airport.
- **Passport / ID**: The UK and Ireland share the Common Travel Area, so a passport isn't strictly required by law, but every airline demands photo ID and most insist on a passport at check-in regardless.
- **Currency**: Euro (EUR) — different from Northern Ireland's pound sterling, despite sharing the island.
- **Tourism tax**: None. Ireland doesn't levy a hotel city tax, unlike Prague, Barcelona or Berlin.
- **Tipping**: Optional but common — 10-15% in restaurants for good service, rounding up is fine in pubs.
- **Best months**: May-September, with June-July the sweet spot (18-22°C, long evenings). St Patrick's Festival (17 March) books out the whole city six months ahead.
- **Budget**: Luxury trip — £250-500+/night all-in. Budget-tier trip — £103-190/night all-in, roughly €40-50 (£34-43) a day for food, a pub pint and one paid attraction on top.

## Booking Dublin Hotels in 2026: Rates and the Dates to Avoid

Dublin room rates swing hard by date, sometimes more than by hotel — the same budget room can move £40-60 between a quiet Tuesday in February and a Saturday in July. The cheapest stretch of the year runs November through March, excluding the Christmas/New Year fortnight, when even the Georgian luxury hotels drop well below their summer rates. Two recurring dates spike the whole city and are worth planning around rather than into:

- **St Patrick's Festival, around 17 March** — the single biggest date on the Irish calendar. The entire city books out months ahead and prices climb hardest of the year, even at the budget end.
- **Major Aviva Stadium and Croke Park fixtures** — international rugby weekends, GAA finals and large concerts can sell out the Ballsbridge and Docklands hotel clusters specifically, even when the rest of the city has rooms.

Unlike Las Vegas's resort fee or Prague's per-night city tax, Dublin's headline price is genuinely the number you pay — there's no hidden line item to budget around. The variable that matters here is simply the calendar: book midweek and outside St Patrick's week, and this guide's £103-£190 budget tier holds.
