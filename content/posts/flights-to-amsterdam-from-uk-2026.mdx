## Quick answers

**What's the cheapest airline to Amsterdam from the UK?** On headline price, **easyJet** — off-peak returns are frequently £40-£70 from its budget-airport bases. Once you add a checked bag, **KLM** (which includes 23kg on most fares) closes much of the gap, so compare the all-in price, not just the sticker fare.

**How long is the flight?** About **1 hour 10 to 1 hour 20 minutes** from London, and roughly an hour from northern UK airports. It's one of the shortest international routes you can fly from Britain.

**Which UK airport is cheapest?** Usually **Luton, Gatwick or Southend** on easyJet — but KLM's wide regional network means flying direct from your local airport (Newcastle, Leeds Bradford, Norwich, Aberdeen, Cardiff, Bristol and more) often beats a cheap London fare once you count the cost of getting to London.

**When should I book?** For most months, **4-8 weeks ahead**. For King's Day, tulip season, summer and New Year, **8-12 weeks ahead**.

**Do I need a visa?** No — UK passport holders get **90 days visa-free** in the Schengen area. Check ETIAS status before you travel, as the scheme is rolling out across 2026.

## Airline-by-airline comparison (2026)

Three airlines dominate the UK-Amsterdam route, and they compete in genuinely different ways — one on raw price, one on network reach and included baggage, one on airport convenience.

### easyJet — the price leader

**Typical fare 2026:** £40-£70 return off-peak, £90-£160 at peak, hand-luggage only.
**UK airports:** London Gatwick, Luton and Southend, plus Manchester, Bristol, Liverpool, Glasgow, Edinburgh, Belfast and Newcastle.
**Pros:** Consistently the lowest headline fare on the route. Frequent daily flights from its big London bases. Perfect for a hand-luggage-only weekend, where the low fare stays low. Seat sales appear regularly, especially in January and after the summer.
**Cons:** A hold bag is a paid add-on (£25-£50 each way), so the "cheapest fare" can climb once you need luggage. Allocated seating and speedy boarding are extra. No included food or drink.

### KLM — the network and baggage choice

**Typical fare 2026:** £70-£130 return on standard fares (most include a 23kg checked bag), more at peak or on flexible fares.
**UK airports:** Around seventeen — including Heathrow, London City, Birmingham, Manchester, Newcastle, Leeds Bradford, Humberside, Norwich, Aberdeen, Edinburgh, Glasgow, Bristol and Cardiff. This is the widest UK regional coverage of any carrier on the route by a large margin.
**Pros:** Flies from more UK airports than anyone else, so most travellers have a direct service from a local airport. Most fares include a checked bag, a hot drink and a snack. Schiphol is KLM's home hub, so connections onward are seamless if Amsterdam is a stopover rather than your destination.
**Cons:** Higher headline fare than easyJet. The cheapest "Light" fares are now hand-luggage only too — check the fare type before assuming a bag is included.

### British Airways — the convenience option

**Typical fare 2026:** £75-£140 return, cheapest "Basic" fares are cabin-bag only.
**UK airports:** London Heathrow and London City.
**Pros:** London City is fast through security and close to the City and Canary Wharf — ideal for business travellers. Avios earning and BA lounge access on eligible fares. A more central London departure than the budget airports.
**Cons:** Only serves two London airports, so no help for regional travellers. Cheapest fares are hand-luggage only, like easyJet, but usually cost more.

**The honest read:** if you're travelling hand-luggage only from the South East, easyJet almost always wins on price. If you need a checked bag, live outside London, or want the simplest single-airport experience, KLM's fare is often better value than it first looks once the bag is added. British Airways is worth the premium mainly for London City's speed and for Avios collectors.

## Fly or take the Eurostar train?

Amsterdam is one of the few European capitals where the train is a genuine alternative to flying for UK travellers, thanks to the direct Eurostar service.

**The Eurostar direct** runs from **London St Pancras to Amsterdam Centraal in about 4 hours**, city centre to city centre, with a generous luggage allowance and no baggage fees. For Londoners, once you count the time and cost of getting to an airport, checking in, security, and the train from Schiphol into the city, the door-to-door difference versus flying is smaller than it looks — and the train drops you straight in the centre.

**When the train wins:**
- You're travelling from London or the South East.
- You want city-centre-to-city-centre with no airport hassle.
- You're carrying more luggage than a budget-airline hand bag allows and want to avoid checked-bag fees.
- You value the lower carbon footprint of rail over a short-haul flight.

**When flying wins:**
- You're travelling from anywhere outside the South East — a direct KLM or easyJet flight from your local airport beats trekking to London for the train.
- You want the outright cheapest option — an off-peak easyJet fare is usually cheaper than the equivalent train ticket.
- You're on a tight time budget from a northern UK city, where flying is both faster and cheaper.

Compare both for London departures; default to flying everywhere else. The Dutch railway operator [NS](https://www.ns.nl/en) runs the Schiphol-to-city trains at the flying end either way.

## Baggage compared, honestly

On a short-haul route this cheap, baggage is often where the real price difference lives — a "cheap" fare can quietly double once a hold bag is added.

| Airline | Cabin bag | Checked bag | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| easyJet | 1 small under-seat bag free; larger cabin bag needs Plus/Up Front seat | Paid add-on, ~£25-£50 each way | Hand-luggage only by default — the key to keeping the fare low |
| KLM | Cabin bag + personal item on most fares | 23kg included on standard fares; "Light" fares are cabin-only | Check the fare type; standard fares are strong value with a bag |
| British Airways | Cabin bag + personal item | Included on standard fares; "Basic" fares are cabin-only | London City and Heathrow only |

**Two rules that save the most money.** First, **add any hold bag at the time of booking, never at the airport** — every carrier charges far more at the bag drop than online, sometimes double. Second, **compare the all-in price with baggage included**, not the headline fare: an easyJet fare at £50 plus a £40 return hold bag is £90, which can land above a KLM fare that already includes 23kg. For a hand-luggage-only weekend, easyJet is almost unbeatable; the moment you need the hold, re-run the comparison.

## The booking windows that actually save money

Short-haul European routes behave differently from long-haul — the booking curve is shorter and there's less benefit to booking many months out except at peak.

- **Off-peak months (Jan, Feb, late Sep, Oct, Nov):** Book **4-8 weeks out**. Booking much earlier rarely helps on these dates, and easyJet seat sales in this window can drop fares further.
- **Summer (Jul-Aug) and Christmas/New Year:** Book **8-12 weeks out**. These dates have fixed, predictable demand and fares only rise as they approach.
- **King's Day (27 April) and tulip season (late Mar-mid May):** Book **8-12 weeks out** — Amsterdam's single biggest demand spike of the year. Accommodation and flights both surge, so lock in early.
- **Weekend breaks:** Friday-evening-out, Sunday-evening-back is the most-demanded pattern and therefore the priciest. Shifting to a Thursday or Saturday departure, or an early-morning outbound, often saves £20-£50.

Set a price alert for your target dates rather than checking sporadically — on a competitive route like this, fares move often, and easyJet's seat sales in particular can appear and disappear within days.

## Entry rules for UK travellers in 2026

UK passport holders visiting the Netherlands need:

- **No visa** for stays up to **90 days in any 180-day period** across the Schengen area.
- A **passport issued within the last 10 years** and **valid for at least 3 months** after your planned departure date from the Schengen area. Both conditions are enforced at the border — an older passport still showing plenty of validity can be refused if it was issued more than 10 years ago.
- **ETIAS travel authorisation** once the scheme launches — it is rolling out across 2026, so check the current status before you travel. When live it's a quick online application with a small fee, valid for multiple trips.
- UK travellers use the **e-gates** at Schiphol, which the Netherlands has kept open to British passport holders, keeping arrival fast.

Always check the [UK government's Netherlands travel advice](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/netherlands) shortly before you fly, as entry requirements and ETIAS timing can change.

## Schiphol to central Amsterdam

This is one of the best airport transfers in Europe and worth knowing before you land. A **direct train from Schiphol to Amsterdam Centraal takes 15-20 minutes** and costs around **€5-6**, running several times an hour from the station directly beneath the terminal. From Centraal, most canal-ring hotels are a short tram ride or a 10-20 minute walk.

A taxi to the centre costs €40-50 and can be slower in traffic, so the train is both faster and far cheaper for almost everyone. If you're staying near Sloterdijk, Zuid or Amstel stations, check whether a direct train there saves you a change at Centraal. ## Common mistakes UK-Amsterdam travellers make

1. **Judging airlines on the headline fare alone.** easyJet's £50 fare plus a £40 hold bag can cost more than KLM's £90 fare that already includes 23kg. Always compare all-in.
2. **Defaulting to a London airport.** Regional travellers routinely overpay by flying via London when a direct KLM flight from Newcastle, Leeds, Aberdeen, Cardiff or Bristol is cheaper once the London trek is priced in.
3. **Booking travel over King's Day or tulip season without realising.** Late April and early May carry Amsterdam's biggest price spikes — check the dates before you commit.
4. **Adding baggage at the airport.** Every carrier charges far more at bag drop than online. Add it at booking.
5. **Ignoring the Eurostar for London trips.** For Londoners with luggage, the direct train city-centre-to-city-centre can beat flying on total convenience even at a similar price.
6. **Assuming an old passport is fine.** The Schengen 10-year issue rule catches UK travellers out — a passport issued more than 10 years ago can be refused even with months of validity left.
7. **Flying with a checked bag for a two-night trip.** Amsterdam is a hand-luggage city break — travelling cabin-only on easyJet keeps the fare low and skips bag reclaim at both ends.
