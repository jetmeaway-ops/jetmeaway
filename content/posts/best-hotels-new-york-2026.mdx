## The Scout's Take: Where to Actually Stay in New York

New York's hotel market is overwhelmingly a **Manhattan story**. Nearly every bookable hotel a first-time visitor will consider — luxury, mid-range or budget — sits on the island itself, packed into a handful of neighbourhoods: **Midtown and Times Square** (the theatre district and tourist core), the **Upper West and Upper East Sides** (flanking Central Park), **SoHo and the Financial District** (downtown, closer to the 9/11 Memorial and Wall Street), and **Chelsea** (between Midtown and downtown, near the High Line). This guide's 49 hotels sit across all of them.

**Midtown and Times Square** put you within walking distance of Broadway, the Empire State Building, and Penn Station — useful both for general sightseeing and, this year, for the World Cup shuttle to MetLife Stadium. **The Upper West Side** is the classic Central Park base — quieter, more residential, with the American Museum of Natural History and Lincoln Center on the doorstep. **SoHo and the Financial District (FiDi)** suit travellers who want downtown character, the 9/11 Memorial, and fast PATH/ferry links to New Jersey. **Chelsea** splits the difference, with the High Line and Chelsea Market as its calling cards.

Outside Manhattan, **Brooklyn** (Williamsburg, Downtown Brooklyn) is a single subway stop from Lower Manhattan and typically the value play for travellers who've done Manhattan before, while **Queens** (Long Island City) offers skyline views at a discount and the fastest run to LaGuardia. Neither borough currently has live-bookable inventory deep enough for this guide's 49-hotel list, so treat them as a strong next-trip option — see our [UK Practicalities](#uk-practicalities) section for how to reach both by subway.

For first-time visitors: Midtown or the Upper West Side. For downtown character: SoHo or FiDi. For the tightest World Cup budget and match-day connection: this guide's [budget tier](#budget-hotels), all still inside Manhattan's subway network.

## Going to the 2026 World Cup in New York / New Jersey?

This is the big one. New York/New Jersey is the marquee host city of the 2026 tournament: it stages the **World Cup final on 19 July 2026** and hosts **England vs Panama on 27 June 2026**. The venue for both is New York New Jersey Stadium — everyday name: MetLife Stadium — in East Rutherford, New Jersey, a few miles west of Manhattan across the Hudson at the Meadowlands Sports Complex.

The single most important thing to understand is geography. The stadium is in New Jersey, not in Manhattan, so the "perfect" base depends on what you want from the trip. For pure match-day logistics, the New Jersey waterfront (Jersey City and Hoboken) is closest to the stadium and cheaper than Manhattan, with direct rail — though this guide's 49 live-bookable hotels are concentrated on the Manhattan side, where the bulk of accommodation supply and the fuller New York experience sit. For the full New York experience either side of the football, Midtown Manhattan near Penn Station is the strongest compromise, because Penn Station is where the dedicated match-day NJ Transit service to the stadium departs.

**How to reach the ground on match day:** NJ Transit runs special event trains from **New York Penn Station** and **Hoboken Terminal** to **Secaucus Junction**, where you change onto the **Meadowlands Rail Line** that delivers you right at the stadium. Budget 60-75 minutes door-to-door from Midtown and set off at least three hours before kick-off — security, bag checks and crowds run heavy for a final. Driving is possible from the New Jersey side, but stadium parking sells out and the New Jersey Turnpike chokes on event days, so rail is far more reliable.

## Beyond the Hotel — New York's Essentials

A few non-football experiences worth planning around your stay:

- **Top of the Rock or Empire State Building at sunset** — both observation decks give the classic Manhattan skyline shot; Top of the Rock includes the Empire State Building in its view, which the Empire State's own deck obviously doesn't.
- **The 9/11 Memorial and One World Observatory** — the twin reflecting pools are free and deeply moving; the Observatory deck 1,776 feet up is downtown's answer to the Empire State Building.
- **Central Park by bike or on foot** — 843 acres in the middle of Manhattan; rent a bike near Columbus Circle and ride the full loop in under two hours.
- **Broadway at TKTS, Duffy Square** — same-day discount tickets (20-50% off) for dozens of shows if your evening is flexible.
- **The High Line** — a 1.45-mile elevated park built on a former freight rail line, running from Chelsea's Meatpacking District up to Hudson Yards.
- **Brooklyn Bridge walk at golden hour** — 20-30 minutes on foot from the Manhattan side, with the best skyline photo angle in the city from the Brooklyn Bridge Park side.
- **Chinatown and the Lower East Side for food** — the cheapest, most authentic eating in Manhattan, a world away from Midtown's tourist-priced menus.
- **The Metropolitan Museum of Art, Upper East Side** — one of the world's great museums, pay-what-you-wish for NY State residents but a fixed fee for visitors; budget half a day minimum.

## UK Practicalities

- **Direct UK flights**: British Airways, Virgin Atlantic and Norse Atlantic fly LHR/LGW-JFK/EWR direct in 7-8 hours. JetBlue and Delta operate transatlantic joint-venture routes from Heathrow, and United flies Heathrow-Newark direct. [Search flights to JFK](/flights?to=JFK).
- **Airports**: JFK is the main gateway for direct UK flights (AirTrain + subway/LIRR, 45-60 minutes to Manhattan). LaGuardia (LGA) is closest to the city but has minimal transatlantic service. Newark (EWR) has the fastest rail link into Penn Station (25-30 minutes) and suits the New Jersey/stadium side of a trip best.
- **Visa**: ESTA required (esta.cbp.dhs.gov, $21, usually instant, valid 2 years).
- **Currency**: US dollar (USD). New York is close to cashless — tap-to-pay works on the subway directly. **Hotel tax**: roughly 14.75% plus $3.50 per room, per night, on top of the quoted rate — always check the all-in total.
- **Getting to Brooklyn/Queens**: both boroughs are a single subway ride from Manhattan — the L train reaches Williamsburg from Union Square in about 15 minutes; the 7 or E/M trains reach Long Island City from Midtown in 10-15 minutes.
- **Tipping**: 18-20% restaurants, $1-2 per drink, $2-5 per bag, $2-5 per night housekeeping.
- **Best months**: April-June and September-November (12-24°C, fewer crowds, lower rates). June-August is hot, humid, and — in 2026 — overlaps the World Cup, the year's most expensive window. December-February is coldest and cheapest outside the holidays.
- **Budget**: Manhattan luxury trip — £400-800+/night all-in during the 2026 World Cup window; budget-tier trip — £160-460/night all-in on the same dates, likely lower outside June-August. Compare your actual travel dates — this guide's prices reflect a peak-demand window, not a typical one.
