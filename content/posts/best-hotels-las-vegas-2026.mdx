## The Scout's Take: The Strip, Downtown, or Off-Strip?

The **Las Vegas Strip** is a 4.2-mile section of South Las Vegas Boulevard. Everything you've seen in a Vegas movie — the Bellagio fountains, the Eiffel Tower replica, the giant pyramid, the Venetian canals — is here. Strip hotels charge a daily resort fee ($35–65 per night on top of the room rate) that covers amenities you may or may not use. The rooms are deliberately designed to disorient you — no clocks, blackout curtains, layouts that require passing the casino floor to reach any amenity. The Strip works if you accept the premise.

**Off-Strip hotels** (Virgin, Hard Rock, the Palms, the Rio) offer similar facilities without the Strip price premium but require a taxi or rideshare ($12–20) for most activities.

**Downtown Fremont Street** is the original Las Vegas — the Glitter Gulch that existed before the Strip was built. A 1,500-foot pedestrian canopy with the largest LED screen in the world running shows on the hour. Smaller casinos, cheaper gambling, the vintage Vegas energy. 20 minutes from the Strip by rideshare. Most UK travellers who fly home thinking Vegas was too much have never been here.

For first Vegas: Center Strip (Bellagio, Caesars, Park MGM). For repeat visitors who've done the Strip: Downtown or off-Strip. For couples who hate resort fees: Virgin Hotels.

The Lake of Dreams nighttime show, the Encore Beach Club pool (the most in-demand pool experience in Vegas on summer weekends), Lakeside and SW Steakhouse restaurants.

The Grand Canal Shoppes with working gondolas, St Mark's Square recreation, Canyon Ranch Spa (the most serious spa on the Strip), and 11 pools across three levels.

The Garden of the Gods pool collection (six pools, Roman statuary, a city block in size — the most dramatic pool setting on the Strip), Hell's Kitchen by Gordon Ramsay, the Forum Shops.

## Beyond the Casino Floor — Las Vegas's Essentials

A few non-gambling experiences worth planning around your stay:

- **High Roller Observation Wheel at sunset** — 550-foot wheel on the LINQ promenade, 28 cabins. The Strip view from 550 feet as the neon begins to compete with the last light. Open-bar cabin upgrade entirely worth it.
- **Fremont Street Light Show at 9pm and 10pm** — the LED canopy runs shows on the hour. Stand in the middle of the street for the full overhead effect. Free.
- **Helicopter to the Grand Canyon** — Las Vegas is the closest major city to the South Rim (270 miles). Papillon and Maverick are the established operators. Book a week ahead in high season.
- **Cirque du Soleil "O" at the Bellagio** — 90-minute show on a 1.5-million-gallon stage that alternates between solid platform and water. B-seat tier on the sides gives the best view of the water effects.
- **The Mob Museum, Downtown** — the National Museum of Organized Crime in the former Federal Courthouse where mafia figures were tried. Genuinely educational, plus the basement speakeasy bar.
- **Red Rock Canyon at dawn** — 25 miles west, 2,000-foot sandstone cliffs in the Mojave Desert. The contrast between the Strip and Red Rock (30 min by rental car) is one of the most complete landscape transitions available anywhere.

## UK Practicalities

- **Direct UK flights**: Virgin Atlantic LHR-LAS direct, 10 hours. BA via LAX or Phoenix. TUI and seasonal charter from regional airports.
- **Airport**: Harry Reid International is directly on the Strip — Mandalay Bay and Luxor are visible from the terminal. Rideshare to Bellagio takes 8 minutes, $12. The easiest airport-hotel transfer of any major city.
- **Visa**: ESTA required (esta.cbp.dhs.gov, $21, instant, valid 2 years).
- **Currency**: USD. **Resort fees** are the hidden cost — add $35–65 per night to every Strip room rate. Tipping: 18–20% restaurants, $1–2 per drink at bars, $2–5 per bag, $5–10 per night for housekeeping.
- **Best months**: March–May and September–November (22–28°C, low humidity, pool season without extreme heat). June–August is 40–45°C — the pool is the only outdoor option by noon. December–February is cool, the cheapest rates.
- **Budget**: £150–300/night total hotel cost (room + resort fee), £60–100/day food and drinks, £80–200 for shows. A 4-night trip is typically £800–1,500 per person all-in, excluding gambling losses.
