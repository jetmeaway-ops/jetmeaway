## The Scout's Take: The Strip, Downtown, or Off-Strip?

The **Las Vegas Strip** is a 4.2-mile section of South Las Vegas Boulevard. Everything you've seen in a Vegas movie — the Bellagio fountains, the Eiffel Tower replica, the giant pyramid, the Venetian canals — is here. Strip hotels charge a daily resort fee ($35–65 per night on top of the room rate) that covers amenities you may or may not use. The rooms are deliberately designed to disorient you — no clocks, blackout curtains, layouts that require passing the casino floor to reach any amenity. The Strip works if you accept the premise.

**Off-Strip hotels** (Virgin, Tuscany, Ellis Island, the Palms, the Rio) offer similar facilities without the Strip price premium but require a taxi or rideshare ($12–20) for most activities — or in several cases just a 10-minute walk.

**Downtown Fremont Street** is the original Las Vegas — the Glitter Gulch that existed before the Strip was built. A 1,500-foot pedestrian canopy with the largest LED screen in the world running shows on the hour. Smaller casinos, cheaper gambling, cheaper rooms, the vintage Vegas energy. 20 minutes from the Strip by rideshare. Most UK travellers who fly home thinking Vegas was too much have never been here.

For first Vegas: Center Strip. For repeat visitors: Downtown or off-Strip. For tight budgets: this guide's [budget tier](#budget-hotels) — Downtown and the locals' casinos are where £30 a night is still real.

### On the Strip, Downtown or Off-Strip — How to Choose

The **Strip** is the choreographed fantasy with the resort fee as the price of admission. **Downtown Fremont** is the original Vegas: cheaper rooms, cheaper gambling, the LED canopy, vintage energy, 20 minutes away. **Off-Strip and the locals' casinos** trade the boulevard walk for the lowest prices in the city and free parking. First Vegas, go Center Strip; repeat visitor or tight budget, Downtown; road-trippers, the locals' casinos.

## Beyond the Casino Floor — Las Vegas's Essentials

A few non-gambling experiences worth planning around your stay:

- **High Roller Observation Wheel at sunset** — 550-foot wheel on the LINQ promenade, 28 cabins. The Strip view from 550 feet as the neon begins to compete with the last light.
- **Fremont Street Light Show at 9pm and 10pm** — the LED canopy runs shows on the hour. Stand in the middle of the street for the full overhead effect. Free.
- **Helicopter to the Grand Canyon** — Las Vegas is the closest major city to the South Rim (270 miles). Papillon and Maverick are the established operators. Book a week ahead in high season.
- **Cirque du Soleil "O" at the Bellagio** — 90-minute show on a 1.5-million-gallon stage that alternates between solid platform and water.
- **The Mob Museum, Downtown** — the National Museum of Organized Crime in the former Federal Courthouse. Genuinely educational, plus the basement speakeasy bar — and it's next door to the budget tier's Downtown Grand.
- **Red Rock Canyon at dawn** — 25 miles west, 2,000-foot sandstone cliffs in the Mojave Desert. 30 minutes by rental car from the west-side locals' casinos.
- **Hoover Dam day trip** — 45 minutes east on Highway 93, the 1936 engineering monument across the Colorado River. Pairs naturally with a Boulder Highway hotel base.
- **The Peppermill Restaurant fire-pit lounge** — 2985 S Las Vegas Blvd, North Strip. The 24-hour coffee shop that has been Las Vegas's locals' breakfast since 1972 — purple velour booths, the sunken fire pit, portions sized for two. The most authentic vintage-Vegas meal still operating on the boulevard.
