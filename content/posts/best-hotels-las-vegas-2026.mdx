## The Scout's Take: The Strip, Downtown, or Off-Strip?

The **Las Vegas Strip** is a 4.2-mile section of South Las Vegas Boulevard. Everything you've seen in a Vegas movie — the Bellagio fountains, the Eiffel Tower replica, the giant pyramid, the Venetian canals — is here. Strip hotels charge a daily resort fee ($35–65 per night on top of the room rate) that covers amenities you may or may not use. The rooms are deliberately designed to disorient you — no clocks, blackout curtains, layouts that require passing the casino floor to reach any amenity. The Strip works if you accept the premise.

**Off-Strip hotels** (Virgin, Hard Rock, the Palms, the Rio) offer similar facilities without the Strip price premium but require a taxi or rideshare ($12–20) for most activities.

**Downtown Fremont Street** is the original Las Vegas — the Glitter Gulch that existed before the Strip was built. A 1,500-foot pedestrian canopy with the largest LED screen in the world running shows on the hour. Smaller casinos, cheaper gambling, the vintage Vegas energy. 20 minutes from the Strip by rideshare. Most UK travellers who fly home thinking Vegas was too much have never been here.

For first Vegas: Center Strip (Bellagio, Caesars, Park MGM). For repeat visitors who've done the Strip: Downtown or off-Strip. For couples who hate resort fees: Virgin Hotels.

### The Strip Icons

The eight properties on the 4.2-mile Strip — the boulevard's flagship hotels for first-time and repeat Vegas visitors. Each charges a daily resort fee but each delivers the choreographed-fantasy version of Las Vegas these hotels exist to sell.

The 8-acre lake with the choreographed fountains (free from the sidewalk, every 15–30 min from 3pm to midnight), the Conservatory and Botanical Gardens (changed 5 times yearly), and the highest concentration of Michelin-starred restaurants on the Strip.

**2. Wynn Las Vegas** — North Strip, 3131 S Las Vegas Blvd. Steve Wynn's post-Bellagio flagship — 2,716 rooms, the most consistently high-quality room product on the Strip. The Lake of Dreams nighttime show, the Encore Beach Club pool (the most in-demand pool experience in Vegas on summer weekends), Lakeside and SW Steakhouse restaurants.

<HotelPhoto hotelName="The Venetian Resort" city="Las Vegas" />

**3. The Venetian and Palazzo** — North Strip, 3355 S Las Vegas Blvd. The largest hotel complex in the world (7,117 suites — all rooms are suites, minimum 650 sq ft). The Grand Canal Shoppes with working gondolas, St Mark's Square recreation, Canyon Ranch Spa (the most serious spa on the Strip), and 11 pools across three levels.

<HotelPhoto hotelName="Caesars Palace" city="Las Vegas" />

**4. Caesars Palace** — Center Strip, 3570 S Las Vegas Blvd. 3,960 rooms across multiple towers — the original 1966 Vegas megaresort and still the most iconic Roman-themed property in the world. The Garden of the Gods pool collection (six pools, Roman statuary, a city block in size — the most dramatic pool setting on the Strip), Hell's Kitchen by Gordon Ramsay, the Forum Shops.

**5. Park MGM** — Center Strip, 3770 S Las Vegas Blvd. The former Monte Carlo, completely reinvented as a **smoke-free** hotel — the only casino-resort on the Strip where you can breathe freely throughout the property. **6. The Cromwell** — Center Strip, 3595 S Las Vegas Blvd. The only standalone boutique hotel on the Strip — 188 rooms, French-inspired design, Giada De Laurentiis restaurant (the first celebrity chef restaurant in Vegas headlined by a woman), and Drai's Rooftop Nightclub (the most architecturally distinctive rooftop on the Strip, with views the length of the boulevard).

**7. Nobu Hotel at Caesars Palace** — Center Strip. 181 rooms within Caesars that operate as a hotel-within-a-hotel with direct access to Nobu Restaurant. Japanese aesthetic in the Caesars complex — quieter than the main Caesars tower, with the Nobu pool as the amenity. For food-focused travellers.

Resorts World Las Vegas** — North Strip, 3000 S Las Vegas Blvd. The newest mega-resort on the Strip (opened 2021) — 3,506 rooms across three hotel brands (Hilton, Conrad, Crockfords) sharing one resort complex. ## Best Las Vegas Hotels for Specific Trips

Las Vegas hides its real costs and its real choices behind the neon. Here's how the 10 hotels above sort by traveller type, whether the priority is a pool you'll actually use, an all-suite room, a smoke-free floor, or a Strip stay without the resort fee.

### Best Las Vegas Hotels for Value (and the Resort-Fee Reality)

Every Strip hotel adds a resort fee of $35–65 a night on top of the room rate, so the real-value play is to sidestep it. **Virgin Hotels Las Vegas**, just off the Strip, charges **no resort fee** — the single biggest saving on this list, with a $12–15 rideshare to Center Strip. **Golden Nugget** Downtown on Fremont Street offers cheaper rooms and cheaper gambling in the vintage-Vegas Glitter Gulch, and **Park MGM** is the most reasonable Center Strip rate. Always budget the resort fee before you compare Strip prices.

### Best Las Vegas Hotels for Families With Kids

The biggest pool resorts win for families. **Resorts World** (opened 2021) has the largest pool complex on the Strip, **The Venetian** has 11 pools and all-suite rooms with space for a family, and **Golden Nugget** has the pool with a water slide running through a shark tank. **Caesars Palace's** Garden of the Gods — six pools across a city block — rounds out the family-friendly headliners.

### Best Las Vegas Hotels for Couples and Honeymoons

**The Cromwell** is the romantic outlier — the only standalone boutique hotel on the Strip, 188 rooms with Drai's Rooftop above. **The Waldorf Astoria** is the quiet luxury choice with no casino floor and a 27th-floor spa with the best Strip view in the city. **Wynn Las Vegas** delivers the most polished room product for a couple who want the full Strip experience done well.

### Best Las Vegas Hotels for Suites

**The Venetian and Palazzo** is the answer — the largest hotel complex in the world, and every one of its 7,117 rooms is a suite of at least 650 square feet, the most space-per-dollar on the Strip. **Nobu Hotel at Caesars Palace** is the boutique-suite alternative, a quieter hotel-within-a-hotel with direct access to the Nobu restaurant.

### Best Las Vegas Hotels for the Pool

Vegas is a pool city, and the pool is the main daytime event from spring to autumn. **Caesars Palace's** Garden of the Gods is the most dramatic — six pools with Roman statuary across a city block. **The Venetian** has 11 pools on three levels, **Resorts World** has the largest single complex, and **Wynn's** Encore Beach Club is the most in-demand pool party on summer weekends. Book a hotel whose pool you'll actually use — in July it's the only comfortable outdoor option by noon.

### Best 5-Star and Luxury Las Vegas Hotels (Bellagio, Wynn, Venetian, Waldorf Astoria)

The Strip's luxury tier delivers scale no Miami or LA hotel matches. The benchmark addresses are **Bellagio** (the definitive Strip experience — the fountains, the Michelin restaurants), **Wynn Las Vegas** (the most consistently polished rooms on the boulevard), **The Venetian** (all-suite, with the serious Canyon Ranch Spa) and the honorable-mention **Waldorf Astoria** (European-style city-hotel luxury with no casino). All are priced like a Beverly Hills flagship — for far more room.

### Best Smoke-Free and No-Casino Las Vegas Hotels

A real consideration most guides skip: the Strip's casino floors are smoky and hard to avoid in most hotels. **Park MGM** is the only fully smoke-free casino-resort on the Strip — you can breathe throughout the property. The **Waldorf Astoria** has no casino floor at all, the only Strip hotel where you reach the lift without crossing a slot machine. **Virgin Hotels**, off-Strip, keeps its casino separate from the tower. These three are the calm-air picks.

### On the Strip, Downtown or Off-Strip — How to Choose

The **Strip** (Bellagio, Wynn, Venetian, Caesars, Park MGM, Cromwell, Nobu, Resorts World) is the choreographed fantasy — the fountains, the canals, the Eiffel Tower replica — with the resort fee as the price of admission. **Downtown Fremont** (Golden Nugget) is the original Vegas: cheaper gambling, the LED-canopy light show, vintage energy, 20 minutes away. **Off-Strip** (Virgin Hotels) trades the boulevard walk for no resort fee and a quieter stay. First Vegas, go Center Strip; repeat visitor, try Downtown or off-Strip.

## How Las Vegas Compares to Miami and Los Angeles

Las Vegas sits cheaper than Los Angeles for comparable luxury and rough level with Miami — though the Strip resort fee narrows that gap considerably. A room at Bellagio or Wynn Las Vegas is priced like a Beverly Hills Hotel or Peninsula Beverly Hills in LA, or a Faena or Setai in Miami; a Cromwell or Park MGM sits closer to a mid-range West Hollywood boutique or a South Beach Art Deco hotel. The Strip's advantage is scale — no single Miami or LA hotel approaches a Venetian's 7,117 suites or a Caesars Palace's six-pool Garden of the Gods complex.

The three cities are the American luxury-resort triangle for UK travellers. Miami is beach-and-Latin (Art Deco South Beach, the Wynwood arts district, ocean drives). Los Angeles is film-industry-and-canyons (Beverly Hills, Malibu, the Hollywood Hills). Las Vegas is choreographed-fantasy-and-desert (the Strip, the Mojave, the Grand Canyon hour-by-helicopter). All three pair naturally — Vegas + LA is a 4-hour drive across the Mojave; Vegas + Miami is a one-stop flight and a complete climate change. UK luxury travellers increasingly book two-city American luxury trips combining one of each.

## Beyond the Casino Floor — Las Vegas's Essentials

A few non-gambling experiences worth planning around your stay:

- **High Roller Observation Wheel at sunset** — 550-foot wheel on the LINQ promenade, 28 cabins. The Strip view from 550 feet as the neon begins to compete with the last light. Open-bar cabin upgrade entirely worth it.
- **Fremont Street Light Show at 9pm and 10pm** — the LED canopy runs shows on the hour. Stand in the middle of the street for the full overhead effect. Free.
- **Helicopter to the Grand Canyon** — Las Vegas is the closest major city to the South Rim (270 miles). Papillon and Maverick are the established operators. Book a week ahead in high season.
- **Cirque du Soleil "O" at the Bellagio** — 90-minute show on a 1.5-million-gallon stage that alternates between solid platform and water. B-seat tier on the sides gives the best view of the water effects.
- **The Mob Museum, Downtown** — the National Museum of Organized Crime in the former Federal Courthouse where mafia figures were tried. Genuinely educational, plus the basement speakeasy bar.
- **Red Rock Canyon at dawn** — 25 miles west, 2,000-foot sandstone cliffs in the Mojave Desert. The contrast between the Strip and Red Rock (30 min by rental car) is one of the most complete landscape transitions available anywhere.
- **Hoover Dam day trip** — 45 minutes east on Highway 93, the 1936 Depression-era engineering monument across the Colorado River between Nevada and Arizona. Powerplant tour, the Mike O'Callaghan–Pat Tillman Memorial Bridge viewing platform (the highest concrete-arch bridge in the western hemisphere) and the Lake Mead reservoir behind. The closest American Southwest infrastructure landmark to the Strip and a natural pairing with a Grand Canyon helicopter day.
- **The Peppermill Restaurant fire-pit lounge — off-Strip locals' breakfast** — 2985 S Las Vegas Blvd, North Strip. The 24-hour coffee shop that has been Las Vegas's locals' breakfast since 1972 — purple velour booths, the Fireside Lounge with its sunken fire pit in a pool of water (predates every modern Strip resort, and looks it), portions sized for two, and the cocktail list that drinks at 8am alongside the eggs. The Peppermill is what Las Vegas was before the choreographed-fountain era — the version of the city the locals never let go of, and the most authentic vintage-Vegas meal still operating on the boulevard.

## UK Practicalities

- **Direct UK flights**: Virgin Atlantic LHR-LAS direct, 10 hours. BA via LAX or Phoenix. TUI and seasonal charter from regional airports.
- **Airport**: Harry Reid International is directly on the Strip — Mandalay Bay and Luxor are visible from the terminal. Rideshare to Bellagio takes 8 minutes, $12. The easiest airport-hotel transfer of any major city.
- **Visa**: ESTA required (esta.cbp.dhs.gov, $21, instant, valid 2 years).
- **Currency**: USD. **Resort fees** are the hidden cost — add $35–65 per night to every Strip room rate. Tipping: 18–20% restaurants, $1–2 per drink at bars, $2–5 per bag, $5–10 per night for housekeeping.
- **Best months**: March–May and September–November (22–28°C, low humidity, pool season without extreme heat). June–August is 40–45°C — the pool is the only outdoor option by noon. December–February is cool, the cheapest rates.
- **Budget**: £150–300/night total hotel cost (room + resort fee), £60–100/day food and drinks, £80–200 for shows. A 4-night trip is typically £800–1,500 per person all-in, excluding gambling losses.
