Labuan Bajo sits on the western tip of **Flores**, in Indonesia's East Nusa Tenggara province, and exists for one reason: it's the harbour gateway to **Komodo National Park**. From here, day boats and liveaboards run out to see wild **Komodo dragons** on Rinca and Komodo islands, the famous **Padar Island** viewpoint over three curved bays, **Pink Beach**, the mantas at **Manta Point**, and the easy snorkelling islands of **Kanawa** and **Kelor**. Back in town, a row of hilltop bars catches one of Indonesia's best sunsets. UK visitors get a **30-day visa on arrival**, pay in **Indonesian Rupiah (IDR)** — bring cash — and fly into **Komodo Airport (LBJ)** on a short domestic hop from Bali or Jakarta. The dry season, roughly **April to October**, is the best window for boats. ## What to Do in Labuan Bajo

Labuan Bajo is a base, not a big-sights town — the point is the water. This is the **harbour gateway on Flores to Komodo National Park**, and almost everyone here is either heading out on a boat or coming back from one:

- **Komodo dragons on Rinca and Komodo islands** — walk with a ranger to see the world's largest lizards in the wild. Rinca is closer to town and often quieter than Komodo island itself.
- **Padar Island viewpoint** — the postcard hike up to a ridge overlooking three curved bays; a short but steep, hot climb that rewards an early start.
- **Pink Beach** — one of the park's rare pink-sand beaches, a common snorkel and swim stop on boat itineraries.
- **Manta snorkelling at Manta Point** — drift-snorkel or dive with manta rays inside the park, conditions permitting.
- **Kanawa and Kelor islands** — closer, gentler islands with easy snorkelling and beaches, good for shorter or half-day boat trips.
- **Liveaboard or day boat** — choose a day trip for the highlights on a budget, or a 2–3 day liveaboard to reach quieter dive sites and catch sunrise on Padar.
- **Hilltop sunset bars** — end the day at one of the town's west-facing rooftop and hillside bars, watching the sun drop behind the islands.

Book boat tours in town through your hotel or a harbour operator, and budget extra **cash** for the separate **Komodo National Park fees**. Dry season (April–October) gives the calmest seas and best visibility.

## Labuan Bajo Hotels FAQs

**Where is Labuan Bajo and why do people go there?**
Labuan Bajo is a harbour town on the western tip of Flores island in eastern Indonesia. It's the launch point for Komodo National Park — the only place in the world to see wild Komodo dragons, plus Padar Island's viewpoint, Pink Beach and the manta rays at Manta Point. Almost every visitor uses it as a base for boat trips out to the islands.

**Do UK travellers need a visa for Labuan Bajo, Indonesia?**
UK passport holders get a 30-day visa on arrival (VOA) for Indonesia, which covers Labuan Bajo. You pay the fee on landing (payable by card at most airports) or arrange an e-VOA online in advance. It's extendable once for another 30 days. Always check the latest rules on gov.uk before you travel, as fees and conditions change.

**How do I fly to Labuan Bajo?**
Fly into Komodo Airport (LBJ) in Labuan Bajo. There are no direct long-haul flights — you connect through Bali (Denpasar, DPS) or Jakarta (CGK) and take a short domestic hop on to LBJ. The Bali–Labuan Bajo flight is roughly 1 to 1.5 hours and runs several times a day.

**What currency is used in Labuan Bajo and should I bring cash?**
Indonesia uses the Indonesian Rupiah (IDR). Bring cash. ATMs exist in Labuan Bajo town but can run out of money or be offline, and many homestays, warungs and boat operators only take cash. Draw out enough in town before heading to the islands, where there are no ATMs at all.

**When is the best time to visit Labuan Bajo?**
The dry season, roughly April to October, is the best window for boat trips — calmer seas, reliable sailings and clearer water for snorkelling. The wet season (November to March) brings rougher crossings and some cancelled tours. July and August are the busiest and priciest months, so April–June and September–October are the sweet spot.

**How do I book Komodo National Park boat tours and pay the park fees?**
Boat tours are booked in Labuan Bajo — through your hotel, a local operator on the harbour, or online in advance. You'll pay separate Komodo National Park entrance and conservation fees on top of the boat price, usually collected in cash at the ranger post or by your operator. Confirm exactly what's included before you pay, and budget extra cash for park fees.

**Should I do a day trip or a liveaboard from Labuan Bajo?**
Day trips (fast boat, out and back in a day) are cheaper and fine if you want to see the highlights — Padar, a dragon walk on Rinca or Komodo, and one snorkel stop. Liveaboards (2–3 days sleeping on the boat) reach further, quieter dive sites and catch sunrise on Padar, but cost more. If you have limited time and a tight budget, a day trip covers the essentials.

**Can I actually see Komodo dragons near Labuan Bajo?**
Yes — that's the main reason people come. Wild Komodo dragons live on Komodo and Rinca islands inside the national park, both reached by boat from Labuan Bajo. You walk with a trained ranger who keeps a safe distance. Rinca is closer to town and often quieter than Komodo island itself.

**What is Padar Island and is it worth it?**
Padar Island has the postcard viewpoint of Komodo National Park — a short but steep hike up to a ridge overlooking three curved bays and beaches. It's a highlight of almost every boat tour and worth the early start. Wear proper shoes; the trail is rocky and hot.

**Where can I see manta rays near Labuan Bajo?**
Manta Point, inside Komodo National Park, is the well-known spot for snorkelling and diving with manta rays. It's a standard stop on many boat itineraries, though sightings depend on conditions and season. Ask your operator whether Manta Point is included before booking.

**Which area of Labuan Bajo should I stay in?**
Staying in or near Labuan Bajo town puts you close to the harbour, tour operators, restaurants and ATMs — handy for early boat departures and cheap eats. The waterfront resorts north of town (around Waecicu) trade walkability for sea views and quiet. Budget travellers almost always base in town for convenience and price.

**Do Labuan Bajo hotels have swimming pools?**

**Is Labuan Bajo expensive compared to Bali?**
Rooms can be as cheap as Bali or cheaper at the budget end, but the boat tours, park fees and the extra domestic flight add up. Food and simple homestays are affordable; the Komodo boat trips are where most of your money goes. Bring more cash than you think you'll need.

**How many days should I spend in Labuan Bajo?**
Three to four days is a comfortable amount — a full-day or overnight Komodo boat trip, a spare day for a second island run or diving, and time to enjoy a hilltop sunset bar. If you want a liveaboard plus land time, allow five days or more.

**Can I visit Kanawa and Kelor islands from Labuan Bajo?**
Yes — Kanawa and Kelor are closer islands often included on shorter or half-day boat trips, with easy snorkelling and beaches. They're a gentler alternative to the full park run if you're short on time or prefer calmer water. Confirm which islands your specific tour visits.

**Are there good sunset spots in Labuan Bajo?**
Labuan Bajo is famous for its sunsets over the harbour and islands. There's a cluster of hilltop bars and rooftop restaurants above the town with wide west-facing views — a popular way to end a day of boating. Several hotels also have their own terraces facing the water.

**Is Labuan Bajo good for diving and snorkelling?**
It's one of Indonesia's top diving areas — Komodo National Park has strong currents, healthy reefs, mantas and turtles. Snorkellers are well catered for on standard boat tours, while divers usually book dedicated dive day-trips or liveaboards. The dry season offers the best visibility.

**Do I need to book Labuan Bajo hotels far in advance?**
In the busy dry-season months, especially July and August, the better-value rooms and resorts do fill up, so booking ahead helps. Outside peak season you'll find more availability. The budget homestays turn over quickly and can often be booked closer to your dates.

**Is Labuan Bajo safe for tourists?**
Labuan Bajo is a small, tourist-oriented town and is generally considered safe, with standard travel precautions. The bigger practical risks are sea conditions on boat trips and running short of cash, so choose reputable operators and carry enough Rupiah. Check current gov.uk travel advice before you go.

**What should I pack for a Komodo boat trip?**
Bring reef-safe sunscreen, a hat, sturdy shoes for the Padar and dragon-island hikes, a dry bag, seasickness tablets if you're prone, and plenty of cash for park fees and extras. Water and snacks are handy too — options are limited once you're out on the islands.
