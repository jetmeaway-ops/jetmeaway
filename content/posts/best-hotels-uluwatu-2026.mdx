## What to See and Do in Uluwatu

Uluwatu is Bali's clifftop south — dramatic scenery and ocean over rice terraces and cafes. Here's what to build a trip around.

**Uluwatu Temple (Pura Luhur Uluwatu) at Sunset.** The sea temple perched on a cliff 70 metres above the Indian Ocean is Uluwatu's signature sight — go for the late-afternoon light and stay for the nightly **Kecak fire dance**, a chanting-and-flame performance staged in the temple's cliff amphitheatre as the sun drops. Dress modestly (a sarong is provided) and mind the temple monkeys, who are expert at snatching sunglasses and phones.

**The Surf Breaks.** Uluwatu is a world-class surf destination. **Uluwatu, Padang Padang, Bingin, Impossibles** and nearby **Dreamland** are the marquee reef breaks — mostly intermediate-to-advanced over shallow reef. Even non-surfers should watch a session from a cliff warung with a cold drink.

**The Coves.** White-sand beaches reached by steep cliff stairs: **Padang Padang** (the postcard one), **Bingin** (surfers and sunset warungs), **Dreamland and Balangan** (broader, easier access) and **Green Bowl** (quiet, stair-heavy, at the tip). Each rewards the climb down.

**Clifftop Beach Clubs.** One of Uluwatu's defining experiences — beach clubs built into the cliffs with infinity pools and long sunset views over the ocean. Even on a budget trip, one clifftop sunset session is worth building in; most welcome non-guests for a minimum spend.

## Explore more of Indonesia

Uluwatu is one corner of Bali's south. If you're building a wider trip, compare these nearby bases: [best hotels in Nusa Dua](/blog/best-hotels-nusa-dua-2026) for calm, family-friendly swimming beaches and big resorts just east along the Bukit, and [best hotels in Canggu](/blog/best-hotels-canggu-2026) for the flatter, more walkable cafe-coworking-and-nightlife scene up the coast. Many visitors split their Bali time across two or three of these.

## Uluwatu Hotels FAQs

**Do UK travellers need a visa for Bali in 2026?**
UK passport holders get a visa on arrival (VOA) for Indonesia, valid for 30 days and extendable once for another 30. You buy it at Bali's Denpasar airport (or online in advance as an e-VOA) for around 500,000 IDR — roughly £25. Keep the payment receipt; you'll need it if you extend.

**How do I get from the airport to Uluwatu?**
Fly into Bali's Ngurah Rai / Denpasar airport (DPS). Uluwatu sits on the Bukit Peninsula just south of the airport, so the transfer is short by Bali standards — around 30 to 40 minutes to most Uluwatu hotels, depending on traffic and exactly which clifftop or surf village you're heading to. Hotels can arrange a pickup, or use the Grab and Gojek ride apps.

**Are there direct flights from the UK to Bali?**
No — there are no direct flights from the UK to Bali (DPS). Every routing involves at least one stop, usually through a Gulf or Asian hub such as Doha, Dubai, Singapore, Kuala Lumpur or Hong Kong. Total journey time is typically 16 to 20 hours including the connection.

**What currency is used in Bali?**
The Indonesian Rupiah (IDR). Prices in this guide are rough pound estimates only — always check the live rate on the hotel's page. Cards work at bigger hotels and beach clubs, but carry cash for warungs, scooter fuel, temple entry and small guesthouses. ATMs are widespread; withdraw larger amounts to save on per-transaction fees.

**What is the cheapest area to stay in Uluwatu?**
The inland villages and the lanes back from the surf breaks — around Pecatu, Ungasan and the roads behind Bingin and Padang Padang — hold the deepest budget stock. **When is the best time to visit Uluwatu?**
The dry season, roughly April to October, is Bali's best stretch and also Uluwatu's prime surf window — reliable offshore winds, drier days and the cleanest waves on the Bukit reefs. July and August are the busiest and priciest. The wet season (November to March) is cheaper and greener but brings humidity and afternoon downpours.

**Is Uluwatu good for surfing?**
Uluwatu is one of the world's great surf destinations. The reef breaks here — Uluwatu itself, Padang Padang, Bingin, Impossibles and nearby Dreamland — are world-class but mostly suit intermediate-to-advanced surfers over shallow reef. Beginners are usually better off learning on the beach breaks around Kuta and Canggu first, then progressing here.

**What is there to see and do in Uluwatu?**
The clifftop Uluwatu Temple (Pura Luhur Uluwatu) at sunset, with its nightly Kecak fire dance; the white-sand coves at Padang Padang, Bingin, Dreamland and Green Bowl reached by cliff stairs; the surf breaks; and the clifftop beach clubs for sunset drinks. It's Bali's dramatic southern edge — dramatic scenery over nightlife.

**Should I watch out for the monkeys at Uluwatu Temple?**
Yes. The macaques around Uluwatu Temple are notorious for snatching sunglasses, phones, hats and anything loose — sometimes to "trade" them back for food. Keep valuables zipped away, don't tease or feed them, and hold onto your glasses. Otherwise they're part of the temple's character rather than a real danger.

**How do I get around Uluwatu?**
The Bukit Peninsula is spread out and hilly, so most visitors either rent a scooter, hire a private driver by the day, or use the Grab and Gojek apps. Scooters are cheap and flexible but the roads are steep and busy — only ride if you're confident and licensed. Note that Indonesia drives on the left, same as the UK.

**Is Uluwatu or Canggu better?**
Uluwatu is the clifftop surf-and-sunset south — dramatic scenery, world-class reef breaks, sunset beach clubs, and a more spread-out, resort-and-villa feel. Canggu is flatter, more walkable, and heavier on cafes, coworking and nightlife. Many visitors split their trip between the two, or add nearby Nusa Dua for calmer, family-friendly beaches.

**Are beach clubs a big part of Uluwatu?**
Yes — the clifftop beach clubs are one of Uluwatu's signatures, built into the cliffs with infinity pools and long sunset views over the Indian Ocean. They range from relaxed to see-and-be-seen, and most welcome non-guests for a minimum spend. Even on a budget trip, one sunset session at a clifftop club is worth building in.

**Is Uluwatu family-friendly?**
It can be, but with caveats. Many of Uluwatu's beaches are reached by steep cliff stairs and have reef and strong currents rather than gentle swimming, which suits surfers more than small children. Families wanting calm, sandy swimming beaches often prefer nearby Nusa Dua. Uluwatu's resorts themselves, with big pools and space, work well for families.

**What should I wear to Uluwatu Temple?**
Uluwatu Temple is an active Hindu temple, so modest dress is required — shoulders and knees covered. A sarong and sash are usually provided or rented at the entrance. It's an easygoing dress code by temple standards, but turning up in beachwear alone won't get you in respectfully.

**How many days should I spend in Uluwatu?**
Three to four nights lets you catch a couple of sunset sessions, explore two or three of the coves, see the temple Kecak dance and still have beach-club downtime. Surfers chasing the reef breaks often stay a week or more. Many UK visitors fold Uluwatu into a wider Bali trip alongside Ubud, Canggu or the Nusa islands.
