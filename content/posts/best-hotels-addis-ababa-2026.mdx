  - q: "Do I need to worry about altitude in Addis Ababa?"
    a: "Take it seriously on day one. Drink plenty of water, go easy on alcohol the first night, do not plan a hard hike up Entoto on your arrival day, and give yourself a slow start. Most people adjust within a couple of days."

  - q: "Why does Ethiopia have a different calendar and clock?"
    a: "Ethiopia uses its own calendar of 13 months — twelve months of 30 days plus a short thirteenth month — and it runs several years behind the Gregorian calendar, so the Ethiopian year on the ground will not match the one on your phone. Ethiopia also commonly uses a 12-hour clock that starts at dawn rather than midnight, so local one o'clock can mean seven in the morning. Always confirm whether a stated time is 'Ethiopian time' or 'international time' when booking tours, drivers and domestic flights."

September, just after the rains, is when the countryside is at its most spectacular."

## What to See in Addis Ababa

Start with **Lucy**. The **National Museum of Ethiopia** holds the 3.2-million-year-old hominid fossil found in the Afar region in 1974 — known here as **Dinkinesh**, 'you are marvellous' — along with an outstanding collection of Ethiopian archaeology and art. It is a modest building for such an enormous thing, and it is the single best reason to spend a morning in Addis. Allow a couple of hours and take the guided explanation if one is offered; the context is what makes the visit.

The **Ethnological Museum** sits inside **Haile Selassie's former palace** on the university campus, which means you tour the emperor's own bedroom and bathroom on the way round an excellent collection covering Ethiopia's many peoples, languages and religious traditions. Nearby, **Holy Trinity Cathedral** is the grandest Ethiopian Orthodox church in the capital and the burial place of Haile Selassie and Empress Menen. **Unity Park**, in the grounds of the National Palace, opened the imperial compound to the public and pairs well with either.

Then the harder history. The **Red Terror Martyrs Memorial Museum** documents the killings of the Derg period in the 1970s and 1980s, often with survivors as guides. It is small, free to enter and genuinely difficult, and most visitors find it the most affecting hour of their trip. For a complete change of register, **Merkato** — one of Africa's largest open-air markets — is a sprawl of spice, coffee, textiles and recycled metalwork that repays going with a guide and taking nothing valuable with you.

For air and views, head up **Mount Entoto** on the northern ridge, where eucalyptus forest covers the slopes and the whole city spreads out below — this is where Emperor Menelik II settled before Addis was founded down on the plain. And whatever else you do, sit through a full **coffee ceremony**: green beans roasted over coals in front of you, ground by hand, brewed in a clay jebena and poured in three rounds. Ethiopia is the birthplace of coffee, and the ceremony is the country's central social ritual rather than a tourist performance. Pair it with **injera** — the sour teff flatbread that arrives under stews you scoop up by hand — and **doro wat**, the slow-cooked spiced chicken that is the national dish.

Addis is also **the hub for the northern historic circuit**. The rock-hewn churches of **Lalibela**, the castles of **Gondar**, the obelisks of **Axum** and the **Simien Mountains** are all reached by domestic flights routed through the capital. Before you plan any of it, read the current [UK FCDO travel advice for Ethiopia](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/ethiopia) — advisories in some regions have changed repeatedly in recent years —, which is the reliable source for what is currently open and how to reach it.

## Choosing Where to Stay in Addis Ababa

- **Bole** — the usual first choice. The airport district, running out along Bole Road, with the biggest concentration of modern hotels, restaurants and cafés and the shortest transfer to ADD. Convenient rather than atmospheric.
- **Kazanchis** — the UN, diplomatic and business quarter, close to the Economic Commission for Africa and the main conference venues. Well placed for the city centre and full of four- and five-star hotels.
- **Piazza** — the old town on the northern side, with Italian-era architecture, the city's oldest streets and some of the cheapest rooms. The most character per pound in Addis, and handy for Holy Trinity Cathedral and the museums.
- **Old Airport** — the quiet residential option on the western side, popular with embassies, NGOs and expat families. Calm and leafy, but you will rely on cars to get anywhere.

## Altitude, Safety and the Practical Stuff — the Honest Version

**Addis sits at roughly 2,350 metres**, and the altitude is not a footnote. Plenty of arrivals feel breathless on a flight of stairs, flat by mid-afternoon or mildly headachy for the first day or two. The fix is unglamorous: drink water, go easy on alcohol the first night, do not book a hike up Entoto for your arrival day, and let the first 24 hours be slow. The upside is a climate most people love — mild days, genuinely cool evenings, and none of the heat visitors expect from an African capital. Pack a fleece.

Addis is the **seat of the African Union** and a major **Ethiopian Airlines hub**, which gives the city a diplomatic, pan-African, in-transit character — a lot of the people in your hotel lobby are here for a summit or a connection. It also means the city is well set up for international visitors, with drivers, guides and English widely available.

On safety, be clear-eyed: **Ethiopia's security situation varies by region, and some regions carry active advisories.** Check the [UK FCDO travel advice for Ethiopia](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/ethiopia) linked above before you book and again before you fly, and confirm that any onward domestic trip is in an area currently advised as safe — this matters far more here than the usual city-crime checklist. **Do UK travellers need a visa for Ethiopia?**
Yes. UK passport holders need an **Ethiopia e-visa, applied for online in advance** of travel. Apply before you fly and allow processing time rather than assuming you can sort it at the airport. **What currency is used in Addis Ababa?**
The **Ethiopian Birr (ETB)**. Card acceptance is limited outside the bigger hotels and a handful of restaurants, so **carry cash** and expect to use it for most day-to-day spending. US dollars are useful for hotels, tour operators and domestic flight bookings, and many visitors carry some alongside birr. The pound figures in this guide are rough conversions for guidance only, not exact quotes.

**Are there direct flights from London to Addis Ababa?**
Yes. **Ethiopian Airlines flies direct from London to Addis Ababa Bole International Airport (ADD) in roughly 8 hours.** ADD is also one of the largest connecting hubs in Africa, so a great many visitors pass through Addis on the way somewhere else — which makes a stopover night here easy to arrange and often very cheap.

**Is Addis Ababa safe for tourists?**
Addis itself is generally calm and most visits are trouble-free, but ⚠️ **Ethiopia's security situation varies significantly by region, and some regions carry active advisories.** Read the UK FCDO travel advice for Ethiopia before booking and again before you fly, and check that any onward domestic trip is in an area currently advised as safe. In the city, take normal precautions: use hotel cars or a ride app at night, keep phones out of sight in crowded markets, and ask reception which specific areas to avoid.

**Do I need to worry about altitude in Addis Ababa?**
Take it seriously on day one. Addis sits at roughly **2,350 metres**, high enough that many arrivals feel breathless on stairs, tired by mid-afternoon or mildly headachy for the first 24 to 48 hours. Drink plenty of water, go easy on alcohol the first night, do not plan a hard hike up Entoto on your arrival day, and give yourself a slow start. Most people adjust within a couple of days.

**Do I need a yellow fever certificate for Ethiopia?**
⚠️ It depends on your route. A **yellow fever certificate may be required** if you are arriving from or transiting a country with yellow fever transmission risk, and some onward countries require proof of vaccination if you have been in Ethiopia. **Do I need malaria tablets for Addis Ababa?**
⚠️ Malaria risk in Addis Ababa itself is generally considered **low because of the roughly 2,350-metre altitude**, but it is a **real risk in Ethiopia's lowland areas** — including places many visitors go on from the capital. Get advice from your GP or a travel clinic well before you fly about antimalarials for your specific itinerary, and pack repellent. This is general information, not medical advice.

**Why does Ethiopia have a different calendar and clock?**
Ethiopia uses its own **13-month calendar** — twelve months of 30 days plus a short thirteenth month — and it runs several years behind the Gregorian calendar, so the Ethiopian year on the ground will not match the one on your phone. Ethiopia also commonly uses a **12-hour clock that starts at dawn** rather than midnight, so local one o'clock can mean seven in the morning. Always confirm whether a stated time is Ethiopian or international when booking tours, drivers and domestic flights.

**When is the best time to visit Addis Ababa?**
The **dry season, roughly October to May**, is the easiest time to travel — clear skies, comfortable days and good conditions for the northern historic circuit. The **main rains fall June to September**, when Addis is green and lush but roads and unpaved tracks elsewhere get difficult. September, just after the rains, is when the countryside is at its most spectacular.
