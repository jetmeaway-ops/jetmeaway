**Do UK citizens need a visa for Tajikistan?**

If you plan to travel into the Pamirs (GBAO region), you also need a separate GBAO permit, which can usually be added to the e-visa application. Always use the official government e-visa site, not a third-party reseller.

**What is there to do in Dushanbe?**
Dushanbe is a green, walkable capital in the Varzob valley. The highlights are the long, tree-lined Rudaki Avenue and Rudaki Park, one of the world's tallest flagpoles, the National Museum of Tajikistan, the Navruz Palace, and the buzzing Mehrgon and Green bazaars. The Hisor (Hissar) Fortress makes an easy half-day trip, and the city is the main launch point for the epic Pamir Highway.

**When is the best time to visit Dushanbe?**
Spring (April–June) and autumn (September–October) are the most pleasant — mild and green, with the Varzob valley at its best. Summers get hot in the city, though they're the season for heading up into the Pamirs (roughly July–September). Winters are cold but quiet.

**What currency is used in Tajikistan?**
The Tajikistani somoni (TJS). We quote all hotel prices in pounds as from-prices for UK travellers; you'll settle in somoni or, at the international hotels, often by card. Carry some cash for bazaars, taxis and smaller venues, where cards are less reliable.

**Is alcohol available and what should I wear in Dushanbe?**
Tajikistan is a secular, Muslim-majority country. Alcohol is served — the international hotels and many restaurants have bars — and dress is relaxed in the capital. Modest, respectful clothing is appreciated at religious sites, but Dushanbe is easy-going day to day.

**What languages are spoken in Dushanbe?**
Tajik (a form of Persian) is the national language and Russian is very widely spoken, especially in hotels, business and transport. English is limited outside the international hotels and the tourism trade, so a few Russian or Tajik phrases — and a translation app — go a long way.

**Can I use Dushanbe as a base for the Pamir Highway?**
Yes — Dushanbe is the classic western launch point for the Pamir Highway (the M41). Most overland trips into the Pamirs start or end here, and this is where you arrange the GBAO permit, a driver or vehicle, and supplies. Bear in mind the FCDO advice for the GBAO/Pamir region can differ from the advice for the capital, so check it carefully before committing.

**Is Dushanbe a safe city for tourists?**
Dushanbe itself is generally calm and travellers report feeling comfortable, but always check the [latest UK FCDO advice for Tajikistan](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/tajikistan) before you go — it can differ between the capital and outlying regions, particularly near the borders and in the GBAO/Pamir area. Take normal city precautions, keep copies of your visa and permits, and use registered taxis or your hotel's driver.
