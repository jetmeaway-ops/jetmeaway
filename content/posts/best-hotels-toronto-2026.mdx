## The Scout's Take: Stadium-Side or City-Centre?

Toronto's hotel question for 2026 comes down to one trade-off. Base yourself stadium-side — in Liberty Village, the Exhibition Place lakefront, or West Queen West — and you can walk to BMO Field, soak up the pre-match atmosphere on King Street West, and skip the worst of the match-day transit crush. Base yourself downtown, in the Entertainment District or the Financial District, and you trade a short streetcar ride to the stadium for being in the dead centre of the city: the CN Tower, the waterfront, Union Station, and the theatre and restaurant districts on your doorstep.

For fans whose trip revolves around the football, stadium-side wins. Hotel X is the only hotel actually inside Exhibition Place, and the Liberty Village bars fill with supporters on match days.

For travellers who want the whole city — and who don't mind a 5-to-15-minute transit hop to the ground — downtown wins. The Fairmont Royal York, 1 Hotel, the Ritz-Carlton and the St. Regis all sit within a short walk of Union Station, which is the hub for the GO Transit train that drops you at Exhibition in about five minutes.

For tight budgets, this guide's [budget tier](#budget-hotels) does the same trade-off in miniature: Chinatown, Kensington Market and the Don Valley corridor put you a 10–20 minute ride from everything at a third of the Financial District's price.

## Going to the 2026 World Cup in Toronto?

Toronto is one of two Canadian host cities for the 2026 FIFA World Cup, staging matches at Toronto Stadium — everyday name BMO Field — beside Lake Ontario at Exhibition Place on the city's western lakefront. The ground sits within the grounds of the historic Canadian National Exhibition, a short hop from downtown but its own self-contained pocket of the city, framed by the lake on one side and the Liberty Village neighbourhood on the other.

Where you base yourself depends on your priorities. For the shortest possible walk on match day, stay inside or beside Exhibition Place — **[Hotel X Toronto](/hotels/la_lpb54c8?city=Toronto)** is the only full-service hotel within the grounds, roughly 10 minutes on foot from the gates. The next-best options are in **Liberty Village**, the converted-warehouse district immediately north of the stadium, where a brisk 15-minute walk gets you to the turnstiles and the bars fill with supporters before kick-off. **West Queen West**, home of **The Drake Hotel**, is a 20-minute walk or a single streetcar ride east along King Street West. If you'd rather stay central, the **downtown and waterfront** hotels are all a short, reliable transit ride away.

Getting to the stadium on match day is straightforward if you use transit and plan ahead. The 509 Harbourfront and 511 Bathurst streetcars both terminate at Exhibition Loop, right beside BMO Field. From Union Station, the GO Transit Lakeshore West line reaches Exhibition GO in about five minutes — the fastest option from downtown. Driving is actively discouraged: Exhibition Place parking sells out, surrounding streets close, and you'll spend longer hunting for a space than the transit ride takes. Buy a rechargeable PRESTO card or simply tap a contactless bank card on the TTC and GO, leave your hotel about 90 minutes before kick-off, and you'll arrive with time to spare.

## Frequently Asked Questions

Every question below targets a real search Toronto-bound UK travellers run before a trip — match-day logistics, airport transfers, visa rules, neighbourhood choices and budget planning. The answers are deliberately specific so the page is genuinely useful, not just decorative.

### Where should I stay for the match at Toronto Stadium (everyday name: BMO Field), Exhibition Place?

Stay in Liberty Village or along the waterfront in the Exhibition Place area for the shortest walk to the stadium. Hotel X Toronto sits inside Exhibition Place itself, a 10-minute walk from the gates, and 1 Hotel Toronto in the Entertainment District is two GO Transit stops away. The Drake Hotel in West Queen West is a 20-minute walk or a short streetcar ride along King Street West. Downtown hotels such as the Fairmont Royal York and The Ritz-Carlton are a 15-minute transit ride via Union Station.

### How do I get to the stadium on match day?

The 509 Harbourfront and 511 Bathurst streetcars both stop at Exhibition Loop right beside BMO Field. From Union Station, the GO Transit Lakeshore West line reaches Exhibition GO in about five minutes. Driving is discouraged on match days because Exhibition Place parking sells out and surrounding streets close — use transit, leave 90 minutes before kick-off, and buy a rechargeable PRESTO card or use contactless tap-to-pay on the TTC and GO.

### What is the closest hotel to BMO Field in Toronto?

Hotel X Toronto is the closest hotel to BMO Field — it stands inside Exhibition Place on the lakefront, roughly a 10-minute walk from the stadium gates. After that, hotels in Liberty Village put you within a 15-minute walk, while waterfront and Entertainment District properties such as 1 Hotel Toronto are a short streetcar or GO Transit ride away.

### Which Toronto neighbourhood is best for first-time visitors?

Downtown and the Entertainment District are best for first-timers — you are walking distance from the CN Tower, the waterfront, Union Station, the Rogers Centre and the theatre district, with the densest cluster of hotels and restaurants. Yorkville suits travellers who want quieter, upmarket streets and designer shopping, while Liberty Village and West Queen West are better for a younger, bar-and-brunch crowd staying close to the stadium.

### How far is Toronto Pearson Airport from downtown?

Toronto Pearson (YYZ) is about 22km west of downtown. The UP Express train runs from the airport to Union Station in 25 minutes for around CA$12, departing every 15 minutes. A taxi or rideshare takes 30–50 minutes depending on traffic and costs roughly CA$60–75. From Union Station every downtown and waterfront hotel is a short walk, streetcar or taxi away.

### When is the best time to visit Toronto in 2026?

June and July are warm (22–28°C) and line up with the World Cup, so book hotels early as rates rise across host-city match dates. September brings the Toronto International Film Festival and pleasant weather, while May and early October are the quietest, cheapest shoulder months. Winters are cold and snowy but the underground PATH network links much of downtown.

### Do I need a visa to visit Canada from the UK?

UK passport holders do not need a visa for short tourist stays but must hold an Electronic Travel Authorisation (eTA), applied for online before departure for around CA$7. It is valid for up to five years and linked to your passport. Apply through the official Government of Canada website well ahead of travel — never through third-party sites that charge inflated fees.

### Is Toronto walkable for tourists?

Downtown Toronto is very walkable — the waterfront, Entertainment District, Financial District and St Lawrence Market are all within a 25-minute stroll, and the underground PATH network connects much of it weatherproof. For longer hops the TTC streetcars, subway and buses are cheap and frequent, and Exhibition Place beside BMO Field is a direct streetcar or GO Transit ride from downtown.

### What is the cheapest area to stay in Toronto?

Chinatown and Kensington Market have the lowest room rates in the downtown core — guesthouses and budget inns from around £76–88 a night, a 10–15 minute walk to the Financial District and PATH network. The Don Valley/east-end corridor and a cluster of no-frills inns near the Rogers Centre run a similar band. You trade polish for a genuinely central, walkable base at a fraction of the Financial District rate.

### How much does a budget Toronto hotel cost per night in 2026?

Real bookable rates in the budget tier run roughly £76–160 a night — guesthouses in Chinatown and Kensington Market from ~£76–88, motel-style inns near the Don Valley Parkway from ~£104–108, and hostel dorm-adjacent private rooms around £108–160. Rates rise sharply across World Cup match dates, so book early if your trip lines up with a fixture.

### What is the UP Express and is it worth it from YYZ?

The UP Express is the dedicated airport train linking Toronto Pearson (YYZ) directly to Union Station downtown in 25 minutes, departing every 15 minutes, for around CA$12 one-way (cheaper with a PRESTO card). It's the fastest and most predictable way into the city — a taxi or rideshare covers the same route in 30–50 minutes depending on traffic for roughly CA$60–75. For most UK travellers landing with luggage, the UP Express is the better first move: it drops you at Union Station, within walking distance of the Financial District and Entertainment District hotels in this guide.

### What are Toronto's PATH tunnels and how do they help hotel guests?

PATH is the world's largest underground shopping and pedestrian network — over 30km of climate-controlled tunnels connecting more than 75 buildings across the Financial District and Entertainment District, including Union Station, the Fairmont Royal York, the Ritz-Carlton and dozens of office towers, food courts and shops. In a Toronto winter (or a summer downpour) it means you can walk from a downtown hotel to Union Station, the subway or a neighbouring hotel without stepping outside. Most of the Financial District and Downtown hotels in this guide connect into PATH directly or within a block.

### Can I walk to the CN Tower from downtown hotels?

Yes — the CN Tower sits at the southern edge of the Entertainment District, and most downtown and Financial District hotels in this guide are within a 10–20 minute walk. The Fairmont Royal York, the Ritz-Carlton and 1 Hotel Toronto are all under 15 minutes on foot; hotels further north in Yorkville or west in Liberty Village are a short streetcar or subway ride instead.

### Are there direct flights from the UK to Toronto?

Yes. Air Canada and British Airways both fly London Heathrow to Toronto Pearson direct in around 7h45m, and WestJet adds direct options from Gatwick and other UK airports on a seasonal basis. It's one of the most competitively priced long-haul UK routes thanks to the number of carriers flying it. [Search live UK flights to Toronto (YYZ)](/flights?to=YYZ).

### What currency does Toronto use and do UK cards work?

Canada uses the Canadian Dollar (CAD). UK debit and credit cards work at the vast majority of hotels, restaurants and shops via tap-to-pay, and contactless bank cards work directly on the TTC subway, streetcars and buses without needing a PRESTO card. Check your card's foreign transaction fees before you travel — a fee-free travel card saves the most over a multi-day trip.

### Is there a hotel tax in Toronto?

Ontario charges 13% HST (Harmonized Sales Tax) on hotel rooms, plus most Toronto hotels add a Municipal Accommodation Tax on top — budget roughly 15-19% above the quoted room rate for the all-in price. Our hotel pages show the total including taxes and fees before you book, so there's no surprise at checkout.

### What are the best months to visit Toronto for good weather and lower prices?

May and late September–early October are the sweet spot: mild temperatures (15–22°C), thinner crowds, and rates well below the June–July World Cup peak. June and July are warm (22–28°C) but line up directly with the tournament, so book early if travelling then. September also brings the Toronto International Film Festival, which pushes some downtown rates back up.

### Is Toronto safe for tourists?

Toronto is consistently ranked among the safest large cities in North America. Downtown, the Financial District, Entertainment District, Yorkville and the waterfront are busy and well-patrolled at all hours. Standard city awareness applies late at night around Yonge-Dundas Square and parts of Kensington Market, but there's no neighbourhood in this guide that requires special caution for a typical UK visitor.

### Where is the Financial District and who should stay there?

The Financial District is Toronto's downtown business core, clustered around Bay Street and King Street West, connected end-to-end by the PATH tunnel network. It suits travellers who want the shortest possible walk to Union Station, the CN Tower and the theatre district, plus business travellers who value being inside the PATH system in bad weather. The Ritz-Carlton, St. Regis and several mid-range towers in this guide sit here.

### Where is the Entertainment District and who should stay there?

The Entertainment District runs west from the Financial District toward the waterfront and Rogers Centre, packed with theatres, restaurants and nightlife plus a growing cluster of condo-hotels. It suits first-time visitors who want to be near the CN Tower, the harbour and a short GO Transit hop to Exhibition Place for match days — 1 Hotel Toronto, Shangri-La and BISHA in this guide sit here.

### Where is Yorkville and who should stay there?

Yorkville is Toronto's quiet, upmarket district north of the Financial District — cobblestone side streets, designer shopping on Bloor Street, and the city's most concentrated run of 5-star hotels including the Four Seasons, the Hazelton and the Yorkville Royal Sonesta. It suits travellers who want luxury and calm over nightlife, a 15–20 minute walk or short subway ride from downtown's main sights.

### Where is the Distillery District and is it worth staying near?

The Distillery District is a pedestrianised Victorian-industrial quarter east of downtown — cobblestone lanes, galleries, restaurants and the city's best-preserved 19th-century architecture, with almost no traffic noise. Few hotels sit directly inside it, but it's a 15–20 minute walk or short streetcar ride from most downtown and Financial District hotels in this guide, and worth an evening even if you don't sleep there.

### Where is Kensington Market and who should stay there?

Kensington Market is Toronto's bohemian, market-stall neighbourhood next to Chinatown — vintage shops, street food and some of the cheapest guesthouse beds in the downtown core. It suits budget travellers and younger visitors who want atmosphere and low prices over polish, a 15-minute walk from the Financial District and University of Toronto.

### Where is Queen West and who should stay there?

Queen West (and West Queen West further along) is Toronto's design-and-gallery strip — independent shops, cafés and The Drake Hotel, regularly named among the world's coolest neighbourhoods. It sits between downtown and Liberty Village, making it a genuinely walkable base for both the city centre and BMO Field on match days.

## Beyond the Hotel — Toronto's Essentials

A few non-match experiences worth planning around your stay:

- **CN Tower at sunset** — 553m freestanding tower with a glass floor and the EdgeWalk hands-free ledge walk for the brave. The Entertainment District's landmark and the best skyline orientation point in the city.
- **St Lawrence Market** — a National Geographic-rated "world's best food market," a short walk from the Financial District and Distillery District. Go hungry.
- **The Distillery District** — Victorian-era industrial buildings turned into a pedestrianised quarter of galleries, restaurants and craft distilleries. No traffic, no chain stores, one of the most photogenic corners of the city.
- **Toronto Islands ferry** — a 15-minute ferry from the downtown waterfront to a chain of car-free islands with beaches and skyline views back at the city — the classic summer half-day trip.
- **Kensington Market** — bohemian market stalls, vintage shops and some of the city's best cheap eats, next to Chinatown.
- **Royal Ontario Museum (ROM)** — Canada's largest museum of world cultures and natural history, in a striking crystal-shaped extension on Bloor Street near Yorkville.
- **Niagara Falls day trip** — about 90 minutes by car or coach from downtown, one of the most-visited natural landmarks in North America and an easy add-on to a Toronto stay.

- **Direct UK flights**: Air Canada and British Airways fly LHR–YYZ direct in around 7h45m. WestJet adds seasonal direct options from Gatwick and other UK airports. [Search flights to YYZ](/flights?to=YYZ).

- **Currency**: CAD. **Taxes**: 13% HST plus a Municipal Accommodation Tax add 15–19% to the quoted hotel rate — always check the all-in price. Tipping: 15–20% restaurants, CA$1–2 per drink, CA$2–5 per bag, CA$3–5 per night housekeeping.
- **Best months**: May and late September–early October (15–22°C, quieter, cheaper). June–July is warm (22–28°C) but lines up with the World Cup — book early. Winters are cold and snowy, softened by the PATH tunnel network.
- **Budget**: Downtown luxury trip — £250–450/night all-in. Budget-tier trip — £76–160/night all-in, £30–45/day food and fun using markets and the PATH food courts. A 4-night budget trip built on this guide's cheap tier can land under £700 per person before flights.
