## Quick answers

**What's the cheapest airline to Copenhagen?** **Ryanair and easyJet** on headline price (from ~£22-£45 off-peak). **SAS, Norwegian and BA** are fuller-service.

**How long is the flight?** About **1 hour 50 minutes** from the UK.

**Which UK airport is cheapest?** Usually a **London** airport on a budget carrier, but Copenhagen flies direct from Edinburgh, Manchester and more — flying **local** is often cheapest.

**When should I book?** **4-8 weeks** ahead most months; **8-12 weeks** for summer, the Christmas season and New Year.

**Do I need a visa?** No — **90 days visa-free** in Schengen. Check ETIAS status before you travel.

**Money?** Denmark uses the **krone, not the euro** — pay by card in kroner; the city is pricey.

## Airline-by-airline comparison (2026)

### Ryanair & easyJet — the price leaders

**Typical fare 2026:** £22-£55 return off-peak, hand-luggage only.
**Pros:** The lowest headline fares. Ryanair from Stansted; easyJet from Gatwick and its regional bases including strong Edinburgh service. Ideal for a hand-luggage weekend.
**Cons:** Cheapest fares are cabin-only; a larger cabin bag, hold bag and allocated seat all cost extra.

### SAS & Norwegian — the Scandinavian carriers

**Typical fare 2026:** £60-£130 return; standard fares usually include a bag.
**Pros:** SAS is the flag carrier with a full-service product and a bag on standard fares; Norwegian offers a good mid-market option. Both are strong from Edinburgh and London.
**Cons:** Higher headline fare than the budget carriers; cheapest "light" fares are cabin-only, so check the fare type.

### British Airways

**Typical fare 2026:** £70-£140 return; "Basic" fares are cabin-only.
**Pros:** From Heathrow, Avios earning, a bag on standard fares.
**Cons:** Pricier than the budget carriers.

**The honest read:** hand-luggage only, Ryanair or easyJet win on price. Checking a bag or wanting a fuller service, SAS or Norwegian's standard fares are often good value. BA suits Avios collectors from Heathrow.

## UK airport comparison — fly local

Copenhagen is served from a good spread of UK airports, so the cheapest option is often your **nearest** one. London (Stansted, Gatwick, Heathrow) has the most choice, but **Edinburgh** in particular has strong direct service (easyJet, Norwegian, Ryanair, SAS), and Manchester, Bristol and Birmingham also fly direct. For regional travellers, always price your local direct flight before assuming a cheap London fare wins once the London trek is added.

## Money in Copenhagen: krone, not euro

A quick but important point: **Denmark keeps its own currency, the Danish krone (DKK)** — it is not in the euro despite being in the EU and Schengen.

- **Copenhagen is largely cashless**, so a card works almost everywhere — you rarely need cash at all.
- When a card machine offers to **charge you in pounds instead of kroner, always choose kroner** (declining dynamic currency conversion avoids a hidden markup).
- **Avoid airport currency exchange**, where rates are worst.
- Budget generously: **Copenhagen is one of Europe's more expensive cities** for food, drink and going out.

## Common mistakes UK-Copenhagen travellers make

1. **Assuming Denmark uses the euro.** It's the krone — pay by card in kroner and decline "pay in pounds."
2. **Under-budgeting for the city.** Copenhagen is expensive; a cheap flight is only part of the cost.
3. **Routing through London unnecessarily.** Edinburgh and other UK airports fly direct — your local one may be cheapest.
4. **Judging budget fares on the headline price.** A £30 Ryanair fare plus a £45 hold bag can match an SAS fare that includes one.
5. **Booking the Christmas/Tivoli season late.** December is Copenhagen's peak — book 8-12 weeks ahead.
6. **Adding baggage at the airport.** Always add it online at booking.
7. **Assuming an old passport is fine.** The Schengen 10-year issue rule catches UK travellers out.
