Let us be honest about what Florianópolis is, because it is not a year-round destination. "Floripa" is a city of around half a million people spread across an island and a slice of mainland in Brazil's far south, and it is a **genuinely seasonal beach place**. From December to February it is hot, gloriously busy and expensive — New Year and Carnival are the two most extreme weeks in the Brazilian calendar and rates here reflect it. From March to May and September to November it is warm, calm and far cheaper. From June to August it is winter: cool, often grey and too cold for most people to swim, though the whales arrive and hotel prices bottom out. ## What to See and Do in Florianópolis

The headline fact about Florianópolis is that it has **more than 40 beaches**, and they are genuinely different from one another. On the east coast, **Praia Mole** and **Joaquina** get the Atlantic swell — Joaquina has hosted international surf competitions and has tall white **dunes** behind it where you can hire a board for a few reais and **sandboard** down. On the north coast, **Jurerê Internacional** is the upmarket enclave of villas and high-end beach clubs, while **Canasvieiras**, **Ingleses** and **Praia do Santinho** offer calmer, shallower water and the biggest concentration of family hotels. In the centre-east, **Campeche** and **Armação** are quieter, more laid-back stretches popular with surfers and remote workers.

**Lagoa da Conceição** is the island's other centre of gravity: a large saltwater lagoon ringed by bars and restaurants, the hub of the nightlife and the place where the kitesurfing, windsurfing and stand-up paddling happen. From there, **Barra da Lagoa** sits where the lagoon meets the sea, and the **Costa da Lagoa** boat trip runs up the water to a cluster of restaurants reachable only by boat or on foot — one of the best half-days on the island.

The **historic centre**, on the island side of the water, is worth a morning. The **Mercado Público** is the place for oysters and seafood, the **Hercílio Luz bridge** — restored and reopened after decades closed — is the city's emblem, and the colonial square and cathedral around Praça XV show the Azorean Portuguese roots of the place. For a harder day out, the hike to **Lagoinha do Leste** in the south is the island's classic walk: a couple of hours over a headland to a beach with no road access and nothing built on it.

And between roughly **July and November**, **southern right whales** come to the Santa Catarina coast to calve. Boat trips run from the south of the island and the nearby mainland coast, and in a good season the whales are sometimes visible from shore. It is a genuine reason to consider a winter trip, when hotel prices are at their lowest.

Before you book, read the UK government's current [foreign travel advice for Brazil](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/brazil), which covers entry requirements, safety and health, and check it again in the week before you fly. For beaches, events and opening hours, Brazil's official tourism site [Visit Brasil](https://www.visitbrasil.com/) is the reliable starting point.

## Where to Stay in Florianópolis: Choosing Your Base

The island is bigger than people expect — roughly 50km long — and the beaches are spread all the way around its edge. Choosing your base matters more here than in most city destinations, because the wrong choice means an hour in the car before every swim.

**Lagoa da Conceição** is the best all-round pick for a first visit. It sits in the middle of the island, which means the shortest drive to both coasts, and it has the densest cluster of bars, restaurants and nightlife outside the peak-season beach clubs. The trade-off is that it is a lagoon, not an ocean beach — Praia Mole and Joaquina are a short drive over the hill.

**Jurerê Internacional** is the upmarket choice: villas, smart restaurants and the beach clubs that give Floripa its January reputation. The beach is calm and good for families, and outside the summer months rates drop a long way. In peak season it is one of the priciest stretches of coast in Brazil.

**Canasvieiras and Ingleses** in the north are the family answer. Calm, shallow, warmer water, restaurants and supermarkets on the doorstep, and the biggest concentration of affordable hotels in this guide. They are developed and busy rather than wild — which is exactly the point if you are travelling with children.

**Campeche** is the laid-back option, with a long open beach, a surf culture and a visible digital-nomad community in the cafés. Quieter than Lagoa, more spread out, and best with a car.

**The Centro** is the honest budget and transport choice. It has the cheapest rooms, the main bus terminal and the Mercado Público, and it is where several of the strongest-value 4★ hotels in this guide sit. But be clear-eyed: **the centre is not on the best beaches**. You will be 20 to 45 minutes from the sand people come for, and you will want a hire car or a lot of patience with the buses. Brazil drives on the right, and hiring a car is the single biggest upgrade you can make to a Florianópolis trip.

## When to Go — the Honest Version

Florianópolis is a **seasonal beach destination** and pretending otherwise would do you no favours. **December to February** is the Brazilian summer: hot, brilliant, and packed. Rates multiply, New Year and Carnival are the two most extreme weeks of the year, minimum stays appear, and the north-island road can crawl. If that energy is what you want, book months ahead and budget properly.

**March to May and September to November** are the sweet spot. The sea is still swimmable at the edges of that window, the beaches empty out, restaurants take walk-ins and hotel rates fall to the sort of numbers in this guide. This is when the £14-a-night pousadas and the £49 four-star rooms actually exist.

**June to August** is winter. It is cool — jacket weather in the evening, and too cold for most people to swim — and some beach businesses close. In exchange you get the lowest prices of the year, the whales, the biggest surf swell, and an island that belongs to the people who live there.

## Florianópolis Hotels FAQs

**How much does a hotel in Florianópolis cost per night?**

**Do UK passport holders need a visa for Brazil?**
No. UK passport holders do not need a visa for short tourist stays in Brazil and can normally stay up to 90 days. You will need a passport valid for the length of your stay, and border officers may ask to see proof of onward travel and accommodation. Entry rules do change, so check the current UK government advice for Brazil before you book and again in the week before you fly.

**What currency does Florianópolis use?**
The Brazilian Real, written BRL or R$. Every price in this guide is shown in pounds as a rough conversion so UK readers can compare like for like — you will be charged in reais on the ground, and the pound-to-real rate moves, so treat our figures as a guide rather than a fixed quote. Cards work almost everywhere on the island, including beach kiosks; carry a little cash for buses and small pousadas.

**Are there direct flights from the UK to Florianópolis?**
No, and it is worth planning for. There is no direct UK service to Florianópolis. The usual route is London to São Paulo Guarulhos (GRU) direct in around 11 and a half hours, then a domestic hop of roughly an hour to Florianópolis (FLN). The alternative is a European connection through Lisbon or Madrid to São Paulo and then the same short hop. Allow a full travel day in each direction, and give yourself a comfortable connection window at GRU because immigration and the terminal change take time.

**When is the best time to visit Florianópolis?**
Florianópolis is in the southern hemisphere and it is a genuinely seasonal beach destination. December to February is peak summer — hot, packed, and by far the most expensive, with New Year and Carnival the two most extreme weeks of the year. March to May and September to November are the sweet spot: warm enough for the beach, far quieter and much cheaper. June to August is winter — cool, often grey, and too cold for most people to swim, though it is when the whales come and when hotel rates are at their lowest.

**Is Florianópolis expensive?**
It depends entirely on when you go. In the Brazilian summer, and especially between Christmas and Carnival, Floripa is one of the priciest places in Brazil — hotels raise rates several times over, restaurants queue out of the door and many pousadas impose minimum stays. Outside that window it is very reasonable: this guide has 36 budget properties from £14 a night, and 4★ hotels near good beaches from under £55. Shifting your trip by a month is the single biggest saving available.

**Which beach or area should I stay in?**
Lagoa da Conceição if you want nightlife, restaurants and the most central island base. Jurerê Internacional for upmarket beach clubs and a smart, expensive scene. Canasvieiras and Ingleses in the north for calm, family-friendly sand and the biggest cluster of affordable hotels. Campeche for a laid-back surf and digital-nomad feel. The Centro for the cheapest rooms and the best bus links — but not for the beaches, which are a drive away.

**Is the centre of Florianópolis a good place to stay?**
It is the practical choice, not the beautiful one. The Centro sits on the island side of the Hercílio Luz bridge and holds the highest concentration of cheap and mid-range hotels, the main bus terminal and the Mercado Público. What it does not have is a good beach — you are 20 to 45 minutes by car or bus from the sand people come for. Stay there if you are on a budget, moving on quickly, or arriving late; stay at a beach if the beach is the point.

**Is Lagoa da Conceição a good base?**
For most first-time visitors, yes. Lagoa sits on a big saltwater lagoon in the middle of the island, which makes it the shortest hop to both coasts, and it has the best concentration of bars, restaurants and nightlife outside the peak-season beach clubs. It is also the watersports hub — kitesurfing, stand-up paddle and windsurfing all happen on the lagoon. The downside is that it is not itself an ocean beach: Praia Mole and Joaquina are a short drive over the hill.

**Is Jurerê Internacional worth the money?**
Only if the scene is what you want. Jurerê Internacional is the upmarket north-island enclave of villas and high-end beach clubs, and in January it is one of the most expensive stretches of coast in Brazil. The beach itself is calm and lovely and it works well for families outside peak weeks. Outside the summer months the beach clubs quieten right down and rates drop sharply, which is when it makes the most sense for a UK budget.

**Are Canasvieiras and Ingleses good for families?**
They are the standard family answer on the island. Both are north-coast beaches with calm, shallow, warmer water, plenty of restaurants and supermarkets, and the biggest concentration of affordable hotels in this guide — several 4★ properties here come in under £90 a night. They are busy, developed and popular with Argentinian and Brazilian holidaymakers rather than quiet or wild, which is exactly what many families want.

**Do I need a car in Florianópolis?**
Ideally yes. Florianópolis is an island roughly 50km long and the beaches are spread all around its edge, so the difference between having a car and not having one is the difference between seeing five beaches and seeing one. Buses do run everywhere and are cheap, but they are slow and often route through the central terminal. Uber works well in the built-up areas and is a reasonable middle path if you only plan a couple of trips. Brazil drives on the right.

**How do I get from Florianópolis airport to my hotel?**
Florianópolis airport (FLN) is on the island itself, in the south, with a modern terminal opened in recent years. Uber is the simplest option for most visitors and reaches the Centro in around 15 to 20 minutes, Lagoa in around 30 and the northern beaches in 40 to 60 depending on traffic. Official taxis wait outside arrivals, and the car hire desks are in the terminal if you have booked one. Summer traffic on the north-island road can be slow, so allow extra.

**Is Florianópolis safe for tourists?**
Florianópolis is generally regarded as one of Brazil's safer cities and it regularly tops the country's quality-of-life rankings, which is a large part of why so many Brazilians move there. That does not mean no precautions: normal city-in-Brazil sense applies. Keep your phone out of sight while walking, do not leave bags on the beach while you swim, use Uber late at night rather than walking through quiet areas, and keep valuables in the hotel safe. Read the UK government's travel advice for Brazil before you go.

**How many beaches does Florianópolis have?**
More than 40, which is the single fact that explains the island. They are genuinely different from one another: big Atlantic surf on the east coast at Mole and Joaquina, calm shallow water on the north coast at Canasvieiras and Jurerê, wilder and emptier sand in the south at Lagoinha do Leste and Naufragados. You will not see them all in a short trip — pick a coast and work along it.

**Where is the best surfing in Florianópolis?**
Praia Mole and Joaquina are the two best-known surf beaches and Joaquina has hosted international competitions; Praia da Barra da Lagoa, Campeche and Praia Brava also break well. The biggest, most consistent swell tends to arrive in the southern-hemisphere autumn and winter, roughly April to September, which conveniently is also the quiet season for hotels. Beginners will find surf schools and board hire at Mole, Barra da Lagoa and Campeche.

**Can you see whales in Florianópolis?**
Yes — southern right whales come to the Santa Catarina coast to calve and are commonly seen roughly between July and November, with the peak usually in the middle of that window. Boat trips run from the south of the island and from nearby coastal towns, and in a good year the whales can sometimes be seen from the shore. It is a genuine reason to consider a winter trip, when hotel prices are at their lowest.

**What is the sandboarding at Joaquina?**
Behind Joaquina beach sit tall white sand dunes, and boards are hired at the roadside for a small fee so you can slide down them. It is a Floripa institution, takes half an hour and costs very little, and the walk back up the dune is the hard part. Go early or late in the day in summer, because the sand gets very hot.

**Do I need to speak Portuguese in Florianópolis?**
Brazilians speak Portuguese, not Spanish, and outside the smarter hotels and the international side of Jurerê, English is not widely spoken. A handful of phrases goes a long way: bom dia, boa tarde, por favor, obrigado (men) or obrigada (women), a conta por favor. Download an offline translation app for menus and bus timetables. Spanish is more useful than you might expect in summer, because of the large Argentinian holiday crowd.

**Can I drink the tap water in Florianópolis?**
Most visitors stick to bottled or filtered water, and that is the sensible default in Brazil generally. The mains supply is treated, but building tanks and older pipework mean quality varies property to property. Bottled water is cheap and sold everywhere, and many hotels have filtered dispensers — carry a refillable bottle and you will spend almost nothing.

**How many days do you need in Florianópolis?**
Four to five days to do the beaches justice. With three you will see your own stretch of coast and one or two others; with five you can cover a north-coast day, an east-coast surf day, the Lagoa, the historic centre and the Mercado Público, and still have a slow day. Add a sixth if you want the Lagoinha do Leste hike or a boat trip up the Costa da Lagoa. Anything under three nights and the driving starts to dominate the trip.

**What is there to do in Florianópolis besides the beach?**
The historic centre has the Mercado Público for oysters and seafood, the colonial-era Praça XV and cathedral, and the striking Hercílio Luz bridge, which was restored and reopened to pedestrians. Beyond that: the hike to Lagoinha do Leste, the boat trip up the Costa da Lagoa to a restaurant reachable only by water or on foot, sandboarding at Joaquina, kitesurfing on the lagoon, and the Portuguese-founded fishing villages of Santo Antônio de Lisboa and Ribeirão da Ilha, which are the best places on the island for oysters.

**What should I eat in Florianópolis?**
Seafood, and specifically oysters — Santa Catarina produces the large majority of Brazil's farmed oysters and Ribeirão da Ilha is the place to eat them, at waterfront restaurants in a village of Azorean colonial houses. Beyond that: sequência de camarão, a set-menu procession of prawn dishes served in several forms; fresh fish at the Mercado Público; and the pastel and açaí that turn up at every beach kiosk.

**Is Florianópolis good for digital nomads?**
It has become one of Brazil's main bases for remote workers, particularly around Lagoa da Conceição and Campeche, where there are co-working spaces, cafés set up for laptops and a visible international community. The draws are the beaches, the safety record and the cost of living outside peak season. The trade-offs are patchy internet at the cheapest pousadas and a rental market that goes wild every December.

Very often, yes.
