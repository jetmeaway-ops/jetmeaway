**Is Sibiu cheap for UK visitors?**
Yes — Sibiu is one of the best-value city breaks in Europe for British travellers. Yes — Sibiu keeps genuinely cheap beds close to the centre. **What currency is used in Sibiu — euros or lei?**
Romania uses the Romanian leu (lei / RON), not the euro — a common surprise, since Romania is in the EU but kept its own currency. It joined full Schengen in 2025, so there are no more air or land border checks with the rest of the zone, but you still spend lei, not euros. **What is the Bridge of Lies in Sibiu?**
The Bridge of Lies (Podul Minciunilor) is Sibiu's most famous small landmark — a cast-iron footbridge from 1859, one of the oldest of its kind in Romania, linking the Upper and Lower Towns beside Piața Mică. Local legend says it creaks or collapses when someone tells a lie on it, which is why generations of merchants and courting couples have crossed it carefully. It is free to visit, right in the centre, and a two-minute walk from most old-town hotels.

**What are the eyes of Sibiu?**
The eyes of Sibiu are the narrow, hooded roof windows set into the steep tiled roofs across the old town, which look uncannily like half-closed eyes watching the streets below. They are ventilation openings for the attics of the Saxon merchant houses, but their almond shape gives the whole city a famously watchful, slightly surreal character. Spotting them as you wander the three squares is one of the simple pleasures of a Sibiu visit.

**Is Sibiu walkable or do I need transport?**
Sibiu's historic core is very walkable — the Upper Town, the Lower Town and the three linked squares form a compact area you can cross on foot in 15–20 minutes, much of it cobbled and pedestrian-friendly. For anything further out, the city has cheap local buses, and the airport is a short taxi ride. Most visitors barely use transport at all beyond the airport run, which is exactly why staying a few streets out from the squares still works so well here.

**When is the best time to visit Sibiu?**
Late spring (May–June) and September are the sweet spot — warm, pleasant days and thinner crowds. July and August are busiest, and coincide with the huge Sibiu International Theatre Festival and the ASTRA film festival, which are wonderful but push up demand and rates. December is magical, when Piața Mare hosts one of Romania's oldest and prettiest Christmas markets, though rooms fill fast for it. Winters are cold and atmospheric, with snow suiting the old town beautifully.

Add a night if you want to pair Sibiu with Brașov, about an hour away. Sibiu packs a lot of beauty into a small, walkable area.

Just outside town, the ASTRA open-air museum recreates traditional Romanian village life across a huge forested park. The Făgăraș mountains and Saxon fortified churches make superb day trips.

**How do I visit the ASTRA open-air museum from Sibiu?**
The ASTRA Museum of Traditional Folk Civilisation is one of the largest open-air museums in Europe, set in the forested Dumbrava park about 4 km south of the centre. It gathers hundreds of relocated traditional houses, watermills, workshops and churches from across Romania along shaded walking trails around a lake. It is reachable by local bus, taxi or a longer walk, and easily fills half a day — a superb, good-value family outing on a fine day.

**What food and drink is Sibiu known for?**
Sibiu sits in a rich culinary region and was named a European Region of Gastronomy — expect hearty Transylvanian and Saxon dishes, smoked cheeses, sausages, bean soups and slow-cooked meats, alongside excellent Romanian wines from the nearby Târnave vineyards. Local markets and cellar restaurants around the squares serve all of it at prices that barely dent a budget. A good meal with wine here costs a fraction of what you would pay in Western Europe.

**Do people speak English in Sibiu?**
Yes — English is widely spoken by younger people, in hotels, restaurants and tourist sites across Sibiu, and the city has a strong German heritage so some older residents speak German too. Signage in the tourist core is often multilingual. You will have no trouble getting by in English for a city break, though learning a few words of Romanian (bună ziua, mulțumesc) is always appreciated.

**How far is Brasov from Sibiu?**
Brașov is roughly 140 km east of Sibiu, about 2 to 2.5 hours by car or train through beautiful Carpathian scenery. Many travellers pair the two Saxon towns on one Transylvanian trip, using Sibiu for its refined old town and Brașov as the base for Bran (Dracula's) Castle and Peleș Castle. Both are excellent value, and the drive between them passes fortified churches and mountain views worth stopping for.
