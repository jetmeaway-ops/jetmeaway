## The Scout's Take: Old Town, New Town or the Hills?

**Old Town**, west of the Kinta River, is Ipoh's postcard: colonial shophouses, Concubine Lane, the mural alleys, the grand railway station and the original white-coffee kopitiams. It's the most walkable, most atmospheric base and where most first-timers should stay.

**New Town**, east of the river, is the busier commercial grid — more of the city's hawker centres, dim sum halls and mid-range hotels, still a short walk or a couple-of-pounds Grab from Old Town. Base here for food and value.

**The hills and Tambun**, to the north-east, are where the resorts and the hot springs are — The Banjaran's thermal villas, TUI BLUE The Haven's lakeside quiet, and the Lost World of Tambun theme park. Stay out here for nature, spa and family attractions, and Grab into town for the heritage and food.

For a first Ipoh trip, base in or near Old Town; for a budget food weekend, New Town; for a spa or family escape, the Tambun hills.

## Beyond the Hotel — Ipoh's Essentials

A few experiences worth planning your stay around:

- **Concubine Lane (Lorong Panglima)** — the narrow tin-era alley in Old Town, best in the late afternoon for snacks, souvenirs and photos.
- **Sam Poh Tong, Perak Tong and Kek Lok Tong cave temples** — Buddhist shrines inside limestone caverns in the hills ringing the city, most free or donation entry.
- **Ipoh railway station** — the 1917 mock-Tudor "Taj Mahal of Ipoh", a colonial landmark on the edge of Old Town.
- **The Ernest Zacharevic murals and Mural Art's Lane** — large-scale street art scattered through Old Town, an easy self-guided walk.
- **Sin Yoon Loong and Nam Heong kopitiams** — the heritage cafes credited with Ipoh white coffee; pair with toast and a soft-boiled egg.
- **Lost World of Tambun** — hot springs, water park, tin-valley theme park and petting zoo, north-east of the centre.
- **Kellie's Castle** — the unfinished mansion of a Scottish planter, a photogenic ruin about 20 minutes south.
- **Bean sprout chicken (nga choy kai)** — Ipoh's signature dish; Lou Wong and Onn Kee on Jalan Yau Tet Shin are the famous names.

**How much does a hotel in Ipoh cost per night in 2026?**
Ipoh is one of the cheapest city breaks in Malaysia. Budget rooms start around £13 a night at places like Ipoh Boutique Hotel and Nova Hotel, clean mid-range 3-4 star hotels run roughly £25-45, and the two headline luxury stays — WEIL Hotel in the centre and The Banjaran Hotsprings Retreat in the hills — sit around £84 and £350 respectively. **What is the cheapest good hotel in Ipoh?**

**Where should I stay in Ipoh for the first time?**
Old Town and New Town, either side of the Kinta River, are the heart of Ipoh — walkable to Concubine Lane, the colonial railway station, the white-coffee kopitiams and the mural lanes. Base yourself here (WEIL Hotel, M Boutique, Cititel Express, Regalodge or any Old Town budget pick) and you can walk to most of the food and heritage. For the Lost World theme park, hot springs and cave temples, the Tambun area to the north-east is better.

**Is Ipoh worth visiting?**
Very much so, and it is still under-touristed compared to Kuala Lumpur and Penang. Ipoh offers limestone cave temples (Sam Poh Tong, Perak Tong, Kek Lok Tong), the atmospheric Old Town with Concubine Lane and Ernest Zacharevic murals, the birthplace of Ipoh white coffee, cheap Michelin-level street food (bean sprout chicken, dim sum, curry mee), and the Lost World of Tambun hot springs — all at a fraction of big-city prices and two-and-a-half hours by train from KL.

**What is the cheapest area to stay in Ipoh?**
Old Town and the streets around Jalan Sultan Iskandar and Jalan Yang Kalsom hold the biggest cluster of budget hotels and guesthouses, many from £13-25 a night. Tambun, near the hot springs and Lost World, has a few cheap options too. Because Ipoh is compact and Grab rides across town cost a couple of pounds, you can book the cheapest bed and still reach everything easily.

**How do I get to Ipoh from the UK?**
There are no direct flights from the UK to Ipoh. Fly into Kuala Lumpur (KUL) via a Gulf hub or a one-stop European connection, then take the KTM ETS train from KL Sentral to Ipoh (about 2.5 hours, from a few pounds) or drive (roughly 2 hours). Ipoh's own Sultan Azlan Shah Airport (IPH) handles mainly domestic and regional flights, so most international visitors arrive by train or road.

**Is Ipoh a good budget destination for families?**
Yes. Rooms are cheap, food is cheap, and the Lost World of Tambun water park, hot springs and petting zoo are built for kids. Sunway Lost World Hotel sits inside the theme park, Symphony Suites and RPGC Garden Hotel offer family rooms with pools, and self-catering suites like Ipoh Anderson Town Suites give families space for well under £45 a night.

**How many days do you need in Ipoh?**
Two to three days is ideal. Day one for Old Town heritage, Concubine Lane, the murals and a white-coffee kopitiam crawl; day two for the cave temples (Sam Poh Tong, Perak Tong, Kek Lok Tong) and Kellie's Castle; and a third day for the Lost World of Tambun hot springs or a food-focused wander through New Town. Many visitors add Ipoh as a two-night stop between Kuala Lumpur and Penang on the train line.

**What is Ipoh white coffee?**
Ipoh white coffee is the city's most famous export — coffee beans roasted with palm-oil margarine (rather than the sugar-and-margarine mix used elsewhere), producing a smoother, less bitter brew served with condensed milk. It was created in Ipoh's old kopitiams; Sin Yoon Loong and Nam Heong in Old Town are the two heritage cafes credited with it. Almost every hotel breakfast in town serves a version.

**Which Ipoh hotels have hot springs?**
The Banjaran Hotsprings Retreat is the luxury geothermal option — private hot-spring villas set among limestone cliffs, around £350 a night. For a family-priced alternative, the Sunway Lost World Hotel gives you access to the Lost World of Tambun hot springs and water park, and several Tambun-area budget hotels (Nova Hotel, Tambun Warm Hotel) sit near the public hot-spring attractions.

**Are cave temples near the hotels in Ipoh?**
Yes — Ipoh is ringed by limestone hills honeycombed with cave temples, and none are far from a hotel. Sam Poh Tong and Kek Lok Tong sit in the Gunung Rapat area south of the centre, while Perak Tong is a short drive north. From any Old Town or Tambun hotel they are 10-20 minutes by Grab, and entry to most is free or a small donation.

**Do Ipoh budget hotels include breakfast?**
Many do, and where they don't, breakfast in Ipoh is the reason to skip it anyway — a plate of dim sum, a bowl of bean sprout chicken or Ipoh white coffee and toast from a kopitiam costs a couple of pounds. Cheaper hotels like Cititel Express, Regalodge and Hotel Seri Malaysia include simple breakfasts; guesthouses often don't, but the best food is 30 seconds out the door.

**Is Ipoh safe for tourists?**
Ipoh is one of Malaysia's calmer, safer cities, with low crime and a relaxed pace. Normal precautions apply — watch belongings in busy markets, use Grab after dark rather than walking unlit streets — but solo travellers, couples and families generally find it very comfortable. English is widely understood and the city is easy to navigate.

**What is the best time of year to visit Ipoh?**
Ipoh sits on Malaysia's drier west coast, so December to February and June to August tend to be the most reliable, while September to November is the wettest stretch. It is hot and humid year-round (28-33°C), with short afternoon downpours common. There is no bad season — just carry an umbrella and plan cave temples and markets for the mornings.

**Which Ipoh hotel is best for couples?**
For a splurge, The Banjaran Hotsprings Retreat's private thermal villas are the romantic benchmark in Perak. In town, WEIL Hotel offers rooftop-pool city views, M Boutique brings quirky vintage design near Old Town, and Art Cottage or Hotel French Ipoh give small-boutique charm for well under £30. Ipoh's walkable heritage streets and cafe culture suit a slow couples' weekend.

**Can I visit Ipoh on a day trip from Kuala Lumpur?**
You can — the ETS train from KL Sentral takes about 2.5 hours each way — but Ipoh rewards an overnight stay. Cave temples, Old Town heritage and the food scene need more than a few hours, and with budget rooms from £13 there is little reason to rush back. Most travellers stay one to two nights, often en route to Penang.

**Are there hotels inside the Lost World of Tambun?**
Yes — the Sunway Lost World Hotel sits inside the Lost World of Tambun complex, so you get direct access to the water park, hot springs and petting zoo, ideal for families. It is one of the pricier mid-range options at around £78, but it saves transfers and lets you enter the park early. Cheaper Tambun-area hotels are a short Grab ride from the gates.

**What food is Ipoh famous for?**
Ipoh is a genuine food capital. Signature dishes include bean sprout chicken (nga choy kai), Ipoh white coffee, dim sum (the city has some of Malaysia's best), curry mee, caramel custard, Tambun pomelo and heong peng biscuits. Old Town and New Town kopitiams and hawker centres serve most of it for a few pounds — food is a core reason to stay overnight rather than day-trip.

**Do I need a car in Ipoh?**
Not really. The Old Town and New Town core is walkable, and Grab rides across the city cost only a pound or two, making a car unnecessary for most visitors. A car helps if you want to combine outlying cave temples, Kellie's Castle and Tambun in one efficient day, but many travellers simply chain Grab rides or join a half-day tour instead.

**Which Ipoh hotels have a swimming pool on a budget?**

**Is Concubine Lane in Ipoh worth seeing?**
Yes — Concubine Lane (Lorong Panglima) is the most atmospheric street in Old Town, a narrow tin-mining-era alley now lined with souvenir stalls, snack vendors, cafes and photo spots. It is touristy but genuinely fun, especially in the late afternoon, and it anchors a walkable heritage zone that includes the mural lanes, Han Chin Pet Soo museum and the colonial railway station a few minutes away.

**What is the most luxurious hotel in Ipoh?**
The Banjaran Hotsprings Retreat is the clear luxury benchmark — private villas with natural geothermal pools set against limestone cliffs and jungle, around £350 a night.
