## The Scout's Take: Makati, BGC, Ermita or Pasay?

**Makati** is the usual first choice and the one we'd recommend to most first-time visitors. It's the metro's business and dining heart, with Greenbelt and Glorietta malls, the Ayala Museum, the best hotel range at every price, and the Poblacion neighbourhood — Manila's most interesting bar-and-restaurant strip — on its edge. It feels ordered and safe by Manila standards.

**Bonifacio Global City (BGC)** is the newest part of the metro: wide clean pavements, parks, modern towers and a genuine walkability that the rest of Manila does not have. If tidy and comfortable matters more to you than being central, stay here. The trade-off is distance from the old-city sights.

**Ermita and Malate** are the traditional tourist belt — cheap hotels within walking distance of Intramuros, Rizal Park, the National Museum complex and Manila Bay's sunset. Honest assessment: parts of it are gritty and run-down, it quietens quickly after dark, and you'll want Grab rather than walking at night. For history-focused travellers on a tight budget, it still makes sense.

**Pasay and the Mall of Asia area** give you the bay, the biggest mall in the country, the casino resorts and — crucially — the shortest run to the airport. If you have a 6am departure, sleep here.

**Quezon City, Ortigas and Cubao** are where locals live and work. Less polished, often cheaper, well served by the MRT, and fine if you're staying longer or visiting on business.

## What to See and Do in Manila

**Intramuros** is the reason to give Manila a proper day: the walled Spanish colonial old city, with **Fort Santiago** at its riverside corner and the UNESCO-listed **San Agustin Church** — the oldest stone church in the Philippines — at its heart. Walk the walls, hire a bamboo bike, and don't rush it. Just outside, **Rizal Park** and the **National Museum complex** (natural history, anthropology and fine arts, with free entry) make an easy half day. Come back in the evening for the **Manila Bay sunset**, which is genuinely one of the best in Asia.

**Binondo**, across the Pasig, is the world's oldest Chinatown — founded in 1594 — and the best eating in the city if you're willing to wander. The **Cultural Center of the Philippines** on the bayfront covers theatre, dance and design, and the **Ayala Museum** in Makati is the polished, air-conditioned way to get the country's history in two hours. For shopping and escaping the heat, the malls are institutions in their own right: **SM Mall of Asia** in Pasay is one of the largest in the world, and **Greenbelt** in Makati is the pleasant one. Nightlife clusters in **Poblacion** in Makati and in **BGC**.

Out of the city, **Tagaytay and the Taal Volcano** are about two hours south for a crater-lake-inside-a-volcano view and cooler air; **Corregidor Island** is a ferry ride into Manila Bay's WWII history; and **Pagsanjan Falls** is a canoe trip through a river gorge. All of them are traffic-dependent, so leave early.

Before you book, read the [UK Foreign Office travel advice for the Philippines](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/philippines) for current entry rules, safety and weather warnings, and browse the [official Philippines tourism site](https://philippines.travel/) for what's on while you're there. Between them you'll have the practical and the inspirational sides covered.

## Being Honest About Manila's Traffic

We can't write a Manila hotel guide without saying this plainly: **the traffic is legendarily bad**, among the worst in the world, and it will shape your trip more than anything else. A journey that looks like fifteen minutes on a map can take an hour and a half. **Allow 1.5-2 hours or more to reach the airport at peak times**, and don't trust an optimistic map estimate — leave earlier than feels sensible.

The practical answers: use **Grab** rather than street taxis (fixed fares, no arguments, no meter games); use the **LRT and MRT** lines when they go your way, because they're cheap and they bypass the roads entirely; travel outside roughly 07:00-10:00 and 16:00-20:00 where you can; and **pick your hotel by where you'll actually spend your time**, because in Manila a good location is worth more than a good room. Manila's airport also has **separate terminals that aren't walkable from one another** — confirm your terminal with the airline, and if you have to switch terminals, allow hours, not minutes.

One more honest point: many visitors treat Manila as a **1-2 day gateway** before flying on to Palawan, Boracay, Cebu or Siargao — and that is a perfectly good plan. If your trip is about islands, give the capital two nights for Intramuros and the food, then get on the plane.

## Staying Safe in Manila

Manila is not dangerous for ordinary visitors, but it is a big, poor, crowded city and petty crime happens. Sensible precautions cover almost all of it: take **Grab rather than flagging a street taxi**, keep your **phone and wallet out of sight** while walking, don't wear obvious jewellery, **avoid quiet or unlit streets late at night**, don't accept food or drinks from strangers, and use the official taxi ranks or a pre-booked transfer at the airport rather than accepting offers inside the terminal. Stick to Makati, BGC, Ortigas and the main malls after dark and you'll be as comfortable as in any large Asian city. Check the UK Foreign Office advice for anything current before you fly.

## Manila Hotels FAQs

**What is the cheapest area to stay in Manila?**
Ermita and Malate (the old tourist belt near Intramuros and Manila Bay) and Pasay near the airport have the cheapest beds in the metro, with real bookable rooms from around £12-£16 a night. Quezon City and Cubao are close behind. Makati is a little pricier for equivalent quality but still has clean, air-conditioned rooms from about £16-£22 — and for most first-time visitors the small extra cost buys a much easier, safer base.

**How cheap are hotels in Manila?**
Very cheap by UK standards. On midweek dates the budget tier in this guide runs from £12 a night (Frank Lloyd Apartelle) up to about £23 (Guijo Suites Makati and iHotel Uptown BGC), with most clean air-conditioned rooms landing in the £16-£22 band. That is a private room with air-con, not a hostel bunk.

**How much does a budget hotel in Manila cost per night in 2026?**
Roughly £12-£23 a night on midweek dates for a real, currently-bookable private room. The £12-£16 end is basic apartelle and RedDoorz-style accommodation in Pasay, Alabang and Quezon City; £18-£23 buys a tidy air-conditioned room in Makati, BGC or Ortigas with a lift, hot water and reliable Wi-Fi.

**What is the most expensive hotel in this guide?**
Conrad Manila at around £250 a night is the highest from-price here — a five-star bayside hotel in Pasay beside the Mall of Asia complex. Okada Manila is next at around £192. Everything else in the luxury tier sits between roughly £54 and £130, which is remarkable value for genuine five-star rooms.

**Which area should I stay in when visiting Manila?**
Makati is the usual first choice — the safest, most convenient mix of restaurants, malls, transport and hotels at every price. BGC is newer, cleaner and the most walkable part of the metro. Ermita and Malate put you within walking distance of Intramuros and the bay but are gritty in places. Pasay and the Mall of Asia area suit bay views and early flights. Quezon City and Ortigas are more local and often cheaper.

**Is Manila safe for tourists?**
Generally fine for visitors who take normal big-city precautions, but petty crime — pickpocketing, bag snatching and scams — does happen. Use Grab rather than street taxis, keep valuables out of sight, avoid quiet streets late at night, and stick to well-populated areas like Makati, BGC and the main malls after dark. Check the UK Foreign Office travel advice before you go.

**How bad is the traffic in Manila, really?**
It is genuinely one of the worst traffic situations in the world, and you should plan around it. A 5km journey can take an hour at peak times. Allow 1.5-2 hours or more to reach the airport at rush hour, avoid travelling roughly 07:00-10:00 and 16:00-20:00 where possible, and use the LRT/MRT lines when they go your way.

**How long does it take to get to Manila airport from Makati?**
Off-peak, 25-40 minutes. At peak, budget 1.5-2 hours or more. Always leave far earlier than your map app suggests.

**Are Manila's airport terminals connected?**
Not on foot. NAIA's terminals are spread around the airport, and moving between them means a road transfer through Manila traffic. If your connection changes terminal, allow several hours, and confirm your terminal with the airline before you travel.

**Do UK passport holders need a visa for the Philippines?**
British passport holders can normally enter visa-free for tourism for up to 30 days. You'll usually need a passport valid at least six months beyond your stay and proof of onward or return travel. Rules change — check the current UK government advice for the Philippines and with the Philippine embassy before booking.

**Are there direct flights from the UK to Manila?**
No. There is no direct scheduled UK-Manila service, so you connect — most commonly via the Gulf with Emirates (Dubai), Qatar Airways (Doha) or Etihad (Abu Dhabi), or through an Asian hub such as Singapore, Hong Kong or Bangkok. Total journey time is typically around 16-18 hours or more.

**What currency is used in the Philippines?**
The Philippine Peso (PHP, ₱). Pound figures in this guide are rough conversions of live from-prices, shown for comparison only. Cards work in malls, chains and mid-range hotels; carry small peso notes for jeepneys, markets and neighbourhood shops.

**When is the best time to visit Manila?**
December to April is the dry season and the most comfortable window, with December to February the pick. Roughly June to October is the wet season and coincides with typhoon season — heavy rain, street flooding and occasional flight disruption are all realistic. Take travel insurance.

**Does it flood in Manila?**
Yes. In the wet season heavy rain can flood low-lying streets across the metro within an hour, and typhoons occasionally shut down the city and the airport. Newer districts like BGC drain better than older parts of Ermita and Malate.

**Should I use Grab or taxis in Manila?**
Use Grab. It fixes the fare, records the trip and removes the meter arguments that catch tourists in street taxis. It's cheap by UK standards and available across the metro. If you must take a taxi, insist on the meter, and at the airport use the official ranks or a pre-booked transfer.

**Is English widely spoken in Manila?**
Yes, and it's a big advantage for UK visitors. English is an official language, used in signage, menus and business, and spoken confidently by most people you'll deal with. There's essentially no language barrier.

**Can you drink the tap water in Manila?**
Stick to bottled or filtered water. The tap water is treated but visitors commonly get upset stomachs from it. Bottled water is very cheap and most hotels provide it.

**How many days do you need in Manila?**
Two to three. A day for Intramuros, Fort Santiago, San Agustin Church and the National Museum complex; a day for Makati or BGC, the Ayala Museum and the malls; an evening for the bay sunset and Poblacion. Add a day for Tagaytay and Taal or Corregidor.

**Is Manila worth visiting or should I go straight to the islands?**
Honestly, both plans work. Manila is big, hot and congested and it isn't the country's scenic highlight — plenty of visitors use it as a 1-2 night gateway before flying to Palawan, Boracay, Cebu or Siargao, and that's sensible. But for history and food, Intramuros, the National Museum complex, Binondo and Poblacion genuinely reward two days. Give it two nights, then go to the islands.

**What is there to see in Manila?**
Intramuros — the walled Spanish colonial old city with Fort Santiago and the UNESCO-listed San Agustin Church — plus Rizal Park, the National Museum complex (free entry), Manila Bay sunsets, Binondo (the world's oldest Chinatown), the Cultural Center of the Philippines, the Ayala Museum, and the enormous SM Mall of Asia and Greenbelt malls.

**What day trips can you do from Manila?**

Makati. It has the widest hotel range at every price, the best restaurant density, Greenbelt and Glorietta, the Ayala Museum and easy access to the rest of the metro. BGC is cleaner, newer and far nicer to walk around, so choose it if modern comfort matters more than being central.

Excellent value. Five-star rooms start from around £54 (City Garden Grand Hotel) and clean budget rooms from £12, both well below Bangkok, Singapore or Tokyo equivalents. Food, Grab rides and museum entry are all cheap. The real cost of Manila is your time, lost to the traffic.

The right. Take extra care crossing — traffic is dense, motorbikes filter constantly and crossings aren't always respected. Most visitors don't drive here at all; Grab and the LRT/MRT are far less stressful.
