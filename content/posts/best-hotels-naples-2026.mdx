## The Scout's Take: Where to Stay in Naples

Naples rewards you for choosing the right neighbourhood, because the city changes character block to block.

The **historic centre (Centro Storico)**, threaded by Spaccanapoli and Via dei Tribunali, is where first-timers should stay — you wake up among the churches, the street shrines, the sfogliatella bakeries and the legendary pizzerias, with the Archaeological Museum a short walk uphill. It's atmospheric, walkable and central; it's also loud and lived-in, which is the point.

The **Santa Lucia and Chiaia seafront** (Via Partenope, the Lungomare) is the smarter, calmer face of the city — the grand old hotels, the Castel dell'Ovo, the passeggiata along the water, and the closest base to the port for boats to Capri and the coast. You pay more here, and the sea-view rooms are worth requesting.

**Around Napoli Centrale / Piazza Garibaldi** is where the cheap beds cluster and where the Pompeii and Sorrento trains leave from — unbeatable for value and day trips, grittier after dark. **Vomero**, up the hill by funicular, is the quiet residential option with the best rooftop views over the whole bay.

For a first Naples trip, the historic centre. For sea views and calm, the seafront. For the lowest prices and the easiest Pompeii start, the station. The [budget tier](#budget) below is where £31–80 a night is still real.

## Beyond the Hotel — Naples's Essentials

A few experiences worth planning around your stay:

- **A Margherita at its birthplace** — the historic centre around Via dei Tribunali is the global home of pizza. A true Neapolitan Margherita costs about €5–7; expect queues at the famous names and superb quality almost everywhere else.
- **The National Archaeological Museum** — the finest collection of Roman antiquities on earth, most of it excavated from Pompeii and Herculaneum. See it before or after the ruins to make sense of them.
- **Walk Spaccanapoli end to end** — the dead-straight ancient street that splits the old town, past the Cappella Sansevero and its astonishing Veiled Christ sculpture.
- **Pompeii and Vesuvius** — 35 minutes by Circumvesuviana train; combine the ruins with a bus up to the crater rim of the volcano that buried them.
- **A boat to Capri or the Amalfi Coast** — ferries from Molo Beverello reach Capri in under 90 minutes and Sorrento/Positano/Amalfi in summer; the most scenic way to see the bay.
- **The underground city (Napoli Sotterranea)** — Greek-Roman tunnels and cisterns 40 metres below the historic centre.
- **Sunset on the Lungomare** — the traffic-free seafront promenade from Castel dell'Ovo, with Vesuvius across the water and a sfogliatella in hand.

## Naples Hotels FAQs

**What is the cheapest hotel in Naples?**
On the live searches behind this guide, B&B MIRAGLIA is the cheapest bookable stay at around £31 a night — a central B&B with more than 1,100 guest reviews. Up Hotel (from ~£47) and Hotel Poker (from ~£49) are the next steps up. **How much does a budget hotel in Naples cost per night in 2026?**
Roughly £31–81 a night across the budget tier on midweek dates, with the true floor around £31 (B&B MIRAGLIA) and most decent 3-star and 4-star budget rooms landing in the £55–80 band. Weekends and the summer peak run higher, and the big saint's-day and festival weekends higher still. Naples remains noticeably cheaper than Rome, Florence or Venice for an equivalent room.

**Where is the cheapest area to stay in Naples on a budget?**
The streets around Napoli Centrale / Piazza Garibaldi hold the highest concentration of cheap hotels and B&Bs — many of the sub-£65 rooms in this guide sit within a 10-minute walk of the station, which is also where the Circumvesuviana trains to Pompeii and Sorrento leave from. The historic centre (Centro Storico, along Spaccanapoli) has slightly higher prices but puts you in the middle of the sights. For a quieter, safer-feeling budget base, look just uphill towards the Museo/Materdei area.

**Are cheap hotels near Naples Central Station safe?**
The immediate Piazza Garibaldi area is busy, well-connected and fine by day, but it's the grittiest part of central Naples after dark — keep your normal city-centre awareness, use the well-lit main streets at night, and watch bags on the crowded pavements. Thousands of travellers stay here every week precisely because it's cheap and central for Pompeii trains. If you'd rather trade a few pounds for a calmer street, the historic centre or the Chiaia/Mergellina seafront are gentler budget alternatives.

**Can I visit Naples on a budget?**
Yes — Naples is one of the cheapest major cities in Italy. A budget room from this guide runs £31–80, a genuine Margherita in its birthplace costs around €5–7, the Archaeological Museum is a few euros, and much of the historic centre is free to wander. A day trip to Pompeii is under €4 each way on the Circumvesuviana. Two people can do Naples comfortably on far less than Rome or the Amalfi Coast.

**Is Naples cheaper than Rome or Florence for hotels?**
Generally yes. Across every tier Naples undercuts Rome, Florence and Venice — its budget floor (~£31) is well below what those cities charge, its mid-range 4-stars sit around £90–170 where Rome's run higher, and even the seafront 5-stars, while not cheap, cost less than equivalents in Venice. Naples is the value capital of Italy's big-hitters, which is a large part of its appeal.

**What is the best area to stay in Naples for tourists?**
For first-timers, the historic centre (Centro Storico) along Spaccanapoli puts pizza, churches and the Archaeological Museum on your doorstep. For sea views and a smarter feel, the Santa Lucia / Lungomare seafront (Via Partenope) and the elegant Chiaia district are the picks. For the cheapest beds and the easiest Pompeii/Sorrento trains, stay near Napoli Centrale. Vomero, up the hill by funicular, is the calm, residential choice with the best views.

**Is Naples safe for tourists?**
Naples has a rougher reputation than it deserves — the historic centre and seafront are busy with visitors day and night and are perfectly walkable with standard city awareness. Petty theft (pickpocketing, bag-snatching) is the real risk, concentrated around the station, crowded markets and busy transport, so keep valuables secure and stay alert in crowds. Violent crime rarely touches tourists. Treat it like any big southern-European city and you'll be fine.

**Is Naples a good base for visiting Pompeii?**
It's the best base there is. The Circumvesuviana train from Napoli Centrale (Garibaldi) reaches Pompeii Scavi in about 35–40 minutes for under €4, and the same line continues to Herculaneum, Vesuvius bus connections and Sorrento. Staying near the station makes an early start easy, which matters in summer when Pompeii gets very hot by midday. Many hotels in this guide are a short walk from that train.

**How do I get from Naples to the Amalfi Coast?**
Take the Circumvesuviana train (or the faster Campania Express) from Naples to Sorrento in about an hour, then the SITA coastal bus or a ferry to Positano and Amalfi. In summer, direct ferries also run from Naples' Molo Beverello port to Positano, Amalfi and Capri, which is the most scenic option and skips the winding coast road. Naples is the natural launchpad for the whole coast.

**How do I get from Naples to Capri?**
Ferries and hydrofoils leave from Naples' Molo Beverello port (a short walk or taxi from the seafront hotels) and reach Capri in 45–80 minutes depending on the boat. Book ahead in summer and aim for an early sailing to beat the day-tripper crush. Ischia and Procida sail from the same port. A seafront or central hotel puts you within easy reach of the terminal.

**How do I get from Naples airport to the city centre?**
Naples International (Capodichino, NAP) is only about 15–20 minutes from the centre. The Alibus airport shuttle runs to Napoli Centrale and the port (Molo Beverello) for around €5, and taxis charge a fixed flat fare into the centre. Most hotels in this guide are a short, cheap transfer from the terminal — one of the closest big-city airports in Europe.

**Are there direct flights from the UK to Naples?**
Yes — Naples (NAP) has plentiful direct flights from the UK. easyJet, Ryanair, British Airways and others fly from London (Gatwick, Stansted, Heathrow, Luton) and several regional airports, typically in around 2 hours 45 minutes. It's one of the easiest and best-value Italian cities to reach on a short break.

**Where is the best pizza in Naples?**
Naples invented the pizza, and the historic centre is wall-to-wall with legendary pizzerias — the classic pilgrimage names sit along and around Via dei Tribunali and near the station. A true Neapolitan Margherita (San Marzano tomatoes, buffalo mozzarella, a puffy leopard-spotted crust from a wood oven) costs about €5–7. Expect queues at the famous spots; the quality bar across the city is extraordinarily high, so a random neighbourhood pizzeria rarely disappoints.

**What is Spaccanapoli?**
Spaccanapoli is the long, dead-straight, impossibly narrow street that "splits Naples" — it follows the line of the ancient Greco-Roman road through the heart of the historic centre. Walking it takes you past baroque churches, street shrines, sfogliatella bakeries, the Cappella Sansevero (home of the Veiled Christ sculpture) and endless pizzerias. It's the single best introduction to the city, and most historic-centre hotels are on or just off it.

**Is Naples worth visiting?**
Very much so — Naples is raw, loud, historic and utterly unlike the polished Italy of Florence or Venice. You get the world's best pizza at its source, a UNESCO historic centre, the finest Roman-antiquities museum on earth, and the easiest access to Pompeii, Vesuvius, Capri and the Amalfi Coast. Travellers who give it two or three days almost always leave won over by its energy.

**How many days do you need in Naples?**
Two full days covers the city itself — the historic centre, the Archaeological Museum, the seafront and a couple of great pizzas. Add a third and fourth day to use Naples as a base for Pompeii, Vesuvius and a boat to Capri or the Amalfi Coast. Because it's so cheap and so well-connected, many people build a whole week around a single Naples hotel.

**What is the best time to visit Naples?**
April–June and September–October are ideal — warm, sunny and comfortable for both the city and the boat trips, without July–August's heat and crowds. Summer is hot (30°C+) and busiest, though it's also ferry-and-beach season. Winter is mild, cheap and atmospheric, with the historic-centre Christmas nativity scene (presepe) tradition in full swing along Via San Gregorio Armeno.

**Do I need a car in Naples?**
No — a car is a liability in Naples, where traffic and parking are famously chaotic. The historic centre is walkable, the metro and funiculars cover the rest, and trains and ferries handle Pompeii, Sorrento, Capri and the Amalfi Coast. Only hire a car if you're touring rural Campania or Cilento beyond the reach of public transport, and even then pick it up on the way out of the city.

**Is the Naples metro good?**
Better than you'd expect — Line 1 includes the celebrated "Art Stations" (Toledo station is regularly called one of the most beautiful metro stops in Europe), and it links the centre, Vomero and the station cheaply. Combined with the three funiculars up to Vomero, it makes getting around easy. A daily or single ticket costs a couple of euros; validate it before you ride.

**Where should families stay in Naples?**
Families do best on or near the seafront (Santa Lucia / Lungomare) for the promenade, the Castel dell'Ovo and easy port access for boat trips — Hotel Royal Continental (from ~£168) has a rooftop pool the kids will love. On a budget, the roomier 4-star budget picks such as Grand Hotel Capodimonte or Millennium Gold Hotel give more space per pound, and the Botanical Garden area around Hotel del Real Orto Botanico is greener and calmer than the station.

**Which are the best hotels in Naples for couples?**
For a romantic splurge, the seafront Grand Hotel Vesuvio and the hillside Grand Hotel Parker's both pair bay views with rooftop restaurants, and the design-led ROMEO Napoli is the city's most stylish statement. On a mid-range budget, a sea-view room at Eurostars Hotel Excelsior or NH Napoli Panorama delivers the Bay of Naples at sunset for a fraction of Amalfi prices.

**Which Naples hotels have sea views?**
The Santa Lucia seafront strip is where to look: Grand Hotel Vesuvio, Hotel Royal Continental, Eurostars Hotel Excelsior and NH Napoli Panorama all front the Bay of Naples with views towards Castel dell'Ovo, Vesuvius and Capri. Grand Hotel Parker's, up on the Corso Vittorio Emanuele hillside, has arguably the widest panorama of all. Always request a sea-view room explicitly, as city-facing rooms are cheaper and common.

**What are the best luxury hotels in Naples?**
The Grand Hotel Vesuvio (the historic seafront grande dame, once home to Enrico Caruso) and Grand Hotel Parker's (an 1870 hillside classic with a panoramic rooftop) are the traditional benchmarks. ROMEO Napoli is the glossy modern challenger with Michelin dining, a rooftop pool and a spa near the port, and LHP Napoli Palace & SPA is the best-value 5-star of the four. Even the top tier here costs less than equivalent addresses in Venice or Rome.

**Is it better to stay in Naples or Sorrento?**
Stay in Naples for the city, the pizza, the museums and cheaper rooms; stay in Sorrento for a calmer, prettier clifftop resort base right next to the Amalfi Coast ferries. Naples is the better value and the better sightseeing base for Pompeii and Vesuvius; Sorrento is the more relaxing holiday base but costs more. Many travellers split the two, and both are on the same Circumvesuviana line an hour apart.

**Do budget hotels in Naples include breakfast?**
Many do — a lot of the small hotels and B&Bs in the budget tier bundle a simple continental breakfast (or a nearby café voucher) into the rate, though the very cheapest rooms often don't. Either way it barely matters, because a Neapolitan breakfast of a sfogliatella and an espresso at any corner bar costs a couple of euros.
