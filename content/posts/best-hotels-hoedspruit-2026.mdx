## What to do in Hoedspruit

Hoedspruit is above all a **gateway to the Greater Kruger**. The headline activity is the **Big Five game drive** in the surrounding private reserves — **Balule**, **Klaserie** and **Timbavati** — which share unfenced boundaries with Kruger National Park, so lion, leopard, elephant, buffalo and rhino roam freely across them. The Timbavati is famous for its rare white lions. Guided morning and evening drives from the safari lodges give the best sightings, while self-drivers can enter the public Kruger through the nearby Orpen Gate.

Beyond the wildlife, the **Blyde River Canyon** and the **Panorama Route** — God's Window, Bourke's Luck Potholes and the Three Rondavels — are among the most scenic drives in South Africa and easily reached from Hoedspruit. The town is also a **wildlife-conservation hub**: the **Moholoholo Rehabilitation Centre** and the **Hoedspruit Endangered Species Centre** both run visitor tours focused on rescued and endangered animals, making great non-drive days. And whatever you book, don't miss a **bush sunset** — a sundowner drink out in the reserve as the low-veld light fades is the quintessential Hoedspruit moment.

**What currency is used in Hoedspruit and should I carry cash?**
The South African Rand (ZAR). **How do I fly into Hoedspruit from the UK?**
There are no direct UK flights. You fly into Johannesburg (JNB) — direct from London in roughly 11 hours — then take a short onward flight to Hoedspruit Eastgate Airport (HDS), which sits right on the edge of the bush. Airlink runs the JNB-HDS hop. Many visitors instead self-drive from Johannesburg, which takes around 5-6 hours on good tar roads.

**Where is Hoedspruit and why is it a good safari base?**
Hoedspruit is a small town in Limpopo province, in South Africa's low-veld, on the western edge of the Greater Kruger. It is surrounded by the Balule, Klaserie and Timbavati private reserves, which share unfenced boundaries with Kruger National Park itself. That makes it one of the most convenient bases in the country for Big Five game drives without a long transfer from the airport.

**What is the difference between a self-catering lodge and an all-inclusive safari lodge?**
A self-catering lodge or bushveld cottage gives you a room or house with a kitchen — you buy your own food in Hoedspruit town and cook or braai (barbecue) yourself, and game drives are usually booked separately. An all-inclusive safari lodge bundles your room, all meals and typically two guided game drives a day into one nightly rate. Self-catering is far cheaper per night; all-inclusive is simpler and includes the guided wildlife experience.

**Is Hoedspruit a malaria area?**
Yes — Hoedspruit and the surrounding Greater Kruger are classed as a low-to-moderate malaria-risk area, with the highest risk in the wet summer months. Speak to your GP or a travel clinic well before you travel about anti-malarial tablets, and pack insect repellent, long sleeves for dusk and a mosquito net if your room is not screened. This guide is not medical advice — always seek a professional opinion for your own trip.

**What is the best time of year for a safari from Hoedspruit?**
The dry winter, roughly May to September, is the classic game-viewing season: sparse vegetation and animals gathering at waterholes make wildlife much easier to spot, and there are fewer mosquitoes. Days are warm and sunny but early-morning game drives are cold, so bring layers. The green summer (November to March) is lush, hot and better for birds and newborn animals, but sightings are harder and it is the peak malaria-risk window.
