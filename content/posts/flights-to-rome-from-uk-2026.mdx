## Quick answers

**What's the cheapest airline to Rome?** **Ryanair, Wizz Air and easyJet** — the lowest headline fares (from ~£30-£45 off-peak). **BA** runs the most flights; **ITA** is full-service; **Jet2** for baggage-included value.

**How long is the flight?** About **2 hours 30 to 2 hours 50 minutes** from London.

**Fiumicino or Ciampino?** **Fiumicino (FCO)** is the main airport with the fast Leonardo Express train — usually more convenient. **Ciampino (CIA)** is mainly Ryanair; only worth it if the fare is meaningfully cheaper.

**Which UK airport is cheapest?** Usually a **London** airport on a budget carrier, but Rome flies from many UK airports — flying **local** is often cheapest.

**When should I book?** **4-8 weeks** ahead most months; **8-12 weeks** for the spring peak, summer and Easter.

**Do I need a visa?** No — **90 days visa-free** in Schengen. Check ETIAS status before you travel.

## Airline-by-airline comparison (2026)

### Ryanair, Wizz Air & easyJet — the price leaders

**Typical fare 2026:** £30-£60 return off-peak, hand-luggage only.
**Pros:** The lowest headline fares on the route — Wizz Air in particular has shown standout London-Rome deals. High frequency from London's budget airports (Ryanair and Wizz from Stansted and Luton; easyJet from Gatwick, Luton and beyond).
**Cons:** Cheapest fares are cabin-only; a larger cabin bag, hold bag and allocated seat all cost extra. Ryanair mainly uses Ciampino from London Stansted — factor the slower transfer.

### British Airways — the frequency leader

**Typical fare 2026:** £70-£140 return; "Basic" fares are cabin-only.
**UK airports:** Heathrow and Gatwick to Fiumicino; the most flights on the route (around 55 a week).
**Pros:** High frequency and schedule flexibility, Avios earning, a checked bag on standard fares, arrives at the more convenient Fiumicino.
**Cons:** Higher headline fare than the budget carriers; check the fare type for baggage.

### ITA Airways — Italy's flag carrier

**Typical fare 2026:** £75-£150 return.
**Pros:** Full-service, flies into Fiumicino (its hub), good for onward connections within Italy and beyond, bag usually included on standard fares.
**Cons:** Cheapest "Light" fares are cabin-only; pricier than the budget carriers.

### Jet2 — the baggage-included option

**Typical fare 2026:** £70-£130 return, usually with a generous baggage allowance.
**Pros:** Baggage often included, friendly leisure service, strong regional UK coverage — frequently the best all-in value if you're checking a bag.
**Cons:** Higher headline fare; fewer London departures than the budget carriers.

**The honest read:** hand-luggage only, Ryanair, Wizz or easyJet win on price. Checking a bag or flying from a regional airport, Jet2's all-in fare is often better value. BA and ITA suit travellers who want Fiumicino, a bag included and full-service comfort on this slightly longer short-haul.

## Fiumicino vs Ciampino — which Rome airport?

| Airport | Airlines | To the city | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fiumicino (FCO) | BA, ITA, easyJet, Vueling, Jet2, Wizz | Leonardo Express train, ~32 min to Termini | The convenient default — fast train, most carriers |
| Ciampino (CIA) | Ryanair, some Wizz | Coach/bus, ~40 min to Termini | Only if the fare is meaningfully cheaper |

**The convenience call.** Fiumicino is Rome's main airport and has the **Leonardo Express**, a fast non-stop train to Termini — that alone makes it the easier arrival for most travellers. Ciampino is smaller and mainly serves Ryanair, with a coach transfer rather than a dedicated express train. A Ciampino fare should be clearly cheaper than a Fiumicino one to be worth the slower connection — check the all-in cost and transfer time before booking on headline price alone.

## UK airport comparison — fly local

Rome is served from a wide spread of UK airports, so the cheapest option is often your **nearest** one. London (Stansted, Luton, Gatwick, Heathrow) has the most choice and the lowest budget fares, but Manchester, Birmingham, Edinburgh and Bristol all have direct service on easyJet, Jet2, Ryanair or Wizz. For regional travellers, always price your local direct flight before assuming a cheap London fare wins once the London trek is added.

## The booking windows that actually save money

- **Off-peak months (Jan, Feb, Nov):** Book **4-8 weeks out**.
- **Spring peak (Apr-Jun), summer, Easter:** Book **8-12 weeks out** — Rome's busiest season, fares only rise.
- **October value window:** Book **6-10 weeks out** for great weather at lower prices.
- **Weekend breaks:** A Thursday or Saturday departure often undercuts the Friday-evening rush.

Set a price alert for your dates and book when a budget carrier drops a sale fare rather than paying the standing rate.

## Entry rules for UK travellers in 2026

- **No visa** for stays up to **90 days in any 180-day period** across the Schengen area.

Always check the [UK government's Italy travel advice](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/italy) shortly before you fly.

## Common mistakes UK-Rome travellers make

1. **Booking a Ciampino fare without pricing the transfer.** Ciampino has no express train — a coach adds time; the fare should be clearly cheaper to be worth it.
2. **Judging budget fares on the headline price.** A £35 Ryanair fare plus a £45 hold bag can match a Jet2 fare that includes baggage and lands at Fiumicino.
3. **Booking the spring peak late.** April-June is Rome's busiest season — book 8-12 weeks ahead.
4. **Missing the October value window.** Great weather at well below spring and summer prices.
5. **Adding baggage at the airport.** Always add it online at booking.
6. **Assuming an old passport is fine.** The Schengen 10-year issue rule catches UK travellers out.
7. **Trying to do Rome in one night.** The 2.5-hour flight and the sheer amount to see reward at least two full days.
