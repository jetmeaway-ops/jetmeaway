## Quick answers

**What's the cheapest airline to New York?** On price, the **budget long-haul and US carriers** (Norse, JetBlue, American, Delta, United) — from ~£350-£450 off-peak. **BA and Virgin** are the premium direct options.

**How long is the flight?** About **7-8 hours** westbound from London; a little shorter coming home.

**Which UK airports fly direct?** Mainly **Heathrow, Gatwick and Manchester**, with some **Edinburgh** seasonal service.

**JFK or Newark?** Both take direct UK flights and connect well to Manhattan — pick on fare and which side of the city you're staying. **Newark is often handier for downtown and New Jersey.**

**Do I need a visa?** No, but you **must have an approved ESTA** before flying. Apply at least 72 hours ahead.

**When should I book?** **3-5 months** ahead for summer (especially World Cup dates) and Christmas; **8-12 weeks** for spring/autumn.

## Airline-by-airline comparison (2026)

### British Airways — the frequency leader

**Typical fare 2026:** £400-£750 return; cheapest "Basic" fares are hand-luggage only.
**UK airports:** Heathrow and Gatwick to JFK and Newark; the most frequencies on the route.
**Pros:** Multiple daily flights, Avios earning, most fares include a checked bag, strong schedule flexibility if plans change.
**Cons:** "Basic" fares strip out the checked bag and seat selection — check the fare type. Higher headline price than the budget carriers.

### Virgin Atlantic — the premium alternative

**Typical fare 2026:** £420-£780 return.
**UK airports:** Heathrow to JFK daily, with seasonal Manchester service.
**Pros:** Well-regarded onboard product, Flying Club miles, good premium economy. Joint venture with Delta gives wide onward US connections.
**Cons:** Fewer UK gateways than BA; premium positioning means fewer rock-bottom fares.

### US carriers — American, Delta, United, JetBlue

**Typical fare 2026:** £360-£700 return depending on carrier and fare.
**Pros:** Strong competition keeps London fares keen; excellent onward domestic connections if New York is a gateway to elsewhere in the US. JetBlue in particular has shaken up transatlantic pricing.
**Cons:** Baggage and seat policies vary; cheapest fares are often carry-on only. Check what's included.

### Norse Atlantic — the budget long-haul option

**Typical fare 2026:** £350-£600 return, à-la-carte model.
**Pros:** Frequently the lowest headline fare on the route from London Gatwick.
**Cons:** Everything is a paid add-on — checked bags, seats, meals. A realistic all-in fare with a bag can approach a full-service carrier's price, so compare the total, not the sticker.

**The honest read:** for the lowest price travelling light, the US carriers and Norse win. For a checked bag included, more frequencies and schedule flexibility, BA and Virgin are often better all-in value than they first look. Always compare the total price with the baggage you actually need.

## UK airport comparison

**London Heathrow** has by far the most New York choice — BA, Virgin and multiple US carriers, many daily. **London Gatwick** adds Norse and others. **Manchester** has direct service (BA and seasonal Virgin), and **Edinburgh** sees some seasonal direct flights.

From other UK airports, you'll usually **connect** — either a domestic hop to Heathrow, or a one-stop via **Dublin** (which has US pre-clearance, letting you clear US immigration before you fly, so you arrive in New York as a domestic passenger) or a European hub. For regional travellers, compare a connecting fare from your local airport against the cost and hassle of positioning to London before defaulting to Heathrow.

## Direct vs connecting

From **London**, direct is usually both faster and competitively priced given how many carriers fly the route — there's rarely a strong reason to connect. From **regional UK airports**, a one-stop routing can beat a positioning trip to London: the standout option is **Dublin**, where US pre-clearance means you clear American immigration in Ireland and land in New York as a domestic arrival, skipping the long US immigration queue. Compare a connecting fare from your local airport against the all-in cost of getting to Heathrow first.

## The ESTA you need (UK passport holders, 2026)

Most UK passport holders travelling to New York for a holiday or short business trip need an approved **ESTA** (Electronic System for Travel Authorization) before flying, under the US Visa Waiver Program:

- **Apply online in advance** — approval is usually quick, but apply **at least 72 hours before travel** to be safe.
- It covers stays of **up to 90 days** and is valid for two years (or until your passport expires), so it can cover multiple trips.
- There's a **small fee**, and you must apply through the official US system — avoid third-party sites that charge a markup.
- Your **passport must be valid** for the duration of your stay.

If you have an existing valid US visa, you may not need an ESTA. Always check the current rules on the [UK government's USA travel advice](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/usa) before booking.

## The booking windows that actually save money

- **Summer 2026 (Jun-Aug), especially around World Cup dates:** Book **3-5 months ahead**, earlier if you can. This is the highest-demand window of an already-busy year.
- **Christmas / New Year:** Book by **September-October**.
- **Thanksgiving week (late November):** Book **3-4 months ahead** — a sharp, predictable US-travel spike.
- **Spring and autumn shoulder (Mar, Apr, Sep, Oct):** Book **8-12 weeks ahead** for the best balance of price and weather.
- **Deep off-peak (Jan-Feb):** Book **6-10 weeks ahead** — the cheapest fares of the year.

Set a price alert for your dates and lock in when a fare drops below your threshold — transatlantic fares move a lot, and 2026's summer demand makes early booking especially worthwhile.

<a id="common-mistakes"></a>

## Common mistakes UK-New York travellers make in 2026

1. **Leaving a summer booking late.** With the World Cup inflating demand, summer 2026 fares and hotels climb fast — book early.
2. **Forgetting the ESTA until the last minute.** Apply at least 72 hours before travel; without it you won't be allowed to board.
3. **Judging fares on the headline price.** A Norse or US-carrier cheap fare plus a checked bag can match a BA or Virgin fare that includes one.
4. **Ignoring Newark.** It's often quicker to downtown Manhattan than JFK and can be cheaper — compare both.
5. **Positioning to London from a regional airport without checking a Dublin routing.** US pre-clearance in Dublin can make a connecting fare both cheaper and smoother.
6. **Adding baggage at the airport.** Always add it online in advance on transatlantic routes.
7. **Using a third-party ESTA site.** Apply through the official US system to avoid a markup.
