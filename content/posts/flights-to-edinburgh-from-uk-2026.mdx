## Quick answers

**What's the cheapest airline to Edinburgh?** **easyJet and Ryanair** on headline price (from ~£30-£50 off-peak on the London route). **BA** runs the most flights and adds London City.

**Fly or train?** From **London it's close, and the train often wins** door-to-door once transfers and bags are added. From **Scotland's north, NI or further afield, fly.**

**How long is the flight?** About **1 hour 15 minutes** — but ~3.5-4.5 hours door-to-door from London, similar to the LNER train.

**Which UK airport is cheapest?** Usually a **London budget airport**, but Edinburgh flies from Bristol, Birmingham, Belfast and more — fly **local**.

**When should I book?** **3-6 weeks** ahead most months; **6-10 weeks** for August festivals and Hogmanay.

**Do I need a passport?** No — it's a **domestic** flight, but airlines require **photo ID**.

## Fly or take the train to Edinburgh?

This is the first question to ask on the London-Edinburgh route, because the answer is often "take the train."

**The flight** is about 1 hour 15 minutes in the air, but the realistic **door-to-door time is 3.5-4.5 hours** once you add getting to the airport, check-in, security and the tram into the city. **The LNER train** runs London King's Cross to Edinburgh Waverley in about **4 hours 15 minutes, city centre to city centre**, with no airport faff and a generous luggage allowance.

**When the train wins:**
- You're travelling from **London or the East Coast** — King's Cross, Peterborough, York, Newcastle all have fast direct trains.
- You value city-centre-to-city-centre with no airport transfers.
- You're carrying luggage and want to avoid hold-bag fees.
- Booked 8-12 weeks ahead, an LNER Advance fare often beats flying on **both time and cost**.

**When flying wins:**
- You're travelling from **Northern Ireland, the South West, the Midlands or northern Scotland** — no fast rail link, so flying is quicker and cheaper.
- You want the outright lowest headline fare for a hand-luggage weekend from a budget airport.

**The honest read:** for London-Edinburgh, compare the LNER train first — it frequently wins. From most other parts of the UK, fly from your nearest airport.

## Airline-by-airline comparison (2026)

### easyJet & Ryanair — the price leaders

**Typical fare 2026:** £30-£60 return off-peak, hand-luggage only.
**Pros:** The lowest headline fares. easyJet from Gatwick, Luton and other bases; Ryanair from Stansted. Frequent flights, regular sales.
**Cons:** Cheapest fares are cabin-only; hold bag and allocated seat cost extra.

### British Airways — the frequency leader

**Typical fare 2026:** £60-£120 return; "Basic" domestic fares are cabin-only.
**Pros:** The most flights on London-Edinburgh (around 101 a week), from Heathrow, London City and Gatwick; Avios earning; London City is fast and central.
**Cons:** Higher headline fare than the budget carriers.

### From the regions

easyJet, Loganair and others serve Edinburgh from Bristol, Birmingham, Southampton, Belfast and more — usually the cheapest and easiest option from those cities.

**The honest read:** hand-luggage only from London, easyJet or Ryanair win on fare — but check the train too. From the regions, fly your nearest direct route. BA suits London City convenience and Avios collectors.

## The August festival spike

Every August, Edinburgh hosts the **Fringe** (the world's largest arts festival), the **International Festival** and the **Military Tattoo**, drawing huge crowds for most of the month. It's the city's single biggest demand spike — flights rise and hotels rise even more, often selling out months ahead.

- **Going for the festivals?** Book flights and accommodation as early as you can — several months ahead for August.
- **Just want a cheap city break?** Avoid August entirely; fares and hotels fall back sharply in September.

Hogmanay (New Year) is the second big spike — famous worldwide, and priced accordingly around 30 December-1 January.

## Do you need ID or a passport?

Edinburgh is in the UK, so a flight from elsewhere in Britain is **domestic** — no passport is legally required. But **every airline requires an accepted form of photo ID** to fly, and each sets its own list. Most accept a valid passport, and some accept a photo driving licence on domestic routes. **Check your specific airline's accepted-ID list before travelling** and take an ID it accepts — turning up without valid ID risks being denied boarding.

## Common mistakes UK-Edinburgh travellers make

1. **Not comparing the train from London.** The LNER often beats flying on total time and cost — always check it.
2. **Judging a flight on the headline fare.** Add airport transfers (£30+) and a hold bag (£25-£50) and a "cheap" flight can cost more than the train.
3. **Booking August without realising it's festival month.** Fares and hotels spike hard — avoid it unless you're there for the Fringe.
4. **Travelling with no accepted photo ID.** It's domestic, but airlines require ID — take one they accept.
5. **Adding baggage at the airport.** Always add it online, or travel hand-luggage only for a weekend.
6. **Overlooking Hogmanay pricing.** New Year in Edinburgh is a major, pricey spike — book early or avoid.
7. **Flying from the East Coast when the train is faster.** From Newcastle, York or Peterborough, the train usually wins.
