You may spot turtles in the water on responsible, park-licensed boat trips. Please keep your distance, never touch them, and follow the marine-park guidance — this is a genuinely protected habitat, not an attraction."

## Zakynthos Hotels FAQs

**What is the cheapest hotel in Zakynthos?**
On recent searches the lowest real, bookable rate is Portego in Laganas, from around £73 a night — a simple 2-star on the island's main resort strip. Psarou Studios (from ~£74), Bozikis Palace Hotel in Laganas (from ~£81, with 159 reviews) and Villaggio Verde (from ~£82) are the next cheapest. All of these sit in or near Laganas, the party-and-value end of the island. Zakynthos has no £30 room in summer, but £73–90 gets you a real bed within walking distance of a beach.

**How much does a budget hotel in Zakynthos cost per night in 2026?**
Realistically £73–150 a night for the cheaper tier, with the very lowest rates (£73–90) clustered in Laganas. Portego starts around £73, Bozikis Palace ~£81, and a big band of clean 2–4 star hotels runs £107–150. The budget tier in this guide stretches all the way up to around £221 for a beachfront 5-star sold at a budget-season rate, so there's a wide spread — say what you want to pay and there's a real room at it. Travel in May–June or mid-September–October and these rates fall further.

**What is the cheapest area to stay in Zakynthos?**
Laganas — the big resort strip on the south coast — has the lowest rates on the island, with Portego (~£73), Bozikis Palace (~£81) and Villaggio Verde (~£82) all starting well under £100. It's the party end of Zakynthos, so it's lively and young at night. Zakynthos Town, the working port, also has fair-value hotels like the Diana Hotel (~£127). Tsilivi and Alykes on the family side sit a little higher. If price is the priority, Laganas wins; if you want calm, pay a little more for Tsilivi or the town.

**Is Laganas or Tsilivi better for my holiday?**
It depends who you are. Laganas is the party strip — bars, clubs, a long sandy beach and the cheapest rooms on the island, popular with young groups and couples who want nightlife. Tsilivi (and neighbouring Alykes) is the family side: calmer beaches, low-rise resorts, water parks and a gentler evening scene. Both have good beaches. Choose Laganas for value and nightlife, Tsilivi or Alykes for a quieter family week. Zakynthos Town is a third option if you want a real Greek port rather than a resort.

**Where should families stay in Zakynthos?**
Tsilivi and Alykes on the east coast are the family heartland — calm, shallow beaches, low-rise hotels, water parks and a relaxed evening scene, well away from the Laganas party strip. Family-friendly picks in this guide include Tsilivi Beach Hotel, Alamis Hotel & Apartments, Filoxenia Hotel, Andreolas Luxury Suites and Zante Palace, all in the Tsilivi area. Zante Maris Suites near Tsilivi is a smart adults-plus-couples option too. Families who want nightlife nearby sometimes pick the quieter edges of Laganas, but Tsilivi is the safer bet for a calm week with kids.

**Where is best for nightlife and partying in Zakynthos?**
Laganas is the island's party capital — a long strip of bars, clubs and cheap eats behind a big sandy beach, busiest in July and August and aimed squarely at young groups. It also has the lowest room rates on the island, so it's where budget-and-nightlife travellers head. Kalamaki, next door, is a quieter version with some of the same beach. If you want to sleep away from the noise but still visit, base yourself in Tsilivi or Zakynthos Town and taxi over. Laganas Bay is also a protected turtle-nesting zone, so parts of the beach have rules — respect the signs.

**Can you visit Zakynthos on a budget?**
Yes — Zakynthos is one of the better-value Ionian islands. Base yourself in Laganas, where real rooms start around £73–90 a night, use the local buses or a cheap hire car, and eat at tavernas away from the main strip. The island's icons — Navagio Shipwreck Beach and the Blue Caves — are seen by boat trips that are affordable if you book locally. Come in May–June or mid-September–October and both rooms and flights drop from the August peak. Budget Zakynthos is genuinely doable; it's one of Greece's more affordable summer islands.

**What is the best luxury hotel in Zakynthos?**
For pure exclusivity, Lesante Blu — The Leading Hotels of the World, Adults Only (from ~£520) and Contessina Suites & Spa near Tsilivi (from ~£503) are the top of the tier, both adults-only with sea views, pools and spas. If you'd rather spend less for five-star polish, the Lesante Classic (from ~£295) and Domes Aulūs Zante all-inclusive in Laganas (from ~£316) lead on value. All are a world away from the Laganas strip in feel.

**When is the cheapest time to visit Zakynthos?**
May–June and mid-September–October are the sweet spot: warm sea, thinner crowds, and island prices that fall sharply from the July–August peak. August is the hottest, busiest and most expensive month by a wide margin, and it's also peak turtle-nesting season at Laganas. If you can travel in the shoulder season you'll usually pay less for the same room and enjoy a calmer island — this is the single most useful money tip for Zakynthos. Many hotels close from around November to March, so winter isn't really an option.

**Do Zakynthos hotels close in winter?**
Many do. Zakynthos is a seasonal island, and a large share of hotels — especially in the resort areas of Laganas, Tsilivi and Alykes — close from around November to March, reopening for the April/May–October season. Peak is July–August; the best mix of value and weather is May–June and mid-September–October. If you're planning an off-season trip, check the hotel is actually open, and expect far fewer direct UK flights (off-season usually means connecting via Athens).

**Where should first-time visitors stay in Zakynthos?**
It depends on your trip. For nightlife and the lowest prices, stay in Laganas. For a calm family week, choose Tsilivi or Alykes on the east coast. For a real Greek port with harbourfront tavernas and ferry links, base yourself in Zakynthos Town. Many first-timers pick Tsilivi as the all-round base — good beach, family-friendly, close to the town and the airport, and an easy drive to the boat trips for Navagio and the Blue Caves. Match the area to whether you want quiet, value or nightlife.

**How do I get from Zakynthos airport to my hotel?**
Zakynthos airport (ZTH) sits just south of Zakynthos Town, very close to Laganas and Kalamaki (about 5–10 minutes) and around 15–20 minutes from Tsilivi. Most hotels arrange a paid transfer, taxis wait outside the terminal, and package holidays usually include a coach transfer. It's a small airport, so it's quick to clear. Laganas and Kalamaki are the closest resorts to the runway; Tsilivi, Alykes and the north are a slightly longer drive across the island.

**Do I need a car in Zakynthos?**
Not essential, but useful. Zakynthos has buses linking the town and the main resorts, and boat trips cover the famous sights, so a car-free trip works if you stay near a beach and a bus route. But a hire car or ATV opens up the quieter beaches, the mountain villages and the Keri and Vasilikos coasts, and it's cheap on this island. Many visitors rent for a day or two to reach the Navagio viewpoint and the south-coast beaches, then rely on buses and the occasional taxi otherwise.

**How do I get to Navagio (Shipwreck) Beach?**
Navagio Beach — the famous cove with the rusting shipwreck and towering white cliffs — is only reachable by boat. Excursion boats run from Porto Vromi (the closest departure point), Zakynthos Town, Agios Nikolaos and other harbours in summer. There's also a clifftop viewing platform you can drive to for the classic photo from above, though access to the platform has at times been restricted for safety after landslides, so check locally. You cannot walk down to the beach — it's boat-only.

**Can you swim at Navagio Beach?**
Yes, on the boat trips that land on the sand you can swim in the bright turquoise water, though the beach gets very busy in peak summer and boats come and go all day. Note that access has occasionally been limited for safety because of rockfalls from the cliffs above, so the situation can change season to season — follow local guidance and any closures. The clifftop viewpoint gives the iconic photo but no beach access. For a calmer swim, the Blue Caves and Vasilikos beaches are good alternatives.

**What are the Blue Caves and how do I visit?**
The Blue Caves are sea caves and arches at the northern tip of Zakynthos, near Cape Skinari, where sunlight reflects off the white rock and turns the water an intense blue. You visit by small boat from Agios Nikolaos (Volimes) or on a combined Navagio-and-Blue-Caves excursion from the main harbours. Many trips let you swim inside the caves. It pairs naturally with the Navagio viewpoint, which is nearby in the island's north. Book locally in summer for the best price.

**Are the loggerhead turtles at Laganas real, and can you see them?**
Yes — Laganas Bay is one of the most important nesting sites in the Mediterranean for the loggerhead sea turtle (Caretta caretta), and it lies inside the National Marine Park of Zakynthos, a protected area with real rules. Turtles nest on the bay's beaches in summer, so parts of the sand are off-limits at night, boat speeds are limited, and lights are restricted to protect hatchlings. You may spot turtles in the water on responsible, park-licensed boat trips. Please keep your distance, never touch them, and follow the marine-park guidance — this is a genuinely protected habitat, not an attraction.

**Is Zakynthos good for couples?**
Very — especially away from the Laganas strip. Adults-only options like Lesante Blu, Contessina Suites & Spa, Zante Maris Suites, Elegance Luxury Executive Suites, Meandros Boutique and Palazetto Suites are built for couples, with sea views, pools and calm. For romance on a budget, quieter areas like Vasilikos, Tsilivi's edges and Zakynthos Town beat the party beaches. Couples who want nightlife can still enjoy Laganas; those who want sunsets and quiet should aim for the adults-only resorts or the west and south of the island.

**Is Zakynthos expensive compared to other Greek islands?**
No — Zakynthos is one of the more affordable Greek islands, well below Mykonos and Santorini and broadly in line with Corfu and Crete. Real budget rooms start around £73 a night in Laganas, mid-range 4-stars run £125–285, and even the top adults-only suites (£500+) undercut the caldera cave suites of Santorini. It's a strong-value Ionian island, which is a big part of its appeal for UK families and groups. Come in the shoulder season and the value improves further.

**Can you fly direct to Zakynthos from the UK?**
In summer, yes — seasonal charter and low-cost flights run direct from many UK airports to Zakynthos (ZTH) during roughly May–October, taking around 3.5–4 hours. Off-season (November–March) direct flights largely stop and you'll usually connect via Athens on Aegean, Olympic or Sky Express, then take a short domestic hop. Book August flights and hotels three to six months ahead, as both sell out and prices climb. Zakynthos is a flight-first island for UK visitors — most arrive by plane rather than ferry.

**Should I stay in Zakynthos Town?**
Zakynthos Town — the island's working port and capital — is a good base if you want a real Greek town rather than a resort: a harbourfront with tavernas and cafés, Solomos Square, the church of Agios Dionysios, ferry links and fair-value hotels like the Strada Marina, Palatino, Diana and Bitzaro Boutique. It's calmer than Laganas and more authentic than the purpose-built resorts, though the town beach is modest — most people drive or bus to the better beaches. A solid pick for first-timers who want character and value.

**How many days do you need in Zakynthos?**
Four to seven nights suits most trips. That's enough for a boat trip to Navagio Shipwreck Beach and the Blue Caves, a day or two on the beach at your resort, an evening in Zakynthos Town, and time to explore the Vasilikos or Keri coasts by hire car. A week is the classic UK package length and works well. If you're just after sun and a famous beach photo, four nights covers it; longer stays reward renting a car and getting off the main strips.

**Are Zakynthos budget hotels actually good quality?**
Many are genuinely good for the price. The cheapest rooms in Laganas (Portego, Bozikis Palace, Villaggio Verde) are simple 2-star and no-frills apartment stays — clean and well-placed rather than fancy. Move up to £127–180 and you get well-reviewed 3–4 star hotels with pools like Avalon Palace, Zante Palace, Oscar Hotel and Ionian Hill. The budget tier here even includes beachfront 5-stars (Mediterranean Beach Resort, Galaxy Beach Resort) at the top of the band. Check the review count and star rating against the rate and you'll find real value.

**What's the difference between Zante and Zakynthos?**
None — they're the same island. Zakynthos is the Greek name; Zante is the older Italian/Venetian name, still widely used in the UK travel trade, especially for the Laganas party scene ("Zante"). Flights, hotels and maps use both. So a "Zante holiday" and a "Zakynthos holiday" are identical; don't book twice thinking they're different places. The airport code is ZTH.

**Is Tsilivi or Alykes better for a quiet beach holiday?**
Both are calm, family-friendly east-coast resorts and either works for a quiet week. Tsilivi is slightly larger and livelier, with a long sandy beach, a water park and a good spread of tavernas and family hotels like Tsilivi Beach Hotel, Alamis, Filoxenia and Zante Palace. Alykes, a little further north, is quieter still, with a gentle bay good for young children. Neither has the nightlife of Laganas — which is exactly the point. Choose Tsilivi for a touch more to do, Alykes for maximum calm.
