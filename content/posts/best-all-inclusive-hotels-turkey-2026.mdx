The best all inclusive hotels in Turkey for 2026 are the Rixos properties in Belek for ultra all inclusive luxury, Liberty Lykia in Ölüdeniz for couples and families who want scenery, and Hotel SU & Aqualand in Antalya for families chasing waterpark value. Türkiye (the country's official 2022 name change from Turkey) has been the UK's favourite all inclusive destination for over a decade — and in 2026 it's better value than ever, with the Turkish lira working heavily in your favour, a short 4-hour flight from most UK airports, and resorts that compete with the Caribbean at a fraction of the price. Turkey straddles two continents — Europe west of the Bosphorus, Asia east — and the Mediterranean and Aegean coastlines that fringe the south and west are where the all-inclusive industry is concentrated.

But not all all-inclusive holidays are equal. Some resorts water down the drinks, pack in the guests, and call a buffet of bread rolls "unlimited dining." Others genuinely deliver — multiple restaurants, private beaches, branded spirits, and kids' clubs that actually entertain your children.

This is JetMeAway's honest scout guide to the best all inclusive hotels in Turkey for 2026 — broken down by resort area, budget and travel type, with real prices and direct comparison links so you can find the best deal in seconds.

## Why Turkey Is Still the Best Value All Inclusive in Europe

Before we get into the hotels, here's the honest case for Turkey in 2026:

- **Flight time:** 4 to 4.5 hours from London, 3.5 hours from Manchester. No long-haul exhaustion, no jet lag.
- **Value:** Thanks to the strength of the pound against the Turkish lira, your money goes significantly further than in Spain, Greece or Portugal. A 5-star resort in Belek costs roughly what a 3-star in the Algarve does.
- **Weather window:** Türkiye stays warm from late April through to October — giving you one of the longest beach seasons in Europe. September and October are particularly good for couples who want quieter resorts and lower prices.
- **What's typically included:** Most Turkish all inclusive packages cover breakfast, lunch, dinner, unlimited local beer, wine, soft drinks and cocktails, use of pools and beach, daytime entertainment and kids' clubs. Ultra all inclusive upgrades usually add branded spirits, à la carte dining and premium spa access.
- **Day-trip range:** Turkey's resort coastline is within reach of some of the country's most extraordinary heritage sites — the Roman amphitheatre at Aspendos (45 minutes from Belek, still hosting opera in summer), the white travertine terraces at Pamukkale (3 hours from Antalya), and the fairy-chimney landscape of Cappadocia (an internal flight from Antalya/Bodrum, hot-air balloons at dawn). Two days of touring breaks up two weeks of pool deck.

## How Turkey All-Inclusives Compare to Greek Islands and Spain

The three classic Mediterranean all-inclusive markets — Türkiye, Greek Islands, Spain — each ask for a different week. Turkey is the value tier: Rixos and Maxx Royal in Belek deliver ultra-all-inclusive at €120–250 per person per night for 5-star, branded spirits and à la carte dining included. The Greek Islands (Crete, Rhodes, Kos) come in higher: Daios Cove and Blue Palace Crete trade at €280–450 per person per night for comparable 5-star, with the Cycladic island premium pushing Santorini and Mykonos higher still. Spain's Costa del Sol all-inclusives (Marbella, Estepona) trade at €180–320 per person per night for genuine 5-star, with smaller pools and tighter beach footprints than Turkey's mega-resorts.

Experience-wise the three split too. Turkey's Belek strip is built around scale — 500 to 800-room ultra-resorts with aqua parks, 10+ restaurants, branded spirits and theme-park access. The Greek Islands trade scale for landscape — smaller, lower-rise hotels, dramatic geology, white-and-blue Cycladic aesthetic. Spain's Costa del Sol offers the easiest cultural day-trips (Granada, [Seville](/blog/best-hotels-seville-2026), Málaga, Marbella's old town) but the all-inclusive resorts are more modest in scale and inclusion. If you want the most resort for the money, Turkey. If you want the most beautiful coastline, Greece. If you want a Mediterranean resort that pairs with city breaks, Spain.

## The Post-Dinner Çay & Hammam Ritual

Two rituals every Turkey resort weaves into the day:

- **Çay in tulip glasses** — Turkish tea (black, strong, served in a tulip-shaped glass called *ince belli*, "thin-waisted") is the after-dinner ritual the whole country observes. Every all-inclusive in Belek, Bodrum and Marmaris serves it complimentary in the lobby lounge after dinner — no extra charge, no time limit, the same ritual a Turkish family performs at home. Take three, sit on the terrace, watch the resort wind down.
- **The hammam circuit** — the Ottoman steam-bath ritual most ultra-all-inclusives include either complimentary or in a discounted spa package. A proper hammam is 90 minutes: warm marble belly stone for sweating, kese (coarse mitt) exfoliation, soap-foam massage, cold-water rinse, tea on the cool terrace. The Rixos Belek, Maxx Royal Bodrum and Voyage Belek hammams are the genuine versions; cheap resort spas offer a watered-down 30-minute "hammam-style" treatment that isn't the same thing. Ask which version is included before booking.

## What Does All Inclusive Actually Include in Turkey?

This varies by resort and matters more than most people realise before they book. Here's the standard breakdown:

**Standard All Inclusive usually includes:**

- Breakfast, lunch and dinner at the main buffet restaurant
- Unlimited local beer, house wine and soft drinks
- Poolside snacks throughout the day
- Use of pools, beach and sun loungers
- Daytime entertainment programme
- Kids' club (at family resorts)

**Ultra All Inclusive usually adds:**

- Branded spirits and premium cocktails
- À la carte restaurant dining (no extra charge)
- 24-hour food and drink service
- Motorised watersports
- Spa access or treatments

Always check the specific inclusions before booking — the gap between standard and ultra can be significant, especially if you're a fan of a branded gin and tonic by the pool.

## When Is the Best Time to Go to Turkey All Inclusive?

- **May and June** — warm, uncrowded, excellent value. Temperatures around 28°C. Best for couples and adult groups.
- **July and August** — peak summer, hottest weather (35°C+), most lively atmosphere. Best for families in school holidays. Book early and expect higher prices.
- **September and October** — the Scout's favourite. Sea temperature still warm from summer, crowds thin out, prices drop by 20 to 30%, and the resorts feel more relaxed. Ideal for couples, honeymooners and anyone who finds peak summer too intense.

## Do UK Travellers Need a Visa for Turkey?

Yes — UK passport holders currently require an e-Visa to enter Turkey. It costs approximately £15 and can be applied for online at the official Turkish e-Visa portal at [evisa.gov.tr](https://www.evisa.gov.tr). Processing takes around 24 to 48 hours so apply before you travel, not at the airport. Your passport needs at least 6 months validity from your date of entry.

Always check the latest entry requirements via the FCDO ([gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/turkey](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/turkey)) before booking, as rules can change.
