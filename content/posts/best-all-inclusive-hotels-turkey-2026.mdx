## Antalya vs Spain 2026 price comparison

This is the post's core angle, and the tier data backs it up hard. A genuine 5-star all-inclusive in Antalya (Delta by Marriott Lara at £100, Holiday Inn Lara at £110, Crowne Plaza at £128) costs roughly what a 3-star costs on Spain's Costa del Sol, where Marbella and Estepona all-inclusives trade at €180–320 per person per night for actual 5-star. Antalya's budget tier has essentially no Spanish equivalent — Spain's package-holiday budget belt rarely dips under €70–90 a night for a genuine all-inclusive board, where this guide found more than 20 verified Antalya hotels under £100, and the cheapest, Lupo Libero Hotel Spa, at £36.

The gap holds at the top too. The Greek Islands (Crete, Rhodes, Kos) trade even higher than Spain — Daios Cove and Blue Palace Crete run €280–450 per person per night for comparable 5-star. Antalya's Rixos Downtown at £453/night is the outlier at the very top of this guide, but it comes with direct Land of Legends theme-park access that has no real Spanish or Greek equivalent — everything else in the Antalya luxury tier undercuts both markets by a wide margin.

Experience-wise, the three markets split by scale versus landscape versus city access. Antalya's Lara strip is built around size — large ultra-resorts with aqua parks, multiple restaurants and branded spirits — while the city itself offers Kaleiçi's old town and the shortest airport transfers on the coast. The Greek Islands trade scale for scenery; Spain's Costa del Sol offers the easiest cultural day-trips ([Seville](/blog/best-hotels-seville-2026), Granada, Málaga) but its all-inclusive resorts are more modest in both scale and price-to-inclusion ratio. If you want the most resort and the lowest price, Antalya wins on both counts in 2026.
