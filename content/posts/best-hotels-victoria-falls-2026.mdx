## Zimbabwe Side vs Zambia Side — Which to Choose?

Both sides are wonderful, and they are genuinely different experiences:

- **Zimbabwe side (Victoria Falls town):** the classic, head-on panorama. The rainforest walk here faces the widest stretch of the falls and stays spectacular even in low-water months. The town is compact, walkable and lively, with the most restaurants, craft markets and adventure operators, plus the grand colonial Victoria Falls Hotel. This is the favourite first base for many.
- **Zambia side (Livingstone):** quieter and more relaxed, strung along the Zambezi riverfront. It gets you closest to the lip of the falls, and it is the home of **Livingstone Island** and **Devil's Pool** (seasonal). Mosi-oa-Tunya National Park is on its doorstep, and Livingstone Airport is close to town.

You do not have to choose. The **KAZA UniVisa** (explained further down) covers both countries, so basing on one side and day-tripping to the other — or splitting your stay between them — is easy and popular.

## Getting There, Visas & the KAZA UniVisa

There are **no direct flights from the UK**. The usual routes are one-stop, connecting through **Johannesburg**, **Nairobi**, **Addis Ababa**, or a Gulf hub such as **Dubai** or **Doha**, on to one of two airports:

- **Victoria Falls Airport (VFA)** — Zimbabwe side, about 20 km from Victoria Falls town.
- **Livingstone Airport (LVI)** — Zambia side, about 6 km from central Livingstone.

For UK passport holders, both Zimbabwe and Zambia charge a **visa on arrival**. But there is a smarter option if you want to see both sides: the **KAZA UniVisa**. This single visa costs around **US$50**, is bought on arrival at Victoria Falls Airport, Livingstone Airport or the land borders, and covers **both Zimbabwe and Zambia for up to 30 days** — plus day trips to the **Botswana** side (Chobe). It is ideal for anyone crossing the bridge to experience both banks of the falls. Availability and price can change, so **check the current situation before you travel** and carry clean US dollar notes to pay. For official, up-to-date entry guidance, read the UK FCDO advice for [Zimbabwe](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/zimbabwe) and [Zambia](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/zambia).

## When to Go — Chasing the Falls' Flow

Victoria Falls is a different sight depending on the season, and there is no single "best" time — it depends on what you want:

- **February to May — peak flow:** just after the rains, the Zambezi is at full power and the falls are at their most thunderous, with spray rising hundreds of metres. It is breathtaking, but the mist can partly obscure the view and will soak you — bring a poncho.
- **August to January — lower water:** the flow eases, revealing the rock face and delivering clearer photos. This is also when **Devil's Pool** on the Zambia side opens (water levels permitting).
- **September to October — hot and dry:** the best window for game viewing in the national parks, though daytime temperatures climb.

## Beyond the Falls — Things to Do

The falls are the headline, but the region is one of Africa's great adventure hubs:

- Walk the **rainforest trail** along the falls (both sides have their own viewpoints).
- Raft the **Zambezi white water** below the falls — world-class rapids.
- Bungee, zipline or gorge-swing from the historic **Victoria Falls Bridge**.
- Take a **Zambezi sunset cruise** with hippos, elephants and a sundowner.
- Swim at **Devil's Pool** (seasonal, Zambia side) via a Livingstone Island tour.
- Fly over the falls by microlight or helicopter — the **"Flight of Angels"**.
- Go on **game drives** in Zambezi National Park (Zimbabwe) and Mosi-oa-Tunya National Park (Zambia).
- Browse the **craft markets** on both sides for carvings and textiles.

For more ideas and planning on the Zambian side, [Zambia's official tourism site](https://www.zambia.travel/) is a good starting point.

### A quick word on money

The **Zimbabwe side** runs largely on **US dollars** — carry clean, newer notes in small denominations, as cash is king for park fees, tips and markets. The **Zambia side** uses the **Zambian Kwacha**, though US dollars are widely accepted at lodges and for tours. Card acceptance thins out away from the bigger hotels. ## Hwange National Park — Zimbabwe's Great Safari Add-On

If the falls are the headline, **Hwange** is the natural second act. It is **Zimbabwe's largest national park** — around **14,650 km²** — and it sits roughly a **2-2.5 hour drive south-east of Victoria Falls**, which makes it the obvious safari pairing with a falls trip.

Hwange is best known for its **enormous elephant herds**, one of the biggest elephant populations in Africa, alongside lion, buffalo, giraffe, zebra and **over 100 mammal species**. It is also a stronghold for the endangered **African painted dog**, with a Painted Dog Conservation centre near Dete.

Much of the park has **no natural year-round surface water**, so game concentrates around **pumped waterholes** — which is exactly why viewing is so reliable in the **dry season (roughly August to October)**, when the animals have to come and drink. The **green season (November to April)** is lush and much better for birds, but game is more scattered and harder to find.

**How to do it:** day trips run from Victoria Falls, but it is a long day with about two hours on the road each way. An **overnight or two-night operator-run safari** is the better use of the drive — it buys you the dawn and dusk game drives, which is when the park is at its best.

**One honest note:** the lodges and camps inside and around Hwange are **booked direct through safari operators, so they are not on our site**. The straightforward way to do this trip is to **base yourself in Victoria Falls** — where all 37 bookable hotels in this guide are — and book the safari as an excursion from town. If you want to sleep in the park itself, go through an operator for the in-park camps.

It stacks up neatly: the falls, a couple of nights of big-game country in Hwange, and — with the **KAZA UniVisa** explained above — the Zambia side across the bridge, all from one comfortable base.

## Victoria Falls Hotels FAQs

**Which side of Victoria Falls is better — Zimbabwe or Zambia?**
For the classic head-on panorama of most of the waterfall, the Zimbabwe side (Victoria Falls town) wins, and it stays impressive even in low-water months. The Zambia side (Livingstone) is quieter, gets you closest to the lip of the falls, and is home to Livingstone Island and Devil's Pool. If you can, see both — they are barely 15 minutes apart and the KAZA UniVisa covers both countries.

**Can I see both sides of Victoria Falls in one trip?**
Yes, easily. The two viewpoints are separated only by the Victoria Falls Bridge, a 10-15 minute drive or a walk across. Many visitors base on one side and day-trip to the other, using the KAZA UniVisa so they do not need two separate visas.

**What is the KAZA UniVisa and where do I buy it?**
It is a single visa covering both Zimbabwe and Zambia for up to 30 days, plus day trips to the Botswana side (Chobe). It typically costs around US$50 and is bought on arrival at Victoria Falls Airport, Livingstone Airport or the land borders. Availability and price can change, so check before you travel and carry US dollars in cash.

**When is the best time to see Victoria Falls?**
February to May for the fullest, most thunderous flow (heavy spray can obscure the view and soak you); August to January for clearer views of the rock face and calmer water, which is also when Devil's Pool opens. September and October are hot, dry and best for game viewing.

**Will the spray soak me at the falls?**
In high-water months (roughly February to May), yes — the spray drenches the rainforest walk like standing rain. Bring or hire a poncho and protect your phone and camera. In low-water months the spray is far gentler and the views clearer.

**Is Devil's Pool safe, and when is it open?**
Devil's Pool is a natural rock pool on the lip of the falls on the Zambia side, reached via a guided Livingstone Island tour. It only opens when water levels are low enough to be safe — usually around August to December/January, varying each year. Always go with licensed guides and check current opening before booking.

**What currency do I use at Victoria Falls?**
The Zimbabwe side runs largely on US dollars; the Zambia side uses the Zambian Kwacha, but US dollars are widely accepted at lodges. Carry cash, as card acceptance is patchy away from bigger hotels. The pound figures here are rough guidance, not exact quotes.

**How do I get to Victoria Falls from the UK?**
There are no direct flights. Most routes are one-stop via Johannesburg, Nairobi, Addis Ababa or a Gulf hub, connecting to Victoria Falls Airport (VFA, Zimbabwe) or Livingstone Airport (LVI, Zambia). Choose the airport on your chosen side; transfers between the two are easy with the KAZA UniVisa.

**Do I need malaria precautions for Victoria Falls?**
Yes — the region is a malaria area, especially in and after the rains. See your GP or a travel clinic well ahead about antimalarial tablets, pack repellent, cover up at dusk and use the nets provided. This is general information, not medical advice.

**Are the hotels and lodges at Victoria Falls safe?**
The main tourist areas and the listed lodges are established and set up for visitors. Use normal sense: hotel safes, lodge-arranged transfers, no walking alone after dark, and awareness of wildlife (elephants and baboons do wander through). Check the latest UK FCDO advice for both countries before you go.
