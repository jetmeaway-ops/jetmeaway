## Quick answers

**What's the cheapest airline to London?** From **Scotland**, easyJet or Ryanair (from ~£25-£50 off-peak); **BA** runs the most flights and adds London City. From **NI**, easyJet, Aer Lingus and BA; from the far SW and islands, Loganair.

**How long is the flight?** About **1 hour to 1 hour 30 minutes** — but ~3.5-4.5 hours door-to-door.

**Which airport?** **London City (LCY)** is the most central; **Heathrow (LHR)** has the most flights; **Gatwick, Luton, Stansted, Southend** are cheaper but further out.

**Fly or train?** From **Scotland and NI, fly**. From **northern England and the Midlands, the train usually wins**.

**When should I book?** **3-6 weeks** ahead most months; avoid early-morning and Friday-evening business peaks.

**Do I need a passport?** No — UK-to-London is **domestic**, but airlines require **photo ID**.

## Which London airport should you fly into?

London has six airports at very different distances from the centre.

| Airport | Position | To the centre | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| London City (LCY) | East, very central | DLR, minutes to Canary Wharf/City | Business, east/central London — fastest |
| Heathrow (LHR) | West | Elizabeth line / Heathrow Express, ~30-45 min | Most flights, onward connections |
| Gatwick (LGW) | South | Gatwick Express / Thameslink, ~30 min | Cheaper fares, south London |
| Luton (LTN) | North | Fast train link, ~30-45 min | Budget fares, north London |
| Stansted (STN) | Northeast | Stansted Express, ~45-50 min | Budget fares (Ryanair) |
| Southend (SEN) | East | Direct line to Liverpool Street, ~50 min | Quiet airport, Essex/east London |

**The convenience call.** **London City** is the most central and fastest — worth a premium for business or a short central stay. **Heathrow** has the most flights. The others are cheaper but further out, all well linked by express trains. Factor the transfer time into your comparison.

## Airline-by-airline comparison (2026)

### British Airways — the frequency leader

**Typical fare 2026:** £60-£130 return; "Basic" domestic fares are cabin-only.
**Pros:** The most flights on the Scotland-London and Belfast-London routes, into Heathrow and the central London City, plus Gatwick; Avios earning; onward connections.
**Cons:** Higher headline fare than the budget carriers.

### easyJet & Ryanair — the price leaders

**Typical fare 2026:** £25-£60 return off-peak, hand-luggage only.
**Pros:** The lowest headline fares from Scotland and NI, into Gatwick, Luton and Stansted. Frequent flights, regular sales.
**Cons:** Cheapest fares are cabin-only; further-out airports; extras cost more.

### Aer Lingus & Loganair

**Pros:** Aer Lingus competes on Belfast-London (Heathrow); Loganair serves regional and island routes (Inverness, Newquay, Isle of Man, Channel Islands) to London.
**Cons:** Fewer routes; check the fare for baggage.

**The honest read:** from Scotland/NI, easyJet or Ryanair win on fare; BA suits London City convenience, Avios and connections. From the islands and far SW, Loganair is often the only direct option.

## Fly or take the train to London?

The answer depends entirely on where you're starting:

- **From Scotland (Edinburgh, Glasgow, Aberdeen, Inverness):** flying is usually faster door-to-door than the long train, and often cheaper — the default choice, though the train is city-centre and competitive on cost from Edinburgh/Glasgow.
- **From Northern Ireland:** flying (or the ferry-plus-drive) — there's no direct rail, so flying dominates.
- **From northern England and the Midlands (Manchester, Leeds, Newcastle, Birmingham):** the **train usually wins** — fast, frequent services reach London in 2-3 hours city centre to city centre, beating flying once transfers are counted. This is why there are few domestic flights from these cities to London.
- **From the far South West and the islands (Newquay, Isle of Man, Channel Islands):** flying is much faster than the long road/rail journey.

## Which UK cities fly direct to London

## Do you need ID or a passport?

A flight **within the UK** (from Scotland, Northern Ireland, England or Wales to London) is **domestic** — no passport is legally required. But **every airline requires an accepted form of photo ID**, and each sets its own list. Most accept a valid passport, and some accept a photo driving licence on domestic routes. **Check your airline's accepted-ID list and take an ID it accepts.** Note: a flight from the **Republic of Ireland** to London is international, not domestic.

<a id="common-mistakes"></a>

## Common mistakes domestic-London travellers make

1. **Flying from an English rail city.** From Manchester, Leeds or Newcastle the train usually beats flying — check it first.
2. **Booking a business peak.** Early-morning and Friday-evening Scotland-London flights are the priciest — fly midday.
3. **Ignoring which London airport.** A cheap Stansted fare plus a 50-minute train may not beat a London City fare for a central stay.
4. **Travelling with no accepted photo ID.** It's domestic, but airlines require ID — take one they accept.
5. **Adding baggage at the airport.** Always add it online, or travel hand-luggage only.
6. **Assuming a flight is faster than the train.** Door-to-door, for shorter routes the train often wins.
7. **Confusing Republic of Ireland flights with domestic.** Dublin-London is international — different ID rules.
