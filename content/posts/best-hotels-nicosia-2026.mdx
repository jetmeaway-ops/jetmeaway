## Nicosia is the world's last divided capital — what that actually means for visitors

This is the single fact that shapes a trip to Nicosia, and it is worth being clear and practical about it. A **United Nations Buffer Zone**, universally called the **Green Line**, runs straight through the middle of the city. It separates the **Republic of Cyprus** in the south — an EU member state, using the euro — from the **Turkish-occupied north**, which uses the Turkish lira. The division dates from 1974 and remains unresolved. In the old town you will see it plainly: streets that simply stop at a barrel barricade, buildings frozen mid-decay inside the zone, and the closed shell of the old Nicosia International Airport out to the west.

For visitors, the practical picture is simple. **Every hotel in this guide is in the south, in the Republic of Cyprus.** Crossing to the northern side is normal and commonly done: the **Ledra Street checkpoint**, right in the pedestrianised old town, is a walk-through crossing used daily by locals and tourists alike. Take your **passport** — you will show it on both sides — allow a few minutes for the checks, and note the checkpoint hours before you set out. Most people walk rather than drive, because hire-car agreements and motor insurance frequently do not extend across the line. Carry a little cash if you plan to buy anything, since the currency changes at the crossing. Rules and hours can and do change, so read the current UK government travel advice for Cyprus before you go and follow whatever is posted on the day. Beyond that, treat it as what it is for the traveller: a short, orderly walk between two halves of one old city.

## What to see in Nicosia

Nicosia's old town sits inside a near-perfect **star-shaped ring of Venetian walls** with a wide dry moat, built in the 1560s and still the defining shape of the city — the moat is now parkland and one of the nicest walks in the capital. Inside, **Ledra Street** is the pedestrianised spine, lined with shops and cafés and ending at the crossing point. The **Cyprus Museum** holds the island's finest archaeological collection, from Neolithic figurines to Roman statuary, and is the one museum to prioritise if you only have time for one. The **Archbishop's Palace** and **St John's Cathedral** next door are the religious heart of the Republic, the cathedral small but covered inside with 18th-century frescoes. **Faneromeni** — the largest church within the walls — and the **Omeriye** mosque and hammam sit a few streets apart, a reminder of how layered this city's history is. The **Leventis Municipal Museum** tells Nicosia's own story rather than the island's, and the **Ledra Observatory** viewpoint gives you a rooftop look out across the whole divided capital, north and south together.

Then there are the streets themselves. The restored lanes around Faneromeni and the old town have become the city's most enjoyable place to sit, with **buzzy cafés, wine bars and tavernas** that fill with locals and students on warm evenings — this is a real capital going about its business, not a resort strip. Out of town, a hire car opens up the **Troodos mountains** and their cluster of **UNESCO-listed painted Byzantine churches**, the great hilltop **Kykkos Monastery**, and — if you want a beach afternoon — the coast at **Larnaca** or **Limassol**, each roughly 45 minutes away.

## Where to base yourself in Nicosia

**Inside or right next to the Venetian walls** is the choice for atmosphere and walkability. From here Ledra Street, the museums, the cathedrals and the old-town cafés are all on foot, and you can skip the car entirely — the crossing point is a few minutes away too. This is where the boutique hotels and the characterful studios like Unique Studios Phaneromenis 41 sit. The trade-off is parking, which is awkward in the walled town.

**Engomi and Strovolos**, the business districts spreading west and south of the old town, are the value option. They are ordinary working neighbourhoods with modern hotels, serviced apartments and easy parking, generally cheaper than the centre — Strovolos rooms Studios at about £40 is a good example. You commute in by bus or taxi for sightseeing and dinner, which takes minutes rather than a proper journey.

**Near the centre but outside the walls** splits the difference: the streets around the city centre proper hold most of the 3-star hotels, including Asty and Centrum, and keep you within a comfortable walk of the old town while costing less than the boutique properties inside it. For a one- or two-night capital stop, that is usually the sweet spot.

**Is Nicosia really a divided city?**

**Can I cross to the northern side of Nicosia?**
Usually, yes. The Ledra Street checkpoint in the pedestrianised old town is a straightforward, commonly-used crossing on foot, and thousands of people — locals and visitors — pass through it. **When is the best time to visit Nicosia?**
April to June and September to October. Nicosia is inland, with no sea breeze to soften the heat, so it runs hotter than Limassol, Larnaca or Paphos — July and August are genuinely very hot. **Is Nicosia too hot in summer?**
It can be. As an inland capital in a flat plain, Nicosia regularly records the highest summer temperatures on the island, and without the coastal breeze it holds the heat into the evening.
