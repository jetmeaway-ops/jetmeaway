## Why these prices exist (and how to actually get them)

Two patterns drive the whole table:

1. **Shoulder-season timing.** Look at the "cheapest month" column — almost everything bottoms out in **May, June or late August**, just outside the UK school holidays. The same seats cost two to four times more in late July. Timing the *month* matters more than any booking "hack" — we proved that in our [cheapest-month study](/blog/cheapest-month-to-fly-from-uk-2026).
2. **The base-fare trap.** Those £11–£16 short-haul fares are low-cost-carrier *base* fares — cabin bag only. Add a checked bag and seat selection and the price climbs fast. Travel hand-luggage only and the headline price is real.

The Scout Method for turning this table into a booked trip:

1. **Pick by value, not by habit.** If you're flexible on *where*, the top of this table is the cheapest week away you'll ever book.
2. **Fly midweek in the shoulder month** shown for your destination.
3. **Book the flight in its window** — ~72–90 days out for [European short-haul](/flights), ~5 months for long-haul.
4. **Lock the hotel immediately after the flight** — non-refundable rates run 20–40% below flexible ones, and you freeze today's exchange rate.

> **Scout tip:** The cheapest *destination* isn't always the cheapest *trip*. A £11 flight to a pricey city can cost more on the ground than a £30 flight somewhere cheaper to live. Weigh the flight against hotel and daily costs — that's the whole trip, not just the seat.
