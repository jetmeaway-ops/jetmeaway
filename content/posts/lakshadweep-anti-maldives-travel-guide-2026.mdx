## 1. Why it's the "Anti-Maldives"

Lakshadweep is governed by some of the **strictest environmental and tribal protection laws** in India, and every part of the "Anti-Maldives" positioning in this guide traces back to that single structural fact.

**Permit-only.** You cannot book a flight and just show up. Every visitor requires a verified entry permit, and the permit system caps the practical annual tourist intake by making access genuinely difficult to arrange on short notice. The geometry is the protection.

**The vibe.** *Radical simplicity.* You aren't paying for gold-plated faucets — you are paying for a **private lagoon where the coral is so healthy the sea turtles outnumber the people.** Around 40 guests max on Bangaram on a busy week.

**Off-grid by law.** No mass-market chain hotels are permitted. No JW Marriott, no Hilton, no Four Seasons. Just resort-grade infrastructure built and operated under tribal-protection rules. This is structural Hushpitality — the system itself prevents the place becoming the Maldives.

**Fewer tourists than Bhutan.** Bhutan famously caps tourism through its Sustainable Development Fee. Lakshadweep achieves a comparable effect through the permit-and-single-flight bottleneck rather than a pricing mechanism — the practical outcome is similar: a destination that stays quiet because reaching it is genuinely hard, not because it's expensive to reach.

## 2. The Permit, Explained Properly

This is the section most guides gloss over, and it's the single thing that determines whether your Lakshadweep trip actually happens. Get this wrong and no amount of flight-booking skill saves the trip.

**Who needs it.** Every non-resident, full stop — including Indian citizens who aren't registered residents of the Union Territory, and every foreign national including UK passport holders. There is no exemption for a short visit, a transit stop, or a day trip.

**Who issues it.** The **Lakshadweep Administration**, through its tourism arm, **SPORTS (Society for Promotion of Recreational Tourism and Sports)**, based in Kochi. SPORTS is the practical, on-the-ground body that processes permit paperwork, and it's also involved in running some of the government-affiliated guest accommodation on the islands.

**How it actually works for a UK traveller.** In practice, you will very rarely deal with SPORTS directly if you're booking a private resort stay — **Bangaram Island Resort** or **Agatti Island Beach Resort** handles the entire permit application as part of your booking package. The sequence looks like this:

1. Get your **Indian e-Tourist Visa** sorted first (apply online, roughly £20-25, 3-5 working days processing).
2. Confirm your resort booking **4-6 months ahead of travel** — this lead time matters, both for permit processing and for the limited room inventory.
3. Send the resort copies of your passport photo page, your approved Indian visa, and your outline itinerary.
4. The resort (or SPORTS on your behalf) submits the Entry Permit application to the Lakshadweep Tourism office.
5. Processing takes **4-6 weeks**. The permit is emailed to you, usually alongside your final booking confirmation, well before your flight.
6. You carry a printed copy of the permit with your passport and visa when you fly Kochi to Agatti — it's checked on arrival.

**The permit fee** is modest, typically around ₹500 (roughly £5), and is usually folded into your resort package price rather than billed separately.

**The one rule that actually matters:** never book your Kochi-Agatti Alliance Air flight before the resort confirms your permit is being processed. Arriving at Agatti without a valid permit means being turned back at the airport — there is no on-arrival permit option the way there is for, say, the Andaman Islands' Restricted Area Permit, which is issued on landing at Port Blair. Lakshadweep's permit must be secured **in advance**, full stop.

## 4. Getting There — Kochi, Agatti, Alliance Air

**Leg 1: London to Kochi.** BA, Air India, Lufthansa or Etihad, roughly 12 hours including one stop (commonly via Dubai or Frankfurt). Off-peak return fares land around £580; December-January peak season pushes that to £820 or more. [Search flights to Kochi (COK)](/flights?to=COK) to compare live pricing across providers before you commit to dates.

**The mandatory Kochi overnight.** Because your long-haul typically lands late and the onward Alliance Air flight departs the next morning, book at least one night in Kochi before flying to Agatti — don't attempt a same-day connection. This also gives you a buffer if your international leg is delayed. See our [Kerala hotels guide](/blog/best-hotels-kerala-2026) for Kochi properties across every budget, from heritage stays in Fort Kochi to airport-convenient options.

**Leg 2: Kochi to Agatti.** This is the leg most guides underexplain. **Alliance Air is currently the sole operator** on the Kochi (COK) to Agatti (AGX) route, flying an ATR-72 turboprop, and the flight takes roughly **90 minutes**. Because it's a single carrier on a small aircraft with limited daily capacity, seats are coordinated tightly around resort guest schedules — this is the real reason resorts insist on booking this leg for you rather than letting you book it independently. Landing at Agatti is a genuine event in itself: the runway is a narrow strip surrounded by open water on both sides, one of the more dramatic commercial approaches anywhere in India.

**Leg 3: Agatti to Bangaram.** If Bangaram is your base (and for most travellers chasing the full Anti-Maldives experience, it should be), a further **speedboat transfer of around 1.5 hours** completes the journey, arranged directly by the resort to meet your Alliance Air arrival.

**Total door-to-door time from London:** realistically a full day and a half once you include the Kochi overnight — this is not a long weekend destination. Build the travel time into your expectations from the start.

<a id="which-islands"></a>

## 5. Which Islands You Can Actually Visit

This matters enough to be its own section, because it's the most common misconception in Lakshadweep planning. Of the 36 atolls and 10 inhabited islands, **non-Indian tourists in 2026 have realistic access to exactly two: Bangaram and Agatti.**

**Bangaram** is uninhabited outside the resort itself — no permanent local population, which is precisely why it's the one island permitted to hold an alcohol licence. This is the Anti-Maldives peak: a private-feeling island with a hard cap around 40 guests.

**Agatti** is the entry-point island, home to the only airport in the tourist-accessible cluster and a more developed (though still small-scale) tourism setup, including Agatti Island Beach Resort.

**Kavaratti** (the territory's capital), **Kadmat** and **Minicoy** are inhabited by Lakshadweep's indigenous population and governed under tribal-protection rules that, in practice, keep them closed to non-Indian tourism even where Indian domestic travellers occasionally have narrower access. If a booking agent or a blog claims easy foreign-tourist access to these islands, verify it directly with SPORTS Kochi or your resort before planning around it — this is exactly the kind of detail that changes with policy and shouldn't be assumed from older content.

The practical takeaway for a UK-planned trip: build your itinerary around **Bangaram as the core stay, Agatti as the transit and contrast stop**, and don't plan around visiting the inhabited islands unless you've confirmed current access directly with official channels.

<a id="five-day-shape"></a>

## 7. The 5-Day "Secret Turquoise" Shape

**Day 0 — Kochi overnight.** Land at Kochi (COK) from London, transfer to a Kochi hotel (see our [Kerala hotels guide](/blog/best-hotels-kerala-2026)), sleep off the long-haul. If you have energy, Fort Kochi's Chinese fishing nets and colonial old town are worth a couple of hours.

**Day 1 — The Agatti landing.** Alliance Air ATR-72 from Kochi, ~90 minutes. Landing at Agatti is itself a genuine spectacle — the runway is a thin strip of land surrounded by endless blue, one of the world's more scenic commercial approaches. Walk straight off the plane to the lagoon. Take the **boat transfer to Bangaram** — about 1.5 hours by speedboat, arranged by the resort.

**Day 2 — The turtle snorkel.** The lagoons here are shallow and crystal clear. **You don't need a boat** — you can often see hawksbill turtles, eagle rays and parrotfish just by wading waist-deep from the beach. Sunset kayak around the island.

**Day 3 — The dive day or the deep rest day.** If you're PADI-certified or want to do a discover-scuba session, this is the day to book it — Bangaram Reef and the nearby dive sites are the highlight of the trip for divers. If diving isn't your thing, this is simply a day of doing nothing on a beach that belongs to about 40 people.

**Day 4 — The sandbank picnic.** Take a **traditional dhoni** (the local lagoon boat) to a nearby uninhabited sandbank. This is the Anti-Maldives luxury: total silence on a patch of white sand that isn't shared with 200 other guests. The resort packs a picnic, sets up a parasol, and leaves you for several hours before returning for sunset.

**Day 5 — The return via Agatti.** Boat transfer back to Agatti, a final look at the reef wall, then the Alliance Air flight back to Kochi, connecting onward to London (with the same buffer-night logic as your outbound trip if your onward connection is tight).

For a longer, 7-9 day version of this trip, simply extend the Bangaram stay by 2-3 nights and add a genuine Kerala add-on — Munnar's tea-estate hills or the Alleppey backwaters — either side of the Kochi transit, covered in full in our [Kerala hotels guide](/blog/best-hotels-kerala-2026).

<a id="diving"></a>

## 8. Snorkelling & Diving Deep Dive

Lakshadweep's underwater environment is the entire reason the "Anti-Maldives" comparison holds up, and it deserves more than a single paragraph.

**Water conditions.** The lagoons around Bangaram, Agatti and Kadmat are shallow (typically 1-3 metres across most reef-flat areas) and consistently clear, with visibility frequently reaching 30+ metres in the November-March core season. This is shallow enough that a huge amount of the marine life here is visible from a simple snorkel off the beach — no boat, no dive certification required.

**What you'll see.** Hawksbill and green sea turtles are genuinely common, often visible from waist-deep water without any boat trip. Blacktip reef sharks, manta rays (best December-March around Bangaram Reef specifically), eagle rays, parrotfish and hundreds of species of reef fish round out a reef system that remains in notably good health thanks to the same protection laws that make the permit process a hassle in the first place.

**Dive sites worth knowing by name.** **Bangaram Reef** (the manta ray season, December-March), **Agatti Reef Wall** (a dramatic drop-off to around 60 metres, for certified divers), and **Suheli Pitti** (a remote lagoon reachable by speedboat, quieter than the main reef sites). Bangaram Island Resort runs PADI-certified courses from beginner discover-scuba sessions through to advanced open-water certification; budget roughly £80-120 for a guided two-tank dive, or a multi-day course price if you're getting certified from scratch.

**The reef-safe sunscreen rule, again, because it's enforced not suggested.** Chemical sunscreens containing oxybenzone or octinoxate are physically confiscated at Agatti airport as part of the territory's reef-protection regime. Bring zinc-oxide-based, genuinely reef-safe sunscreen from home — don't assume you can buy an acceptable brand locally once you land.

<a id="alcohol"></a>

## 9. Alcohol, Dry Islands & Bangaram

This is one of the most-asked practical questions and deserves a clear answer up front: **Lakshadweep is dry, except for Bangaram Island.**

As a predominantly Muslim Union Territory operating under tribal-protection regulations, alcohol is prohibited on every inhabited island — Agatti, Kavaratti, Kadmat and Minicoy included. **Bangaram** is the sole exception, because it's uninhabited outside the resort and operates under a separate licence specific to the property. If a sundowner is a non-negotiable part of your trip definition, **Bangaram has to be your base**, not just a day-trip add-on.

The resort runs a low-key bar serving wine, beer and a limited spirits selection as part of the meal package. Don't attempt to bring your own alcohol from Kochi — luggage is checked on arrival at Agatti, and confiscated alcohol is destroyed with no refund and no exceptions. Treat the dry-island culture as a genuine feature of the destination rather than an inconvenience to route around; it's directly tied to the same regulatory framework that keeps the crowds and the development pressure off these islands in the first place.

<a id="connectivity"></a>

## 10. Connectivity & the Digital Detox

If you've read this far expecting a workaround for reliable WiFi, there isn't one — and that's genuinely the point of the trip for most people who go.

**Network reality.** Only **BSNL**, the Indian state telecom, has any meaningful coverage on the islands, and even that is patchy outside Agatti and Kavaratti specifically. Popular UK eSIM providers like **Airalo** route their India eSIMs through Airtel, which has essentially zero coverage in Lakshadweep — so a standard India eSIM bought for a mainland India trip **will not work** here. Don't rely on it.

**Resort WiFi.** Bangaram and Agatti Island Beach Resort both run limited satellite-uplink WiFi, reserved primarily for genuine emergencies and brief daily check-ins — realistically 2-5 minutes of email or messaging access per day, no video calls, no streaming. Tell your workplace and family this in advance, set an out-of-office, and mentally prepare for it rather than fighting it on arrival.

**The upside.** Most travellers who go into this expecting frustration come out the other side saying the unplugged days were the actual highlight of the trip. If total disconnection genuinely worries you (health condition requiring monitoring, family situation, work that can't tolerate a gap), base primarily in **Kavaratti** if and when access allows, where BSNL signal is comparatively more usable — though this trades away the Bangaram exclusivity that's the core appeal of the destination.

<a id="survival-list"></a>

## 11. Lakshadweep Survival List for UK travellers

1. **Strictly dry, with one exception.** Alcohol is prohibited on every inhabited island. **Bangaram is the only resort with a licence.** If a sundowner is mandatory for your trip, Bangaram is the only base.
2. **Cash is mandatory.** ATMs are sparse to non-existent and rarely accept international cards. Withdraw all the rupees you'll need in **Kochi** before flying out — there is no fallback once you're on the islands.
3. **BSNL only.** Forget your UK roaming and standard India eSIMs (Airtel-routed ones don't work here). **Only BSNL** has any meaningful signal, and even then it's patchy. This is a genuine **digital detox**, not a marketing phrase.
4. **Reef-safe sunscreen ONLY.** Lakshadweep is a fragile coral ecosystem. Chemical sunscreens (oxybenzone, octinoxate) are confiscated at Agatti airport. Bring **zinc-oxide-based reef-safe brands** from home.
5. **No coral souvenirs.** Taking coral, shells or sand off the islands is a genuine **legal offence** under the Wildlife Protection Act — not a customs formality.
6. **Pack light.** The Alliance Air ATR-72 baggage limit is around 15 kg per passenger, including hand luggage. Bring **swimwear, rashguard, snorkel gear, reef shoes, lightweight cottons**. Skip the formal wear.
7. **Reef-safe etiquette.** Don't touch the coral, don't chase turtles, don't feed fish. The reef is healthy precisely because the rules are enforced.
8. **Sort your Indian visa first.** The Entry Permit application generally needs your approved e-Tourist Visa details, so get the visa moving before you contact the resort about the permit.
9. **Buffer nights, both ends.** Don't book a same-day Kochi-Agatti connection off your international long-haul, and don't book your London flight home on the same day as your Agatti-Kochi return leg — build in at least one buffer night each way in case of delays.
10. **Travel insurance with medical evacuation cover.** On-island medical facilities are minimal; anything serious means evacuation to Kochi. Don't travel without a policy that genuinely covers this.
11. **Pack a headtorch.** Street lighting is minimal to non-existent after dark outside resort grounds.
12. **Carry printed copies of your permit, visa and passport photo page**, separate from your phone, in case of a dead battery with no charging point handy.
