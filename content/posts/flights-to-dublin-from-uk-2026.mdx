<a id="passport"></a>

## Do you need a passport to fly to Dublin?

This is the most-asked question on the route, and the answer has two layers.

**The legal position:** Ireland and the UK share the **Common Travel Area (CTA)**, a long-standing arrangement that lets British and Irish citizens travel, live and work between the two countries without needing a passport. So legally, a UK or Irish citizen does not require a passport to enter Ireland.

**The practical reality:** every airline still requires you to present an **accepted form of photo ID** to board an international flight, and each airline sets its own list. Most require a valid passport; some accept a passport card or other government photo ID on this specific route. Because the accepted-ID list varies by carrier and can change, **the safest approach is simply to travel with your passport** — it's the one document every airline accepts, every time. Turning up with only a driving licence risks being denied boarding if that carrier doesn't accept it.

**Non-UK/Irish nationals** travelling to Dublin must meet Ireland's normal entry requirements, which are separate from the CTA — check the [UK government's Ireland travel advice](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/ireland) and Irish immigration rules for your nationality before booking.

## Airline-by-airline comparison (2026)

### Ryanair — the price and frequency leader

**Typical fare 2026:** £17-£40 return off-peak, hand-luggage only; more at peak.
**UK airports:** A huge network — London Stansted and Gatwick, Manchester, Liverpool, Birmingham, Edinburgh, Glasgow and many more.
**Pros:** The most flights on the route (~396/week), the lowest headline fares, and departures from more UK airports than anyone. Frequent seat sales.
**Cons:** Only a small under-seat bag is free; a larger cabin bag, hold bag, allocated seat and priority all cost extra. The cheap fare climbs fast if you need luggage.

### Aer Lingus — Ireland's flag carrier

**Typical fare 2026:** £25-£60 return; cheapest fares cabin-only, higher tiers include a bag.
**UK airports:** Heathrow, London City, Manchester, Edinburgh, Birmingham and more.
**Pros:** Nearly as frequent as Ryanair (~388/week), a slightly more comfortable product, and its higher fare tiers bundle a checked bag and seat. Seamless connections at Dublin if you're travelling onward (Dublin has US pre-clearance, handy for transatlantic trips).
**Cons:** Headline fare usually a little above Ryanair's; check the fare tier for what's included.

### British Airways — Heathrow and London City

**Typical fare 2026:** £60-£120 return; "Basic" fares are cabin-only.
**Pros:** London City is fast and central; Avios earning; standard fares include a bag.
**Cons:** Only two London airports; pricier than the budget carriers.

### easyJet

**Typical fare 2026:** £30-£70 return, hand-luggage only by default.
**Pros:** Useful from several UK airports; frequent flights; regular sales.
**Cons:** Hold bag and allocated seat cost extra, like Ryanair.

**The honest read:** for the lowest price travelling hand-luggage only, Ryanair wins. For a slightly nicer experience or a bundled bag and seat, Aer Lingus is often the better all-in value once luggage is added. BA suits London City convenience and Avios collectors.

## UK airport comparison — fly local

Dublin is unusual in how widely it's served from the UK — Ryanair and Aer Lingus between them fly it from dozens of airports across England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. That means the cheapest and easiest option is almost always **your nearest airport**, not a trek to London.

The busiest and most competitive routes are from **London (Stansted and Gatwick on Ryanair, Heathrow and City on Aer Lingus and BA), Manchester, Liverpool, Birmingham, Edinburgh and Glasgow**, but plenty of smaller UK airports have direct Dublin flights too. Always price your local direct option first — on this route it rarely pays to route through a bigger airport.

## St Patrick's weekend and event dates

**St Patrick's weekend (around 17 March)** is Dublin's biggest party of the year and its single largest travel spike — flights and hotels both rise sharply for that specific weekend. If being there for the festival matters, book 8-12 weeks ahead. If you just want a cheap Dublin break, avoid that weekend and fares fall back to normal.

Beyond St Patrick's, watch for **rugby Six Nations home weekends** (usually February and March), **major concerts and festivals** at venues like the Aviva Stadium and 3Arena, and **big GAA fixtures** — any of these can lift fares and fill hotels on otherwise ordinary dates. Check the date before you book if your travel is flexible.

## The booking windows that actually save money

- **Off-peak months (Jan, Feb, late Sep, Oct, Nov):** Book **3-6 weeks out** — this short, high-frequency route doesn't reward booking far ahead outside peaks.
- **St Patrick's weekend, summer, Christmas:** Book **6-10 weeks out** (8-12 for St Patrick's specifically).
- **Event dates (rugby, concerts):** Book as soon as the fixture or gig is announced.
- **Weekend breaks:** A midweek or Saturday-out departure often undercuts the Friday-evening rush.

Ryanair and Aer Lingus both run frequent sales, so set a price alert and book when a fare drops rather than paying the standing rate.

## Common mistakes UK-Dublin travellers make

1. **Travelling with only a driving licence.** The CTA means no passport is legally required, but airlines require accepted photo ID — take your passport to be safe.
2. **Booking St Patrick's weekend by accident.** Check the date; that weekend is far pricier than the rest of March.
3. **Routing through London unnecessarily.** Dublin flies from dozens of UK airports — your local one is almost always cheapest.
4. **Judging Ryanair on the headline fare.** A £20 fare plus bags and seat can match an all-in Aer Lingus fare that bundles them.
5. **Adding baggage at the airport.** Always add it online at booking — Ryanair's airport bag fees are steep.
6. **Overpacking for a city break.** Dublin is a hand-luggage weekend; travel cabin-only and keep the fare low.
7. **Forgetting non-UK/Irish nationals need to meet normal entry rules.** The CTA covers British and Irish citizens only.
