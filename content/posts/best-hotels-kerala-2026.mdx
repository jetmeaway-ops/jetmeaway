  - q: "Is Kerala a single trip, or should I plan it as a multi-stop?"
    a: "Multi-stop, always. Kerala is a state, not a city, and treating it as a single-base holiday is the biggest planning mistake UK travellers make. The standard 10-12 day itinerary: Kochi (2 nights, heritage) → Munnar hills (2 nights, tea plantations and cool air) → Alleppey backwaters with overnight houseboat (1-2 nights, the iconic Kerala experience) → Kovalam beach (2-3 nights, the relaxed finale). Pick at least three of those four zones — choosing only one and you've done Kerala wrong. On a tighter budget or timeline, Kochi + Alleppey + Munnar as a triangle works well without the long drive south to Kovalam."
  - q: "When is the best time to visit Kerala?"
    a: "December-February is peak — dry, comfortable 24-30°C, every backwater open and every spice plantation tour running. March-May is very hot (32-38°C) but rates drop 25-40%. June-September is the southwest monsoon, which is genuinely under-rated for two reasons: hotel rates collapse (50-60% lower), and authentic Ayurveda retreats (Kalari Kovilakom, the CGH Earth properties) consider monsoon the *best* time for treatments because the humidity opens skin pores and herb absorption peaks. October-November is excellent shoulder season, post-monsoon greenery, fewer crowds. Best time overall for first-timers: October to March."
  - q: "Is monsoon a good time to visit Kerala for Ayurveda?"
    a: "Yes — counter-intuitively, it's considered the best time by Ayurvedic tradition, not just the cheapest. The southwest monsoon (June-September) raises humidity, which Ayurvedic physicians say opens skin pores and improves herb-oil absorption during treatments like Abhyanga and panchakarma. Kalari Kovilakom and the senior CGH Earth wellness properties actively market monsoon packages for this reason. The trade-off: backwater houseboat trips are less comfortable in heavy rain, and some outdoor sightseeing (Munnar viewpoints, Periyar boat safaris) gets rained off. If your priority is wellness over sightseeing, book monsoon and save 50-60% on rates."

  - q: "What is an Alleppey houseboat overnight, and is it worth it?"
    a: "Yes — and it's the single non-negotiable Kerala experience. The Alleppey backwaters are a 900-km network of canals, lakes, and lagoons, and the traditional way to experience them is on a *kettuvallam* — a converted teak-and-coir houseboat with 1-3 bedrooms, a kitchen, a deck, and a cook on board. You set off around lunchtime, drift through villages where life happens at canoe pace, eat fresh-caught karimeen (pearl-spot fish) cooked banana-leaf style, sleep on board, wake up to a different stretch of water. Book through your main hotel or a CGH Earth-style operator — the unbranded houseboat market has wide quality variation."
  - q: "How much does an Alleppey houseboat actually cost?"

Mid-range 2-bedroom boats with better fit-out run £80-130. Premium boats from heritage operators (Kumarakom Lake Resort's own fleet, CGH Earth-affiliated boats) start around £150-250 and go considerably higher for multi-bedroom groups. Always confirm the price is per boat, not per person, and that all meals and the overnight are included before booking — day-only cruises (no overnight) are a cheaper 3-4 hour option from around £15-25 per person."

The real risk is unlicensed, unbranded operators cutting corners on safety kit; always book through your hotel or a recognisable operator rather than a random tout at the jetty. Swimming in the open backwater lake (Vembanad) isn't recommended due to boat traffic and unclear water quality — house boats have designated swim stops in cleaner side-channels where locals swim."

## The Scout's Take: Backwater Stillness or Cliffside Ocean?

Every Kerala five-star pitches a different version of slowness. The question is whether you want *backwater stillness* or *cliffside ocean drama*.

If you're the kind of traveller who comes for the iconic Kerala photograph — the wooden houseboat drifting through coconut palms, the morning mist on Vembanad Lake, the cook on board grilling karimeen for lunch — **Kumarakom Lake Resort** is the answer. It's the heritage backwater stay in pure form: 28 acres of lakefront, traditional Kerala-style *tharavadu* villas with private pools, a meandering swimming pool that's actually built around the resort, and the original wooden architecture that defined the high-end backwater hotel category in the early 2000s. Mornings here are still — genuinely still, not Instagram-still — and the resort's own kettuvallam houseboat fleet drifts in and out at dawn.

Compare that to **The Leela Kovalam** — same Kerala, completely different mood. Built on a cliff above two private crescent-bay beaches at the southern tip of Kerala, it's the dramatic-coastline counterpart to the calm-backwater play. The hotel sits 50 metres above sea level, with a glass-walled lobby that opens straight onto the cliff edge and an infinity pool that genuinely seems to fall into the Arabian Sea. Sunset cocktails on the terrace are the southern-India benchmark experience. The two private beaches are accessible only by hotel pathway. It's the romantic-getaway Kerala, the post-honeymoon-decompression Kerala, the I-want-to-see-the-sea-from-my-bed Kerala.

For the iconic Kerala photograph and the slowest mornings of your life, Kumarakom Lake Resort wins. For dramatic coastal luxury and sunset cocktails, The Leela Kovalam is the smarter call. And if 2026's budget doesn't stretch to either, the [budget tier below](#budget-hotels) gets you a genuine backwater houseboat night and a Fort Kochi heritage-street base for a fraction of the price — Kerala is one of the rare places where the cheap version of the trip isn't a compromise, it's a different, equally real version of the same state.

Kerala competes directly with Sri Lanka and Bali in the tropical-wellness category — three Indian Ocean destinations that UK travellers shopping multi-week retreats and Ayurveda holidays consistently shortlist together. For UK travellers prioritising authentic wellness alongside Indian Ocean beach time, Kerala typically wins on substance and value; Sri Lanka edges it on the tea-country hill stations; Bali wins for design-led spa aesthetics. ## Beyond the Hotel: 10 Things to Do in Kerala (2026)

Kerala rewards travellers who slow down and don't try to compress the state into 5 days. Here's the prioritised list:

**1. Alleppey Backwater Houseboat Overnight** — Non-negotiable. Book a private 1-3 bedroom *kettuvallam* through your main hotel or a vetted CGH-Earth-style operator, or a budget 1-bedroom boat direct through an Alleppey guesthouse from around £45 for two. Lunchtime departure, evening anchorage, fresh-caught fish dinner, dawn awakening on the water. The single most photographed Kerala experience.

**2. Munnar Tea Plantations & Eravikulam National Park** — Tea estates carpeting the hills at 1,500m altitude, plus Eravikulam National Park where the endangered Nilgiri tahr (mountain goat) roams free. Tea Museum at Munnar town gives you the production-line context. Half-day for the plantations, separate morning for Eravikulam.

**3. Periyar Tiger Reserve Boat Safari & Bamboo Rafting** — Periyar's lake safari is the gentlest way to see wild elephants in Kerala. Book the 7am boat for the highest sighting odds; December-April is the best window for wildlife sightings overall. The half-day bamboo-rafting trek is the under-rated alternative — small group, on-foot through the reserve buffer zone with a tribal guide.

**4. Fort Kochi Chinese Fishing Nets at Sunset** — The 14th-century shore-operated fishing nets are a UNESCO-symbolic Kerala image. Walk the Vasco da Gama Square promenade at golden hour, watch the nets being lowered, eat at one of the fish-market shacks afterwards (the fish is grilled in front of you for ₹400-600).

**5. Mattancherry Palace & Paradesi Synagogue** — The 1568 Mattancherry Dutch Palace holds Kerala's best Hindu mural collection. The Paradesi Synagogue (the oldest active synagogue in the Commonwealth) is 5 minutes' walk away — a remnant of Kerala's 2,000-year Jewish heritage. Allow 2 hours for both.

**6. Kathakali Performance** — Kerala's traditional dance-drama, with elaborate face painting, elaborate eye-language, and stories from the *Mahabharata*. The Kerala Kathakali Centre in Fort Kochi runs nightly performances; arrive 90 minutes before curtain to watch the make-up application — that's half the show.

**7. Kovalam Lighthouse & Hawa Beach** — Kovalam's two main beaches, separated by the iconic red-and-white striped lighthouse. Lighthouse Beach is busier and shack-lined; Hawa Beach (north of the lighthouse) is quieter and better for swimming. Climb the lighthouse for the best view of both.

**8. Varkala Cliff Sunset** — 45 minutes north of Kovalam, Varkala's red-sandstone cliffs drop dramatically into the Arabian Sea. The cliff-top walking path is lined with cafes, yoga studios, and surf-shop boutiques. Time your visit for late afternoon and stay for sunset over the cliff edge.

**9. Authentic Ayurveda Treatment** — Be specific: a "tourist Ayurvedic massage" at a beach hotel is a tourist spa, not Ayurveda. Real Ayurveda is doctor-prescribed, multi-day, and follows a *prakriti* (body-type) consultation. Book through CGH Earth, Niraamaya, or (for the serious option) Kalari Kovilakom. Minimum useful stay is 7 nights; 14-21 for a panchakarma cycle. Monsoon season (June-September) is, by tradition, the best time for treatment.

**10. Theyyam Ritual Performance** — North Kerala only, October to May. Theyyam is a 1,500-year-old ritual where village performers possessed by deities dance in elaborate red-and-gold costumes. Performances are religious, not theatrical, and start at 11pm and run until dawn. Book through Taj Bekal, a Kannur-based homestay, or budget guesthouse — independent ritual schedules are unreliable, so confirm through your accommodation.

**11. The Morning Abhyanga Oil Massage** — The defining Kerala wellness ritual: a 90-minute warm-oil full-body massage performed by two therapists working in synchronised mirror-image strokes from head to toe. The oil — typically sesame infused with Ayurvedic herbs, prescribed to match your *prakriti* (body-type) — is poured in slow streams and worked through every joint. Every Ayurveda resort on this list offers it as a standalone treatment or as the first step in a multi-day panchakarma. Even at the heritage hotels (Brunton Boatyard, Taj Malabar) it's the morning ritual that defines a Kerala wellness day. Allow 90 minutes, schedule it before breakfast, and don't shower for at least an hour afterwards so the oil absorbs.

**12. The Onam Sadhya Banana-Leaf Feast** — Onam is Kerala's harvest festival (late August / early September), and the centrepiece is the *sadhya* — a vegetarian feast of 24-28 dishes served on a fresh banana leaf, eaten with the right hand only. Coconut-based curries, rice, sambar, avial, payasam (the closing sweet of rice pudding and jaggery). Every traditional Kerala home serves it during Onam week; the heritage hotels (Brunton Boatyard, Kumarakom Lake Resort, Taj Bekal) run formal Sadhya lunches year-round on request. Even outside Onam season, the abridged version (12-14 dishes) is the most authentic single meal you can eat in Kerala — book a day ahead at your hotel, or ask a homestay family to cook it.

## Booking Kerala Hotels in 2026: Rates and Dates to Avoid

Kerala rates swing hardest around two windows: **Onam** (late August/early September, Kerala's own harvest festival, when domestic demand spikes) and the **Christmas/New Year fortnight** (peak season overlapping with UK half-term and the dry-season sweet spot).
