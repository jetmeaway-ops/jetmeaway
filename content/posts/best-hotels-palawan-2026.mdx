## The Scout's Take: El Nido, Coron or Puerto Princesa?

**El Nido** is the postcard — Bacuit Bay's limestone lagoons, Big and Small Lagoon, Seven Commandos beach, and the tour boats that made Palawan a bucket-list name. It's the busiest and most tourist-geared of the three, with the deepest choice of budget beds. Stay in town for walkability, Corong-Corong for sunset quiet, or Nacpan Beach for empty sand.

**Coron**, on Busuanga to the north, is the diver's Palawan — karst lakes you swim in and a bay full of WWII shipwrecks. Fewer budget rooms, a more low-key town, and a slightly different rhythm; most people reach it by ferry from El Nido or a Manila-hopper flight.

**Puerto Princesa** is the capital and main gateway — flatter, more built-up, cheapest to fly into, and the launch point for the underground river. It's the value base: easy flights, cheap rooms, and day trips out to Sabang and Honda Bay.

For a first trip chasing the lagoons, go El Nido; for wrecks and karst lakes, Coron; for cheapest access and the underground river, Puerto Princesa. Many travellers combine two of the three.

## UK Practicalities

- **Getting there**: No direct UK flights. Connect via Manila (MNL) or a Gulf/Asian hub, then a domestic flight to Puerto Princesa (PPS), El Nido's Lio airport (ENI) or Coron's Busuanga (USU). [Search flights to Palawan (PPS)](/flights?to=PPS). Check the [UK government entry rules for the Philippines](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/philippines) before you fly.
- **Transfers**: El Nido is a 5–6 hour van ride from Puerto Princesa (~£8–12) or a short hop into Lio. Coron is reached by ferry from El Nido (3.5–4 hours) or by air. Tricycles and vans handle short hops cheaply.
- **Fees**: El Nido's Eco-Tourism Development Fee (~₱200 / £3, valid ~10 days) is usually collected before your first tour. Coron, Puerto Princesa and the underground river charge their own small environmental, terminal and permit fees — budget a few pounds per person.
- **Currency**: Philippine peso (PHP). Carry cash for small towns; card acceptance is patchy outside resorts. Tipping is appreciated but not obligatory.
- **Best months**: November–May is dry (peak December–February and Easter). June–October is wet/typhoon season — cheaper and greener, with occasional ferry disruption. May and November are the value shoulder.
- **Budget**: Budget-tier trip — £30–50/day per person all-in with tours. Mid-range — £70–120/day. Island-hopping tours run roughly £15–25 each including lunch and gear.
