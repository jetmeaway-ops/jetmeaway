## What to See in Houston

Start with the reason most people fly here. **Space Center Houston** is the official visitor centre for **NASA's Johnson Space Center**, out at Clear Lake about 25 miles south-east of downtown, and it is comfortably the biggest draw in the city. Alongside the exhibition halls — Saturn V, spacecraft, moon rocks — it runs a tram tour into the working Johnson Space Center campus itself, including the historic **Mission Control** room from which the Apollo missions were flown. Book online in advance, arrive early for the tram, and allow at least half a day; realistically, with the drive each way, it eats a day.

Back in town, the **Museum District** sits just south of downtown around Hermann Park and holds around a dozen institutions within walking distance of each other. The **Museum of Fine Arts, Houston** is one of the largest art museums in the United States. The **Houston Museum of Natural Science** is the family favourite, strong on dinosaurs, gems and its butterfly centre. And the **Menil Collection**, in a low Renzo Piano building among the bungalows of Montrose, is free to enter and one of the finest private collections anywhere in America — modern art, surrealism, antiquities and tribal art in beautifully lit galleries. Next door, the **Rothko Chapel** is a small non-denominational space built around fourteen Mark Rothko paintings; it is free, it takes twenty minutes, and it is the quietest place in Houston.

For the city itself, walk **Buffalo Bayou Park**, the green corridor that runs west from downtown along the bayou — trails, art installations, kayak launches and the best skyline views in Houston, especially at dusk. **Hermann Park** gives you the **Houston Zoo**, a Japanese garden, a lake with pedal boats and a miniature railway that children love. Sport is a big part of the city's identity: the **Astros** play baseball at **Minute Maid Park** downtown, the **Texans** play NFL football at **NRG Stadium** by the Medical Center, and the **Rockets** play basketball at **Toyota Center** downtown — all three venues are central and easy to reach. The **Galleria** in Uptown is one of the largest shopping centres in the country, complete with an indoor ice rink.

And then there is the food, which is the most underrated reason to come. Houston is among the most ethnically diverse cities in the United States and eats like it: **Tex-Mex** everywhere from taquerias to old-guard institutions, a superb **Vietnamese** scene along **Bellaire Boulevard** in the south-west, **Gulf seafood** including oysters and crawfish in season, and proper **Texas barbecue** built on brisket and smoke. Some of the best meals in the city cost under £15 and come from a strip mall. The official [Visit Houston tourist board](https://www.visithoustontexas.com/) is a good place to check current exhibitions, festivals and fixtures for your dates.

## Choosing Where to Stay in Houston

- **Downtown** — best for sport, the theater district, the convention centre and anyone who wants to walk to dinner. Minute Maid Park, Toyota Center and Discovery Green are all here, and it is the hub of the METRORail line. Expect to pay for parking.
- **Galleria and Uptown** — the shopping district and the address for Houston's smartest hotels, a few miles west of downtown. Good restaurants, good hotels, no meaningful public transport; you will drive.
- **Museum District and Texas Medical Center** — the pick for museums, Hermann Park, the Zoo and NRG Stadium, and essential if you are visiting the Texas Medical Center, the largest medical complex in the world. The rail line runs straight through it, which makes this the least car-dependent base in the city.
- **Montrose and Midtown** — Houston's most walkable neighbourhoods for restaurants, bars and independent shops, with the Menil and the Rothko Chapel on the doorstep. Fewer hotels than the districts above, but the best food-and-nightlife base.
- **Near IAH or Hobby** — the cheapest rooms in this guide and the obvious choice for an early or late flight. IAH is about 23 miles north, Hobby closer in to the south-east. Both mean driving everywhere.

## Getting Around Houston — the Honest Version

Be clear-eyed about this before you book: **Houston is enormous and built for cars.** The city sprawls across hundreds of square miles of freeway grid, and **public transport is limited once you leave the downtown METRORail corridor** — that one light-rail line, linking downtown with Midtown, the Museum District, the Medical Center and NRG Park, is genuinely useful and covers several of the places visitors want, but nothing else does. Space Center Houston, the Galleria, Buffalo Bayou's western reaches and every budget hotel in this guide are off it. **Budget for a hire car, or for Uber and Lyft**, and treat that as a fixed trip cost rather than an extra. Remember you drive on the right, distances between attractions are long, and parking is free out in the suburbs but charged downtown and in Uptown.

The weather deserves the same honesty. **Summers, roughly June to September, are seriously hot and humid** — mid-thirties Celsius with heavy humidity — and the **Atlantic hurricane season runs from around June to November**, with Houston close enough to the Gulf to see tropical storms, torrential rain and flooding in a bad year. Most trips pass without incident, but if you travel in that window take insurance that covers weather disruption and watch the forecasts. **Spring and autumn are far more pleasant**: March to May and October to November give warm days, lower humidity and much better conditions for the parks. Before booking, read the current [UK government travel advice for the USA](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/usa), which covers entry requirements, the ESTA, health and safety guidance and is updated regularly.

## Houston Hotels FAQs

**Do UK travellers need a visa for the USA?**
UK passport holders do not need a full visa for a short tourist trip, but they do need an approved **ESTA** under the **Visa Waiver Program**. You apply online in advance, there is a fee, and approval is **not automatic or instant** — some applications are referred and take days. Apply through the official US government ESTA site only, well before you fly, and check the current rules before booking, because entry requirements change. If your ESTA is refused you will need a proper visa from the embassy instead, which takes far longer.

**What currency is used in Houston?**
The **US Dollar (USD)**. Cards are accepted almost everywhere, including for very small purchases, and contactless works fine — but carry a little cash for tips, valet parking and food trucks. Pound figures in this guide are rough conversions for guidance only and move with the exchange rate, so treat them as a guide to the price band rather than an exact quote.

**Are there direct flights from the UK to Houston?**
Yes. **British Airways and United both fly London to Houston George Bush Intercontinental (IAH) non-stop**, with a flying time of roughly **10 hours** westbound; the return east is usually a little quicker. All non-stop UK services use IAH rather than Hobby, so if a ticket routes you into Hobby you are changing planes somewhere in the US first.

**Which Houston airport should I use — IAH or Hobby?**
Houston has two. **George Bush Intercontinental (IAH)** is the international gateway, about **23 miles north** of downtown, and it is where every UK non-stop lands. **William P. Hobby (HOU)** is the smaller, older airport to the south-east, closer in and handling mostly domestic and short-haul international routes. Check which one your ticket says before booking an airport hotel — they are on opposite sides of a very large city.

**Do Houston hotels charge resort fees and taxes on top of the room rate?**
Often, yes, and this catches UK travellers out. **US hotels commonly add a daily resort, destination or amenity fee on top of the advertised room rate, plus state and local hotel occupancy taxes** — none of which are included in the headline nightly price the way an all-in UK or European rate usually is. **Always read the total at checkout, not the from-price.** JetMeAway adds no markup and no booking fee of its own, but we cannot remove a fee the hotel itself charges.

**Is tipping expected in Houston?**
Yes, and it is a genuine budget item rather than a nicety. In restaurants and bars **15 to 20 percent** is normal, and some bills already include a service charge for larger groups, so check before adding more. Taxi and rideshare drivers, housekeeping, bellhops and valet parkers are all customarily tipped a few dollars. Budget roughly a fifth on top of every sit-down meal and you will not go far wrong.

**Do I need a car in Houston?**
Effectively yes. **Houston is enormous and built for cars**, and public transport is limited beyond the downtown METRORail line. Most visitors hire a car or budget for Uber and Lyft, and either is a real cost to plan for. Parking is free at most of the budget and suburban hotels here but usually charged downtown and in Uptown. Remember you drive on the right.

**How good is public transport in Houston?**
METRORail runs a small light-rail network linking downtown with Midtown, the Museum District, the Texas Medical Center and NRG Park, and that one corridor is genuinely useful. Beyond it you are on slow buses or in a car. Neither Space Center Houston nor the Galleria is on the rail line. Plan a rail-and-rideshare mix rather than assuming you can do Houston the way you would do London.

Three full days covers the essentials without rushing: one for **Space Center Houston**, which is a half to full day once you add the drive; one for the **Museum District and Hermann Park**; and one for **Buffalo Bayou Park**, downtown and eating your way through Montrose or Bellaire Boulevard. Four or five days lets you add a sports fixture, the Galleria and a day trip to Galveston.

**What should I budget per day in Houston besides the hotel?**
A realistic figure for a UK visitor is about **£55 to £90 a day per person**. Food is the biggest variable — taquerias, Vietnamese noodle houses and barbecue counters feed you well for under £15 a meal, a downtown steakhouse several times that. Then transport, the Houston-specific cost: a hire car with fuel and parking, or £20 to £40 a day in rideshare fares. Museum entry, match tickets or Space Center Houston sit on top, and remember to add 15 to 20 percent tips to restaurant bills.

**What is Space Center Houston and how long should I allow?**
**Space Center Houston** is the official visitor centre for **NASA's Johnson Space Center**, and the single biggest draw in the city. As well as the exhibition halls it runs a **tram tour into the Johnson Space Center campus**, including the historic **Mission Control** room. Allow **at least half a day**, and realistically most of a day once you add the drive each way. Book tickets online in advance, arrive early for the tram, and check the current schedule as tour availability varies.

**How do I get to Space Center Houston from downtown?**
By road. It sits at **Clear Lake, roughly 25 miles south-east** of downtown towards Galveston Bay, a drive of around **35 to 50 minutes** depending on traffic. There is no rail link, so it is a hire car, a rideshare or an organised tour. On-site parking is charged. If you rely on rideshare, plan the return leg too — it is a suburban area and cars are not always instantly available.

**What is the best time of year to visit Houston?**
**Spring and autumn**, comfortably. **March to May and October to November** give warm but manageable temperatures, lower humidity and the best conditions for Buffalo Bayou Park or Hermann Park. Winter is mild by UK standards and cheap. **June to September is seriously hot and humid**, which is worth knowing before booking a summer family trip.

**Is there a hurricane season in Houston?**
Yes. The Atlantic **hurricane season runs roughly from June to November**, and Houston sits close enough to the Gulf coast to be affected by tropical storms, heavy rain and flooding. Most trips are completely unaffected, but if you travel in that window take travel insurance covering weather disruption, watch local forecasts and follow the current UK government travel advice for the USA.

**How hot does Houston get in summer?**
Hot and, more to the point, **very humid** — regularly the mid-thirties Celsius with humidity that makes it feel considerably worse, from June into September. Everything indoors is heavily air-conditioned, so plan outdoor sightseeing for early morning or evening, carry water, and do not underestimate how draining a midday walk is compared with a UK summer day.

**Is Houston safe for tourists?**
Houston is a large American city and the usual big-city rules apply rather than anything exotic. Keep valuables out of sight in a parked car, be aware of your surroundings around quiet downtown blocks late at night, use rideshare rather than walking long distances after dark, and ask your hotel which specific streets to avoid — reception staff give straight, current answers. The Museum District, Hermann Park, the Galleria and the stadium districts on event days are busy and well used. Read the UK government travel advice for the USA before you go.

**Which museums are worth visiting in Houston?**
The **Museum District**, just south of downtown around Hermann Park, holds around a dozen institutions within walking distance of each other. The headline three: the **Museum of Fine Arts, Houston**, one of the largest art museums in the country; the **Houston Museum of Natural Science**, strong on dinosaurs, gems and its butterfly centre; and the **Menil Collection**, a superb modern and antiquities collection that is **free to enter**. The **Rothko Chapel** sits beside the Menil, a quiet non-denominational space built around fourteen Mark Rothko paintings.

**Is anything in Houston free to visit?**
Yes — more than in most US cities. The **Menil Collection** is free to enter, and so is the **Rothko Chapel** next door. **Buffalo Bayou Park** and **Hermann Park** are free public spaces, and the bayou trails give you the best skyline views in the city at no cost. Several Museum District institutions run free or reduced-price sessions on certain days, so check current times on their own websites before planning around it.

**Can I watch sport in Houston?**
Houston is a serious sports city with three major-league teams close to the centre. The **Astros** play baseball at **Minute Maid Park** downtown, walkable from most downtown hotels. The **Texans** play NFL football at **NRG Stadium** by the Medical Center, on the METRORail line. The **Rockets** play basketball at **Toyota Center**, also downtown. Seasons differ, so check what is on for your dates and buy through official team ticketing.

**What is the food like in Houston?**
Genuinely outstanding, and the most underrated reason to come. Houston is one of the most ethnically diverse cities in the United States and the food reflects it: **Tex-Mex** everywhere, a remarkable **Vietnamese** scene along **Bellaire Boulevard** in the south-west, **Gulf seafood** including oysters and crawfish, and **Texas barbecue** in the brisket-and-smoke tradition. Eating well here does not require a big budget — some of the best meals in the city come from strip-mall taquerias and noodle houses.

**Is the Galleria worth visiting?**
If you like shopping, yes — the **Galleria** in Uptown is one of the largest shopping centres in the United States, with hundreds of stores and an ice rink in the middle of it. It also anchors the Uptown hotel district, so several of this guide's smarter hotels sit a short drive away. If shopping is not your thing you can skip it entirely without missing anything of Houston's character.

**Is Houston good for a family trip?**
Very. **Space Center Houston** is a genuine highlight for children, the **Houston Museum of Natural Science** and its butterfly centre are built for families, the **Houston Zoo** sits inside Hermann Park with a miniature railway alongside it, and a baseball game at Minute Maid Park is an easy family afternoon. The practical catches are the summer heat and the driving distances — plan indoor time in the middle of the day and hire a car with enough space.
