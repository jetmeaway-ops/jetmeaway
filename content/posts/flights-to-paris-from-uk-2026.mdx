## Quick answers

**What's the cheapest airline to Paris?** **easyJet** — the most-booked carrier on the route, with off-peak returns often £40-£60 hand-luggage only. Vueling, Air France and BA follow.

**Fly or train?** For **Londoners it's close** — the Eurostar (2h15, city centre to city centre) rivals flying on time and price once transfers are counted. From **outside London, fly**.

**How long is the flight?** About **1 hour 15 minutes** from London.

**Which airport?** **Charles de Gaulle (CDG)** for the best rail link to central Paris; **Orly (ORY)** for the south of the city; **Beauvais (BVA)** only if the fare saving beats the 75-90 minute coach transfer.

**When should I book?** **4-8 weeks** ahead most months; **8-12 weeks** for summer, Christmas and big events.

**Do I need a visa?** No — **90 days visa-free** in Schengen. Check ETIAS status before you travel.

<a id="fly-vs-train"></a>

## Fly or take the Eurostar?

Paris is the route where this question matters most, because the answer genuinely depends on where you live.

**The Eurostar** runs from **London St Pancras to Paris Gare du Nord in about 2 hours 15 minutes**, city centre to city centre, with a generous luggage allowance and no bag fees. On Fridays from spring 2026 there are around 18 Eurostar trains each way alongside a growing number of flights — the two modes now compete hard.

**When the train wins:**
- You're travelling from **London or the South East** — no airport trek, no check-in, and you arrive in the heart of Paris.
- You're carrying more luggage than a budget hand bag allows.
- You value predictability and a lower carbon footprint.

**When flying wins:**
- You're travelling from **anywhere outside London** — a direct flight from Manchester, Birmingham, Edinburgh or your local airport beats travelling to St Pancras first.
- You want the **outright cheapest** option and can travel hand-luggage only — a budget flight can dip below the equivalent train fare, especially short-notice.
- Your dates clash with peak Eurostar pricing, where train fares can spike sharply.

**The honest read:** for a London-based weekend, compare both — they're close enough that the winner changes by date. From everywhere else in the UK, fly.

## Airline-by-airline comparison (2026)

### easyJet — the price and frequency leader

**Typical fare 2026:** £40-£70 return off-peak, hand-luggage only; more at peak.
**UK airports:** Gatwick, Luton, Southend and Bristol, Manchester, Edinburgh and others.
**Pros:** The most-booked carrier on London-Paris (around 64% of bookings). High frequency, low fares, ideal for a hand-luggage weekend.
**Cons:** Hold bag, allocated seat and speedy boarding all cost extra — the cheap fare climbs once you add luggage.

### Vueling — the budget challenger

**Typical fare 2026:** £45-£80 return, hold bag extra on cheapest fares.
**Pros:** Often price-competitive with easyJet, useful frequencies from London.
**Cons:** Cheapest fares are cabin-only; check baggage terms carefully.

### Air France — the full-service option

**Typical fare 2026:** £70-£130 return; standard fares usually include a checked bag.
**Pros:** Flies into CDG with seamless onward connections, most fares include a bag, a snack and allocated seating. Good from regional UK airports.
**Cons:** Higher headline fare than the budget carriers; cheapest "Light" fares are cabin-only, so check the fare type.

### British Airways — Heathrow and London City

**Typical fare 2026:** £75-£140 return; "Basic" fares are cabin-only.
**Pros:** London City is fast through security and central; Avios earning; Heathrow connections.
**Cons:** Only two London airports; cheapest fares are hand-luggage only.

**The honest read:** hand-luggage only from London, easyJet or Vueling almost always win on price. Need a checked bag or flying from a regional airport, Air France's standard fare is often better value than it looks. BA is worth the premium mainly for London City's speed.

## Which Paris airport should you fly into?

| Airport | Distance to centre | Transfer | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Charles de Gaulle (CDG) | ~25km NE | RER B train, 35-45 min | Best rail link, most flights, onward connections |
| Orly (ORY) | ~13km S | Orlyval + RER, or tram, 35-45 min | South Paris, often slightly quieter |
| Beauvais (BVA) | ~80km N | Coach, 75-90 min | Only if the fare saving beats the long transfer |

**The Beauvais trap.** Some budget fares land at Beauvais, marketed as "Paris." It's around 80km away, with a coach transfer of 75-90 minutes each way that adds cost and time. A Beauvais fare has to be meaningfully cheaper than a CDG or Orly fare to be worth it once you price the transfer — check the all-in cost and total journey time before booking on headline price alone.

## UK airport comparison

London has the most choice by far — up to five airports fly to Paris, with more than 50 flights a day at peak across the carriers, plus the Eurostar. That competition keeps London-Paris fares among the lowest in Europe.

From **regional UK airports** (Manchester, Birmingham, Edinburgh and others), the choice is narrower and the Eurostar isn't an option — so flying is the clear pick, usually on easyJet or Air France. If you're a regional traveller, always price your local direct flight rather than assuming you need to route through London.

## The booking windows that actually save money

- **Off-peak months (Jan, Feb, late Sep, Oct, Nov):** Book **4-8 weeks out**.
- **Summer (Jul-Aug) and Christmas/New Year:** Book **8-12 weeks out** — fixed demand, fares only rise.
- **Event dates (fashion weeks, major fixtures):** Book **8-12 weeks out** or earlier — both flights and hotels spike hard on these specific dates.
- **Weekend breaks:** Friday-out, Sunday-back is the priciest pattern; a Thursday or Saturday departure often saves £20-£50.

Set a price alert for your dates and, for London trips, watch the Eurostar fare alongside the flight — whichever drops first wins.

## Entry rules for UK travellers in 2026

- **No visa** for stays up to **90 days in any 180-day period** across the Schengen area.
- A **passport issued within the last 10 years** and **valid for at least 3 months** after your planned departure from Schengen — both are enforced at the border.
- **ETIAS travel authorisation** once the scheme launches in 2026 — check the current status before you travel.
- UK travellers use the **e-gates** at CDG and Orly where available, keeping arrival fast.

Always check the [UK government's France travel advice](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/france) shortly before you fly.

## Common mistakes UK-Paris travellers make

1. **Not comparing the Eurostar for London trips.** From London the train can beat the plane on total time and convenience — always check both.
2. **Booking a Beauvais fare without pricing the transfer.** Beauvais is 80km out; the coach adds 75-90 minutes each way.
3. **Judging airlines on the headline fare.** easyJet's £50 fare plus a £40 hold bag can cost more than an Air France fare that includes one.
4. **Ignoring event dates.** Fashion weeks and big fixtures spike specific dates — check before you book.
5. **Adding baggage at the airport.** Always add it online at booking.
6. **Assuming an old passport is fine.** The Schengen 10-year issue rule catches UK travellers out.
7. **Flying with a checked bag for two nights.** Paris is a hand-luggage city break.
