## 4. Drinking ages and drug law — what actually applies where

This is the section parents will want you to have read, and honestly, the section that keeps trips out of trouble. Legal drinking age is 18 across nearly every destination on this list — but "legal" and "how it's actually enforced" aren't the same thing, and a couple of specific rules trip up UK students every summer.

**The 18-everywhere baseline.** Spain, Greece, Bulgaria, Portugal, Czechia, Hungary, Poland, Serbia, Latvia, Lithuania and Croatia all set the drinking age at 18, matching the UK. Two exceptions worth knowing: **Germany** allows beer and wine from 16 but spirits only from 18, and **Austria** varies by state — generally 16 for beer/wine, 18 for spirits. The **Netherlands** raised its drinking age to 18 for everything in 2014 (it used to be 16 for weaker drinks) — a lot of outdated "Amsterdam is 16" advice online is simply wrong now.

**ID matters more than you'd think.** Bars and off-licences in Krakow, Prague and Budapest check ID more consistently on tourists than UK venues do — bring your passport or a UK driving licence, not just a student card, and expect to be asked even if you're clearly over 18.

**Amsterdam and cannabis — the part people get wrong.** Cannabis is **not legal** in the Netherlands. It is decriminalised and tolerated for personal use through licensed coffeeshops under a specific enforcement policy, with a working possession/purchase limit (around 5g). Buying from a street dealer is illegal and is genuinely how most tourist drug problems start — fake or dangerous product, robbery, or a straightforward police matter. Anything beyond cannabis is fully illegal with real enforcement, including spot checks at Schiphol on departure. Treat "tolerated" as exactly that — not a green light for anything else.

**Malia, Zante and the balcony problem.** This isn't a legal issue, it's a physical one that UK Foreign Office advisories flag every single summer: balcony-hopping between hotel rooms has caused multiple serious injuries and deaths among young UK tourists in these specific resorts. Never climb a balcony, never lean over a railing after drinking, and treat any hotel room above the ground floor with the same caution you'd want a younger sibling to use.

**Package operators sometimes handle this better than DIY.** Because branded "18-30" or grad-trip packages from TUI or Jet2Holidays publish an explicit age policy and check ID at check-in or the resort desk, there's less ambiguity than booking a DIY room where the age policy is buried in a hotel's own terms. If your group has anyone who might still be 17 on departure, read every hotel's minimum-age policy individually before paying, or consider a package with a clearly stated 18+ (or family-friendly) rule instead.

<a id="safety"></a>

## 5. Safety: what actually goes wrong on these trips

Not to lecture — but the actual risk profile of a first no-parents trip is different from what most safety guides cover, and worth five minutes before you book.

- **Heatstroke and dehydration**, not crime, is the single most common reason UK students end up in a foreign A&E on these trips. Day-drinking in 30°C+ heat (Antalya, Zante, Sunny Beach in July-August) dehydrates faster than people expect. Alternate alcoholic drinks with water, and take a genuine midday break out of direct sun.
- **Drink spiking** is a real risk in high-density nightlife resorts. Never leave a drink unattended, watch it being poured where possible, and agree a "everyone's phone charged, everyone checks in" rule with the group before the first night out.
- **Moped and ATV rentals without a valid licence** are illegal in every country on this list and voids most travel insurance if you're injured. Resort touts renting "no licence needed" scooters are not operating legally — decline, however cheap it looks.
- **Flight refusal for pre-flight drinking.** Ryanair, easyJet, Jet2, TUI and Wizz Air can and do refuse boarding to visibly intoxicated passengers, with no refund. Every summer, UK student groups lose the cost of their outbound flight this way. Save the big session for arrival night, not the airport.
- **Travel insurance is not optional for this trip type.** Buy it the day you book flights, not the week before you fly, and check specifically that it doesn't exclude alcohol-related incidents outright (some budget policies do) — you want cover that still applies to an ordinary accident that happened to occur on a night out, not one that funds reckless behaviour.
- **112 works everywhere in the EU** (and the UK) as the single emergency number for police, ambulance or fire. Save it, and save your travel insurer's 24-hour emergency line as a screenshot, not just in an email you might not have signal to open.

None of this should put you off — these resorts host hundreds of thousands of UK 18-25s every summer without incident. The point is to know the two or three things that actually cause the problems, because they're specific and avoidable.

## 7. Package vs DIY — the real maths, destination by destination

The honest answer is "it depends," and it depends predictably.

**Where DIY wins clearly (20-30% cheaper):** Krakow, Budapest, Prague, Belgrade, Sofia, Riga, Vilnius, Barcelona, Amsterdam, Berlin. These are city-break destinations where UK tour operators don't dominate the market with block-bought charter capacity — you're competing against individual hotel and flight prices, not a bulk-buyer's discount, so a comparison search consistently beats a bundled "student break" product.

**Where the gap narrows (10-20%):** Zante, Malia, Sunny Beach, Magaluf, Antalya. TUI, Jet2Holidays and similar operators buy charter flight seats and resort room allocations in bulk months ahead, at a cost-per-unit an individual traveller can't access. DIY is still usually cheaper, but by a smaller margin, and a well-timed early-bird package deal can occasionally beat DIY outright — worth a direct comparison before you commit either way.

**For context** — a typical UK "grad package" to one of these destinations sells at **£700-£1,200/person all-in for 7 nights**. Booking the same hotel + flight separately on JetMeAway routinely lands the same trip at **£300-£650/person** for the city-break destinations, £400-£750 for the resort destinations. The savings sit mostly in the operator's margin, not in the experience.

## 8. Booking timeline for summer 2026

Celebration trips cluster around two fixed dates: **A-Level results day** (the Thursday before the third Thursday of August) and **GCSE results day** (the third Thursday of August itself). Both are known well in advance even though your actual grades aren't.

- **April-May 2026:** Book flights and hotel now against a provisional date range spanning results week. This is 10-14+ weeks out and captures the best fares — European short-haul prices bottom out in this window and climb steadily as summer approaches.
- **June:** Still workable, expect 10-20% higher fares than April/May booking for the same dates.
- **Mid-July onward:** Last-minute booking for an August trip. Expect to pay 40-80% more for flights and rooms than the April price, and popular party-resort hotels sell out of the cheap room categories first.
- **Cancellation flexibility matters more than usual here** — grades are unknown until results day, so where possible pick a hotel rate with free cancellation up to a week or two before travel, even if it costs a few pounds more than the non-refundable rate.

## FAQ

**What's the cheapest 'first squad trip' for UK students in summer 2026?**
Sunny Beach (Bulgaria) is the rock-bottom winner — 7 nights total cost from around £230-£350 per person sharing, including flights. Krakow and Sofia come next at £220-£440. For sun-and-sea on a tighter budget, Golden Sands and Zante's cheaper rooms land around £280-£450 for 7 nights all-in.

**Is it safe to book separately for a first 'no-parents' trip?**
Yes — pay by credit card so any element over £100 is Section 75 protected, buy travel insurance the day you book (with cancellation cover and Scheduled Airline Failure cover), and use a single itinerary app like TripIt to keep all confirmations in one place. JetMeAway lists only verified providers — no random listings.

**Are 'graduation packages' actually a rip-off?**
Not always — but the headline is usually inflated. The same Antalya 4-star all-inclusive sold by an operator at £680/person for 7 nights regularly comes in around £540 booked direct on JetMeAway.

**How young can you be to book a hotel solo in Europe?**
Most European hotels accept guests from 18, but a handful of 'party resort' hotels in Magaluf, Ibiza and Sunny Beach require all guests in the room to be 18+ on arrival. Always read the property's age policy before booking.

**Can we book one room for a group of friends?**
Yes — most 3-star/4-star hotels offer triple and quad rooms, ideal for 3-4 friends sharing and dropping the per-person cost 40-50% versus solo rooms.

**What's the legal drinking age in the main student celebration destinations?**
18 in almost every destination on this list, with Germany allowing beer/wine from 16 (spirits at 18) and Austria varying by state. The Netherlands has been 18-for-everything since 2014.

**Is Zante, Kavos or Malia actually dangerous for first-time travellers?**
Generally safe with normal precautions. The real risks are self-inflicted — heatstroke, balcony injuries (a recurring problem specifically in Zante and Malia), and drink spiking. Read the UK Foreign Office's resort-specific advice before you go.

**What is the realistic total cost including 'hidden' extras like transfers and eSIM?**
Add roughly £40-£70 per person on top of the flight+hotel total for transfers, an eSIM, one big night out, and travel insurance.

**Do I need a passport, and how long should it be valid?**
Yes, a full UK passport with at least 3 months' validity beyond your return date for EU/Schengen destinations. UK passport cards and driving licences are not accepted abroad.

**Which destinations are cheapest from Manchester, Birmingham and Edinburgh rather than London?**
Krakow, Budapest and Prague all have strong direct Manchester routes at prices close to London's. Edinburgh has fewer direct budget routes to Sofia, Belgrade, Riga and Vilnius — expect £20-£40 more or a connection.

**Is it cheaper to book flights and hotel as a package or separately?**
For classic UK charter resorts (Zante, Malia, Sunny Beach) packages can be competitive if booked early. For city breaks (Krakow, Budapest, Prague, Barcelona, Amsterdam, Berlin) DIY booking is almost always 15-30% cheaper.

**What's the single most common mistake groups make booking these trips?**
Booking flights before the whole group agrees dates. Second: not checking a hotel's minimum-age policy before paying a non-refundable rate.

**How much should each person budget as a realistic minimum for 2026?**
£280-£400 all-in for the cheapest tier (Sunny Beach, Sofia, Krakow, Belgrade), £380-£550 for the mid tier (Budapest, Prague, Riga, Vilnius, Lisbon), £500-£850 for the premium tier (Barcelona, Ibiza, Amsterdam), with Ibiza able to exceed £900 in August.

**Are there destinations genuinely not suitable for under-18s?**
Ibiza and Malia's strip-adjacent 'party hotels' are effectively 18+ by house policy. Amsterdam's coffeeshop culture rewards extra caution for an under-18 group. None are formally banned, but they reward more preparation.

**What happens if someone in the group is still 17 when the trip departs?**
Check every hotel's specific age policy individually — city hotels are usually fine, party-resort properties often require all guests to be 18. Some airlines also want a signed parental consent letter for minors travelling without a parent.

**Are drugs legal in Amsterdam, and what should students actually know?**
No — cannabis is decriminalised and tolerated through licensed coffeeshops, not fully legal. Street dealers are illegal and the actual source of most tourist trouble. Harder drugs are fully illegal with real enforcement, including at Schiphol.

**How do package holidays handle the under-18 problem DIY booking runs into?**
Tour operators publish explicit age policies and check ID at the airport desk or resort, removing ambiguity — a genuine reason to consider a package if your group's ages are uncertain.

**What is Ryanair, easyJet, Wizz Air or Jet2's policy on rowdy groups?**
All can refuse boarding to visibly intoxicated passengers with no refund. Every summer, UK groups lose their outbound flight cost this way — pace pre-flight drinking.

**Which destinations have the best package-vs-DIY savings for 2026?**
Krakow, Budapest, Prague, Belgrade, Sofia, Riga and Vilnius show the biggest DIY savings (20-30%) versus bundled products, because they lack dominant UK charter-package infrastructure.

**Do I need travel insurance, and what should it cover?**
Yes, non-negotiably. Look for alcohol-incident cover specifically (many standard policies exclude it), personal liability, and lost-passport/repatriation cover. Annual student policies run £30-£45/year.

**What's the cheapest way to get from the airport to the resort?**
Public buses or trains almost everywhere — Sofia's airport bus is under £1, Prague's AE express is around £3, Budapest's 100E bus about £4.50. Compare against a shared taxi split across the group.

**How far in advance should a group book a summer 2026 trip?**
10-14 weeks ahead for the best fares. Book in April-May against a provisional results-week date range, since exact results dates are known even though grades aren't.

**Is it worth getting an ISIC or NUS card before a celebration trip?**
Marginally — small discounts on museums, hostels and transport, but neither meaningfully discounts flights or resort hotels for this trip type.

**What should a group do if someone gets seriously ill or has an accident abroad?**
Call 112 (works across the EU and UK) and separately call your travel insurer's 24-hour emergency line as soon as it's safe. Save both numbers offline, not just in an email.

**Can UK students under 18 legally rent a moped or ATV on these trips?**
Almost never without a full driving licence held for the required minimum period. "No licence needed" resort rentals are operating illegally and void most travel insurance if you're injured.

**What's the difference between booking through JetMeAway and a 'student break' company?**
JetMeAway is a comparison engine showing live prices across verified providers with no markup added by us. A 'student break' company is typically a reseller adding its own margin on top of prices it has already bought.

**Do these destinations require any vaccinations or health prep?**
No mandatory vaccinations beyond standard UK immunisations. Standard travel-health advice and a solid travel insurance policy with medical evacuation cover apply.

**What's the best way to split costs fairly within a group?**
Use a shared expense app (Splitwise, Tricount) from the day of booking, and agree a per-person cap for the big night out before you travel.

**Which destinations are best for a mixed group that wants some culture alongside the party?**
Krakow, Prague, Budapest and Belgrade pair serious nightlife with genuinely worthwhile daytime sightseeing within walking distance of the party districts — a better fit for a split-minded group than pure beach resorts.
