## The Scout's Take: Grossbasel, Kleinbasel or the Station?

**Grossbasel** — the left-bank Old Town — is where you want to be for a first visit: Marktplatz, the Münster, Barfüsserplatz and the main museums are a flat walk apart, and the Rhine is at the bottom of the hill. It carries the higher rates.

**Kleinbasel**, the right bank across the Mittlere Brücke, holds the Messe trade-fair halls, a livelier riverside bar strip, and generally cheaper hotels a tram-ride from the sights — the value play if there is no fair on.

**Gundeldingen** (the 'Gundeli'), directly behind **Basel SBB** station, is where the genuinely cheap beds cluster — chain hotels, serviced apartments and self-check-in rooms, all a few tram stops from the centre. Every Basel hotel guest gets a **free BaselCard** covering trams and buses, so staying a stop or two out costs you nothing in transport.

For a first trip: Grossbasel. For value: Kleinbasel or the station. For the lowest prices: this guide's [affordable tier](#budget) — and, if you are stretching every franc, the tram-linked border suburbs of Saint-Louis (France) and Weil am Rhein (Germany).

## Basel Hotels FAQs

**How much does a cheap hotel in Basel cost per night?**
On the searches we ran while writing, the lowest genuine rates start around £76 a night — City Pop 2Night's self-check-in rooms — with the well-reviewed B&B Hotel Basel from about £83 and Aparthotel Adagio from £84. Most solid budget rooms sit in the £80–130 band, with a handful of design hotels and central apartments reaching about £175 at the top of the tier. Switzerland is an expensive country, so that £76 floor is the cheaper end, not a bargain-basement number; there is no £30 Basel hotel the way there is in Southern Europe.

**Is Basel expensive for hotels?**
Yes, by European standards. Basel is a wealthy Swiss business and pharma city, prices are in Swiss francs, and even simple 2–3 star rooms rarely dip below about £75–85 a night. Trade-fair weeks (Art Basel in June, watch and industry fairs) push rates far higher. The upside is that the cheaper end is still clean, safe and central — you are paying Swiss prices for Swiss reliability, not for glamour.

**What is the cheapest area to stay in Basel?**
Gundeldingen (the 'Gundeli' quarter directly behind Basel SBB station) and Kleinbasel — the right bank of the Rhine around the Messe trade-fair halls — hold most of the lower rates, and both are a short tram ride or walk from the Old Town. The apartment blocks and chain hotels near the station (ibis, B&B, City Pop, VISIONAPARTMENTS) are where the genuinely cheap beds cluster. Staying a tram stop or two out, rather than on Marktplatz, is the single biggest Basel saving.

**Which month is cheapest to visit Basel?**
January to March and November are the quietest and cheapest, outside the Christmas market weeks. Avoid Art Basel in June and the big industry fairs, when city-wide rates can double or triple and rooms sell out months ahead. Midweek nights are usually cheaper than weekends in this business-driven city — except during a fair, when the pattern flips.

**Are there hostels or budget apartments in Basel?**
Yes. Silo Design & Boutique Hostel occupies a converted grain silo at Erlenmatt with both dorm and private rooms; Hyve Hotel Basel is a modern pod-style hostel-hotel. For self-catering, VISIONAPARTMENTS (from ~£95), Aparthotel Adagio (~£84), the Swiss Star serviced flats and HITrental's Marktplatz apartments give you a kitchen — often the best value for families and longer stays, since eating out in Basel is where the francs really go.

**Can I stay cheaper across the border in France or Germany?**
Sometimes, yes — Basel is a tri-border city. Saint-Louis in France (tram 3) and Weil am Rhein in Germany (tram 8) are both reachable on the Basel tram network and can be cheaper for hotels and for eating out. The trade-off is a border crossing each way and a slightly less central base. For a first Basel trip most people still prefer to stay in the city itself; for a longer or tighter-budget stay, the German and French suburbs are a real option.

**Do hotels in Basel give you a free travel card?**
Yes — this is Basel's best-kept money saver. Every guest staying in a Basel hotel, hostel or campsite receives a BaselCard, which includes free use of public transport (trams and buses) for the length of your stay, plus 50% off many museums and attractions. It is issued at check-in. Factor it in: it can save a family a meaningful amount over a few days, and it makes staying a tram ride out of the centre painless.

**What is the best-rated hotel in Basel?**
Grand Hotel Les Trois Rois — a riverside grande dame dating to 1681 and one of the oldest city hotels in Europe. It sits directly on the Rhine at the edge of the Old Town and holds the three-Michelin-starred Cheval Blanc by Peter Knogl. It is the city's only five-star and its benchmark address; rooms start around £507 a night, so it is a splurge, but nothing else in Basel matches it for pedigree.

**Where is the best area to stay in Basel for sightseeing?**
Grossbasel — the left-bank Old Town around Marktplatz, the red-sandstone Münster cathedral and Barfüsserplatz — puts you within a flat walk of most sights, the Rhine and the main museums. Hotels like Märthof, Nomad, SET by Teufelhof and the central apartments sit here. If you would rather be by the water or near the trade fair, Kleinbasel on the right bank is a tram-ride away and generally cheaper.

**Is Basel a good base for Art Basel?**
It is the base for Art Basel — the fair takes place at Messe Basel in Kleinbasel every June. If you are attending, book as far ahead as you can and expect rates several times the off-season figure; hotels near the Messe (Novotel, Pullman, Hyperion, ibis Styles, Hotel Wettstein) are walkable to the halls. Outside fair week those same hotels are among the better-value 3–4 star options in the city.

**How do I get from EuroAirport (BSL) to Basel city centre?**
EuroAirport Basel-Mulhouse-Freiburg sits about 5 km northwest of the centre, in France. Bus 50 runs to Basel SBB station in roughly 15–20 minutes; ask for the free ticket from the airport machine using your onward reservation, or your BaselCard covers city transport once you are checked in. A taxi to the Old Town takes about 15 minutes. Use the 'Switzerland' exit at the airport to reach the Basel-bound transport.

**Are there direct flights from the UK to Basel?**
Yes — easyJet flies to EuroAirport Basel (BSL) direct from several UK airports including London Gatwick, Luton and others, in about 1 hour 40 minutes to 2 hours. It is one of the most convenient Swiss cities to reach from the UK without changing planes. Search live fares before you fix your dates, as budget-airline prices swing a lot by day.

**Is Basel walkable?**
Very. The Old Town core is compact and largely flat, the Rhine is crossed by several bridges plus the charming little ferries pulled across by the current, and trams reach everywhere else. Most visitors barely use anything but their feet and the free tram card. It is one of the easier Swiss cities to do without a car.

**Which hotels in Basel are near the SBB train station?**
Hotel Euler and Hotel Schweizerhof sit directly opposite Basel SBB; Radisson Blu, ibis Basel Bahnhof, Hotel Resslirytti and the Gundeldingen apartments are all a short walk behind it. The station is the arrival point for trains from Zurich, Geneva and the rest of Switzerland, so a station-side base is handy if you are combining Basel with the wider SBB rail network.

**Is Basel safe for tourists?**
Basel is a very safe, orderly Swiss city with low crime, well-lit streets and reliable public transport late into the evening. Normal city sense around the station at night is all that is needed. Families, solo travellers and older visitors all find it comfortable.

**What currency is used in Basel and are euros accepted?**
The Swiss franc (CHF), not the euro. Some shops and hotels near the borders accept euros but give change in francs at a poor rate, so pay in francs or by card. Prices everywhere — meals, transport, coffee — are high by UK standards, which is worth planning around more than the hotel rate itself.

**How many days do you need in Basel?**
Two full days covers the Old Town, the Rhine, a swim or a ferry crossing in summer, and one or two of the world-class museums. A third day lets you add Fondation Beyeler, the Vitra Design Museum just over the German border, or a day trip by train. Basel also works well as a one-night art-and-river stop on a wider Swiss rail trip.

**What are the best hotels in Basel for families?**
Self-catering wins here: Aparthotel Adagio (~£84), VISIONAPARTMENTS (~£95) and the Swiss Star serviced flats give you a kitchen and space, which tames Basel's high eating-out costs. ODELYA sits in quiet parkland near the zoo, Novotel has family rooms near the Messe, and the free BaselCard transport makes getting kids around effortless. The zoo (Zolli) and the Rhine ferries are both big hits with children.

**What are the best hotels in Basel for couples?**
Grand Hotel Les Trois Rois for a romantic splurge on the Rhine; Nomad, Volkshaus and ART HOUSE for design-led boutique stays; SET by Teufelhof for an arty Old Town base with its own theatre and wine cellar. For a lower budget, Dasbreitehotel and Hotel D bring boutique style at 3–4 star prices.

**Which hotels in Basel are right on the Rhine?**
Grand Hotel Les Trois Rois has the finest riverside position, with Rhine-facing rooms and terrace. On a budget, Hotel Rheinfelderhof sits by the Mittlere Brücke almost on the water, and Merian am Rhein and several Kleinbasel hotels are a few steps from the promenade where locals swim and drift downstream in summer.

**Can you swim in the Rhine in Basel?**
Yes, and it is a beloved local ritual in summer. Baslers float down the Rhine on warm days using a Wickelfisch — a waterproof dry-bag that doubles as a float for your clothes — getting out at the steps downstream. The water is clean and the current does the work. It is free, it is the most Basel thing you can do, and riverside hotels put you right by the entry points.

**Is Basel or Zurich better for a city break?**
Zurich is bigger, busier and better for shopping and nightlife; Basel is smaller, more walkable and unmatched for art — Kunstmuseum, Fondation Beyeler, Tinguely and Art Basel itself. Basel is also the cheaper of the two on average hotel rates and has direct UK flights of its own. For a culture-and-river weekend, many travellers prefer Basel; for a first Swiss city trip with day trips, Zurich edges it. See our Zurich guide to compare.

**Do hotels in Basel have air conditioning?**
Most of the 4-star and modern chain hotels do, but many smaller and older 2–3 star Basel hotels rely on fans or openable windows, as Swiss summers are traditionally mild. If you are travelling in a June or July heatwave and it matters, check the individual hotel page before booking — we list amenities per property.

**Are hotels in Basel cheaper on weekends?**
Usually yes, because Basel runs on business and pharma travel — Sunday and Saturday nights are often cheaper than midweek in normal weeks. The exception is trade-fair and event weeks, above all Art Basel in June, when weekend rates spike and rooms vanish. If your dates are flexible, a fair-free weekend is often the cheapest way to see Basel.

**How far is Basel from other Swiss cities by train?**
Fast and easy on the SBB network from Basel SBB: Zurich is about 55 minutes, Bern around 55 minutes, Lucerne about 1 hour, Geneva under 3 hours, and Interlaken about 2 hours. Basel is Switzerland's northern rail gateway, so it pairs naturally with a wider rail itinerary — and your onward Swiss train tickets are separate from the free city BaselCard.
