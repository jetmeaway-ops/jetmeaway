## Quick answers

**What's the cheapest airline to Manchester?** From **Belfast**, easyJet or Aer Lingus (from ~£25-£50 off-peak). From **Scotland and the South West**, Loganair.

**How long is the flight?** About **1 hour** for most routes — ~3-4 hours door-to-door.

**Which UK cities fly?** Mainly **Belfast, Scotland (Aberdeen, Inverness, Edinburgh) and the South West (Exeter, Southampton)** — plus the islands.

**Fly or train?** From **London, Birmingham and Leeds, the train wins**. From **Belfast, the far South West and northern Scotland, fly**.

**When should I book?** **3-6 weeks** ahead most months; **6-10 weeks** for summer, events and Christmas.

**Do I need a passport?** No — it's a **domestic** flight, but airlines require **photo ID**.

<a id="uk-cities"></a>

## Which UK cities fly direct to Manchester

Domestic flights to Manchester concentrate where the rail journey is long or crosses water:

- **Belfast** — Belfast City (Aer Lingus, easyJet) and Belfast International (easyJet) — a sea crossing, so flying dominates.
- **Scotland** — Aberdeen, Inverness and Edinburgh (Loganair) — long by rail.
- **South West** — Exeter and Southampton (Loganair), plus Newquay.
- **Islands** — Isle of Man and the Channel Islands.

Cities with fast rail links — London, Birmingham, Leeds, Liverpool, Sheffield — are better served by train, so there are few or no flights on those routes.

### easyJet & Aer Lingus — the Belfast leaders

**Typical fare 2026:** £25-£60 return off-peak, hand-luggage only (easyJet); Aer Lingus cheapest fares cabin-only.

**Typical fare 2026:** £50-£110 return depending on route.
**Pros:** The main operator from Scotland (Aberdeen, Inverness, Edinburgh) and the South West (Exeter, Southampton) to Manchester — often the only direct option from those cities.
**Cons:** Higher headline fare than the ultra-budget carriers; check the fare for baggage.

**The honest read:** from Belfast, easyJet or Aer Lingus win on fare. From Scotland and the South West, Loganair is usually the only direct choice. From fast-rail cities, take the train instead.

<a id="fly-vs-train"></a>

## Fly or take the train to Manchester?

Manchester is one of the best-connected UK cities by rail, so the mode depends heavily on your origin:

- **From London, Birmingham, Leeds, Liverpool, Sheffield:** the **train wins** — fast, frequent services (London-Manchester about 2-2.5 hours) beat flying door-to-door. Take the train.
- **From Belfast:** fly (or ferry-plus-drive) — a sea crossing means no direct rail.
- **From the far South West (Exeter, Newquay) and northern Scotland:** fly — the rail journey is very long.

Match the mode to your city: rail for the well-connected cities, air for the ones without a fast link.

<a id="hub"></a>

## Manchester as an international hub

Manchester is the UK's largest airport outside London, with a huge international and long-haul network. Many domestic flights (from Belfast, Scotland and the South West) act as **feeders**, letting travellers connect onto European and long-haul flights at Manchester rather than routing through London. If Manchester is a **connection** rather than your destination, book the domestic and onward legs on one ticket where possible, and allow enough connection time.

## Do you need ID or a passport?

Manchester is in the UK, so a flight from elsewhere in Britain is **domestic** — no passport is legally required. But **every airline requires an accepted form of photo ID**, and each sets its own list. Most accept a valid passport, and some accept a photo driving licence on domestic routes. **Check your airline's accepted-ID list and take an ID it accepts.**

## Common mistakes UK-Manchester travellers make

1. **Flying from a fast-rail city.** From London, Birmingham or Leeds the train beats flying — check it first.
2. **Judging fares on the headline price.** Add airport transfers and a hold bag and a "cheap" flight can cost more.
3. **Travelling with no accepted photo ID.** It's domestic, but airlines require ID — take one they accept.
4. **Tight connections onto international flights.** If Manchester is a hub, allow enough connection time and book on one ticket.
5. **Adding baggage at the airport.** Always add it online, or travel hand-luggage only.
6. **Ignoring event dates.** Concerts and football spike hotels — check the calendar.
7. **Assuming there's a London-Manchester flight.** The route is dominated by rail — take the train.
