## Quick answers

**What's the cheapest way to fly to Bangkok?** A **one-stop** routing via Doha, Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Istanbul or Singapore — from ~£450-£650 wet-season. **Direct** on EVA, Thai or BA costs more but saves hours.

**How long is the flight?** About **12 hours** direct; **14-18 hours** one-stop including the layover.

**Which UK airports fly direct?** Mainly **London** — EVA and Thai from Heathrow, BA from Gatwick. Elsewhere, connect via a hub.

**Do I need a visa?** UK travellers enter **visa-free** for tourism, but the length is **changing from 60 to 30 days** — check the current rule. Complete the **free TDAC** online before arrival.

**When should I book?** **3-5 months** ahead for the Nov-Feb peak and Christmas; **8-12 weeks** for the cheaper wet season.

## Direct vs one-stop: the honest trade-off

Bangkok is one of the classic long-haul routes where this is the key decision.

**Direct (EVA Air, Thai Airways, British Airways) wins on:**
- Total journey time — about 12 hours versus 14-18 hours one-stop.
- Simplicity — one flight, no missed-connection risk, no hunting for a transit gate.
- Arrival freshness — you're on the ground hours sooner.

**A one-stop routing (Qatar, Emirates, Etihad, Turkish, Singapore) wins on:**
- Price — typically £100-£300 cheaper than a direct fare.
- Choice of UK departure airport — connect from almost anywhere, not just London.
- Onboard product and the option of a stopover in the Gulf, Istanbul or Singapore.

**Our honest read:** direct is worth a premium of around £150-£200 for the time saved on a 12-hour trip, especially if you value arriving fresher. Beyond that gap, the one-stop option is the better-value choice for most travellers — and from a regional UK airport, a one-stop routing is often the only sensible option anyway.

## Airline-by-airline comparison (2026)

### Direct: EVA Air & Thai Airways (Heathrow)

**Typical fare 2026:** £600-£1,000 return depending on season.
**Pros:** Non-stop from Heathrow in about 12 hours. EVA Air is highly rated with two 23kg bags on most economy fares; Thai Airways offers a generous 30kg allowance and a warm onboard service. Fastest way to Bangkok.
**Cons:** Higher fares than one-stop routings; Heathrow only.

### Direct: British Airways (Gatwick)

**Typical fare 2026:** £600-£1,050 return.
**Pros:** Non-stop from Gatwick, Avios earning, a bag on standard fares.
**Cons:** Cheapest "Basic" fares include zero checked baggage — check the fare type. Gatwick only.

### One-stop: Qatar, Emirates, Etihad, Turkish, Singapore

**Typical fare 2026:** £450-£800 return, 1 stop.
**Pros:** Usually the cheapest options, excellent onboard products, 25-30kg baggage, and connections from many UK airports. Optional stopovers in the Gulf, Istanbul or Singapore.
**Cons:** The layover adds several hours; total journey 14-18 hours. Check the connection time before booking on price alone.

**The honest read:** for the fastest trip, EVA, Thai or BA direct from London. For the lowest price and departures from regional airports, a one-stop Gulf or Asian carrier. Compare the all-in price with the baggage you need.

## The booking windows that actually save money

- **Peak cool-dry season (Nov-Feb) and Christmas:** Book **3-5 months ahead** — demand is high and fares only rise.
- **Songkran (mid-April):** Book **3-4 months ahead** — Thai New Year is a major travel spike.
- **Wet season (Jun-Oct):** Book **8-12 weeks ahead** — the cheapest window; booking too early rarely helps.
- **Direct vs one-stop:** Re-check both close to booking — the gap between a direct EVA/Thai/BA fare and a one-stop Gulf fare changes with demand.

## Visa and arrival card (UK passport holders, 2026)

- **Visa-free tourism, but the length is changing.** UK passport holders enter Thailand visa-free — the stay has been **60 days per entry**, and in May 2026 the Thai cabinet approved cutting it to **30 days**, taking effect after publication in the Royal Gazette. Because this is in flux, **check the current visa-free length** on the [UK government's Thailand travel advice](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/thailand) before you book, especially for a longer trip.
- **Passport validity:** at least **6 months** from your date of entry.
- **Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC):** everyone must complete this **free** online form before arrival, on the official Thai government site — it replaced the paper card. Avoid third-party sites charging a fee for it.

<a id="common-mistakes"></a>

## Common mistakes UK-Bangkok travellers make

1. **Assuming the 60-day visa-free stay still applies.** It's being cut to 30 days — check the current rule before planning a long trip.
2. **Paying a third-party site for the TDAC.** The arrival card is free on the official Thai government site.
3. **Comparing a BA Basic fare to a full EVA/Thai fare.** BA Basic includes no checked bag — compare the all-in price.
4. **Booking peak season or Christmas late.** Nov-Feb and the festive fortnight are the priciest — book 3-5 months ahead.
5. **Overlooking the wet-season value window.** September-October fares are the cheapest of the year, and the rain is usually short-lived.
6. **Ignoring one-stop routings from a regional airport.** From outside London, a one-stop Gulf fare is often cheaper and just as practical as a direct London flight.
7. **Adding baggage at the airport.** Always add extra bags online in advance on long-haul.
